## Overview

Empire hosts interview Michael Weisz, the new CEO of Kamino, on why he left traditional fintech to build onchain credit infrastructure. The through-line is a single question: is tokenization pointless until you can borrow against the tokenized asset? Weisz argues that the missing layer is onchain credit — a market where private and illiquid assets can function as collateral. The conversation covers Kamino's "invisible distribution" strategy, why Goldman Sachs will not lend against private assets, why venture holdings cannot yet be used as collateral, and how his personal upside is tied to the $KMNO token rather than equity.

## Key Points

- Central thesis: tokenization creates little value on its own; the value arrives when tokenized assets become borrowable collateral.
- Weisz explains why he left fintech for crypto and specifically why he joined Kamino.
- Kamino's "invisible distribution" strategy — the company aims to be the credit layer underneath other products rather than the consumer-facing brand.
- Why Goldman will not lend on private assets, and what that gap implies for onchain credit markets.
- Why venture capital positions cannot yet be used as collateral, and which classes of assets actually make good collateral.
- Whether DeFi can attract top asset managers, and what it takes to bring institutions onchain (framed as four pillars).
- Mechanics of collateral and liquidations when running Kamino, plus operational discipline — "running Kamino like a public company."
- Why he chose a token over equity, and what that means for his own incentives.

## Implications

The investment-relevant claim is that the credit layer, not the tokenization layer, is where durable economics accrue — so assets that are hard to borrow against today (private credit, venture, illiquid funds) are the addressable market. The risk sits in liquidation mechanics and collateral quality, which is exactly where Weisz says the design work is concentrated.
