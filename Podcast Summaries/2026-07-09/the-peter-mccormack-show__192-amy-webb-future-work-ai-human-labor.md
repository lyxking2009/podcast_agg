# #192 - Amy Webb - The Future of Work, AI & Human Labor

## Key Points

- AI is not just another technology shift — it's a civilisation-level reordering of work, power, wealth, biology, and society
- The real AI question isn't which jobs disappear, but which *people* become vulnerable — the concept of "thinkers vs think-nots"
- Human capital may stop being the main driver of productivity and wealth — GDP and unemployment could rise together
- The economy may thrive while having less use for human labour
- China has a coherent AI strategy; Western governments are failing to plan for the transition
- AI companies arguably owe people for the data and knowledge they have absorbed — Amy proposes "contribution credit" as a concept
- UBI has limits as a solution to AI-driven labor displacement
- Synthetic biology and longevity are converging with AI in ways that will reshape society
- Current structures of regulation, politics, and economics no longer make sense for the world we are entering
- The "super cycle" of AI + wearable devices + biotechnology is a civilisation-level convergence

## Overview

Amy Webb, quantitative futurist and CEO of Future Today Strategy Group, joins Peter McCormack for a wide-ranging conversation about AI's impact on work, society, and human biology. She argues that AI represents a civilisation-level reordering — not just a technology shift — and that the real vulnerability isn't job loss but the emergence of "thinkers vs think-nots." The discussion covers China's AI strategy, why governments are failing to plan, the concept of "contribution credit" as an alternative to UBI, and the convergence of AI with synthetic biology and longevity research.

## Implications

- The "thinkers vs think-nots" framework suggests AI will create a new class divide based on ability to leverage AI, not just job displacement
- Governments that fail to develop coherent AI strategies risk falling behind China's coordinated approach
- The convergence of AI + biology + wearables may be more transformative than AI alone

## Notable Quotes
