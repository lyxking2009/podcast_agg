# Adam Mosseri: AI is a tailwind for authenticity

## Key Points

- Instagram is shifting from baker's-dozen specialist teams (12-13 people) to lean "pods" of 4-6 generalist engineers plus one "product staff" role — a blend of PM, design, data science, and research
- The "product staff" role is an evolution of the PM: a generalist who can do some design, data science, and research work, leveraging AI tools that make basic waterfall analysis and data pulls possible without dedicated specialists
- Adam is bullish on designers because they have "taste" — in a world where AI makes building easier, figuring out *what* to build becomes more valuable
- The Instagram algorithm doesn't actually have a detailed semantic understanding of your interests — it works with large embedding vectors that are illegible to humans. Only now are LLMs making those vectors interpretable (e.g., "deep pour over coffee snobbery")
- AI-generated content is a *tailwind* for Instagram: in a world of synthetic abundance, people will seek out creativity, authenticity, and real people. Instagram is the largest creator platform
- Two biggest product failures: Facebook Home and the first version of Reels
- Hiring priorities trending up: curiosity, willingness to try things and fail, self-awareness. Trending down: large organizational leadership (fewer mega-teams)
- AI is resetting who succeeds — people who were mediocre coders can now thrive as product builders; designers are programming; engineers are pulling data

## Overview

Adam Mosseri, Head of Instagram (3B+ monthly users), discusses how product teams are being fundamentally restructured in 2026. The canonical team has shrunk from ~13 specialists to 6-7 person pods with generalist "product staff" who leverage AI tools. He explains why the Instagram algorithm was less sophisticated than people assumed — working with opaque embedding vectors rather than semantic understanding — and how LLMs are now making those vectors interpretable. On AI content, Mosseri argues it's a tailwind for Instagram because in a synthetic-content world, people will seek out authentic creators. He shares hiring advice (curiosity + willingness to fail > domain expertise) and reflects on his biggest failures (Facebook Home, early Reels).

## Implications

- Product management is evolving into "product staff" — a generalist operator role. Teams that don't adapt risk being outcompeted by leaner pods
- The algorithm transparency trend (LLMs interpreting embeddings) could reshape user trust and regulatory conversations around recommendation systems
- Creator platforms with authentic human content may have a structural advantage as AI content floods the internet
- Token spend on AI tools is approaching parity with engineering salaries — expect budget caps and ROI scrutiny within 1-2 years
