# TIP829: Kaspi Stock ($KSPI): The Cheapest E-Commerce Monopoly in the World

## Key Points

- Kaspi.kz is Kazakhstan's dominant super-app combining payments, e-commerce, and fintech — used daily by most of the country's population
- The company started as a tier-2 retail bank and leveraged its banking license to build a tech-driven super-app
- Three business segments: payments (core), marketplace (e-commerce), and fintech (lending, insurance)
- Kaspi trades at only ~7x earnings despite strong profitability — one of the cheapest e-commerce platforms globally
- The fintech business plays a critical role in monetization, providing lending and financial services on top of the payments/marketplace base
- Turkey expansion through Hepsiburada acquisition is the key growth vector — but comes with execution risk
- Competitive environment in Kazakhstan is limited — Kaspi's network effects create a near-monopoly
- Risks include: oil-linked currency (Kazakhstani tenge), governance questions from the Culper short report, geopolitical exposure
- High dividend yield is part of the investment thesis — Kaspi returns significant cash to shareholders

## Overview

Daniel Mahncke and Shawn O'Malley deep-dive into Kaspi.kz (NASDAQ: KSPI), the Kazakhstani super-app that most of the country uses daily. They trace the company's evolution from a tier-2 bank to a payments/e-commerce/fintech monopoly, analyze its three business segments, and debate whether the ~7x earnings valuation is justified given the risks (oil-linked currency, governance concerns, Turkey expansion execution). The episode includes a valuation discussion and whether KSPI deserves a spot in the Intrinsic Value Portfolio.

## Implications

- Super-app models in emerging markets can achieve monopoly-like positions with very attractive valuations
- Banking licenses in developing markets are extremely valuable foundations for tech businesses
- The market may be underpricing Kaspi's earnings power due to Kazakhstan-specific risk premiums
