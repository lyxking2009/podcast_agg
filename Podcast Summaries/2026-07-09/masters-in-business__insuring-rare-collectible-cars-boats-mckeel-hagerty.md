# Insuring Rare and Collectible Cars and Boats with McKeel Hagerty

## Key Points

- Hagerty started as a small family insurance agency in 1984 and grew into the leading specialty insurer for classic and collector vehicles and boats
- The company has evolved beyond insurance into a lifestyle brand — hosting events, producing content, and building a community of automotive enthusiasts
- Hagerty went public via SPAC in 2021 and has focused on building a membership model alongside insurance
- The collector car market has matured significantly — vehicles are now treated as alternative assets alongside art, wine, and watches
- Valuation of collectible vehicles requires deep expertise in provenance, condition, rarity, and market trends
- The boat/marine insurance segment is a natural extension — similar risk profile and enthusiast community
- Technology is transforming how specialty insurance is underwritten, with better data on vehicle condition and market values
- Hagerty's Driver's Club membership creates a recurring revenue stream beyond premiums

## Overview

McKeel Hagerty discusses how his family's small insurance agency grew into the dominant specialty insurer for collector vehicles and boats. The conversation covers the evolution of collectible cars as alternative assets, the unique challenges of insuring rare vehicles (valuation expertise, limited claims data, community trust), and how Hagerty has built a lifestyle brand around automotive enthusiasm. The company's strategy combines insurance with membership, events, and content — creating a flywheel that deepens customer engagement and provides better underwriting data.

## Implications

- Alternative asset classes (collectible cars, boats) require specialized insurance expertise that generalists can't replicate
- Community-driven business models create durable competitive moats in niche markets
- The intersection of insurance and lifestyle/content is an underexplored strategy in financial services
