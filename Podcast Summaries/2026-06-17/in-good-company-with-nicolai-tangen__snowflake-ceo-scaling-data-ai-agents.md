## Key Points

- Snowflake powers analytics for half the world's largest companies with 13,000+ customers
- AI model companies (like Anthropic) are Snowflake's biggest competitive threat — they're "industrializing software"
- Consumption-based pricing aligns revenue with value creation, unlike traditional per-seat models
- Top engineers are 50-100x more productive with AI-assisted "spec-driven coding development"
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) enables language models to talk to data sources across systems
- Sridhar's failed startup Neva taught him that consumer products need 10-100x better experience, not incremental gains
- Weekly "War Rooms" cross-functional meetings drive fast decision-making at Snowflake

## Overview

Nicolai Tangen interviews Sridhar Ramaswamy, CEO of Snowflake, about the intersection of data, AI, and enterprise software. Ramaswamy explains how Snowflake's consumption-based model differentiates it from traditional SaaS, and why AI model companies — not hyperscalers — are his biggest competitive concern. He describes how AI is transforming Snowflake's own engineering (spec-driven development, 50-100x productivity gains) and customer experience (Snowflake Intelligence's agentic interface). The conversation covers MCP as an interoperability layer, the cultural transformation at Snowflake (war rooms, meritocracy), and Ramaswamy's personal journey from Tamil Nadu to Silicon Valley.

## Implications

- AI model companies are becoming the "front door to computing," threatening traditional data platforms
- Consumption-based pricing may become the dominant model as AI makes software creation cheaper
- Spec-driven coding development could become the standard for engineering teams
- MCP and similar protocols will be critical for enterprise AI interoperability
- The "industrialization of software" means traditional SaaS economics are under pressure
