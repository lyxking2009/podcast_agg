## Key Points

- Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in U.S. business adoption at 41% vs 39.5%
- AI spending is surging but remains small for most firms — median firm spends only $11/employee/month
- "Tokenmaxxing" is overrated — firms are using multiple AI vendors and showing cost discipline
- The predicted "SaaSpocalypse" hasn't materialized — traditional SaaS companies continue to grow
- Government actions like the Fable 5 ban may backfire, creating a "forbidden fruit" effect
- DeepSeek's growth is overrated — only 0.4% of businesses use it
- Google is "extremely underrated" in AI due to its distribution advantage

## Overview

Ara Kharazian, lead economist at Ramp, joins Big Technology to discuss real data on AI spending in U.S. businesses. The conversation reveals that while AI spending is the fastest-growing category Ramp has ever observed, it remains relatively small — the median firm spends just $11 per employee per month. Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in business adoption, driven by products like Claude Code. The "SaaSpocalypse" hasn't materialized: traditional SaaS companies like Salesforce and Figma continue to grow, and seat-based pricing still dominates 60-75% of software spend. DeepSeek's growth, while rapid, is from a tiny base. Google is positioned as extremely underrated because it doesn't rely solely on token revenue and can offer better cost controls.

## Implications

- AI spending will continue to grow but cost discipline is becoming a key theme
- Multi-vendor AI strategies are becoming the norm, reducing lock-in to any single provider
- Traditional SaaS is more resilient than feared — the "SaaSpocalypse" narrative is overblown
- Government regulation of AI (like Fable 5 ban) may paradoxically boost regulated companies' brands
- Google's AI position is stronger than commonly believed due to Workspace integration
