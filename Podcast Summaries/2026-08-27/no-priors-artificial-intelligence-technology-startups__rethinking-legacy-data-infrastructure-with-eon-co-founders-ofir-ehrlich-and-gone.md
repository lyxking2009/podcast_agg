# Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein — No Priors: Artificial Intelligence | Technology | Startups

## TL;DR
Eon co-founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein discuss how their company transforms cloud backup into a secure data foundation for enterprise AI, helping organizations map, classify, and access scattered data across business units. They highlight the growing value of proprietary datasets, citing Google's $10 million purchase of Spirit Airlines' data out of bankruptcy as evidence that data is becoming a moat. The conversation also covers emerging security risks from AI agents with legitimate permissions and how the AI transformation is happening faster than the cloud migration.

## Key points
- Eon's core offering is a cloud-based data foundation that maps and classifies enterprise data across multiple hyperscalers, ingests structured and unstructured data from various sources, and provides cost-effective storage for protection and recovery while enabling querying, searching, and applying AI models on the ingested data.
- Data is becoming a company's most valuable asset and moat because compute and models have near-zero switching costs, so proprietary datasets differentiate enterprises—illustrated by Google's $10 million purchase of Spirit Airlines' data out of bankruptcy, with AI company Mercor as another bidder.
- Enterprise data is often locked in silos across business units, and data team leaders struggle to even identify what exists; Eon solves this by continuously mapping, classifying, and ingesting data without compromising production systems or compliance, maintaining audit trails and preventing sensitive information from leaking into AI training.
- Security threats now include AI agents with legitimate permissions—rogue non-human actors can drop tables or modify data rapidly; Eon's detection and recovery methodology for ransomware extends to these agents, but the velocity of attacks is much higher, causing widespread fear among enterprises.
- The rise of non-technical builders using tools like Lovable leads to uncontrolled AI agents that use company data without awareness of security or compliance, creating a new class of internal actors not bound by organizational rules.
- The enterprise stack must adapt to agentic workloads: dashboards may proliferate because tracking agent actions requires oversight; security focus is shifting to non-human identity management and endpoint protection for agents running on laptops and connected to multiple networks.
- The AI transformation is happening faster than cloud migration because AI is easier for non-technical stakeholders to understand, prompting C-level pressure to adopt AI quickly; new consumption models like forward-deployed engineers (e.g., Cognition) and product-led growth are accelerating adoption.
- Most companies are still at the beginning of AI adoption; existing data infrastructure is ill-equipped to handle the explosion of agent-generated data, noise, and cost per token, so reimagining data foundations is critical for enabling a culture of builders and agents.
