## TL;DR
Edward Chancellor, author of Devil Take the Hindmost, walks Wilfred Frost through 400 years of manias — tulips, the South Sea Bubble, railway mania, the dot-com crash — and argues we are in a bubble now, with tighter money as the thing that finally ends one.

## Key points
- Chancellor’s thesis is blunt: we are in a bubble and it will burst, and the historical record makes that legible rather than mysterious.
- The pattern repeats across centuries — 17th-century Tulip Mania, the 1720s South Sea Bubble, the 1840s British railway craze, the dot-com crash — because human nature does not change.
- Hyper-connectivity makes modern irrationality spread faster than in any prior mania, compressing the time between narrative and positioning.
- The number one feature of a bursting speculative bubble is tighter money — not a change in sentiment, not a bad earnings print.
- Most bubbles feature a messianic leader figure: Jensen Huang, Elon Musk, Sam Altman or Dario Amodei. Every mania is more efficient when organised around a leader who generates enormous confidence.
- Long-term winners of technological revolutions usually only become visible after the shakeout, which argues for standing back and waiting rather than buying the theme early.
