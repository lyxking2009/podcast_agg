# Can the Stock Market Survive a Tech Meltdown? — The Compound and Friends


## TL;DR
Michael Batnick and Matt Zambonaro discuss the violent tech-led selloff and rotation into other sectors after the latest AI-related shock, including Nvidia's 30% drawdown and the broadening of earnings to the S&P 493. They examine whether value can continue to outperform, historical dot-com analogs, and what hot CPI plus falling stocks means for forward returns. The episode concludes with a make-the-case for MSCI and a look at retail's recent underperformance.

## Key points
- Last week's two-day blow-up saw 57 S&P 500 stocks fall more than 10%, with 32 of them in tech; Micron fell 23% while Nvidia was a relative outperformer at -12%, despite being at the center of the AI trade.
- Nvidia's forward earnings estimates hit an all-time high on Friday, and forward earnings have grown 3,246% since ChatGPT launched while the stock is up 944%, meaning earnings growth has tripled stock price growth.
- The S&P 493 are expected to grow earnings 32.6% year-over-year this quarter, outpacing the Magnificent Seven's 22.6% for the first time in four quarters, supporting the narrative of earnings broadening.
- The forward EPS ratio of the S&P 493 relative to the Mag Seven bottomed on August 31 and is up 6.5%, while the corresponding price ratio is up 7.3% since its October low, indicating that earnings revisions are justifying the rotation.
- When Mag Seven companies beat earnings in Q3, their stocks fell an average of 3% the next day, a bearish signal despite large beats; four of six have reported so far this quarter.
- Value outperformed growth over a six-day period by the widest margin since 2000, pushing the value-versus-growth ratio to a three-year high, but whether this is a durable regime change depends on whether the AI trade has peaked.
- The S&P 500 value index has technology as its largest sector at similar weight to the broad index, and its top holdings include Tesla and Apple; different value ETFs show wide return dispersion, with VLUE up 37% YTD versus IVE up 14%.
- Nvidia has fallen almost 30% from its all-time high, and the stock carries significant key-man risk around CEO Jensen Huang; news of his relationship with Mega Speed's Alice Wong surfaced, and he emailed his team about it.
- Historical dot-com analog shows that even if tech peaked, the rest of the market can work: from March 2000 peak to October 2002 bottom, tech and communication services fell 70% while the rest of the S&P 500 was up 3%.
- When the equal-weight S&P 500 makes a new high while the Nasdaq 100 is 10% off its high, historically the Nasdaq catches up; excluding the tech bubble, the Nasdaq 100 averaged a 21.6% gain over the next 12 months and took a median 113 trading days to reach a new high.
- October's hot CPI report pushed Fed funds futures projections for early 2028 from 4.37% to 4.54%, but historical data show that when stocks rise on a hot CPI day, the S&P 500 averages a 6.2% gain over the next three months with a 91% positive rate.
- MSCI shows a multi-year base with revenue tripled since 2015 and operating margin at 56%; a breakout above 700 could lead to 1000, though risk management would use a trend line from the 2022 bottom for stop placement.
- Retail's historical underperformance relative to the S&P does not reliably signal economic trouble; the XRT recently ripped after a period of underperformance, but such moves may not mean much for the broad market.
