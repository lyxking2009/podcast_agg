# Pricing Nvidia's "Key Man Risk" with Gene Munster — RiskReversal Pod


## TL;DR
Gene Munster discusses Nvidia's Jensen Huang 'key man risk' after China-related news, estimating a 25% probability of departure priced into the stock versus his 10-15% view. He argues the episode underscores AI's geopolitical importance, likely accelerating US data center buildout. The conversation also covers Apple's hardware super cycle, Google's agent risk, and the upcoming Anthropic IPO.

## Key points
- Gene Munster estimates Nvidia stock would fall about 20% if Jensen Huang left, and with the stock down 5% on the news, the market is pricing in roughly a 25% chance of his departure; Munster thinks the actual risk is only 10-15%.
- China targeting Jensen Huang signals the geopolitical significance of AI leadership, and Munster believes both Democrats and Republicans will need to build many data centers in the US as a result.
- Data center capex growth was about 80% in 2025, expected high-30s percent in 2026 (down from 42% before the election), but Munster suspects the 2028 street estimate of 15% growth is far too low, likely closer to 30-40%.
- Although the Nvidia-China issue could theoretically give hyperscalers an excuse to cut capex, Munster believes they fundamentally want to build data centers and will not use it as an out.
- Google initially faced existential risk from chatbots, then successfully funneled search traffic into AI overviews, but now faces a new challenge from personalized agents; daily active users of agents are only about 25 million versus 5.5 billion internet users.
- Munster differentiates token pricing: average AI agent IQ is about 130, high-price tokens cost 10x low-level tokens; he expects 90% of the world to trade on commoditized tokens while 10% use frontier models, with the latter more than offsetting pricing.
- The DeepSeek selloff in early 2025 drove the Nasdaq down about 20%, whereas the recent pullback was only 14% because it lacked tariff and model uncertainty, but still reflects concerns about the pace of AI buildout.
- Data center cost breakdown is 85% hard costs like chips and 15% soft costs; even if soft costs rise 30%, total cost would rise only about 5%, so hyperscalers will likely still proceed.
- Gene Munster returned his Apple Duo but bought a new iPhone Pro; he estimates Duo return rates around 25%, and maintains his bet with Dan Nathan that Apple will sell 20 million Duo units in fiscal 2027 versus Nathan's 5 million estimate.
- Apple services revenue is about 27.5% of total revenue, and Munster expects services percentage to dip to mid-20s during the hardware super cycle before rising again; he forecasts Apple's topline growth averaging about 10% annually through 2030.
- Munster rates the importance of a successful Anthropic IPO at 90 out of 100, noting the market is nervous and expectations have been tempered, but believes it will be a buying opportunity and the stock needs to trade up regardless of pricing.
