# How One ENS Vote Reignited the DAO Governance Debate: Uneasy Money

**Show:** Unchained
**Date:** 2026-07-03

## Key Points
- A routine two-year renewal for the ENS DAO's Security Council failed on chain, and Nick Johnson (ENS Labs founder/CEO) voted against it with ~50% of active supply.
- Some X users cast Johnson as a villain seizing a $130M treasury, but the situation is more nuanced.
- ENS co-founders Nick Johnson and Alex Van de Sande are proposing to hand day-to-day treasury and protocol decisions to a foundation instead of the DAO.
- The ENS governance fight mirrors similar dynamics in Aave — well-funded DAOs face the question of who actually controls significant treasuries.
- Johnson's conviction in token governance has weakened — he never trusted ETH-weighted voting.
- The ENS treasury has underperformed a savings account, raising questions about DAO treasury management effectiveness.
- The fundamental question: once a protocol accumulates enough money to matter, can any voting mechanism decide who controls it?

## Overview
Kain Warwick and Taylor Monahan host ENS co-founders Nick Johnson and Alex Van de Sande to discuss the failed Security Council renewal vote and the backlash it generated. The conversation explores whether well-funded DAOs can effectively govern through token voting, why Johnson wants to move ENS governance to a foundation, and how the ENS situation parallels Aave's governance challenges. The episode lands on a fundamental question about DAO governance at scale.

## Implications
DAO governance is hitting a wall as treasuries grow large enough to attract power struggles. Token-weighted voting may be fundamentally flawed for protocols with significant treasuries. The ENS proposal to move governance to a foundation represents a broader trend of DAOs centralizing decision-making as they mature. The $130M ENS treasury underperforming a savings account highlights DAO treasury management challenges.
