# How Ethereum Institutional Intends to Grow Ethereum's Market Share

**Show:** Unchained
**Date:** 2026-07-03

## Key Points
- Sharplink, BitMine, and Joe Lubin launched two new organizations to convince Wall Street to build on Ethereum, backed by 50+ institutional supporters.
- Joseph Chalom (CEO of Sharplink, board member of Ethereum Institutional) argues Ethereum's real competition isn't Solana or Canton — it's institutional inertia: the reluctance of large institutions to touch financial rails they don't already trust.
- Ethereum Institutional differs from Etherealize and the Enterprise Ethereum Alliance in its approach to institutional adoption.
- Robinhood building on Arbitrum counts as a win for Ethereum — even Layer 2 adoption expands the Ethereum ecosystem.
- For ETH to capture value from tokenized real-world assets (past $31B), the network needs to demonstrate that value flows back to ETH holders.
- Chalom pushes back on claims that the Ethereum Foundation's culture is broken.
- On Strategy's preferred stock drama: Chalom says plainly that 'Michael Saylor is in a pickle.'

## Overview
Laura Shin interviews Joseph Chalom about the launch of Ethereum Institutional, a new organization backed by 50+ institutional supporters aiming to get Wall Street building on Ethereum. Chalom makes the case that Ethereum's biggest competitor is institutional inertia, not other blockchains. He discusses how Robinhood's Arbitrum L2 is still an Ethereum win, the challenge of capturing RWA value for ETH holders, and his blunt assessment that Michael Saylor is 'in a pickle' with Strategy's preferred stock situation.

## Implications
Institutional Ethereum adoption is being organized through new industry groups (Ethereum Institutional) with serious institutional backing. The $31B+ tokenized RWA market is the prize, but ETH value capture remains the key question. Robinhood's L2 adoption validates the Ethereum ecosystem strategy even for non-mainnet deployments. Michael Saylor's Strategy faces growing scrutiny over its preferred stock structure.
