# America, the 250 Year Old Startup

**Show:** Everybody's Business
**Date:** 2026-07-03

## Key Points
- The hosts frame America's 250th birthday by asking: if the US were a startup, would you invest? Economist Martha Gimbel says historically yes — every era of American investment has paid off — but warns investors must eventually diversify beyond a single bet.
- The Supreme Court's Trump v. Slaughter decision expanded presidential power to remove independent agency heads, increasing political risk and policy swing volatility between administrations. The Fed was exempted (5-4), which economists welcomed to preserve inflation expectations anchoring.
- Birthright citizenship ruling (6-3 defeat for Trump admin) matters economically: immigration has been a core driver of American innovation and growth. The Budget Lab found that even future immigration policy changes have already lowered long-run productivity growth projections.
- Consumer sentiment is at its lowest since the 1930s despite near-record low unemployment. People rate the current labor market fine but are deeply pessimistic about the future — something in the water is making them distrust the stability.
- The US deficit is ~6% of GDP, unprecedented with such low unemployment. Economists who weren't deficit hawks are now worried about spending pushing up interest rates and forcing painful future decisions.
- America's 250th birthday celebration is split between two dueling organizations: the congressionally-created America 250 and Trump-backed Freedom 250. Freedom 250 has claimed ~$60M in federal funding vs. A250's $25M, and the celebrations reflect partisan divisions.
- Trump's financial disclosures reveal $1.2B in crypto income from World Liberty Financial, meme coins, and related businesses — a jaw-dropping figure that raises corruption concerns given regulatory decisions affecting those same businesses.
- World Cup visitors to the US are most impressed by free refills, portion sizes, and ranch dressing — which has become an unlikely cultural ambassador. Hidden Valley is planning a European tour and TSA-friendly packaging.

## Overview
A Fourth of July special examining America at 250 through a business lens. The show opens with economist Martha Gimbel evaluating America Inc. as a startup investment, finding historical success but growing concerns about political risk, immigration restrictions, and unsustainable deficits. A segment on the dueling 250th birthday organizations reveals how even celebrations have become partisan. The show closes with lighter fare: World Cup visitors discovering ranch dressing as America's greatest export, and Trump's $1.2B crypto windfall disclosures.

## Implications
Political risk is now a material factor for US investment decisions. The Supreme Court's expansion of presidential power over independent agencies increases policy volatility between administrations. Immigration restrictions are already depressing long-term productivity growth projections. The deficit at 6% with full employment is historically unusual and may force painful fiscal adjustments. Consumer sentiment disconnect from actual economic data suggests deeper structural anxiety about the future.
