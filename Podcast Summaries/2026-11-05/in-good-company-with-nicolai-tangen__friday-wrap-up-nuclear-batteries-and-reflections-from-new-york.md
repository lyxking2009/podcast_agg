# Friday Wrap-Up: Nuclear, Batteries and Reflections from New York — In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen

## TL;DR
In this Friday wrap-up from New York, Nicolai Tangen and Stille Marte Skag discuss NextEra Energy CEO John Ketchum's outlook that AI will drive a sixfold increase in US power demand by the mid-2040s, requiring nuclear, batteries, and massive grid investment. NBIM made its first battery platform investment via a 10% stake with Brookfield and is expanding its unlisted renewable infrastructure portfolio after valuations became more attractive. Tangen also shares insights from a month of meetings in New York, including heightened cyber risk and investor concerns about inflation, credit, and the AI build-out.

## Key points
- NextEra Energy CEO John Ketchum expects US power demand to grow sixfold by the mid-2040s after years of flat demand, driven by AI and data centers; big tech companies alone plan to spend over $600 billion on data centers and chips this year.
- NextEra is spending about $1 billion per week on infrastructure build-out, reflecting the scale of needed generation and grid investment.
- The US midterm elections featured significant protests against data center build-outs due to concerns about higher electricity prices and AI-driven job losses, which could affect permitting and licensing after the election.
- Hyperscalers that have already secured substantial energy supply saw share price increases, while new players face more difficulty, redrawing the competitive landscape.
- Data centers require around-the-clock power, making nuclear baseload and small modular reactors increasingly important, alongside batteries to time-shift solar generation.
- Norges Bank Investment Management made its first battery platform investment this week, taking a 10% stake in a venture with Brookfield that could become the largest battery platform in the US.
- NBIM received an unlisted renewable energy infrastructure mandate in 2019 but moved slowly at first due to high valuations and competition; it accelerated after green investing fell out of favor, making projects more profitable, with deals in Spain, Portugal, the US, and a 25% stake in German grid operator TenneT last year.
- As of now, the unlisted renewable infrastructure portfolio is about 0.5% of the fund, rising to 1% when all committed deals are closed, and the completed deals are equivalent to 10% of Norway's total energy generation capacity.
- Tangen is spending his fourth consecutive month in New York, where the fund has about 60% of its capital and 100 employees, meeting with roughly 50 US CEOs, heads of big banks, and other investors, and attending IPOs including Entropic.
- Feedback from New York meetings highlights heightened cyber risk, with an October surge in attacks referred to as "Dark October," plus concerns that inflation may be worse than expected, geopolitical tension, election uncertainty, credit risk, and the pace of AI build-out.
- Tangen maintains a long-term focus despite election and earnings noise, and shares his morning routine: daily gratitude meditation, coffee, reading newspapers, then emails.
