# TIP852: Hermes and LVMH Stock: Time to Buy Luxury? w/ Daniel Mahncke & Shawn O'Malley — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)

## TL;DR
Daniel Mahncke and Shawn O'Malley revisit Hermes (RMS) and LVMH (MC), the two luxury houses they previously passed on, now that both sit near multi-year lows. They argue the sell-off reflects a genuine demand reset — China, aspirational buyers and Gulf tourism — but that the two businesses are diverging sharply in quality of economics. Both update their valuation models and decide whether either earns a place in the Intrinsic Value Portfolio.

## Key points
- Hermes is down more than 50% from its February 2025 peak and trades at ~32x earnings for the first time in a decade; LVMH has fallen to a five-year low after two straight years of declining sales — something that did not happen even in 2009.
- The luxury industry has lost roughly 70 million customers since 2022; the Chinese market that tripled between 2017 and 2021 has shrunk ever since, and a Gulf war emptied Champs-Elysees stores of their highest-spending tourists.
- Luxury is not as non-cyclical as assumed: as brands scale they sell more to 'aspirational buyers' (roughly $1,500-$5,000/yr spenders) who need their paychecks and are now walking away, rather than to the ultra-wealthy who keep spending.
- Businesses are diverging: Richemont grew ~20% in the same quarter LVMH grew ~3%, and Hermes still earns ~41% margins while LVMH's fashion-and-leather division has given back seven points of margin over five years.
- The top 0.1% of luxury clients keep spending while aspirational demand evaporates, so exposure to the aspirational customer is now the key risk factor to underwrite.
- Both hosts had passed previously on valuation grounds despite liking the businesses; Mahncke notes that even at the lows Hermes still carries a 30-plus multiple but is 'significantly cheaper than we can usually buy'.
- The macro overlay matters because its effects are showing up at the company level: paying a premium while those consequences are already visible is not the margin-of-safety setup they look for.
- They frame the Chinese property bust through a five-to-six-year playbook and study how prior luxury downturns (2009, 2016, 2020) ended to gauge the recovery shape.
- Final decision point: Daniel updates both intrinsic-value models and decides whether Hermes (RMS) or LVMH finally qualifies for The Intrinsic Value Portfolio.
