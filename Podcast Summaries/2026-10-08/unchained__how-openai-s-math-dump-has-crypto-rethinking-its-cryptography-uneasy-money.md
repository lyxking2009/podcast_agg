# How OpenAI's Math Dump Has Crypto Rethinking Its Cryptography: Uneasy Money — Unchained

## TL;DR
The episode covers the fallout from OpenAI's release of 722 math papers hinting at faster brute-force attacks on elliptic curve cryptography, including Justin Drake's warning and debunking of MetaMask rumors. It also explores the Near Protocol hack where funds were returned after aggressive pursuit, ZachXBT's undercover infiltration of DPRK money laundering networks, and a Swiss investor complaint against Safe Foundation/Gnosis. The discussion concludes with governance token economics and the shutdown of Blast and Abstract L2s.

## Key points
- OpenAI released 722 papers, many formalized in Lean, suggesting potential faster methods to brute force elliptic curve cryptography, though not a direct break. Justin Drake tweeted 'don't panic' but advised moving large holdings (e.g., >50 BTC) to wallets using different hashing functions, sparking concern about unprecedented AI-driven cryptographic advances.
- The MetaMask validator key rotation rumor was falsely linked to the math dump; the actual incident involved a 0.36 ETH fee redirect due to a configuration change, prompting proactive security rotation. The hosts clarify that MetaMask's withdrawal keys remain with users, and the situation was not a cryptographic vulnerability.
- If elliptic curve cryptography were broken, the hosts argue the first targets would be large infrastructure providers and traditional finance, not small wallets. Powerful actors would likely use such capability for geopolitical advantage rather than stealing crypto, and the timeline concern includes Chinese open-weight models catching up within months.
- AI models have rapidly improved in mathematical reasoning; two years ago they struggled with basic arithmetic, but now they can solve advanced math problems, raising questions about future cryptographic security and whether AI-generated math will become incomprehensible to humans.
- The Near Protocol hacker returned stolen funds after the team applied aggressive pressure, including Alex from Aurora's 'we've identified you' message. The attacker sent dust to the Lazarus (Ronin) address, similar to the Oiler hacker, indicating a non-nation-state actor; no bounty was paid.
- ZachXBT conducted an undercover operation, befriending Chinese money launderer Jimmy Green, to infiltrate DPRK laundering networks. He spent over $350,000 of his own money, gained detailed intel on laundering mechanics, and published findings to expose shameless laundering and pressure exchanges.
- Safe Foundation/Gnosis faces a Swiss foundation intervention from investors who accuse the board of laziness and failing to return value, citing Safe's assets falling from $66B to $30B. The hosts defend Gnosis's decade of contributions (Cow Swap, Safe) despite academic inefficiency, noting it's one of the few successful ICO-era teams.
- Ben DiFrancesco argues that governance tokens must provide economic security; as regulatory excuses fade, projects need real value accrual mechanisms like fees or buybacks to prevent token value dropping below the cost to attack the protocol.
- Blast and Abstract L2s shut down this week, seen as end-of-cycle capitulation. The hosts note there are too many L2s and that differentiation is difficult, joking that building a wallet is a 'kiss of death' for projects.
