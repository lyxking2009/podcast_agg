RBC's 11th annual fintech conference saw record engagement with 430 delegates and 740 investor interactions. ## Key points
- RBC's 11th annual fintech conference attracted its highest engagement in five years, with 430 delegates and 740 investor interactions.
- Private investors are actively seeking new fintech investments, while public investors are evaluating how AI affects their existing fintech holdings.
- Winners will be companies that demonstrate high gross retention and growth that is at least partly driven by AI features or products.
- Fintech's specialized and highly-regulated nature provides protection from the disruption affecting broader software, but the market is bifurcated: successful companies trade at robust multiples while perceived losers trade near cyclical lows.
- Fintechs that are strongly embedded with their end customers have the most insulation from disruption and can go on the offensive with new products.
- Wealthtech and insurtech are attracting the most investor interest and are expected to see strong M&A activity over the next 12 to 18 months.
- Capital markets software has strong interest, but incumbent providers face threats from customers who have their own AI budgets and may build in-house.
