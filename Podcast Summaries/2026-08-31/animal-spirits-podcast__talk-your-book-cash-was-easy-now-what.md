# Talk Your Book: Cash Was Easy… Now What? — Animal Spirits Podcast

## TL;DR
Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson interview Brandon Clark of Federated Hermes about income investing after the end of zero rates. They discuss why investors are too comfortable in cash, the highest long-term bond yields since 2007, and the rise of options-based income ETFs. Clark explains Federated Hermes' approach using high-dividend stocks and index call spreads to generate tax-efficient distributions, while the hosts debate how many investors actually spend versus reinvest that income.

## Key points
- Investors are overly focused on nominal income and remain parked in cash and money market funds, ignoring reinvestment risk and the potential for bond price appreciation if the Fed cuts rates.
- Long-term Treasury yields are at multi-decade highs, with the 30-year at 5.295% on August 17, 2026, the highest since June 2007, but 2022 PTSD keeps many investors away from duration.
- Brandon Clark advises gradually moving out the yield curve, particularly in the 0-2 year space, to lock in yields before the Fed potentially lowers rates.
- Federated Hermes manages roughly $900 billion in assets, primarily in money market funds, and entered the ETF business about five years ago to expand retail distribution.
- The covered call and derivative income ETF category has surged, with some single-stock covered call products offering distributions as high as 80%, but they still carry full equity downside risk.
- Federated Hermes' PAYR ETF combines a high-dividend blue-chip portfolio with an index call spread overlay, selling S&P 500 calls and buying further out-of-the-money calls to limit upside loss while generating income.
- Using options allows the fund to classify distributions as return of capital, which can lower current taxable income but reduces cost basis and may increase future capital gains when shares are sold.
- Michael Batnick estimates only about 30% of investors in these income products actually use the distributions for spending; many likely reinvest them or treat the products as total return strategies.
- Investors tend to think in nominal terms rather than real after inflation, favoring high nominal yields even when real yields are modest.
- The period from 2013 to 2019 with near-zero rates made income scarce; now that the Fed moved from 0% to 5%, the environment is healthier for income, and further rate increases would be less damaging than the 2022 shock.
