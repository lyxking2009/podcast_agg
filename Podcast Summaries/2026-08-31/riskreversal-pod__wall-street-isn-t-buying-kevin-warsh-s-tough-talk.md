# Wall Street Isn't Buying Kevin Warsh's Tough Talk — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Dan Nathan and Guy Adami discuss Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, which reiterated a hawkish stance but signaled no immediate rate hike, as the S&P 500 sits near all-time highs and the VIX approaches 14. They highlight consumer weakness in retail earnings, rising credit delinquencies, and David Rosenberg's warning that tightening into weak growth risks a policy misstep. The conversation also covers Nvidia's strong quarter and its risky circular AI investments, plus potential midterm election volatility and geopolitical/trade tensions.

## Key points
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks were status quo: hawkish tone but no explicit rate hike signal; markets interpreted as relief, with S&P 500 less than 1% from all-time high and VIX under 15.
- David Rosenberg warns Fed hawks face weak growth backdrop: nine of 18 FOMC participants want to hike rates into a 1.5% real GDP growth environment vs 2% potential, risking policy misstep; upcoming BEA PCE deflator revisions and Warsh task force conclusions pending.
- Consumer discretionary earnings show deterioration: Walmart, TJ Maxx, Best Buy, Dick's Sporting Goods all signaled weak consumer; trade-down retailers also struggling, indicating broad-based consumer pressure.
- Credit metrics deteriorating: American Express and Capital One stocks down ~15-16% from highs, while Visa and Mastercard at all-time highs; 90-day delinquency rates at highest since 2008-09, but market ignoring.
- Inflation is not falling; it's rising less fast; cumulative inflation and wage growth not keeping up are hitting paycheck-to-paycheck consumers, contributing to weak consumer confidence.
- Nvidia reported 100% revenue growth and ~$100B quarterly run rate, but concerns about its ecosystem investments creating circular relationships: Nvidia is OpenAI's largest investor, Microsoft is OpenAI's largest investor and largest customer, Nvidia is CoreWeave's largest investor, CoreWeave's largest customer is Microsoft; two points of failure: Nvidia and OpenAI.
- Nvidia's investment strategy likened to betting on every roulette number; if AI boom continues, Nvidia could become $10T company, but failure could resemble Nortel, Lucent, Sun Microsystems, Cisco.
- Nvidia customers are developing own chips (Google TPU, AMD Radeon, Amazon Trainium, OpenAI claimed chip at 1/3 cost), potentially pressuring Nvidia's margins and sales.
- Salesforce jumped 25-26% after earnings, suggesting software trade may continue; IGV and buybacks provide support, but sector headwinds not over.
- Midterm election years historically see larger drawdowns averaging 16-17.5% vs 10% other years; current complacency (VIX ~14) plus election administration controversies (DOJ observers in Wyoming primary) could trigger 5-10% correction.
- Trade and geopolitical tensions: Canada trade dispute with Mark Carney's government has 60%+ Canadian approval for walking away; Iran waiting until after midterms; CIA director in Moscow warning Russia not to threaten Western Europe.
- Upcoming earnings to watch: Dell (hardware demand, memory costs) and Broadcom (trading at support $355-360, current $375, potential 15-20% move).
