# Beezie & Collector Crypt CEOs on Why Onchain Collectibles Are Exploding — Empire

## TL;DR
Tom (Collector Crypt) and Andrea (Beezie) discuss the explosion in onchain collectibles, especially Pokemon cards, which are outperforming Bitcoin and the S&P. They explain their 'vending machine' models: users buy digital packs, reveal tokenized graded cards, and can sell back, hold, or redeem. The conversation covers market structure problems, token strategies, growth metrics (20x volume), and expansion into luxury gamified commerce.

## Key points
- Pokemon cards and other collectibles have outperformed both Bitcoin and the S&P this year, driven by nostalgia, cross-generational appeal of the IP, and the dopamine rush of opening packs. Both Beezie and Collector Crypt are capitalizing on this by bringing the pack-opening experience onchain with transparent odds.
- Beezie and Collector Crypt use a 'vending machine' model: users buy digital packs, reveal a random graded card that is vaulted and tokenized, and can either keep the NFT, sell it on a marketplace, redeem the physical card, or accept an instant buyback at 80-100% of market value.
- Collector Crypt sources cards at a 10-15% discount to market via extensive buying networks, 40+ annual card shows, and algorithmic floor bidding on eBay auctions; this allows the platform to offer packs with positive expected value relative to eBay prices (e.g., $1,000 in buys yields $1,100 of cards in a $50 machine).
- Revenue is generated through the spread between discounted card acquisition and user behavior: low-tier cards are often sold back and re-circulated up to 55 times before being held, while users who keep only rare cards effectively purchase them at about 1% under market price.
- Both platforms emphasize transparency and fairness over traditional digital repack platforms and eBay, which charge up to 13% fees and suffer from fraud and lack of liquidity; their take rates are under 10%, positioning them as user-acquisition strategy against well-funded competitors with 20-25% take rates.
- Collector Crypt has a token (no tokens sold since launch) and has been conducting programmatic and idiosyncratic buybacks via a mysterious wallet every four hours; more details on value accrual are expected next week.
- Beezie is exploring a token but faces legal/regulatory challenges due to not being pre-product; the company competes with lower margins and community-focused approach, already surpassing $200 million in cumulative volume.
- Growth is driven by crypto-native communities, especially Solana NFT enthusiasts, and migration from non-crypto platforms like GameStop and Courtyard; Collector Crypt's average lifetime user spends $15,000-$17,000.
- Collector Crypt's monthly volume grew from ~$20 million in July 2024 to $300-400 million in mid-2025 (a 20x increase), and the company is building a 94,000 sq ft robotics-enabled facility to scale 10x further.
- Beezie is launching a new category next week with a major web2 reseller (15 million annual users across 80 countries), targeting luxury assets like Birkin bags; this marks expansion beyond collectibles into gamified commerce, which Andrea sees as appealing to Gen Z and female shoppers.
- Tom sees the ultimate goal as building the largest liquidity layer for collectibles on chain, enabling open infrastructure where third parties can build zero-fee marketplaces, and reimagining the collectibles ecosystem to put power back in collectors' hands.
