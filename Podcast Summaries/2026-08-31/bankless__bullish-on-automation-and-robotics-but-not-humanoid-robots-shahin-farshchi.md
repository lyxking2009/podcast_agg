# Bullish on Automation and Robotics, but not Humanoid Robots | Shahin Farshchi — Bankless

## TL;DR
Shahin Farshchi, general partner at Lux Capital, argues that the hype around robotics is justified, but his excitement stems from the convergence of AI and cheap manufacturing rather than humanoid robots. He explains how automation is becoming more accessible to a wider range of industries, and discusses the impact on labor, the role of specialized vs general-purpose robots, and the current state of AI in robotics. Farshchi highlights companies like Physical Intelligence and Formic as examples of this new wave.

## Key points
- Farshchi is bullish on robotics due to the combination of advanced AI and cheap manufacturing, which enables building complex systems at low cost for new use cases, but he is not particularly excited about humanoid robots.
- Automation is a broad category that includes not just physical robots but also financing, engineering, software, and maintenance; robots are a component of automation solutions tailored to specific problems.
- Historical parallel: the integration of microprocessors in the 1970s led to widespread robotics in automotive manufacturing; today's AI and commoditized hardware are doing the same for industries that previously couldn't afford automation.
- General-purpose humanoids will not dominate all tasks; specialized robots with different form factors will proliferate for specific applications, though they may share common components like cameras, sensors, and actuators.
- The robotics industry is transitioning from being hardware-centric to capability-centric, similar to how personal computing shifted from hardware specs to internet-based services in the 1990s.
- Contrary to fears, automation historically correlates with lower unemployment and higher-quality jobs; the real enemy of both labor and automation is cheap labor, as seen in countries with low wages.
- Formic, a Lux portfolio company, offers robots as a service, abstracting away the machine and selling output; its customers adopt automation not to cut headcount but because they cannot find workers for high-churn jobs.
- Exciting near-term opportunities are in industrial and commercial settings (e.g., factories, warehouses) rather than domestic robots, because the problems are more constrained and economics are clearer.
- AI in robotics currently relies on sensing, perception, planning, and action; new vision-language models (VLMs) and vision-language-action models (VLAs) show promise but are still less reliable and slower, and a hybrid approach is likely.
- Training data for robotics is scarce compared to internet text; companies like Physical Intelligence and XDOF are working to generate and open-source models and datasets to accelerate adoption.
- Key companies to watch: Physical Intelligence (open-source VLA models) and Formic (robots as a service), both Lux Capital investments.
