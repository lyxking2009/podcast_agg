## Key Points

- Simple-looking systems can produce massive cascading effects from tiny disturbances (sand pile analogy) — our social and economic order is more fragile than it appears.

## Overview

Peter McCormack interviews Rupert Russell, a Harvard sociologist and documentary filmmaker, about the hidden power of prices in shaping global politics and conflict. The conversation spans from historical examples (Roman Empire, French Revolution, Treaty of Versailles) to modern commodity markets, exploring how price movements in essential goods like food and energy create cascading political consequences. McCormack, coming from a libertarian/Austrian economics perspective, engages with the uncomfortable reality that free markets can produce destabilizing price dynamics. Russell uses chaos theory (the sand pile analogy) to argue that our social order is more fragile than it appears, with small price disturbances capable of triggering massive political upheaval.

## Implications

Understanding the relationship between commodity prices and political stability is essential for investors and policymakers. Price distortions in food and energy are not just economic events — they are potential triggers for political crises. The 'global game of Monopoly' framing suggests that participants in financial markets should understand not just the economics but the political consequences of price movements. For Bitcoin advocates, this conversation provides context for why sound money and predictable pricing systems matter for social stability.
