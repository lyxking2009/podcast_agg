## Key Points

- Anthropic engineers ship 8x more code per quarter compared to 2021-2025, demonstrating the transformative impact of AI-assisted development on engineering productivity.
- Coding is no longer the bottleneck — AI has lifted the ceiling of what anyone is able to do. The challenge shifted from 'can we build it?' to 'how ambitious can we be?'
- High agency and high accountability go together at Anthropic. Engineers have freedom to code with AI but must articulate the hypothesis of what they're trying to solve.
- Claude 'routines' have changed how Fiona operates as a manager — she uses AI agents for asynchronous work, handling context switching across 20+ running agents.
- The team started 'pair-wise programming lunch' to combat loneliness from engineers working primarily with their AI agents instead of each other.

- The 'bad vs. sad' quality framework distinguishes between objectively incorrect output (bad — fixable) and output that doesn't match subjective taste (sad — needs human judgment).
- All managers at Anthropic start as ICs first — management is not a separate track but a natural evolution of IC excellence.
- PM and data science roles are transforming alongside engineering — AI is not just an engineering tool but reshapes every function.

## Overview

Fiona Fung, who leads the Claude Code and Cowork teams at Anthropic, discusses what it's like running engineering teams in the AI era. She brings 25+ years of experience from building Visual Studio and TypeScript at Microsoft, launching Facebook Marketplace ($100B+ GMV), and building Meta's first smart glasses and AR products. The conversation covers how AI has fundamentally changed software engineering — from coding as bottleneck to coding as commodity — and what that means for team structure, hiring, management, and career development. Fiona shares practical insights on using Claude routines for management, the tension between agency and accountability, and why the biggest unsolved problem isn't technical but cultural: maintaining team cohesion when everyone works primarily with AI agents.

## Implications

Engineering leaders should prepare for a world where coding productivity is 8x+ higher — the bottleneck shifts to ambition, taste, and systems thinking. Hiring should prioritize creative builders and deep systems experts over pure coding ability. Management practices need to evolve to handle AI-agent-heavy workflows, including new forms of collaboration (like pair-wise programming lunches) to prevent isolation. The gap between AI-adopters and resistors will widen — organizations need explicit strategies to bring people along.
