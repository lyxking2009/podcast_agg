# How Wall Street Is Bracing For the Post-Jensen Huang Nvidia Era — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Odd Lots discusses Nvidia after CEO Jensen Huang's departure amid scandals involving a subordinate relationship and China-linked individual Alice Wayne. Bernstein analyst Stacey Rasgon downgraded the stock, citing key man risk, potential leadership vacuum, and China policy uncertainties. He believes near-term business remains strong but long-term risks and negative headlines make the stock unattractive.

## Key points
- Stacey Rasgon of Bernstein downgraded Nvidia from outperform, cutting his target price in half, citing the fallout from Jensen Huang's departure over scandals including a relationship with subordinate and alleged ties to China-linked individual Alice Wayne.
- Rasgon initially thought Huang might survive the Alice Wayne revelations but changed his view after secondary disclosures about relationships with subordinates, stating 'You can't sleep with your subordinates and then lie to the board about it.'
- Nvidia's revenue concentration: US customers over 60%, Taiwanese customers in the low 20% range, China including Hong Kong about 8%, and Singapore/Malaysia combined roughly 1%. Rasgon suspects potential smuggling to China likely occurs via Taiwanese customers rather than US or direct China sales.
- Rasgon did not cut his near-term revenue estimates and believes customers are not reducing data center purchases next year because of the scandal, but he sees significant long-term risks and further negative headlines.
- Jensen Huang's unique role combined chief technologist, strategist, salesperson, and storyteller with a flat organization of about 60 direct reports. His handshake deals and personal relationships with customers and suppliers are difficult for a new CEO to replicate.
- Nvidia's current technology roadmap: Rubin and Rubin Ultra are already 'baked,' Feynman is next, but longer-term architecture decisions on memory, HBM, and other inflections were previously made by Huang and are now uncertain.
- On China policy, Huang favored selling older-generation chips to China to keep them dependent on US technology; Rasgon agrees with this approach but says it is now too late. China hawks may use the scandal to push tougher export controls, including a crackdown on remote access to US cloud compute from China.
- For the upcoming Nvidia earnings, interim CEO Colette Kress must convince investors that the company is stable, address tough questions on the departure and talent retention, and deliver technically perfect numbers to avoid further stock pressure.
- Employee retention risk is high: many Nvidia employees have net worths in the tens or hundreds of millions of dollars and may choose to leave without Jensen's presence.
- Competitive landscape: While Nvidia's CUDA software ecosystem remains a moat, alternatives from AMD, Broadcom, Google TPUs, and others are growing, and AI-assisted software porting could erode CUDA's advantage. However, the overall market is expanding, leaving room for multiple players.
- Nvidia's role as 'central bank of AI': Jensen was highly bullish on committing company capital for supply and backstops; a new CEO may have a different risk appetite, adding uncertainty to future vendor financing and supply commitments.
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly blamed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick for Nvidia not being placed on a blacklist earlier, signaling an internal US government conflict over China chip export policy.
