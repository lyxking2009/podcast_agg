# Why Saylor's 'Inoculate' Comment May Be a Signal He'll Sell More Bitcoin

## Overview

Laura Shin and guests analyze Michael Saylor's recent statement that Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) would "probably sell some bitcoin to pay a dividend just to inoculate the market." The episode explores what this signals about Strategy's capital structure, the breakdown of the "never sell" narrative, and implications for the broader crypto market.

## Key Points

- **The 32 BTC Sale:** Strategy sold 32 Bitcoin worth approximately $2.5 million — a tiny amount, but it broke the "never sell" promise that sustained the company's premium valuation.
- **Prospectus Analysis:** Glenn Cameron's review of the prospectus reveals a gap between Saylor's public claims and the realities of Strategy's capital structure. ~80% of STRC holders are retail investors.
- **Preferred Share Problem:** Strategy has ~$15 billion in preferred shares with 10-12% dividend rates, translating to ~$1.7 billion per year in required dividend payments. The company has no operating revenue to cover these obligations.
- **No Good Options:** The analysis concludes Strategy faces a situation with no good options. Selling BTC damages the narrative; not selling creates dividend payment pressure.
- **Mike Dudas on ETH:** His firm holds zero ETH — five years of contradictory narratives have left the market unable to properly value it.
- **On Hyperliquid:** Views it as closer to Tether than a competing L1. The no-KYC international market is enormous, and asset quality is the moat.
- **On Solana:** The decline is attributed to memecoin activity peaking and not recovering.
- **AI and Crypto:** Agentic trading will far outpace agentic payments. Incumbents like Visa, Mastercard, and Stripe are moving too fast for new entrants to displace them.
