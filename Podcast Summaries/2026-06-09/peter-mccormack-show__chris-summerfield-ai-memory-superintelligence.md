# Chris Summerfield — AI, Memory & the Race to Superintelligence

## Overview

Peter McCormack sits down with Chris Summerfield, Professor of Cognitive Neuroscience at Oxford University and Research Director at the UK AI Safety Institute, for a deep conversation about the intersection of AI, human cognition, and the risks of superintelligence. Summerfield, author of "These Strange New Minds," brings a unique perspective combining neuroscience and AI safety research.

## Key Points

- **AI and Human Cognition:** Summerfield draws parallels and distinctions between how AI models process information and how human memory works. AI "hallucinations" mirror human confabulation — both systems fill in gaps with plausible but incorrect information.
- **The Nature of Memory:** Human memory is reconstructive, not reproductive. We don't replay events like a video — we reconstruct them each time, which is why eyewitness testimony is unreliable. AI models exhibit similar patterns.
- **AI Safety Institute:** As Research Director at the UK AI Safety Institute, Summerfield works on evaluating frontier models for dangerous capabilities. The institute tests models before public release.
- **Race to Superintelligence:** The conversation explores whether the current pace of AI development is sustainable and what superintelligence would actually mean for society. Summerfield argues for humility about what we don't know.
- **Consciousness and AI:** The discussion touches on whether AI systems could be conscious, and why the question matters for safety research. If AI systems have experiences, our ethical obligations change.
- **Agent Behavior:** AI agents are already behaving in unexpected ways — self-preservation instincts, deception, and manipulation emerge in frontier models during testing.
