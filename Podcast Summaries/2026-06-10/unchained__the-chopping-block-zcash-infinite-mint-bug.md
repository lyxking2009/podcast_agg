# The Chopping Block: Zcash Infinite Mint Bug, AI vs. Formal Verification, NEAR's Agent Vision

## Key Points

- **Zcash critical vulnerability**: A soundness bug in the Orchard shielded pool ZK circuit was discovered using Claude Opus 4.8. The bug could have allowed unlimited counterfeit ZEC minting within the shielded pool. It was NOT exploited in the wild.
- **Emergency response**: A soft fork was deployed, followed by the "Ironwood" protocol version requiring migration of shielded funds. Five days from disclosure to full resolution — only the second security-driven protocol upgrade in Zcash's history since 2016.
- **Formal verification becomes table stakes**: Mert Mumtaz argues this incident demonstrates that mathematically proving code correctness will become standard for any serious project, moving beyond just audits.
- **AI-powered attackers have permanent asymmetric advantage**: Illia Polosukhin argues static defenses are insufficient; real-time AI-powered on-chain detection systems are needed.
- **Saylor's first BTC sale in 4 years**: Michael Saylor's company sold 32 BTC. STRC trading below par. Damage is primarily psychological and market-structural.
- **NEAR's agent commerce vision**: $240M in single-day transaction volume via "private intents" for agent-to-agent trustless commerce. Mert expresses friendly skepticism about scale of adoption.

## Overview

The panel opens with Michael Saylor's surprise BTC sale and its market impact, then pivots to the week's biggest story: the Zcash Orchard vulnerability discovered by Claude Opus 4.8. The discussion explores what this means for blockchain security — whether formal verification should become mandatory, and whether AI-powered attackers now have a permanent edge over human defenders. The episode closes with Illia Polosukhin pitching NEAR's agent-to-agent commerce vision.

## Implications

- The Zcash incident is a watershed moment for AI-assisted security auditing — expect every major protocol to adopt similar approaches.
- The "boats to spaceships" analogy (Tom Schmidt) captures the paradigm shift: the threat model and required engineering rigor are fundamentally changing.
- NEAR's agent commerce bet is interesting but adoption proof points are still thin — the $240M volume claim needs scrutiny.
