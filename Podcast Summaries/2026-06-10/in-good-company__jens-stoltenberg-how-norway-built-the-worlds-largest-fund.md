# Jens Stoltenberg: How Norway Built the World's Largest Fund

## Key Points

- **Norway vs. UK — same oil, different outcomes**: Both produced roughly the same amount of oil and gas. Norway has a $2+ trillion sovereign wealth fund; the UK has none. The key difference: Norway's 78% tax rate on oil/gas and strong state ownership meant all revenues flowed to the state.
- **Three critical political decisions**: (1) Save ALL oil/gas revenues by law; (2) The "golden fiscal rule" (2001): only withdraw the expected real return (~3%); (3) Invest in equities (decided 1997, in the very room they're recording in).
- **The fiscal rule was "extremely controversial" initially**: 3% of a small fund wasn't much money. Now 3% of $2T+ is enormous — more accepted today than when introduced.
- **Ethics council suspended**: Norway can't be invested in defense companies (Lockheed Martin, Boeing) that it buys F-35s from — a paradox highlighted by Stoltenberg's NATO experience. The ethics framework needs revision for the current geopolitical reality.
- **Tech-defense integration challenge**: If the fund can't own defense companies, and tech and defense are increasingly integrated, it can't be a broad-based international fund anymore.
- **Caterpillar paradox**: The ethics council recommended divesting from Caterpillar because a tiny fraction of its trucks sold to the US government were then sold to Israel. If that's sufficient grounds, big tech companies face similar exposure.
- **Top 10 companies = 25% of fund**: Tech concentration is enormous. Norway has earned more from tech companies than almost anyone. But Stoltenberg refuses to try timing the market: "If we had made that decision four or five years ago, we would have lost a lot of money."
- **Private companies (SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI)**: The fund will invest as soon as they go public. Stoltenberg acknowledges the paradox but says it's "far too early" to change policy.
- **Spending slightly less than 3%**: Actual withdrawals have been 2.7-2.8% in recent years, building a buffer for potential market downturns.
- **Main source of wealth is labor, not oil**: Stoltenberg's key message — oil helps but isn't a guarantee. Work, education, and investment for the future matter more.

## Overview

Nicolai Tangen interviews Jens Stoltenberg about the creation and management of Norway's $2+ trillion sovereign wealth fund. Stoltenberg, who was Finance Minister when the first installment was made in 1996, explains the three critical political decisions that made the fund possible, the ongoing tension between ethical guidelines and geopolitical reality (especially around defense investments), and why Norway's wealth ultimately comes from labor, not oil.

## Implications

- The ethics framework overhaul will be closely watched by other sovereign wealth funds navigating ESG vs. security tradeoffs.
- Norway's 3% spending rule is a model for resource-rich nations, but the "Norwegian disease" risk (complacency, low work participation) is real.
- The private-to-public investment pipeline means the fund will miss out on SpaceX/Anthropic/OpenAI value creation until IPO.
