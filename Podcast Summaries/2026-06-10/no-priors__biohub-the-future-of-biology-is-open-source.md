# Biohub: The Future of Biology is Open-Source

## Key Points

- **Biohub is now CZI's primary philanthropic effort** with a $500 million Virtual Biology Initiative. The goal: integrate frontier AI with frontier biology to build predictive world models of cells, proteins, and systems.
- **Mission evolution**: Started 10 years ago aiming to "cure, prevent, and manage all disease by end of century." Now Zuckerberg says that timeline is "too conservative" given AI advances.
- **The core insight**: Biology has structure (like language) that AI can learn. Proteins, genes, cells — they're all tokens. "From a computer's perspective, it doesn't care if it's a cell, a protein, a word, an image."
- **ESMFold2 launched**: Open-source engine for protein biology. Folded 1.1 billion proteins, predicted their structures. Can design proteins and single-chain antibodies digitally — nanomolar binders found in 96-well plate validation.
- **Hierarchical modeling strategy**: Proteins → cells → biological systems. Each layer qualitatively different but connected. You can't model cells without understanding proteins first.
- **Wet lab + AI as single integrated effort**: Unique among organizations. Cryo-EM microscopes, translucent zebrafish imaging, cellular engineering sensors — all feeding data into AI models.
- **Why nonprofit, not venture-backed**: "We'll have a bigger impact by getting this in more scientist hands quicker by doing it as open source." Data generation requires novel scientific approaches, not just purchasing — making it harder to commercialize.
- **Mechanistic interpretability for biology**: Protein language models trained on billions of sequences develop representations that correspond to biological structure and function. You can "open up the black box and understand the biology the model is representing."
- **Virtual Cell is the next big challenge**: A system that can model proteomic, genetic, and transcriptomic layers connected to phenotype. Requires enormous new data generation.
- **Drug development implications**: Predicting off-target effects using single cell atlas data (e.g., kidney cell receptor expression) could dramatically reduce clinical trial failures. "Rare Is One" program enables patient self-organization for rare disease trials.
- **Open-source philosophy**: "We believe that a positive future is one where you build a technology as a tool. You put it in individuals' hands." — Zuckerberg
- **Talent strategy**: AI researchers here could work on language models anywhere, but "those labs don't have the frontier biology part attached." A strong team of 12-24 people can make world-class progress.

## Overview

An in-depth conversation with Mark Zuckerberg, Priscilla Chan, and Alex Rives about Biohub's transformation into CZI's primary mission. The discussion covers the 10-year journey from an ambitious disease-curing vision to a concrete AI-biology integration strategy, the launch of ESMFold2 (open-source protein design), the hierarchical modeling approach from proteins to virtual cells, and why open-source nonprofit is the right structure for this work. The guests explain how mechanistic interpretability is revealing new biology from AI models, and how this could transform drug development.

## Implications

- ESMFold2's protein design capability is a significant step toward programmable biology — the 96-well plate validation proves digital-first drug design works.
- The open-source approach could accelerate the entire field but may limit CZI's ability to capture value.
- The virtual cell, if achieved, would be as transformative for biology as GPT was for language.
- Rare disease patient self-organization ("Rare Is One") combined with AI-designed therapeutics could unlock a long tail of treatments.
