# At The Money: How Fixed-Income Investors Can Use ETFs

## Key Points

- **Bond ETFs revolutionized fixed income access**: Before ETFs, buying bonds required phone calls to multiple dealers with uncertain pricing and uneven access. ETFs brought transparency, exchange trading, and known real-time prices.
- **Over 1,000 bond ETFs in the US** now, spanning treasuries, credit, high yield, emerging markets, with maturity cuts, outcome overlays, hedged products, and active strategies.
- **ETFs passed stress tests in 2020 and 2022**: During COVID crash, even treasuries and investment grade struggled to trade, but ETFs (though at discounts) were tradable in record volume. The rate shock of 2022 further cemented confidence.
- **Current rate environment**: Market has gone from pricing in cuts to pricing in a full hike by year-end and another next year. The 10-year yield has oscillated like a sine wave over 3 years (3.6% to 5% and back).
- **Don't try to time rates — diversify duration**: Recommended approach is intermediate duration (3-7 years), not all short or all long. "Just don't put all your eggs in one basket."
- **TIPS gaining importance**: With supply shocks and energy inflation, TIPS provide portfolio resilience. iShares offers STIP, TIP, and ICPI (1-year) for different inflation exposure levels.
- **Income is the dominant theme**: Most fixed income assets now yield above 4% (vs. ~20% pre-pandemic). Investors are focused on income opportunity rather than rate timing.
- **"Plus sectors" gaining interest**: High yield, emerging markets, securitized assets (mortgages, ABS, CMBS) offer attractive income relative to duration risk. Rick Rieder's BINC (multisector income ETF) is enormously popular.
- **New FOMC chair Kevin Walsh**: Market has already priced in expectations — the question is whether you believe the market's rate path. If you don't, adjust duration accordingly.

## Overview

Barry Ritholts interviews Steve Laipply of BlackRock about the evolution and current state of fixed income ETFs. The conversation covers how bond ETFs democratized access to fixed income, their performance during stress events (COVID, rate shock), and the current investment landscape where income is king and duration diversification is the recommended approach. Laipply highlights the explosion of bond ETF products (1,000+ in the US) and the shift toward "plus sectors" for enhanced yield.

## Implications

- Fixed income ETFs have matured from a niche product to a core portfolio building block — the stress test era is over.
- The income theme will persist as long as yields stay above 4% — this is a structural shift in investor behavior.
- TIPS allocation should be considered mandatory in the current inflationary environment.
