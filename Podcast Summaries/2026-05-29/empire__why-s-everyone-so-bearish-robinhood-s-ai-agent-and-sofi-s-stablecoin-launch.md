# Why's Everyone So Bearish, Robinhood's AI Agent and SoFi's Stablecoin Launch

## Key Points
- Crypto social media sentiment is deeply bearish despite Bitcoin trading around $73,000, with the Fear and Greed Index at extreme fear levels and Bitcoin apparent demand falling to nearly -147,000 BTC — its most negative reading since December 2025.
- The hosts debate whether the widespread bearishness represents a cycle top or a mid-cycle sentiment reset, noting that extreme fear conditions have historically preceded market recoveries but macro headwinds remain real.
- Robinhood launched Agentic Trading in beta, allowing users to connect third-party AI agents via Model Context Protocol to a dedicated wallet that can autonomously execute stock trades, with guardrails including spending limits, trade notifications, and fraud review.
- Robinhood VP of Product Abhishek Fatehpuria noted significant customer demand: 'We've heard a lot of demand from our customers to bring their own tools, LLMs, and agents, and connect them to Robinhood,' with options trading, crypto, and futures support coming in a future phase.
- SoFi launched SoFiUSD — a bank-issued, USD-backed stablecoin — to its 15 million members on Ethereum and Solana, making it the first U.S. national bank to offer a stablecoin directly on a public blockchain, with CEO Anthony Noto framing it as combining 'the speed and versatility of the blockchain with the trust of a bank.'
- SoFi stock surged 12% on the stablecoin announcement, reversing some of its 35% year-to-date decline, with a bullish put/call ratio of 0.16 compared to a 20-day average of 0.39.
- Morpho unveiled Morpho Midnight, a fixed-rate, intent-based credit protocol developed over two years; CEO Paul Frambot explicitly stated it is 'not a sequel to Morpho Blue — it is a completely new paradigm for onchain lending,' as Morpho's TVL has grown to $11.78 billion, making it DeFi's second-largest lending protocol.
- The GENIUS Act, enacted July 2025, has spurred stablecoin market cap growth of nearly 24% to $320.8 billion, and OCC/FDIC proposed implementation rules in early 2026 have created the framework enabling bank-issued products like SoFiUSD.
- Rob Hadick shared takeaways from a Washington D.C. trip, noting that regulatory clarity for crypto is progressing but slowly, with implementation timelines extending into early 2027 before full GENIUS Act compliance is required.
- RoboStrategy, a Nasdaq-listed company mimicking MicroStrategy's capital-raise-to-buy-assets flywheel but applied to robotics equities, has seen its market valuation reach approximately 3x its NAV of $7.31 per share, echoing the premium dynamics seen with MicroStrategy's Bitcoin treasury model.
- The DeFi investability debate centered on whether token valuations, liquidity fragmentation, and lack of clear fundamentals make it structurally difficult to generate alpha in DeFi at current prices.

## Overview
In this weekly roundup, Empire hosts Jason Yanowitz, Santiago Roel, and Rob Hadick (of Dragonfly Capital) examine the pervasive bearish mood that has gripped crypto social media and investor circles despite Bitcoin still trading at elevated levels relative to historical norms. The opening segment digs into why the "timeline" — crypto Twitter and broader crypto social media — feels so negative, exploring whether the sentiment reflects genuine macro headwinds, disillusionment with altcoins and tokens, or simply the psychological effect of a market that has moved sideways after its late-2024 peak. The hosts debate whether this represents a cycle top or a healthy mid-cycle reset, touching on Bitcoin demand data that has turned sharply negative and a Fear and Greed Index reading in extreme fear territory even as BTC trades around $73,000.

The second major block of the episode covers Robinhood's announcement of Agentic Trading — a feature launched in beta that lets users connect third-party AI agents to a dedicated Robinhood wallet and have those agents execute stock trades autonomously. The hosts discuss what this means for the future of retail finance, noting the product also includes an Agentic Credit Card that lets AI agents make purchases on a user's behalf within set monthly limits. The discussion extends to what "agentic finance" means more broadly: a world where AI intermediaries manage investing, payments, and financial decisions with minimal human involvement. The conversation then pivots to SoFi's launch of SoFiUSD, a bank-issued, USD-backed stablecoin available to its 15 million members, positioning it as the first U.S. national bank to issue a stablecoin on public blockchains (Ethereum and Solana). SoFi stock surged 12% on the announcement, and the hosts contextualize the development within the GENIUS Act regulatory framework that passed in July 2025.

Later segments cover Morpho's unveiling of Morpho Midnight — a new fixed-rate, intent-based lending protocol described by its team as a fundamentally different paradigm from Morpho Blue rather than a simple upgrade — and a broader question about whether DeFi is currently "uninvestable" given token valuations, liquidity fragmentation, and regulatory uncertainty. Rob Hadick shares takeaways from a recent trip to Washington D.C., discussing the pace of regulatory clarity for crypto and how the GENIUS Act's implementation rules from the OCC and FDIC are taking shape. The episode closes with an analysis of the NAV premium dynamics for both MicroStrategy (Strategy) and RoboStrategy, a Nasdaq-listed company that mimics MicroStrategy's capital-raising flywheel but applied to robotics equities, and a "content of the week" segment.

## Implications
The episode captures a moment of genuine tension in crypto markets: structurally improving regulatory infrastructure (the GENIUS Act framework enabling bank-issued stablecoins) is arriving at the same time that market sentiment is at multi-year lows. SoFi's stablecoin launch and Robinhood's agentic trading product together signal that traditional fintech is moving aggressively into crypto rails, which could eventually accelerate mainstream adoption but also intensifies competition for native crypto companies. The hosts' debate about whether DeFi is "uninvestable" reflects a broader industry reckoning with the gap between protocol utility and token value accrual.

Robinhood's agentic trading beta is particularly significant as a signal of where consumer finance is headed: the product operationalizes the concept of autonomous AI financial agents for ordinary retail investors, not just institutions. If this model gains traction, it could fundamentally change how retail trading works — reducing human decision-making friction but raising new questions about liability, market manipulation risk, and the concentration of autonomous trading activity. The concurrent launch of SoFiUSD illustrates how stablecoins are becoming a genuine product layer for regulated banks, a trend that will increasingly define the intersection of TradFi and DeFi.
