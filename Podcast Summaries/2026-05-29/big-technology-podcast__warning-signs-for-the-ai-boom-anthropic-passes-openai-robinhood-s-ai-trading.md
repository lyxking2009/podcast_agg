# Warning Signs For The AI Boom, Anthropic Passes OpenAI, Robinhood's AI Trading

## Key Points
- Only 18% of AI tokens are spent on things that actually ship to production, according to data discussed in the episode, calling into question whether massive corporate token consumption reflects genuine productivity gains.
- Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI token budget in just the first four months of the year; COO Andrew Macdonald said the company has been unable to draw a direct line between token spending and features shipped to users.
- Microsoft canceled Claude Code subscriptions for employees in several key product divisions, and Meta took down the internal tokenmaxxing leaderboard employees had created to track and compete on token consumption.
- One consulting client reportedly spent $500 million in a single month on AI after failing to implement token usage limits on Claude licenses, cited as an extreme illustration of the tokenmaxxing problem.
- The 'circular investment problem' describes a self-reinforcing financial loop where hyperscalers fund AI labs, AI labs commit spending back to hyperscalers, enterprises push token consumption to validate valuations, and rising usage figures support further fundraising rounds.
- Anthropic closed a $65 billion Series H funding round at a $965 billion post-money valuation, surpassing OpenAI (valued at approximately $852 billion) to become the world's most valuable private AI company for the first time.
- Anthropic's annualized run-rate revenue crossed $47 billion earlier in May 2026, growing 80-fold year-over-year, with over 1,000 enterprise customers spending $1 million or more annually.
- Robinhood launched 'Agentic Trading' in beta on May 27, 2026, allowing AI agents (Claude or ChatGPT) to autonomously trade equities from a dedicated, isolated wallet with user notifications on all trades.
- Robinhood also debuted an AI Agent Credit Card, enabling chatbots to make purchases on users' behalf via a banking MCP server connection, with 3% cash back.
- The memory chip boom — driven by AI infrastructure demand — has sold out HBM capacity through 2026, with SK Hynix controlling 57-62% of supply; the hosts raise concerns about what happens when enterprise AI spending slows.
- The episode questions whether connecting personal Gmail to ChatGPT represents a reasonable privacy trade-off, framed through the analogy of accepting free house cleaning in exchange for allowing the cleaner to videotape the session for training data.

## Overview
Alex Kantrowitz and regular guest Ranjan Roy from Margins examine what they describe as emerging warning signs for the AI boom, centering on the collapse of "tokenmaxxing" as a corporate productivity strategy. The episode opens by dissecting a wave of high-profile corporate retreats from aggressive AI token spending: Uber burned through its entire 2026 token budget by April with COO Andrew Macdonald openly questioning ROI, Microsoft canceled Claude Code subscriptions across key product divisions, Meta removed its internal token consumption leaderboard, and one Amazon consulting client reportedly spent $500 million in a single month after failing to set usage limits. A key data point anchors the discussion: only 18% of tokens are actually being spent on things that ship, raising serious questions about whether the enormous volumes of token usage driving revenue projections for AI companies are built on real business value or on wasteful, unmonitored consumption.

The hosts situate these revelations within what they call the "circular investment problem." The AI boom, they argue, is increasingly driven by self-referential financial flows: hyperscalers pour billions into AI model companies, which in turn commit to spend those billions back on cloud infrastructure, while enterprises push employees to consume AI tools, inflating token counts that support ever-higher revenue projections and valuations. This circularity, they contend, could unwind quickly if enterprises continue pulling back. Kantrowitz and Roy also look at the memory chip boom — HBM chips are now sold out through 2026 — as another downstream symptom of speculative overbuilding, with Amazon's free cash flow projected to go negative.

Against this backdrop of concern, the episode covers two major news items. First, Anthropic officially surpassed OpenAI to become the world's most valuable private AI startup, closing a $65 billion Series H at a $965 billion valuation (versus OpenAI's roughly $852 billion) with run-rate revenue crossing $47 billion. The hosts discuss what this milestone means competitively and what it signals about the IPO race between the two companies. Second, Robinhood launched "Agentic Trading" in beta, enabling AI chatbots — specifically Claude and ChatGPT — to autonomously execute stock trades from a dedicated wallet, with an accompanying AI credit card for agent purchases. The hosts weigh the innovation and the risks, including user notification safeguards and fraud detection.

The episode closes with a discussion of privacy trade-offs in the expanding AI ecosystem, using OpenAI's Gmail integration as a jumping-off point. Kantrowitz poses a thought experiment: would you let a house cleaner videotape their work in your home in exchange for free cleaning? The framing captures a broader question the hosts raise about what consumers are willing to exchange — in this case, personal email data — for convenient AI services, and whether the terms of those exchanges are adequately understood.

## Implications
The tokenmaxxing retreat signals a potentially significant inflection point for the AI investment narrative. If enterprises are genuinely pulling back from aggressive token consumption — and the evidence from Uber, Microsoft, and Meta suggests they are — the revenue figures that underpin astronomical AI company valuations may begin to deflate. The circular investment dynamic Kantrowitz and Roy describe is particularly worrying: it means a contraction in enterprise AI spending could cascade simultaneously through model company revenues, hyperscaler cloud commitments, and memory chip demand, creating a self-reinforcing downturn rather than a gradual correction. The timing is especially acute given that both Anthropic and OpenAI are reportedly targeting IPOs as early as late 2026, meaning their public-market debuts could coincide with a sobering reassessment of AI ROI across corporate America.

At the same time, Anthropic's valuation milestone — surpassing OpenAI just months after being valued at $380 billion — illustrates that enterprise-focused, capital-efficient AI businesses can still attract extraordinary investor confidence even in a skeptical macro environment. Robinhood's agentic trading launch represents a broader trend: AI agents moving from productivity tools into financial execution roles, with real-money consequences. This raises regulatory and liability questions that the hosts flag but that the industry has not yet fully addressed. Together, these developments paint a picture of an AI ecosystem that is simultaneously overheating at the infrastructure and spending layer while maturing and consolidating at the product and enterprise layer.
