# Uneasy Money: Illia Polosukhin on Why Onchain Commerce Needs Confidentiality

## Key Points
- Illia Polosukhin was one of eight authors on the landmark 'Attention Is All You Need' transformer paper at Google Brain; the architecture arose from a need to process entire document contexts in parallel on GPUs rather than sequentially with recurrent networks, achieving roughly 10,000x speed improvements.
- Polosukhin's core argument is that confidentiality is the decisive unlock for crypto becoming a layer for daily commerce, because businesses will not operate on public ledgers where competitors can inspect their revenues, supplier payments, and salary figures via block explorers.
- NEAR has rolled out 'Confidential Intents,' a cross-chain transaction layer supporting 150+ assets with restricted visibility, allowing users and institutions to opt into privacy while keeping execution verifiably onchain.
- NEAR deployed 'Shield,' an AI-based real-time anomaly detection system operating across chains, designed to block money laundering and illicit activity without converting NEAR into a surveillance network — a balance Polosukhin acknowledges is technically and politically difficult.
- NEAR detected the Litecoin mimble-wimble vulnerability in real-time as part of its chain security monitoring, demonstrating the practical scope of its cross-chain intelligence capabilities.
- Polosukhin critiques the Ethereum Foundation's approach, arguing that decentralization is a tool for achieving redundancy and competitive resilience, not a terminal goal — organizations need explicit missions beyond negatively defined values like 'don't be evil.'
- NEAR is testing 'Iron Claw,' a multi-tenant AI coordination system that tracks organizational decisions and flags non-decisions, which Polosukhin sees as addressing the fundamental credit-assignment failure in traditional hierarchical corporations.
- Polosukhin projects a transition from hierarchical corporate structures to market-based peer-to-peer systems where individuals deploy AI agents for specific tasks, citing NEAR's AI marketplace offering commodity-priced services such as insurance claims processing and background checks.
- For AI agents to operate autonomously in real markets, Polosukhin argues they require universal cross-chain execution, hardware-enforced security via encrypted enclaves (IronClaw), and a high-throughput settlement layer, all components NEAR is actively combining.
- NEAR's Nightshade 3.0 introduces separation of consensus and execution, atomic transactions, and a live private shard, targeting scalability beyond 1 million transactions per second to support agentic commerce at global scale.
- The near.com super-app connects over 35 blockchains through a single account, supporting cross-chain swaps and peer-to-peer settlement — a consumer-facing interface designed to make blockchain infrastructure invisible to end users.

## Overview
In this episode of Unchained's "Uneasy Money" series, hosts Kain Warwick (founder of Infinex and Synthetix), Taylor Monahan (MetaMask security expert), and Luca Netz (CEO of Pudgy Penguins) sit down with Illia Polosukhin, co-founder of NEAR Protocol and one of the eight Google Brain researchers who authored "Attention Is All You Need," the seminal transformer paper that underlies modern large language models. Polosukhin explains how his experience building question-answering systems at Google led to the architectural insight of parallel GPU processing across entire documents, a breakthrough that made transformers 10,000 times faster than recurrent approaches and ultimately gave rise to the current era of generative AI.

The central thesis of the episode is Polosukhin's argument that confidentiality is the missing piece preventing cryptocurrency from becoming a layer for everyday business commerce. He contends that no enterprise will conduct commerce onchain when competitors can freely inspect their revenues, supplier payments, and payroll through a block explorer. NEAR's response is a suite of "Confidential Intents," which support restricted-visibility environments for cross-chain transactions across 150+ assets, allowing businesses and individuals to opt into privacy while still keeping execution verifiably onchain. The conversation also covers NEAR's "Shield" AI system, which performs real-time anomaly detection across chains to prevent money laundering and illicit activity — a deliberate effort to balance non-custodial accessibility against the risk of becoming a vehicle for crime.

The discussion broadens to examine the Ethereum Foundation's current identity crisis and Vitalik Buterin's recent calls for the EF to become smaller and more technically focused. Polosukhin argues that decentralization should be understood as a practical tool enabling redundancy and competition rather than an ideological end in itself, and he criticizes organizations that adopt "don't be evil" as a mission rather than a means. The hosts and guest also explore how organizational structures will change as AI takes on execution roles: Polosukhin describes NEAR's internal experiment with "Iron Claw," a multi-agent coordination system that tracks decisions, surfaces non-decisions, and identifies bottlenecks — pointing toward a future where market-based systems replace corporate hierarchies for many economic activities.

## Implications
The episode makes a compelling case that the next major unlock for crypto adoption will not come from reduced fees or faster settlement times, but from privacy infrastructure. Polosukhin's argument reframes the confidentiality debate: rather than a regulatory concession, privacy is a prerequisite for legitimate business use. If NEAR's Confidential Intents succeed, they would enable a class of institutional and SME commerce that has been effectively locked out of onchain rails by the public-ledger visibility problem. The simultaneous deployment of AI-based fraud detection via Shield addresses the regulatory concern head-on, suggesting that the privacy-versus-compliance trade-off may be technically tractable in ways that earlier privacy coins (Monero, Zcash) could not demonstrate credibly.

The conversation around Iron Claw and AI-driven organizational coordination points to a longer-horizon implication: if AI can handle credit assignment, decision tracking, and execution at the organizational level, the economic rationale for large centralized corporations weakens significantly. This could accelerate the formation of small, agent-augmented teams operating through onchain market mechanisms rather than employment contracts and internal bureaucracy. NEAR's position — straddling both the AI agent infrastructure stack and the confidential settlement layer — would place it at the intersection of two of the most disruptive trends in technology, though the execution risks and competitive pressures from Ethereum L2s, Solana, and other chains remain substantial.
