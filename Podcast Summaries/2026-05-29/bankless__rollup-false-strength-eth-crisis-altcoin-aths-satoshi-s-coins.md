# ROLLUP: False Strength? | ETH Crisis | Altcoin ATHs | Satoshi's Coins

## Key Points
- Bitcoin fell ~5.2% on the week to ~$73,000 while Ethereum dropped ~6.2% to $1,995, breaching the psychologically important $2,000 level.
- BlackRock's IBIT Bitcoin ETF saw its largest single-day sell-off ever at $1.3 billion, raising concerns about institutional conviction.
- David Hoffman's article 'Why I Sold My ETH' generated widespread debate; Vitalik Buterin responded by acknowledging ETH's importance as an asset, noting 'nearly 90% of my net worth is in ETH.'
- Ethereum Foundation announced a pivot toward CROPS — Censorship Resistance, Open Source, Privacy, Security — as its core infrastructure focus.
- Select altcoins (Hyperliquid, Nier, Venice, Zcash) are hitting all-time highs even as majors decline, challenging the 'strong crypto rising tide' narrative.
- Polymarket odds: 55% probability BTC hits $55,000 by end-2026, 20% probability of $35,000 BTC, and 60% probability ETH hits $1,500.
- Hyperliquid ETFs have seen over $101M in inflows since launch, outperforming BTC and ETH ETF flows on a relative basis.
- OpenZeppelin's founder recommended exiting DeFi; Aave's Stani Kulechov and Uniswap's Hayden Adams pushed back, with Adams saying 'This is not a DeFi problem, this is a supply chain OPSEC problem.'
- Standard Chartered projected ETH could reach $40,000 by 2030, comparing its current position to Amazon post-dot-com crash.
- SoFi launched a USD stablecoin to its 14.7 million banking app users, and Cash App added multi-chain USDC support — significant mainstream on-ramps.
- Geopolitical tailwinds: Iran peace deal reportedly near, Strait of Hormuz reopening negotiations underway, oil at ~$93-94/barrel.
- 'Noah Doe' filed a New York lawsuit claiming Satoshi's dormant 3.8 million Bitcoin (~$285B) under abandoned property law, described as a novel legal theory.

## Overview
In this weekly Friday Rollup episode, Bankless co-founders David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams dissect a turbulent week across crypto markets, headlined by a deepening sentiment crisis around Ethereum and selective strength in a handful of altcoins. Bitcoin slipped roughly 5% on the week to around $73,000, while ETH dropped below $2,000, prompting an extended discussion about whether what appeared to be market strength in recent weeks was actually 'false strength.' The episode follows up on David Hoffman's widely-discussed article 'Why I Sold My ETH,' and the hosts probe what it means for the broader Ethereum thesis when even its most prominent advocates are rotating out.

The hosts examine diverging market narratives: majors like BTC and ETH are underperforming, while a select cluster of altcoins — Hyperliquid, Nier, Venice, and Zcash — are hitting all-time highs. Polymarket odds suggest significant downside risk for both BTC and ETH through end-2026, with 55% probability of Bitcoin reaching $55,000 and 60% probability of ETH reaching $1,500. Despite the bearish macro signals, institutional developments continue apace, including Hyperliquid ETF inflows surpassing $101M and SoFi launching a USD stablecoin to its 14.7 million banking app users.

A major discussion thread involves DeFi security, sparked by OpenZeppelin's founder advising friends and family to exit DeFi positions. Founders of Aave, Uniswap, and MakerDAO pushed back, drawing a distinction between AI-driven vulnerability threats and traditional operational security (OPSEC) failures. The episode also covers geopolitical developments — an Iran peace deal reportedly near completion and oil prices at wartime lows — and closes with the unusual legal story of 'Noah Doe,' who filed a New York lawsuit claiming ownership of Satoshi Nakamoto's 3.8 million Bitcoin under abandoned property law.

## Implications
The episode's central tension — 'false strength' in markets — raises a structural question for the crypto cycle: if the rotation thesis (from BTC/ETH into altcoins) is playing out but major assets are also declining in absolute terms, this may not signal a healthy altseason but rather a fragmented, risk-off environment. The Ethereum sentiment crisis, amplified by a prominent insider selling, could accelerate capital rotation away from the asset even as fundamental builders (Vitalik, EF) double down. The Polymarket odds and the IBIT outflow data suggest institutional conviction is softer than the 2024-2025 bull narrative implied.

On the adoption side, SoFi and Cash App integrations represent the continued quiet march of stablecoin infrastructure into mainstream finance — developments that may matter more for long-term crypto utility than short-term price action. The DeFi security debate, if AI-driven exploit risks are real and not just OPSEC failures, could introduce a new class of systemic risk that affects the sector's credibility with institutional entrants. The Satoshi lawsuit, while likely frivolous legally, underscores the unresolved philosophical and legal questions around dormant Bitcoin and could become a recurring distraction.
