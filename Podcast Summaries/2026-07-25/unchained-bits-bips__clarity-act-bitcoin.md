# Bits + Bips: Why Bitcoin Has the Least to Gain From the Clarity Act

## Key Points

- Cole Kennelly (Volmex Labs) argues that Ethereum, Solana, and Hyperliquid have significantly more to gain from the Clarity Act than Bitcoin.
- Bitcoin already operates in a relatively clear regulatory framework as a commodity — the Clarity Act's benefit to BTC is incremental at best.
- Alt-L1s and DeFi protocols face the most regulatory uncertainty today, so clarity legislation would unlock the most value for them.
- BVIV and BVIV-US (Volmex's Bitcoin volatility indices) diverge around IBIT's regulated options market — institutional options activity is creating a structural bifurcation in volatility pricing.
- The Clarity Act sorts crypto assets into three categories: digital commodities (CFTC), investment contract assets (SEC), and payment stablecoins (banking regulators).
- The Senate's August 10 recess deadline is approaching with no floor vote scheduled — odds of passage in 2026 have dropped significantly.
- Cole shares his outlook for an increasingly institutional crypto market by year-end.

## Overview

In this short clip from a longer conversation, Cole Kennelly of Volmex Labs breaks down why the Clarity Act's impact would be uneven across crypto assets. While Bitcoin maximalists may cheer regulatory clarity, Kennelly argues BTC already enjoys commodity status — the real beneficiaries would be Ethereum, Solana, and DeFi protocols currently operating in regulatory gray zones. He also discusses how IBIT's regulated options market is creating structural divergence in Bitcoin volatility pricing (BVIV vs BVIV-US), and shares his institutional outlook for the market.

## Implications

The framing that Bitcoin has the "least to gain" from the Clarity Act challenges the prevailing narrative that regulatory clarity is uniformly bullish for all crypto. If the act passes, the biggest price moves may come from alt-L1s and DeFi tokens rather than BTC. The institutional volatility divergence (BVIV/BVIV-US) suggests sophisticated market participants are already positioning for different regulatory outcomes.
