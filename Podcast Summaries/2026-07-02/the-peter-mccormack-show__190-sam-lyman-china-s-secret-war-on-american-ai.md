## Overview

In this episode, Peter McCormack sits down with Sam Lyman, head of research at the Bitcoin Policy Institute (BPI) and former senior adviser and chief speechwriter to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, to discuss BPI's investigation into China's covert campaign to undermine US AI infrastructure. Lyman presents a detailed "skitso chart" tracing the influence chain: from the Chinese Communist Party, through Shanghai-based American Marxist Neville Roy Singham and his $278 million network of US nonprofits (Code Pink, the Answer Coalition, Tricontinental, People's Forum, BreakThrough News), to the Party for Socialism and Liberation (PSL), which serves as the operational political arm. The PSL has participated in 21 documented campaigns across 14 states that blocked or delayed approximately $24 billion in proposed data center investment — including a $12 billion QTS project in Wisconsin and a $1 billion Google facility in Indiana.

Lyman is careful to distinguish foreign-amplified opposition from genuine organic concern: he stresses that many Americans have legitimate worries about data center water usage, electricity prices, and community character, and that these real grievances provide fertile ground for foreign actors to exploit. The episode covers the mechanics of how a Marxist-Leninist organization with opaque finances can flip sleepy town council votes by busing in protesters, as well as the broader geopolitical stakes: whoever controls AI will control the "information layer of the next economy," and the choice is between an AI ecosystem built on American values of free expression or one shaped by Chinese censorship and state control. Lyman draws a direct parallel to the Cold War-era Communist Control Act of 1954, arguing the threat from China-aligned domestic actors is structurally identical to Soviet-directed communist infiltration — just updated for the nonprofit sector.

The conversation also ranges into adjacent topics: the AI safety "doom" narrative (Lyman estimates 1-2% P(doom) from ASI and suspects frontier AI executives are using catastrophism as a regulatory strategy to build moats), the tension between open-source and closed-weight models, export controls on models like Anthropic's "Fable" (which requires proof of citizenship), and the distillation-attack method by which China's open-source models like DeepSeek leech capability from US frontier models. Throughout, McCormack probes whether the threat is real or overstated, and Lyman consistently grounds his claims in BPI's documented research, congressional inquiries, and the OpenAI bot-campaign report.

## Implications

The episode makes the case that America's AI race with China has an underreported domestic front: foreign-funded activist networks are translating Chinese strategic interests into local zoning defeats and state moratorium legislation, costing the US economy tens of billions in infrastructure investment. If Lyman's analysis is correct, the anti-data-center movement is not simply a grassroots environmental backlash but a structured influence operation that exploits genuine community concerns as "raw material." The policy implication is urgent: without FARA enforcement, mandatory disclosure of foreign funding into advocacy nonprofits, and public education about who finances opposition campaigns, China can continue blocking US AI buildout through legal channels while facing no equivalent domestic opposition at home.

The episode also surfaces a deeper tension in US AI policy: the same openness and free speech norms that make American AI trustworthy also make the country vulnerable to foreign information operations. Lyman's argument that open-source AI (with full weight transparency) is the answer to both the censorship risk and the regulatory-capture risk from frontier companies points toward a Bitcoin-influenced libertarian framework for AI governance — one that prioritizes decentralization and accessibility over state-managed safety. Whether that framework can coexist with legitimate national security concerns around distillation attacks and bioweapon risks remains an open question the episode raises but does not fully resolve.
