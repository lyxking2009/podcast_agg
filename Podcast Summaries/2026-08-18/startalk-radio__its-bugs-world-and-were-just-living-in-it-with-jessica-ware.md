# It’s Bugs’ World and We’re Just Living In It with Jessica Ware — StarTalk Radio

## TL;DR
Neil deGrasse Tyson and co-host Negin Farsad sit down with entomologist Jessica Ware (American Museum of Natural History) to explore the extraordinary world of insects — what actually makes a "bug," how insects trace back 400 million years to a single common ancestor, why dragonflies were the original flying design for living things, how dragonfly eyes and brains work, and why insects have filled every ecological niche on Earth. The episode closes on the sobering collapse of insect populations worldwide — light pollution disrupting firefly mating, urbanization destroying habitat — and the reminder that all life on land came after insects and still depends on them. ## Key points
- **What is a bug?** Whether an insect counts as a "true bug" comes down to how it eats (piercing/sucking mouthparts in the Hemiptera order) — a definitional deep-dive that runs through the show.
- **A 400-million-year lineage:** insects trace back to a single common ancestor; they predate dinosaurs by hundreds of millions of years, were the first animals to fly, and have had enough evolutionary time to fill every ecological niche on the planet.
- **Dragonflies were the original flying design:** the show explores dragonfly eyes (some of the most complex visual systems in nature) and how their mating methods relate to vision; territorial dragonflies may have larger brains than non-territorial ones, and migratory insects may develop bigger brains for navigation.
- **Success by sheer time:** insects' dominance comes from being first to flight and having hundreds of millions of years of head start — enough time to radiate into every niche, from pollinators to predators.
- **Buggy futures:** warming weather will make the future more buggy; New York has cockroach species that "only reluctantly share our spaces."
- **Should we eliminate mosquitoes?** Wiping out the three most dangerous mosquito species would be a net positive for humans, and the things that eat mosquitoes would largely survive.
- **The insect collapse:** insect populations are collapsing worldwide — light pollution disrupts firefly mating, urbanization destroys habitat — and since all terrestrial life came after insects and depends on them, this is an existential biodiversity story, not just an entomology one.
- **Odds and ends:** what bed bugs fed on before beds existed, and just how big insects can get (navel-orange-sized species discussed).
