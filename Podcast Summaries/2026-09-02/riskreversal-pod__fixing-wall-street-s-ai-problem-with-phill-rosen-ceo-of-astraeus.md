# Fixing Wall Street's AI Problem with Phill Rosen, CEO of Astraeus — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Phill Rosen, CEO of Astraeus, discusses building AI-native infrastructure for wealth management firms to unify fragmented data, codify operational rules, and deploy digital workers. Drawing on his fintech background (Orchard Platform, Even Financial, MoneyLion), he explains why deterministic software and traditional machine learning must underpin AI applications, and argues that most AI companies are not model builders but deploy others' models. The episode also covers the broader AI capex bubble, LLM commoditization, and the potential for inference to become a cloud-compute business.

## Key points
- Phill Rosen dropped out of college in the late 1990s and founded Orchard Platform, which used machine learning to programmatically buy unsecured consumer debt for hedge funds and BDCs; he later founded Even Financial, which became MoneyLion Engine and was acquired for $1 billion.
- Even Financial started as a B2B2C marketplace connecting consumer-facing apps to financial institutions via APIs for loans, credit cards, insurance, and mortgages; it grew to about 1,000 channel partners and 1,000 financial institutions before Rosen left.
- At Orchard, Rosen discovered unconventional credit signals like Subaru ownership predicting zero loan defaults regardless of credit score or income, influencing Even Financial's approach to alternative data in underwriting.
- Consumer acquisition channel and intent significantly affect loan performance: borrowers actively searching for loans with a 750 credit score have higher default rates than passive borrowers with the same score, and even SEO keywords impact loan quality.
- Rosen argues that most 'AI companies' are not building models; they fine-tune or deploy existing models. Astraeus is model-agnostic, using primarily Google Gemini and Kimi 3 for inference to reduce cost, maintain data sovereignty, and enable portability.
- Astraeus targets wealth management because fintech 1.0 served mass-market customers who could not leverage complex financial tools; wealth management has affluent clients and a severe data fragmentation problem across legacy systems and vendors.
- Initial Astraeus clients range from $2 billion to $200 billion in AUM, including independent RIAs and private equity rollups; the company launches with about 10 clients and offers 90-day proof-of-value pilots.
- The platform consolidates siloed data (custodians, billing systems, PDFs) into a unified deterministic environment, then codifies each firm's disclosures and operational rules so that AI agents operate strictly within compliance boundaries.
- Astraeus digital workers (agents) handle tasks like tax-loss harvesting, advisor compensation, and client billing, with human-in-the-loop review and audit logs; the platform exposes REST APIs and MCP to allow integration with Claude or Codex for custom agent building.
- Rosen emphasizes that AI should only handle the 'last mile' of analysis or automation, while traditional deterministic software handles heavy data processing, reducing errors and enabling smaller, cheaper models to match larger ones for constrained tasks.
- On AI infrastructure, Rosen questions whether LLM model providers will retain the majority of value or become commoditized like cloud compute; he notes that hosting open-weight models like Kimi 3 in US data centers can yield ~70% margins on inference, excluding training costs.
- Rosen believes the real impact of AI will come when the ecosystem of software libraries and tools around models matures, similar to how the internet's open-source stack developed over 20 years; current chatbot demos are not where most enterprise value will be created.
- Comparing capital intensity, Rosen notes Even Financial raised a $2.7 million seed round while Astraeus raised nearly $11 million, reflecting more available venture capital but also higher expectations for startups today.
