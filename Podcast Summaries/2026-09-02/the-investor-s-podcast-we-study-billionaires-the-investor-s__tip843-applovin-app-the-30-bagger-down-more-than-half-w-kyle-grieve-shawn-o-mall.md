# TIP843: AppLovin (APP): The 30-Bagger Down More Than Half w/ Kyle Grieve & Shawn O'Malley — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)  - The Investor’s Podcast Network

## TL;DR
Kyle Grieve and Shawn O'Malley analyze AppLovin, a mobile advertising platform that has grown revenue over 50% YoY with EBITDA margins near 79%, but whose stock has fallen over 50% in 2026 due to AI disruption fears, competition, and growth deceleration. They break down the business model (AppLovin Ads Manager and Max), competitive advantages from scale and data flywheel, capital allocation including buybacks and executive comp, and risks such as algorithm underperformance and opacity. Kyle's base-case valuation yields ~$480 with a 9% IRR, but both hosts conclude it's a pass due to difficulty understanding the business and staying within their circle of competence.

## Key points
- AppLovin started in 2011 as an app recommendation tool by Adam Foroughi; the app itself failed but the recommendation engine proved valuable, leading to a pivot into mobile advertising technology focused initially on mobile games.
- AppLovin operates four segments: AppLovin Ads Manager (demand-side platform), Max (supply-side mediation), Adjust (measurement/analytics), and Whirl (connected TV). The two core segments are Ads Manager and Max; revenue is reported net, so the gross spread captured is not disclosed but estimated at 30-40%.
- Ads Manager uses predictive algorithms to optimize advertiser spend against ROAS targets, while Max replaced the old waterfall method with real-time auctions; case study TripleDot Solitaire saw average revenue per daily active user rise 20% after switching to Max.
- Competitive advantages include scale economics and a data learning flywheel: owning game studios (later divested to TripleDot for $800M in 2025) provided first-party data to train algorithms; AppLovin is very lean with EBITDA margins ~79% over last 12 months and revenue per employee of $7.6M.
- Market share: Max powers about 55% of top-grossing games and 73% of top mobile games, but faces competition from Google AdMob, Meta Audience Network, Unity LevelPlay, and LiftOff; regulators have not found preferential treatment in AppLovin's auction, unlike Google's antitrust issues.
- Founder and CEO Adam Foroughi controls ~62% voting rights via Class B shares; insider ownership totals ~13% of Class A plus Class B. Executive compensation is criticized: no performance-based incentives, 2023 total comp spiked to $83M due to a one-time performance grant that vested after the stock surged.
- Buyback program: from 2022-2024, AppLovin spent $2.5B to retire ~70M shares at average price ~$35, creating enormous value; during 2025, buybacks continued at average price ~$425, which has so far been less value-accretive. Management believes shares are undervalued.
- Growth levers include e-commerce advertising (28% growth in latest quarter), a new social app called GIST to gather data, expansion into non-gaming apps, open web, and connected TV. However, install volumes have declined YoY in both quarters of 2026, suggesting core gaming market saturation.
- Risks include: intense competition, potential AI disruption, algorithm underperformance (Q2 revenue miss and decelerating growth), regulatory (SEC inquiry closed without action), short seller allegations (Fuzzy Panda, Culper Research), and opacity in disclosures (no segment breakdown, no investor deck).
- Valuation: Kyle's base case assumes 17% revenue CAGR, EBITDA margin ~77%, 13x EV/EBITDA multiple, and 30% margin of safety, resulting in ~$480 intrinsic value and ~9% IRR. Both hosts decide to pass on AppLovin, citing difficulty understanding the business and preferring to stay within circle of competence.
