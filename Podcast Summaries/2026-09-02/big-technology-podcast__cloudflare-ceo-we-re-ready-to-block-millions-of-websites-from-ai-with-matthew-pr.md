# Cloudflare CEO: We're Ready To Block Millions of Websites From AI — With Matthew Prince — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince reveals that AI bot traffic surpassed human traffic for the first time in internet history in May 2026, and projects automated traffic will reach 1000x human levels within five years, driven by AI agents. To protect publishers, Cloudflare will block Google's crawler by default for ad-supported and subscription-supported sites starting mid-September, forcing Google to negotiate compensation like other AI companies. Prince also warns that agentic commerce could consolidate power among a few large companies, threatening small businesses, and calls for new infrastructure like micropayments to sustain the open web.

## Key points
- Cloudflare data shows automated traffic passed human traffic for the first time in history in May 2026, far earlier than previous estimates of late 2027; Prince had expected it in the first half of 2027 as of March 2026.
- Based on current trends, Cloudflare projects automated traffic online will be 1000 times human traffic within five years, driven by AI agents, more model providers, and training crawls; human traffic will not drop but automated traffic will surge.
- Prince argues the web is actually getting healthier: after plateauing around 2015, web creation resumed explosive growth around October 2025, enabled by AI-assisted tools like Lovable, Replit, Base44, and Wix that allow non-technical people to build sophisticated sites and apps.
- Countering concerns about AI slop, Prince acknowledges some low-quality output but insists the net effect is more creativity from humans assisted by AI, and the shift away from App Store to web will revitalize the open internet.
- For publishers, crawler-to-click ratios have roughly doubled in the past year: Google went from 18 to ~36 crawls per click, OpenAI from 1,500 to ~3,000, and Anthropic/Cloudflare from 60,000 to ~120,000, as AI results improve and users click through less.
- Cloudflare categorizes websites into two buckets: the majority (e.g., Cloudflare's own knowledge base) want their content in AI and need efficiency; ad-supported or subscription-supported sites need tools to block unpaid crawlers and negotiate compensation.
- Starting mid-September, Cloudflare will treat Google like other AI crawlers: for ad-supported/subscription-supported sites, Google will be blocked by default with opt-out available, potentially causing millions of sites to drop from Google's index.
- This move comes as publishers like Reddit, People Inc. (Neil Vogel), and USA Today (Mike Reed) publicly threaten to block Google, seeing little revenue left to lose; Prince says Google must pay for content like other AI companies.
- To enable fair exchange, Cloudflare is exploring micropayments infrastructure: Visa handles ~20,000 financial transactions/sec at peak, while Cloudflare handles ~500 million internet transactions/sec; if 1-10% are monetizable day one, payment rails must scale to 10-100 million transactions/sec.
- Prince warns agentic commerce may favor large established companies because agents are infinitely patient and research deeply, lacking emotional brand loyalty or convenience preference, making it hard for new small businesses to gain first customers without rich online information.
- He proposes creating verifiable trust attestations for brands—packaging data on shipping times, returns, reviews, chargebacks—so agents can recognize quality merchants, and calls for industry collaboration with Shopify, PayPal, Visa, MasterCard, American Express, Intuit to equip small businesses.
- Prince is optimistic for media in 2026, predicting content creators will get paid, noting Google's YouTube already pays creators well; he also flags the next problem: if ad-based models disappear, subscription-based agents could widen the digital divide between rich and poor.
