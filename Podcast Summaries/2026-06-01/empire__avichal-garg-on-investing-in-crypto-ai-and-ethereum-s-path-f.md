## Overview

Avichal Garg, Co-Founder and Managing Partner of Electric Capital, joined host Jason Yanowitz on the Empire podcast to lay out how he is thinking about crypto investing in 2026. The conversation ranged from macro allocation strategy to the granular mechanics of why tokens trade at discounts, covering Electric Capital's current thesis across crypto and AI venture. Garg, who co-founded Electric Capital in 2018 after stints at Facebook (building Facebook Connect) and Google (working on Google Assistant), brings both a technical and product lens to evaluating where value accumulates in decentralized networks.

A central theme of the episode is the convergence of AI agents and crypto infrastructure. Garg described this intersection as "historically significant," pointing to open questions about what autonomous code owning a wallet actually means — who is liable, how are transactions authorized, and what governance structures emerge when there is no human in the loop. This framing positions crypto rails not just as financial infrastructure but as the foundational layer for a new class of autonomous economic actors. The discussion of breakout application development followed naturally from this, with Garg outlining the practical ingredients for products that move from niche adoption to mainstream traction within the crypto and AI landscape.

The second half of the conversation focused on specific assets and markets. Garg defended his continued bullishness on both Ethereum and Solana, walking through Ethereum's development roadmap and why he believes the upgrade trajectory justifies optimism despite competitive pressure. He also addressed the structural reasons tokens tend to trade below what their fundamentals might suggest, and covered evolving dynamics in the crypto exchange market. The episode closed with a discussion of DeFi safety improvements, reflecting the ecosystem's broader maturation from high-risk experimentation toward more durable, user-accessible financial primitives.

## Implications

Garg's focus on AI-agent wallets signals where sophisticated crypto venture capital is concentrating attention in 2026. If autonomous agents become meaningful participants in on-chain economies — executing transactions, holding assets, and entering contracts without human oversight — the implications for wallet design, smart contract standards, and regulatory frameworks are substantial. Investors and builders who establish positions in this infrastructure layer early may benefit disproportionately as the use case matures. The unresolved liability questions Garg raised will likely drive significant legal and technical innovation over the next several years.

The continued bullishness on both Ethereum and Solana from a credible, data-driven investor like Garg is notable at a time when the L1 narrative is fragmented. His emphasis on Ethereum's roadmap as a forward-looking catalyst — rather than current metrics — suggests that the market may be underweighting the network's upgrade trajectory. Meanwhile, his structural analysis of token discount dynamics offers a useful framework for investors trying to reconcile on-chain fundamentals with market prices. The convergence of maturing DeFi security practices, clearer token valuation frameworks, and the AI-agent opportunity suggests 2026 could be a productive vintage for disciplined crypto venture deployment.
