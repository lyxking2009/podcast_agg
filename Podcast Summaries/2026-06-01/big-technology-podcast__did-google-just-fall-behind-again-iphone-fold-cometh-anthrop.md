**Date:** 2026-06-01

## Overview

This episode of the Big Technology Podcast brings together host Alex Kantrowitz and guest M.G. Siegler (Spyglass.org) to examine whether Google is falling behind in the AI race a second time. The conversation centers on Google's lackluster Gemini 3.5 Flash launch, the delayed flagship Pro model, and growing competitive pressure from Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's Codex in the coding agent space. Kantrowitz and Siegler argue that Google faces a structural double threat: not only is it short on cutting-edge foundational models, but it is also absent from the emerging wave of AI-powered super apps that automate browser navigation and cross-application task completion. Critically, the hosts point to Google's internal fiefdom culture—where Gmail, Chrome, and other product teams guard their own roadmaps—as a systemic obstacle to building the unified AI experiences that leaner competitors can ship without legacy baggage.

The episode also covers Apple's upcoming WWDC, where a Siri overhaul is expected to headline, possibly powered by Gemini, while the iPhone Fold is pushed to an autumn reveal. Siegler describes the Fold's horizontal flip-phone form factor and speculates that Apple could use the CEO transition from Tim Cook to Ternus to make bold policy moves, such as restructuring App Store revenue sharing. Meta receives critical attention for its disorganized subscription tiers, ongoing layoffs, and frontier AI models that continue to trail competitors. Breaking news mid-recording reveals Anthropic's confidential S-1 filing with the SEC, prompting analysis of its IPO timing advantage over OpenAI and the structural benefits—unit economics, index inclusion, and investor narrative—that position Anthropic favorably heading into public markets.

## Implications

Anthropic's IPO filing represents one of the most consequential near-term events in the AI industry. By moving ahead of OpenAI, Anthropic sets the public market narrative on its own terms, showcasing superior unit economics and a focused enterprise story. If index inclusion follows IPO, the automatic capital inflows from passive funds could be substantial, irrespective of valuation debates. Investors evaluating OpenAI's subsequent offering will inevitably benchmark it against Anthropic, and OpenAI's stagnant monthly active user growth weakens the consumer story that has historically driven its valuation enthusiasm.

For Google, the organizational diagnosis offered in this episode deserves close attention. Capital and talent are not the bottleneck—structural incentives are. Until Google can subordinate individual product roadmaps to a unified AI experience, it will continue ceding ground to purpose-built competitors in the highest-growth segments of the market. Apple's WWDC and the eventual iPhone Fold launch will test whether established hardware platforms can successfully integrate AI as a core differentiator, or whether AI-native companies will capture the value layer above the operating system entirely.
