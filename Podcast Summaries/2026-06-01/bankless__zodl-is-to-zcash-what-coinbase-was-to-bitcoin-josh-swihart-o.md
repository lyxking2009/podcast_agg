# "ZODL is to Zcash What Coinbase Was to Bitcoin" | Josh Swihart on ZEC's Awakening

**Show:** Bankless
**Date:** 2026-06-01

## Key Points

- ZODL (Zcash Open Development Lab) raised $25 million in seed funding from Paradigm, a16z crypto, Winklevoss Capital, and Coinbase Ventures — with most investors putting 75–90% of their allocation into ZEC directly, signaling strong conviction in the asset.
- The percentage of ZEC held in shielded (privacy-protected) addresses has grown from ~11% in early 2024 to ~32% today, a more than 400% increase, serving as ZODL's primary KPI for genuine adoption.
- ZODL's flagship wallet introduced one-click swaps for shielded ZEC, making privacy the default and accessible experience rather than a technical barrier — this product breakthrough drove adoption vertical.
- ZEC has appreciated from approximately $30 three years ago to ~$570 as of May 2026, roughly a 1,500% increase, with the wallet facilitating over $600 million in swaps since October.
- Three converging forces are driving Zcash's momentum: growing skepticism about Bitcoin's financialization and privacy trade-offs, post-quantum computing threats to current encryption, and AI-enabled surveillance expanding data aggregation at unprecedented scale.

- Josh reframed Zcash's identity from "privacy coin" to "unstoppable private money," improving institutional perception and enabling access to NASDAQ/NYSE-listed vehicles and a pending Grayscale ETF.
- Josh sees a narrow ~1,000-day window under the current administration to establish meaningful privacy protections before potential policy shifts, framing urgency around regulatory engagement.
- The anonymity set created by the shielded pool has a reflexive effect: more coins in shielded addresses means less ZEC on exchanges, which supports price appreciation.
- Josh's core framing: financial privacy is not a niche crypto preference but essential infrastructure for human freedom — the absence of private digital money leads to economic dystopia.

## Overview

Josh Swihart, founder and CEO of Zcash Open Development Lab (ZODL), joins Bankless to lay out the case for Zcash's current moment and the organizational changes that have repositioned the project for mainstream relevance. Josh's personal entry into crypto came in 2016 through a practical need — sending money privately to an unbanked friend in India — rooted in his prior work with data tracking and surveillance systems. That background shapes his view that financial privacy is not ideological preference but structural necessity: without private money on the internet, individuals lose agency, security, and true censorship resistance in ways that compound over time.

The episode covers ZODL's formation following a significant governance shift at the Electric Coin Company in early 2026, culminating in a $25 million seed raise from top-tier crypto investors. The funding structure itself was notable — investors placed 75–90% of capital directly into ZEC, treating the asset as the primary conviction play. The shielded pool percentage — now at 32%, up from 11% in early 2024 — serves as ZODL's north-star metric, reflecting actual usage rather than speculative price action. The product catalyst was a redesigned wallet (formerly Zashi, now the ZODL wallet) that made shielded ZEC accessible through one-click swaps, driving over $600 million in transaction volume since October and fueling ZEC's price rise from ~$30 to ~$570 over three years.

Josh articulates three external forces converging to make this moment unusually favorable for Zcash: Bitcoin's increasing financialization is creating skeptics who want more than a store of value; post-quantum computing poses existential risks to current cryptographic standards; and AI is enabling surveillance aggregation at a scale that makes transparent on-chain activity newly dangerous. He frames ZODL's mission as building the institutional accessibility layer — the "Coinbase to Zcash's Bitcoin" — that normalizes privacy rather than keeping it confined to technical subcultures. The regulatory window of roughly 1,000 days under the current administration, where privacy advocates have a foothold in policy discussions, adds urgency to achieving critical mass.

## Implications

ZODL's emergence as a well-capitalized, institutionally-backed entity around Zcash represents a meaningful shift in how privacy-focused crypto projects can be organized and funded. The seed round's structure — with investors buying ZEC directly alongside equity — signals that sophisticated capital is now treating privacy infrastructure as an asset class rather than a regulatory liability. If the shielded pool percentage continues growing and ZEC achieves listings in ETF wrappers, it could normalize privacy-preserving transactions in a way that changes baseline expectations for what money on the internet should look like. The Balaji Srinivasan framing ("ZODL is to Zcash what Coinbase was to Bitcoin") is significant precisely because it maps ZODL's role onto a historically successful onboarding model.

The convergence Josh describes — AI surveillance, post-quantum threats, and Bitcoin's evolving identity — is worth watching as a macro thesis. If even one of these forces intensifies materially (a credible Q-Day announcement, a high-profile AI-driven financial surveillance incident, or Bitcoin ETF holders demanding more from their holdings), demand for Zcash's privacy guarantees could accelerate sharply. The 1,000-day regulatory framing also implies a time-sensitive window: organizations and individuals who want to influence how digital financial privacy gets codified in policy have a narrowing opportunity to do so. Whether ZODL can reach "too big to kill" scale within that window is the central strategic question.
