# Apple’s Most Ambitious Roadmap Ever? + OpenAI vs. Google vs. Anthropic Revisited — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR

Apple is preparing its most ambitious product lineup in years, headlined by a $2,000 foldable iPhone and a new CEO, John Turnis. Guest MG Siegler and host Alex Kantrowitz discuss the company's shift toward risk-taking hardware like touchscreen MacBooks and smart home hubs, and the challenges Turnis faces including supply chain costs, AI positioning, and maintaining iPhone sales. They then reassess the AI race: Google DeepMind appears to have stepped back from frontier model development in favor of cloud compute revenue, OpenAI has regained momentum with GPT-6 and Astra while AGI claims become marketing, and Anthropic is nearing an IPO. The episode highlights a more experimental Apple and an AI industry where hype and reality increasingly blur.

## Key points

- Apple's September 9 event is expected to introduce a foldable iPhone, internally called the iPhone Ultra, priced around $2,000. It features a large interior screen, small external display, Touch ID, split-view multitasking, an advanced hinge, and a less visible crease than competitors. The device is described as roughly passport-sized when folded, and its higher price point is designed to boost average selling price and revenue.
- The foldable iPhone is central to John Turnis's debut as Apple CEO, succeeding Tim Cook who becomes executive chairman. Turnis inherits a strong product pipeline but also immediate challenges: rising memory component prices (3-5x cost increases), potential supply constraints for the foldable, and the need to sustain iPhone sales growth. Cook's retained pay package (targeting $45 million in RSUs) signals his continued active role, especially on geopolitics and supply chain.
- Apple's broader roadmap signals a more risk-taking strategy: a MacBook Pro with touchscreen OLED and Dynamic Island, a 7-inch smart home hub with Siri AI and facial recognition, and future devices like AirPods with cameras, smart glasses, a tabletop robot, and a camera pendant. This is a departure from the past 15 years of refining existing products.
- Guest MG Siegler describes the foldable iPhone as the 'mullet iPhone' — business when folded, party when unfolded. He argues the passport-like form factor could be better for typing and replace an iPad Mini for media consumption, while the host notes foldable phone users 'just look happier.' The device may be positioned as a media-first device, though Apple will market productivity too.
- Samsung's foldable push in 2021 increased overall sales 6% and revenue 11%; Motorola's foldable launch drove sales up 21% and average selling price up 17%. This suggests Apple could see a predictable windfall from its foldable, especially given its brand loyalty and retail strength.
- A SemiAnalysis post argues Google DeepMind is no longer a frontier lab, citing departures from reinforcement learning teams and poor compute allocation. Gemini 3.5 Pro, announced at I/O, appears delayed or abandoned. Instead, Google Cloud Platform under Thomas Kurian is likely the main beneficiary, as compute shifts from model training to cloud sales.
- Shortly after previous recording, Demis Hassabis stepped down as CEO of Google DeepMind, suggesting an internal reset. Google may be pivoting to an AI compute platform strategy, leveraging its TPUs and cloud infrastructure, similar to Microsoft's Azure approach, rather than competing head-to-head on frontier models.
- OpenAI's latest release GPT-6 and Astra have reignited its momentum after falling behind Anthropic. Nvidia's Jensen Huang called it AGI, but the hosts argue AGI claims are now purely marketing, especially since OpenAI no longer has the AGI clause with Microsoft. The hype may escalate as IPO windows open.
- Anthropic is reportedly preparing for an IPO, possibly with ticker 'ENT'. The company may need a major breakthrough or 'one more thing' reveal before going public to maintain momentum against OpenAI's resurgence. The competition between OpenAI and Anthropic is heightened by security breach disclosures that contrast with AGI marketing.
- Apple's AI positioning is considered 'good enough for the masses.' Siri improvements, largely thanks to Google integration, fix long-standing issues like answering sports scores. Apple can rely on its hardware and retail advantages while more advanced agentic AI features roll out later. The company's new products are seen as table stakes for the AI era.
