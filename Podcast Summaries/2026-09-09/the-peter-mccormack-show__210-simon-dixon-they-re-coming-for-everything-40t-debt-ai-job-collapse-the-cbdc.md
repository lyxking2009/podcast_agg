# #210 - Simon Dixon - "They're Coming For Everything": $40T Debt, AI Job Collapse & The CBDC Grid — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR

Simon Dixon discusses the US hitting $40 trillion in debt and the Treasury's efforts to refinance $9.7 trillion, warning of socialized losses and privatized profits. He argues AI will collapse middle-class jobs while forcing governments to issue programmable money and CBDCs tied to social credit. Dixon advocates Bitcoin self-custody and key management as digital resistance against centralized control.

## Key points

- The US national debt has reached $40 trillion, and the Treasury must refinance approximately $9.7 trillion by the end of 2025, increasing quarterly buybacks from $2 billion to $4 billion to manage short-term interest costs rather than printing money.
- Interest expenses now exceed spending on social programs and defense, forcing the government to socialize losses through inflation while profits are privatized via a stock market where the richest 92% own nearly all shares.
- Bond vigilantes are demanding higher long-term yields, threatening mortgage and car loan rates; the Federal Reserve will eventually have to intervene by buying bonds through bank accounts, expanding its balance sheet.
- AI companies are competing directly with the US government for debt financing, needing to raise about $800 billion this year and $1.3 trillion next year to fund data centers, while Big Seven/Ten companies capture 30–40% of the S&P 500.
- SpaceX's public listing illustrates the pump-and-dump dynamic: shares soared to nearly $190 then fell to $110 before recovering to ~$140, with early investors and VCs selling into retail demand after lockup expirations.
- The middle class is feeling severe inflation pressure: Simon cites school fees up 50% in 6–7 years, a regular pub dinner for four costing £200, and Nando's for six at £120, with wages not keeping pace.
- AI will cause rapid job losses in call centers and office work, as models can work 24/7 for $20 per week with no labor rights; there is no chance of creating enough new jobs to replace the 50% of roles at risk.
- Governments plan to introduce programmable money, stablecoins, and central bank digital currencies as a rescue during the AI-driven credit collapse, tying them to data collection and social credit ratings.
- The 'you'll own nothing and be happy' agenda includes digital ID for age verification, social credit systems, and AI surveillance, creating a control grid where those without assets become dependent on state payments.
- Bitcoin self-custody is a key form of digital resistance: owning your private keys, running nodes, and using multi-signature wallets protects against cyberattacks; the recent Coldcard vulnerability allowed AI brute force to steal ~2,000 BTC from ~500 users who used single-signature with a faulty seed generation method.
- Simon's personal story: his father lost his fortune in the dot-com crash after a divorce, tax investigation, and risky stock speculation, which drove Simon to become a stockbroker and later investment banker to understand the system.
- Financial discipline is essential: know your balance sheet, cash flow, and numbers; spend less than you earn and invest the difference in assets monthly, even if it's £10, while building a long-term plan and resisting short-term urgency manipulation.
- The world divides into three types: those who own assets, those who hold debts and are controlled via social credit, and a small digital resistance movement using self-custody, open-source AI, and decentralized infrastructure.
- The UK is in a worse position than the US: debt-to-GDP at 95%, £3 trillion debt, interest costs ~£285 billion this year, while needing to increase defense spending to 3% of GDP; millionaires are leaving for tax-neutral jurisdictions, and a housing market correction is underway with average prices up only 4.9% in three years vs 10.4% CPI.
