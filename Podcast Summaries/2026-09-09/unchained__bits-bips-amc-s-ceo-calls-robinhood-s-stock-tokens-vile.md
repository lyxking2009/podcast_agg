# Bits + Bips: AMC's CEO Calls Robinhood's Stock Tokens 'Vile.' — Unchained

## TL;DR

AMC CEO Adam Aron publicly attacked Robinhood's tokenized AMC shares, calling them 'vile' and accusing the platform of undermining capital formation, while Robinhood executives defended the products as legal and innovative; the dispute coincided with a 21% overnight jump in AMC's stock to $3.07. The hosts dissect the mechanics of synthetic equity tokens versus equity-backed depositary tokens, questioning whether such derivatives solve real problems or are speculative excess.

## Key points

- AMC CEO Adam Aron condemned Robinhood's tokenized AMC shares as 'worthless, outrageous, disgusting, horrible, inexcusable, vile,' stating AMC had nothing to do with the issuance and did not approve it.
- Robinhood's tokenized AMC shares are issued in Jersey by Robinhood Assets, provide price exposure but no ownership or shareholder rights, are not registered in the US, and are not offered to US citizens.
- Vlad Tenev responded 'What's the problem?' and later said Robinhood supports stock tokens; chief legal officer Dan Gallagher, a former SEC member, told AMC to 'Send your lawyers, we'll train them.'
- AMC shares jumped nearly 21% overnight to $3.07 following the conflict, as reported by The Block.
- The core accusation is that synthetic equity separates ownership from a company's control over capital raising and sows distrust; however, supporters note there are 13.4 billion such tokens and the structure resembles an inverse ADR, requiring underlying shares or a derivative to transmit price.
- Chris Perkins argues synthetic products are common on Wall Street and Robinhood likely did its homework; the real conflict is between CEO concerns about capital formation and the SEC's mandate to ensure balanced capital formation, but futures and spot assets generally help each other.
- Another host counters that derivatives do not create capital formation, they transfer risk, and many recent tokenized structures are 'derivatives for the sake of derivatives,' lacking clear use cases like commodity hedges or mortgage-backed securities.
- The discussion highlights microstructure differences: Coinbase perpetual futures on tokenized US stocks vs equity-backed depositary tokens; the latter can actually broaden the investor base and support capital raising, contrary to AMC's criticism if properly structured.
- The hosts also touch on venture capital's 'shiny object syndrome,' meme coins, and Robinhood's L1 launch as imitation, contrasting with transformative innovations like DeFi, transparency, and internet money.
