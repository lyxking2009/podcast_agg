# Carmen Li's Plan to Build a Futures Market for Compute

**Show:** Odd Lots
**Host:** Bloomberg
**Date:** 2026-06-15
**Duration:** 32:56

## Key Points

1. Carmen Li is building a futures market for compute — a derivatives exchange that would allow market participants to hedge or speculate on the price of computational resources, similar to how commodities futures work for oil or wheat.
2. The core thesis is that compute is becoming a commodity: standardized, fungible, and increasingly central to the economy. GPU clusters for AI training represent a massive and growing cost that many businesses need to manage.
3. The project faces the classic cold-start problem of any new derivatives market: you need both hedgers and speculators to create liquidity, but neither side wants to show up first.
4. Li draws parallels to the early days of financial futures markets, where initial skepticism gave way to enormous volume once the use cases became clear. Energy futures were once considered exotic.
5. The compute futures concept could help AI companies manage capital expenditure risk — if you can lock in compute prices for future training runs, you can budget more effectively.
6. There are significant technical challenges: defining standardized compute units, creating reliable settlement mechanisms, and preventing manipulation in a market where supply is dominated by a few large players (NVIDIA, cloud providers).
7. The conversation explores how this fits into the broader financialization of tech infrastructure, similar to how bandwidth, storage, and even attention (via advertising futures) have been financialized.

## Overview

Carmen Li joins Odd Lots to discuss her ambitious plan to create a futures market for compute resources. The conversation explores how GPU compute is becoming a commodity, the parallels to early financial futures markets, and the technical and market-structure challenges of creating a derivatives exchange for computational power. Li argues that as AI makes compute one of the most important inputs to the economy, the ability to hedge compute prices will become essential for businesses — similar to how airlines hedge fuel costs.

## Implications

If successful, compute futures could transform how AI companies budget for training runs and manage infrastructure costs. This would also create new speculative opportunities and potentially improve price discovery in the GPU/cloud compute market. The concept aligns with the broader trend of financializing tech infrastructure.
