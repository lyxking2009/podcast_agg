# Talk Your Book: The Case for Investing in Emerging Market Bonds

**Show:** Animal Spirits Podcast
**Host:** The Compound
**Date:** 2026-06-15
**Duration:** 31:32

## Key Points

1. Emerging market fundamentals have improved significantly: better balance sheets, lower debt levels, and more independent central banks compared to a decade ago.
2. EM countries often offer significantly higher real (inflation-adjusted) interest rates than developed markets, creating an attractive yield pickup.
3. Geopolitical risk is often mispriced — markets overreact to political headlines in the short term, creating buying opportunities for long-term investors focused on economic fundamentals.
4. Currency dynamics are a major component of EM bond returns. Investors must choose between USD-denominated bonds (no direct currency risk) and local currency bonds (exposure to FX fluctuations).
5. The current environment with potential US dollar weakness may be favorable for local currency EM debt.
6. AI could both help (productivity gains) and challenge (disrupting traditional manufacturing) emerging market economies.
7. Active management is presented as advantageous in EM bonds due to the complexity, geopolitical risks, and credit research required.

## Overview

Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson are joined by Eric Fine from VanEck to discuss the investment case for emerging market bonds. The conversation covers how EM has evolved with improved fundamentals, the nuances of geopolitical risk analysis, the role of currency dynamics in returns, and the potential impact of AI on EM economies. Fine makes the case that EM bonds deserve portfolio allocation through actively managed vehicles like the VanEck EMBX ETF.

## Implications

For investors with an EM allocation, the discussion suggests reconsidering the balance between USD-denominated and local currency bonds given potential dollar weakness. The improved fundamentals in many EM countries may justify larger allocations than traditional models suggest.
