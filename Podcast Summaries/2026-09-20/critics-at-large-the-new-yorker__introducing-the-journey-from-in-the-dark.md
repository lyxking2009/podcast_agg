# Introducing: “The Journey,” from In the Dark — Critics at Large | The New Yorker

## TL;DR

In this episode of The Journey, reporter Ava Kofman investigates the case of Kayla Elliott, a Texas mother who became a gestational surrogate for California couple Guojun Xuan and Silvia Zhang through an agency the couple secretly owned. After giving birth, Kayla discovered that the couple had recruited dozens of surrogates simultaneously, leading to a sprawling child welfare and law enforcement investigation. Police found at least 21 children—many under age three—living in the couple's Arcadia mansion, with evidence of abuse, and the episode follows surrogates like Stacy King and Alexa Fasold as they grapple with whether to hand over babies they are carrying.

## Key points

- Kayla Elliott, a 26-year-old Texas mother of four with tied tubes, joined Mark Surrogacy after hearing about a friend's wife's success; she was matched with Guojun Xuan and Silvia Zhang, a couple from Arcadia, CA, who claimed to have one child and had tried 10 rounds of IVF.
- Kayla received $4,500 per month plus expenses, underwent medical screenings and hormone injections, and had an embryo transfer in July 2024; she gave birth to a baby girl on March 13, 2025, in Corpus Christi.
- The agency, Mark Surrogacy, was actually opened and operated by Silvia Zhang and Guojun Xuan themselves, enabling them to recruit surrogates directly while concealing their identities and the true scale of their family.
- Shortly after giving birth, Kayla learned via Facebook group chat that the couple had used multiple surrogates concurrently and had far more children than the one they claimed; other surrogates included Crystal Calderon, Amber Applegarth, Olympia Belmonte, and others across several states.
- Kayla posted a TikTok exposing the situation, which went viral, prompting an FBI agent to ask her to remove it; she later recorded a phone call in which Silvia admitted to being jailed four days and insisted she was not selling babies.
- In May 2025, Arcadia police arrested Silvia Zhang and Guojun Xuan after a two-month-old baby was admitted to an ICU with severe head injuries; doctors suspected abuse, leading police to search their 10,000-square-foot mansion.
- Inside the home, police found rooms filled with cribs, at least a dozen toddlers and infants with shaved heads, makeshift classrooms, and extensive CCTV; footage showed nanny Chunmei Li hitting the injured baby and other nannies spanking children.
- A DCFS caseworker told Kayla that the couple had 21 children, most under age three; the children were taken into temporary foster care, and a juvenile dependency court would decide whether the parents could regain custody.
- The episode highlights that surrogacy in the US is largely unregulated: there is no licensing department or federal oversight for surrogacy agencies, and contracts typically require confidentiality and strip surrogates of custody rights.
- Several surrogates were still pregnant at the time of the parents' arrest, including Stacy King (due October 2025 in Virginia) and Alexa Fasold (due around the same time in Pennsylvania); they faced legal and emotional dilemmas about handing over the newborns.
- Stacy King initially contacted authorities and lawyers, who advised her to follow the contract, but after seeing police affidavit details of alleged abuse, she and her family decided to prepare to keep the baby, despite legal risk.
- Silvia Zhang, in recorded calls with Kayla and Stacy, denied trafficking and abuse, claimed she wanted a family and was a good mother, and asked surrogates not to talk to reporters; her legal representative declined to comment for the series.
