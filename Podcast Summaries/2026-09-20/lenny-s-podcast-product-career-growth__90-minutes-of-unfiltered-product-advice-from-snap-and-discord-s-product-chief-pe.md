# 90 minutes of unfiltered product advice from Snap and Discord’s product chief | Peter Sellis — Lenny's Podcast: Product | Career | Growth

## TL;DR

Peter Sellis, former first PM at Snapchat and head of product at Discord, shares unfiltered product advice. He explains why teams should be designed like terrorist organizations with ideological culture and clear decision-making, why most product managers are net negative, and why Snap's ads business underperformed despite massive user engagement. He also covers growth from core users, the importance of taste through restraint, and contrarian takes on ambition and product management.

## Key points

- Peter Sellis was the first product manager at Snapchat, spent seven years building core consumer products, then became head of product at Discord, helping it grow significantly before leaving to explore new opportunities.
- He designs teams like terrorist organizations: an ideological culture where everyone understands why they do what they do, coupled with an extremely clear organizational structure defining who is trusted with which decisions, minimizing collaboration costs and enabling fast, autonomous decision-making.
- Collaboration has hidden coordination costs—executives rarely say 'stop collaborating,' but over-collaboration slows teams to the speed of the slowest node; Sellis aggressively internalizes these costs to avoid unnecessary meetings and alignment overhead.
- In high-growth startups, product managers should make decisions founders would have made six months to two years earlier; this requires a culture of trust and clear ownership so PMs can act without constant approval.
- He managed Nikita Beer at Discord after acquiring his startup; Sellis describes Beer as extremely spiky talent, like Liam Neeson in Taken—incredible for specific high-stakes problems but hard to direct for routine tasks. Building teams that can adopt such spiky talent is essential.
- Most product managers are bad and often net negative because the skill distribution is skewed: top PMs exit to become founders or executives, while low performers stay, dragging the median below the average. The lucrative, ill-defined nature of PM roles during the ZIRP era worsened this.
- Snap's ads business underperformed for three reasons: its user base skews extremely young (harder to target and measure for advertisers), the app opens to the camera rather than a feed (no natural ad placement), and core value is messaging (messaging apps have weak ad monetization).
- Despite these challenges, Snap scaled to multiple billions in ad revenue faster than Twitter, Reddit, or Pinterest after going public, and its consumer product portfolio—camera, messaging, maps, astrology among teens—is exceptional, though often underappreciated.
- Snapchat Plus subscription product may have been more impactful if launched four years earlier; subscription businesses grow linearly with cohort penetration, and time is an enemy, but it could have relieved pressure on the ads business.
- Evan Spiegel's taste is exemplified by his restraint: Sellis saw Spiegel say no to better ideas than most startups ever launch. The museum curator analogy—if a museum has all its collection on the walls, the curator hasn't done anything—illustrates that saying no is a key muscle of product taste.
- Systems thinking is best practiced by mathematically modeling systems—using stocks, flows, distributions, and levers in a spreadsheet—and by asking 'why' five times to reach first principles, rather than vague frameworks.
- Growth typically comes from the core of the business: at Snap, improving Android performance after the 2018 redesign drove a multi-year growth renaissance; at Discord, focusing on making the product perfect for intentional multiplayer gamers—despite apparent 100% penetration—unlocked the fastest growth since the pandemic.
- Core product value should be defined both conceptually and with metrics: at Snap it was 'fastest way to share a moment with your best friends' with measurable components; at Discord it was 'best way to talk and hang out with your friends before, during, and after playing games' with specific retention-linked metrics.
- SpaceX is the greatest example of burning the ships: they are sunsetting the Falcon 9—the world's best launch system—to build Starship, avoiding the innovator's dilemma by competing against themselves, driven by a mission to make humanity multi-planetary.
- Ambition and the ability to make tomorrow not look like today is rare; PMs must elevate team ambition, especially as AI lowers the cost of building, by credibly pushing for bigger visions rather than empty 'what if we did more' statements.
- The most important career decision is choosing your partner—spouse or life partner—because that relationship has outsized influence on growth, support, and intellectual sounding board, far exceeding any podcast or career advice.
- People with great taste often stop one step before everyone else: they know when a trend or product is about to become cringe and move on, similar to knowing which built features to leave on the shelf rather than ship.
- A spicy contrarian view: certain inalienable traits like gender, race, ethnicity, and socioeconomic background cannot be learned and are essential for building great consumer products for specific cohorts, though this is not actionable for hiring and remains uncomfortable to discuss.
- For ads businesses, 'long-term greedy' means prioritizing advertiser ROI today—even at the cost of short-term revenue—because long-term revenue is causally linked to delivering ROI now, enabling compounding growth through retention and budget increases.
- A great PM embodies three oxymorons: extreme confidence bordering on arrogance balanced with genuine humility in giving credit; highly organized and process-driven yet comfortable with ambiguity to the point of despising authority; and long-term patient thinking combined with an almost annoying bias for day-to-day action.
- Peter Sellis's favorite AI product is Hex, a multiplayer data workspace that changed his workflow by allowing real-time collaborative data exploration during meetings; his recommended game is Clair Obscure: Expedition 33, praising its artistic and musical quality.
- At Ustream, his first product role involved the Shiba Inu puppy cam, a 24/7 live stream of puppies that taught him about consumer attachment and the soothing power of ambient content.
