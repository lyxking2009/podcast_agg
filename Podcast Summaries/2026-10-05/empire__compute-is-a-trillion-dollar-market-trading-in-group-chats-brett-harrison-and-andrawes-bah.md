# Compute Is A Trillion-Dollar Market Trading In Group Chats | Brett Harrison & Andrawes Bahou — Empire

## TL;DR

Brett Harrison (Architect) and Andrawes Bahou (Compute Desk) explain why GPU compute, the largest unit of expenditure in the AI economy, still clears through text messages and opaque brokers. They argue compute is a heterogeneous commodity that urgently needs standardised benchmarks, futures and hedging instruments, and outline the phantom-cluster financing structures that make pricing even harder.

## Key points

- Compute rental and purchase is the main unit of expenditure in the AI economy, with single contracts running from $100 million to $10 billion, and there is no standard instrument to hedge the price.
- The market has fragmented from three hyperscalers into roughly 500 GPU-as-a-service providers, a classic sign of commoditisation: capital for hardware plus a site with power is now nearly all that is required to enter.
- Because both supply and demand cardinality exploded, a broker layer emerged, and most real buying and selling still happens over text messages, phone calls and WhatsApp — leaving a wide, opaque price gap between hyperscaler and neocloud pricing.
- Compute is not a fungible commodity like Bitcoin: value depends on memory per chip, networking, cooling design, GPU management software and utilisation, which is precisely what makes it hard to value and list.
- The 'phantom compute' problem: developers raise debt against long-term lease or buyout agreements signed for clusters that do not yet exist, with order lead times of four to sixteen weeks, so buyers must verify that quoted capacity is real.
- A fast-growing inference provider such as Instinct's Noah spends roughly 40% of his time sourcing compute, calling peers for vendor lists and weeding real capacity from fake under competitive RFQs.
- Buyers face a two-dimensional trade-off between commitment length and volume; paying up to roughly 2x above clearing price to get capacity immediately is often rational because lost revenue outweighs degraded unit economics.
- A working compute futures market would untangle that inflexibility by letting buyers hedge price and duration separately, which is the core thesis behind Architect's exchange and its ambition to compete with venues like the CME.
- The panel flags the risks in GPU-backed lending and the fact that hyperscaler free cash flow is turning negative as capex commitments outgrow operating cash generation.
- The conversation draws an explicit parallel to Bitcoin's 2017 OTC era — dislocated, relationship-driven pricing that only got fixed once the market became large enough to demand real infrastructure.
