# The Bond Pivot: Jim Bianco on Why it’s Time to Start Buying Bonds — The Master Investor Podcast with Wilfred Frost

## TL;DR
Jim Bianco explains why he has shifted from bearish to cautiously constructive on US bonds, arguing that mid-5% yields now fairly compensate for sticky inflation and strong nominal growth. He discusses Fed credibility issues, the 12-0 September vote, Scott Bessent's Treasury interventions, global bond market spillovers, the split between AI and non-AI equities, retail trading dynamics, and constraints on debt monetization. His overriding message is that a bear market is time, not price, and investors should consider how long they can wait for recoveries.

## Key points
- Jim Bianco argued that after six years of being too expensive, US bonds are now fairly valued, with average yields around 5.5% and modified duration also around 5.5. A 1% yield increase causes about a 5.5% price loss, but the 5.5% coupon cushions it, unlike 2020 when duration was 7 and yield was 2%. He has started to shift into long-duration bonds, particularly long-term TIPS as a hedged position, and would add more if yields reach high-5% or 6%, assuming no dramatic economic change.
- Yields have risen because nominal GDP (inflation plus growth) has moved up in developed markets. Inflation is stuck around 3% because goods deflation from China reversed: from -0.5% per year pre-COVID to +2% post-COVID due to deglobalization, tariffs, supply chain problems, and wars. Services inflation remains sticky. Meanwhile, the US economy is strong, evidenced by crude oil demand near a record 20 million barrels per day despite high prices.
- Bianco said bond traders can stop panicking when central bankers start panicking, but the Fed has not been vigilant enough. During the rate-cutting cycle from September 2024, the fed funds rate fell 1.75% while the 10-year Treasury yield rose 1.3%, the first time in over 50 years that long-term rates rose during a cutting cycle. The market is signaling that the Fed needs to worry more about inflation, deficits, and strong growth; the October meeting before midterms may see no hike, which could further damage credibility.
- Bianco argued that Fed dissent would enhance independence by showing 12 independent voters, but the September unanimous 12-0 rate hike was a mistake because it concentrated pressure on Chairman Warsh and suggested the chairman can bend the committee. Before the meeting, public comments suggested 6 for hike, 5 for hold, 1 unknown; a 7-5 vote would have been optimal to insulate Warsh from political pressure. He expects the October meeting is still in play but the political calendar may prevent a hike.
- Treasury Secretary Bessent intervened in the yen to prevent Japan selling Treasuries, selling euros instead to buy yen. He announced a $6 billion buyback program, larger than expected, funded by issuing T-bills rather than longer-term debt, which some call a form of QE. Bianco believes Bessent is being dictated to by President Trump, who claims expertise on interest rates; Bessent's 'I am the house' statement challenges the market and may not work long-term.
- France and Japan are driving global yields for their own reasons. In France, presidential candidate Mélenchon has floated debt default, and energy problems from Russian refinery attacks push inflation and nominal GDP up. Japan finally has 2% inflation and 3%+ yields, highest in 30 years, which may encourage repatriation of its over $1 trillion Treasury holdings, adding upward pressure on US yields. World bond markets are correlated, so US yields are affected.
- Bianco described US equities as two stock markets: about 50 AI-related stocks (Mag 7, semis, equipment makers, SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI) make up around 45-48% of the S&P, while the other 450 are 52-55%. Their correlations have diverged. AI stocks are less sensitive to interest rates and economic growth because of high growth potential; non-AI stocks are supported by strong current US economy with Q3 GDP estimates above 3.5%.
- Retail traders now account for over 20% of daily trading volume (peak 24% in 2021). Drivers include decimalization, fractional shares, social media communities, and gambling-like products such as zero-DTE options and proposed hourly expirations. COVID shut sports gambling and pushed sports bettors like Dave Portnoy into daily stock commentary. This shift reduces sensitivity of stock prices to discount rates and changes market dynamics, making it more short-term oriented.
- Previous debt monetization occurred via QE in US/Europe and directly in Japan, but Bianco argues it cannot happen now because monetization is stimulative and would push inflation higher in the current 3% inflation environment. He notes US total debt (public + private) is 364% of GDP, same as 2006, but the mix has shifted toward government. It's a concern but not an emergency; likened to slightly high blood pressure, not a heart attack.
- Bianco is constructive on gold but disappointed by its 'cryptozation'—it now trades like a degenerate gambling asset, causing 80-120% run-ups and 50% crashes. On crypto, he is a bull if it builds decentralized, permissionless finance for 2 billion unbanked in developing regions; he viewed the Bitcoin ETF and 'number go up' as anathema and welcomed the Clarity Act failure. He cites M-Pesa in Kenya as a model for mobile money lifting people out of poverty.
- Bianco expects future annual returns of roughly 4% for cash, 5% for bonds, and 6% for stocks over several years (the '4-5-6 market'). He says 60/40 still works but not because stocks and bonds hedge each other; bonds now belong in portfolios for their yield. He suggests those with risk tolerance can allocate part of equity portfolio to alternatives like gold and crypto.
- His main advice: a bear market is time, not price. The stock market always comes back, but the question is how long—13 years as after 2000 or 5 months as after COVID 2020. Investors should consider their age and time horizon; younger investors can dollar-cost average through a long bear, older investors may not have that luxury. His biggest current worry is energy prices, due to refined product shortages from refinery fires and possible export bans.

## Notable quotes
> "bond traders can stop panicking when central bankers start panicking" — Jim Bianco
> "If the bond market is offering me mid-5% yields, I want to start getting in. If it wants to offer me high-5% yields, I want to get in more. If it gets to six, I want to get in even more." — Jim Bianco
> "A bear market is time not price. And what I mean by that is everybody says, "The stock market always comes back." It does. But, is it going to be 2000 where the market peaks and it comes back in 13 years to a new all-time high? Or is it going to be 2020, during COVID, where the market peaks and goes down, and in 5 months it makes a new all-time high?" — Jim Bianco
