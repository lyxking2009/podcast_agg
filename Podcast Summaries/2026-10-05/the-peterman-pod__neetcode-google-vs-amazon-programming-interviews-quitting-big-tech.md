# Neetcode: Google vs Amazon, Programming Interviews, Quitting Big Tech — The Peterman Pod

## TL;DR

Ryan Peterman interviews NeetCode (Navdeep Singh), the best-known coding-interview creator, about quitting Amazon two months into his first job, the difference between Amazon's and Google's engineering cultures, and what actually determines interview outcomes. His verdict: roughly 90% of LeetCode performance is memorisation, and interviews are far more of a numbers game than most candidates believe.

## Key points

- NeetCode quit Amazon after only a couple of months as a new grad, with no backup plan and no savings — a decision he calls 'really dumb' in hindsight even though it worked out.
- His reason was cultural rather than technical: Amazon felt cutthroat rather than merely intense, with visibly burned-out teammates and a team where most people had left within a year.
- The LeetCode questions for Amazon were surprisingly easy, but the leadership-principles interview was heavily weighted — and he admits he 'lied for every single question', answering as the high-agency personality type the company screens for rather than his own risk-averse instincts.
- He estimates at least 90% of LeetCode performance is people memorising solutions in one form or another, though he insists some underlying understanding is still required so you are not blindly pattern-matching.
- His practical benchmark: if you can solve roughly a third of LeetCode mediums, you have a pretty good chance of passing an interview.
- He argues interview outcomes are largely luck of the draw — successful candidates were usually asked a question they happened to be able to answer, not necessarily better engineers.
- A Caltech anecdote illustrates the range: at a Google-hosted college competition his stacked team solved 3 of 13 competitive-programming problems, while Caltech solved all 13.
- He says LeetCode is 'definitely not' an accurate measure of intelligence, comparing it to the SAT — correlated with things that matter but not a direct measure.
- His real thesis is that passion and work ethic, not raw intelligence, are the bottleneck: he attributes his own success simply to making a lot of LeetCode videos when most people would not have kept at it.
- He likens interview prep to fitness: training on a leg-press machine barely improves your squat, so practising competitive programming may not transfer to a live interview where communication matters.
- He notes he is still surprisingly decent at LeetCode after seven years, but says hand-coding has atrophied since most people, himself included, no longer type solutions out.
