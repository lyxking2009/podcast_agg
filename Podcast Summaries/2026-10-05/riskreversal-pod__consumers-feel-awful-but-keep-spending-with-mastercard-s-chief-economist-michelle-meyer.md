# Consumers Feel Awful But Keep Spending with Mastercard's Chief Economist Michelle Meyer — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Mastercard Chief Economist Michelle Meyer discusses consumer resilience despite high interest rates and weak sentiment. She forecasts 5.5% holiday retail sales growth year-over-year, strongest in four years, with half driven by inflation and half by real volume. Meyer highlights labor market health, AI's emerging influence on shopping behavior, and expects global economic expansion into 2027.

## Key points
- Holiday retail spending (excluding autos and gas) is forecast to grow 5.5% year-over-year, the strongest in four years, with about half of the gain attributed to inflation and half to real volumes, indicating healthy underlying demand.
- The primary driver of consumer spending is the labor market, which remains healthy with unemployment just above 4%, broadening job creation across sectors including construction and manufacturing driven by infrastructure and AI investment.
- Higher interest rates transmit unevenly: households with fixed-rate mortgages at 2.5-3% feel less impact, while new homebuyers face 30-year fixed rates above 7% and stretched affordability.
- Consumers are supported on aggregate by healthy balance sheets and positive wealth effects, but spending growth is faster for upper-income households; lower-income spending is still increasing, not contracting.
- The disconnect between weak consumer sentiment surveys and strong actual spending partly reflects measurement issues and the cumulative effect of higher price levels, though wage and asset price inflation have offset some of the burden.
- Wage growth is hovering close to headline inflation, and a persistent gap could impair real wages; diesel prices pose a threat to pass into underlying goods inflation if input costs rise.
- Mastercard data reveals AI subscription users visit a wider number of merchants, particularly in beauty services, specialty food stores, restaurants, and florists, suggesting AI tools enable more search and specialization.
- AI users show earlier engagement in the holiday shopping season compared to non-AI users, who concentrate spending at the very end of the season.
- Meyer emphasizes watching labor market breadth and turnover: job openings to unemployed ratio is just above one, indicating a reasonably balanced market that supports wage growth without overheating.
- The Mastercard Economics Institute's SpendingPulse metric provides high-frequency, granular insight into consumer spending by sector and transaction value versus number of transactions.
- Looking ahead to 2027, Meyer expects continued global economic expansion, with investment, particularly AI-related capex, a key driver of US GDP growth.
- Meyer notes consumers have more power due to information access, shopping across merchants, and lower barriers for small businesses, which changes retail dynamics.
