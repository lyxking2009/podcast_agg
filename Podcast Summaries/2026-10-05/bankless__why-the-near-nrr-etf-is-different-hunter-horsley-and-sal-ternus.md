# Why the Near $NRR ETF is Different | Hunter Horsley and Sal Ternus — Bankless

## TL;DR

Bitwise's Hunter Horsley and Sovereign's Sal Ternus join David Hoffman to mark the launch of NRR, the first Near ETF, which pulled in roughly $50 million of inflows in its first three days. The conversation covers Bitwise's deep operational involvement in Near (validator and MPC network operator), how Near Intents turns usage into protocol revenue and buybacks, and a governance proposal to cut NEAR inflation from 2.5% to 1.6%.

## Key points

- Bitwise launched NRR, the first Near ETF, which saw roughly $50 million of inflows in its first three days — Horsley notes nobody needs to buy a Bitwise product, so the inflows reflect genuine conviction in Near.
- Bitwise manages about $16 billion and has been involved with Near for a long time: institutional research coverage and valuation analysis over a year ago, an ETP in Europe, and a staking business that makes it around the fourth-largest validator on Near.
- Bitwise also operates the MPC network and chain signatures that underpin Near Intents, which Sal argues takes ecosystem alignment beyond passive fund ownership.
- Sovereign's whole thesis is to own NEAR but also actively grow the network, effectively treating the token as an equity stake in the system — Ternus was early and publicly argued Near was 2-4x underpriced at ~$1.30 in February; NEAR later traded near $5 after touching $5.50.
- Near Intents works by letting a user express a desired outcome and having a market of solvers compete to achieve it; today it covers digital-asset swaps and, after the Ondo integration, 400 tokenised equity products.
- Intents has processed more than $32 billion in volume, and take rates on those transactions generate gross fees and retained net revenue that funds NEAR buybacks.
- A key nuance: the buybacks do not burn tokens — NEAR is held in a permanent capital vehicle, so Ternus says calling it 'deflationary' is imprecise, though the mechanism still removes tokens from circulating supply.
- A governance proposal would reduce NEAR inflation from 2.5% to 1.6% over a two-year linear curve, with a second phase opening research into whether a fixed supply is feasible.
- Horsley argues mainstream investors no longer sort crypto assets by CoinMarketCap ranking, and that Near's strongest pitch is its connection to AI — the co-inventor of LLMs building a chain designed for both humans and agents.
- He characterises 2026 as the year investors got comfortable with Bitcoin and started looking beyond it for additional exposure, with Near a first-class candidate.
