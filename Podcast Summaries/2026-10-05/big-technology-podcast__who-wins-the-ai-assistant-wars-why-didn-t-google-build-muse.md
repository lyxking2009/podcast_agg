# Who Wins The AI Assistant Wars? + Why Didn’t Google Build Muse? — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
MG Siegler joins to discuss the escalating personal AI assistant war, covering Instinct, Meta Muse, OpenAI Dots, Microsoft Copilot, Grokbot, and Apple's Siri. He argues that despite early skepticism, AI assistants are likely here to stay, with distribution, integrations, and trust as key differentiators. The conversation also examines why Google, despite owning critical services like Gmail and Calendar, hasn't fielded a competitive assistant, attributing it to organizational conflicts and execution failures.

## Key points
- The personal AI assistant landscape rapidly evolved from Instinct's early lead to major launches by Meta (Muse), OpenAI (Dots), Microsoft (Copilot), and Grokbot, suggesting a new product category with staying power.
- Instinct differentiated by using existing messaging apps, no new app required, and proactively prompting users with tasks like bill payment or subscription management; its early hook was saving money by analyzing credit cards and bills, and it reportedly reached a $10B valuation within months.
- A key challenge for AI assistants is the 'blank page problem' (users don't know what to ask) combined with the 'boil the ocean problem' (services try to do too much); successful products use proactive nudges and start with simple, delightful tasks.
- AI agents could disrupt subscription businesses by automatically canceling unused services, and also impact banking by optimizing cash and credit card usage, threatening industries that rely on consumer inertia.
- Amazon blocked Meta's Muse from shopping on its platform, likely to protect its own advertising business and Alexa Plus, while Shopify embraced Muse, highlighting growing silos and platform rivalries.
- Meta Muse launched as a standalone app with WhatsApp integration and a cloud-based virtual private machine for security, allowing it to operate without needing a frontier model initially; its upcoming Watermelon model may add capabilities.
- Despite Meta's distribution advantages (billions of users across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp), many users express hesitation to grant Meta access to Gmail and Calendar due to trust issues, whereas OpenAI ChatGPT users are more comfortable connecting.
- OpenAI Dots requires the $100/month Pro subscription and is positioned more for enterprise/work use despite its cutesy mascots, reflecting OpenAI's broader shift from consumer to enterprise focus.
- Microsoft's unified Copilot SuperApp targets enterprise users, aiming to protect its business from OpenAI's encroachment, while Apple's Siri AI, baked into billions of devices, suffers from a legacy trust deficit but could win by default due to device access.
- Anthropic has not launched a consumer personal assistant; it merged Claude and Cowork into a single interface and is likely to evolve Cowork into a professional assistant, possibly delaying consumer features.
- Google possesses critical integrations (Gmail, Calendar, Search) but its assistant Gemini Spark is underwhelming; the company's many teams and conflicting incentives prevent a cohesive product, exemplified by pre-announced models like 3.5 Pro that never shipped.
- MG Siegler believes Meta has the best chance due to distribution and WhatsApp integration, even without a frontier model, while OpenAI's 1.2B weekly users and product execution make it a strong competitor; Apple is a dark horse due to device access.
- Upcoming movies about Meta and OpenAI could influence public perception and trust, potentially affecting adoption of their AI assistants.
