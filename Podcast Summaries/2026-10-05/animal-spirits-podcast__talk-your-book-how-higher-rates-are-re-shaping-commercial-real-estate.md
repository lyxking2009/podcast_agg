# Talk Your Book: How Higher Rates Are Re-Shaping Commercial Real Estate — Animal Spirits Podcast

## TL;DR
Jerry Bagley, head of real estate at Benefit Street Partners, discusses how rapidly rising interest rates are reshaping commercial real estate. He argues that while higher rates pressure valuations and refinancings, they create opportunities in real estate credit, particularly in multifamily, industrial, and hospitality, while office remains largely a no-go. Bagley emphasizes the importance of having a cushion in credit investments and expects more capitulation and distress to create attractive entry points.

## Key points
- The 10-year Treasury yield is screaming higher (as of September 28 recording), which is slowing transaction volume, making refinancings trickier, and putting pressure on valuations across commercial real estate.
- Jerry Bagley believes fear is not the right response to higher rates; instead, investors should be cautious but opportunistic, as the rate spike may force capitulation, which can be healthy by clearing out problems.
- Credit is now more attractive than equity in real estate because a 30-40% equity cushion before losses provides safety; Bagley favors a 3-5 year credit horizon over a 7-10 year equity hold.
- Refinancing needs are large: many loans taken in 2021 with 5-year terms, floating structures, and bank loans from 2019-2021 at richer valuations are maturing; lenders have been extending to avoid foreclosures, but eventually resolution is needed.
- Office is almost categorically avoided: it has clear winners but many unpredictable assets; Pimco recently had a negative headline; office is diverse (suburban, small town) and pain is concentrated in non-Class A, older buildings.
- Multifamily is favored: valuations are tough but cash flows haven't disappeared; occupancy issues and concessions exist but not the same as losing half the building in office; long-term fundamentals remain strong, though oversupply needs to be worked through.
- Within multifamily, outcomes have bifurcated: newer Class A assets preserved value better, while older assets in sunbelt markets like Dallas or Austin saw more severe depreciation even though capital continues to chase the sector.
- Sentiment in the industry is that hope is being stomped on: sayings like 'survive to 25, exist to 26' reflect disappointment; there is no rule that low rates will return, especially given strong economic growth, AI investment, and productivity gains.
- Comparing treasuries to real estate is apples-to-oranges because treasuries don't appreciate or generate rent growth; real estate can hedge inflation, but today Bagley prefers credit because you can earn high single-digit to low double-digit returns with a shorter duration.
- Competition as a lender will decrease due to the rate spike causing fear and caution among other lenders; scale matters and larger asset managers are consolidating, giving consistent lenders an opportunity to differentiate and take market share.
- Construction lending is depressed because borrowing costs have roughly doubled from 5-6% to 8-10% or more; record originations from the early 2020s must be reckoned with, leading to recapitalizations or foreclosures in the next year or so.
- Industrial and warehouse real estate is attractive partly because banks pulled back from lending there, allowing alternative credit providers like Benefit Street Partners to step in and gain market share.
- Hospitality looks good due to a resilient consumer with strong spending; no near-term storm clouds are visible, and inflation should support ADR (average daily rate) growth.
- Bagley is less optimistic on real estate equity because of negative leverage and uncertain cap rate adjustments; he prefers credit due to the cushion it provides and more conviction given the uncertainty ahead.
