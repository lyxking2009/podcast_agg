# CME's Terry Duffy on the Disconcerting State of Finance Regulation — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
CME Group CEO Terry Duffy, in a live Odd Lots exit-interview-style conversation, warns that US financial regulation is being eroded by the CFTC's blanket approval of 2,500 self-certified contracts—including prediction markets he argues violate core principles on manipulation susceptibility. He also criticizes perpetual futures as misclassified swaps that circumvent Dodd-Frank, raises concerns about offshore platforms like Hyperliquid lacking KYC, and says 24/7 trading is inevitable but hindered by banking infrastructure. Duffy shares career lessons on risk and exit strategies.

## Key points
- Terry Duffy, CME Group chairman and CEO since 2002 and a 46-year veteran of the exchange, is transitioning to executive chairman to ensure a smooth handover to incoming CEO Lynn Fitzpatrick.
- Since the second Trump administration began, 2,500 self-certified contracts have been submitted to the CFTC with zero denials, which Duffy says undermines the Commodity Exchange Act and core principle three (susceptibility to manipulation).
- Duffy criticizes CFTC-approved prediction contracts like Nathan's hot dog eating totals and George Santos' State of the Union appearance, arguing they have no economic hedging purpose and are readily manipulable.
- He rejects the prediction-market argument that insider information is beneficial, stating it violates a level playing field and compares it unfavorably to President Trump's $100,000 faster information feed on Truth Social.
- Duffy distinguishes CME's partnership with Future Sports, an index-based sports product that helps team owners monetize value without selling, from gambling-style prediction markets on individual player stats.
- He explains that CME did not list a 24/7 perpetual oil contract because perpetuals are swaps, not futures, due to the funding rate mechanism where parties pay each other, triggering Dodd-Frank swap regulations and ordinary income tax treatment.
- Duffy argues that perpetual futures lack expiration dates, making them unsuitable for institutions managing $40 trillion in debt that require date-certain risk management, and their funding rates create uncertainty about who decides the rate and timing.
- On Hyperliquid, Duffy notes founder Jeff Yan claims the platform is just plumbing and users should be regulated, but Duffy argues it violates core principle nine (fair and ready market access) because it has no KYC and auto-liquidation can cascade due to leverage up to 250%.
- Duffy says Hyperliquid's business is 98% driven by TradeXYZ, and without KYC, unknown participants can distort market outcomes, which is incompatible with US law if the platform wants to operate in the US.
- He believes 24/7 trading is inevitable, but the main obstacle is the banking system: a $76 trillion US equity market cannot be pre-funded over a weekend while banks are closed, so rates and equities cannot go 24/7 until banking does.
- Duffy criticizes the CFTC for allowing Kalshi's prediction market on compute while blocking GPU futures, arguing the GPU market will be multiples of crypto by 2030 and needs risk-offset instruments for AI leadership.
- Career advice: after losing $150,000 of borrowed money in 1981, Duffy learned that anyone can enter a trade but exiting is harder; he advises young people to know their exit strategy before entering and to accept being wrong.
