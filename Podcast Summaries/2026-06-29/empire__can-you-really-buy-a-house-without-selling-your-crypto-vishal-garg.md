## Overview
In this episode of Empire, host Jason Yanowitz sits down with Vishal Garg, founder and CEO of Better.com, to explore one of the most consequential intersections of crypto and traditional finance in 2026: the ability to buy a home using cryptocurrency as collateral without selling it. Better and Coinbase recently closed the first Fannie Mae-approved crypto-backed conventional mortgage — a 30-year fixed-rate loan where the borrower pledges bitcoin or USDC rather than liquidating holdings. The deal represents years of behind-the-scenes regulatory work, culminating in government-backed legitimacy for digital assets as mortgage collateral.

Garg explains the core inefficiency in today's mortgage market: the system was designed around bank deposits, which represent only $5 trillion in U.S. household wealth, while $35 trillion sits in stocks, bonds, and digital assets that have historically been inaccessible for home financing. Better's strategy is to build the infrastructure to unlock that capital — both on the borrower side (accepting crypto collateral) and on the lender/funding side (tapping Sky stablecoin liquidity through a $500 million partnership with Framework Ventures). The episode also covers Better's partnership with OpenAI to apply AI to the notoriously fragmented and paper-intensive mortgage origination process.

On the funding side, Garg discusses how the Framework Ventures deal — which includes a $45 million equity stake and access to $500 million in Sky ecosystem credit — positions Better as the first conforming mortgage originator to deploy tokenized capital at institutional scale. By removing traditional warehouse lenders as intermediaries, Better expects to cut annual funding costs by over 100 basis points, potentially offering rates below 5% while the broader industry charges over 6%. The full product rollout is planned for later in 2026, with an active waitlist already indicating strong demand.

## Implications
The Fannie Mae stamp of approval on Better and Coinbase's crypto mortgage is a watershed moment: it formally integrates digital assets into the U.S. conforming mortgage market, setting a precedent that could reshape how the $12 trillion mortgage asset class is funded and collateralized. If tokenized capital can consistently undercut traditional warehouse lending costs by 100+ basis points, it challenges the entire business model of conventional mortgage funding and could compress rates industry-wide. The expansion roadmap — from bitcoin and USDC to tokenized equities — suggests this is not a niche product but the early infrastructure for a much broader transformation of how Americans leverage their wealth to buy homes.
