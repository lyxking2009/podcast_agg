## Key Points
- Ethlabs is a new independent nonprofit R&D lab co-founded by five former Ethereum Foundation researchers with an explicit mission: make Ethereum and ETH win
- The five co-founders are Ansgar Dietrichs (Executive Director), Caspar Schwarz-Schilling, Barnabé Monnot, Josh Rudolph, and Julian Ma
- Ethlabs is designed to complement — not compete with — the Ethereum Foundation, which is refocusing on "CROPS" (censorship resistance, openness, privacy, security)
- Key work streams include L1 scaling (targeting 3x annual improvement), L2 interoperability, block construction, time to finality, and direct DeFi builder engagement
- Funded by donors including Bitmain, SharpLink, Joe Lubin, and 50+ individuals; 2–3 year runway with no strings attached
- Unlike the EF, Ethlabs explicitly advocates for ETH value accrual, treating ETH and Ethereum success as inseparable
- The organization operates with a startup mentality — lean, talent-dense, and market-responsive — with an annual community review for accountability

## Overview
Host David Hoffman sits down with Ansgar Dietrichs and Caspar Schwarz-Schilling, two co-founders of Ethlabs, to discuss the newly launched independent R&D nonprofit. Ethlabs emerged from a recognition that the Ethereum Foundation was strategically narrowing its focus to preserving core protocol properties (censorship resistance, openness, privacy, security), leaving a gap in applied research aimed at driving Ethereum adoption and ETH value. The five founders — all veterans of the EF's research team — created Ethlabs to fill that gap with a startup ethos: fast feedback loops, transparent work, and explicit alignment with Ethereum's commercial success. The episode covers Ethlabs' organizational structure, funding model, technical priorities, and vision of Ethereum as the single hub for global internet finance.

## Implications
Ethlabs represents a meaningful decentralization of Ethereum's research layer. By separating advocacy-oriented R&D (Ethlabs) from neutrality-focused protocol stewardship (EF), the ecosystem gains an organization that can openly champion ETH as a monetary asset and push aggressively on adoption without the EF's institutional constraints. If Ethlabs succeeds in its scaling and interoperability roadmap, it could meaningfully strengthen Ethereum's competitive position against both alternative L1s and the fragmented multi-chain narrative — and the explicit goal of making ETH a "trillion-dollar asset" signals a deliberate attempt to align researcher incentives with the broader token holder community.
