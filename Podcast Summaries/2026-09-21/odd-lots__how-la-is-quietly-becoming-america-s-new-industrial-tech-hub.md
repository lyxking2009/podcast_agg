# How LA Is Quietly Becoming America's New Industrial Tech Hub — Odd Lots

## TL;DR

This live Odd Lots episode features Chris Power, founder of Hadrian, and Tom Mueller, founder of Impulse Space and first employee of SpaceX, discussing Southern California's re-emergence as an industrial-tech hub. Power argues America follows a historical pattern of financializing and offshoring manufacturing, while Mueller counters with SpaceX's launch and satellite production numbers. They detail their companies: Hadrian builds software-powered autonomous factories producing space components, missiles, and submarine parts, while Impulse Space builds in-space transportation vehicles. The conversation covers robotics, AI, tariffs, and the need to outproduce China in advanced manufacturing as a matter of national security.

## Key points

- Chris Power frames US manufacturing decline as a recurring historical cycle: great powers with the strongest industrial capacity and reserve currency eventually financialize, offshore production, and lose their edge; the Netherlands ceded to Britain, Britain to America, and now America faces China, with reserve currency changes roughly every 150 years.
- Tom Mueller pushes back on the declinist narrative, noting that SpaceX launched 165 rockets last year—more than any country—and put 10,000 satellites in orbit, three times the rest of the world combined, demonstrating that complex manufacturing can still happen in the US.
- Hadrian operates as a design-agnostic autonomous contract manufacturer, described by Chris Power as 'Foxconn to every defense brand'; it produces spaceflight components, entire missiles, and 30% of a Virginia-class submarine, using software-driven digital twins and lights-out operations.
- Impulse Space, founded by Tom Mueller in 2021 after leaving SpaceX, provides in-space transportation: a precision maneuvering satellite with three units currently on orbit (two recently came within 200 meters of each other) and Helios, a high-energy upper stage that can increase Falcon 9 payload to the Moon or Mars by a factor of five.
- According to Chris Power, the key to autonomous manufacturing is not humanoid robots but software: technicians currently perform high-judgment setup and trigonometry in their heads to avoid crashing machines; closing the loop with software lets them load a part and walk away, reducing risk and enabling 24/7 operations.
- Southern California's aerospace ecosystem is thriving due to a dense pool of ex-SpaceX and legacy aerospace talent; Impulse Space is located across the street from Northrop Grumman's former TRW facility, and Tom Mueller quips that software is done in Silicon Valley while hardware is done in LA.
- Both founders advocate a hybrid vertical integration model: keep 10-15% of challenging development work in-house to learn quickly, but outsource production at scale; trust is the main factor, with Chris Power stating that manufacturing is about buying and selling trust, and legacy suppliers often fail on schedule.
- Chris Power warns that the US is dangerously behind in mass manufacturing for defense: in war games the US missile inventory is depleted in seven days, while China has produced 2,600 ships to America's six, and the next conflict will be won by production volume and logistics.
- Tom Mueller sees robotics as essential for space exploration and development; building on the Moon or Mars requires armies of robots with dexterity, vision, and AI, because the environment is too dangerous and expensive for human labor.
- On trade policy, Chris Power argues that tariffs are an imperfect but necessary tool to counter China's explicit subsidization of energy, labor, capital, and exports; he supports banning Chinese components from drones and robots even if it raises consumer costs, because industrial capacity is a national security issue.
