# Providing Capital to Tech Companies: Masters In Business with Ann Berry — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Ann Berry, founder and managing partner of Threadneedle, discusses her journey from a poverty-stricken childhood in London to roles at Goldman Sachs, Cornell Capital, and Wheelhouse before launching her own firm. She emphasizes the importance of operational experience in investing, focusing on execution and cash flow, and shares insights on venture capital, AI's ROI challenges, and the evolving role of media in consumer businesses. Berry also offers career advice for newcomers, urging them to embrace AI and broaden their experiences beyond finance.

## Key points
- Ann Berry earned a 'double first' in economics at Trinity College, Cambridge, which she describes as equivalent to summa cum laude but within a small subset, marking her early academic rigor.
- Berry grew up in government-assisted housing and was the first in her family to attend college, which motivated her to pursue financial stability through a career in financial services.
- She started at Goldman Sachs in mergers and acquisitions in London, then moved to private equity doing European leveraged buyouts, before attending Harvard Business School.
- Returning to Goldman Sachs in New York during the 2008 financial crisis, Berry witnessed Lloyd Blankfein's frequent voicemails providing updates and saw the firm become a bank holding company.
- At Cornell Capital, Berry learned entrepreneurial hustle by sourcing deals through relationships, exemplified by flying to meet family owners of an industrial services business and offering a four-week exclusive close.
- As CEO of Pure Star, an outsourced laundry company for hotels, during the pandemic, Berry listened to frontline workers who flagged mass cancellations from Asia in February 2020, enabling early preparation of PPE and sanitation equipment.
- Berry called former CEO Mike Grebe to apologize for being an annoying mid-level private equity board member who gave spreadsheet-based advice without understanding operational realities, reinforcing her belief that operating experience makes a better investor.
- Her investment philosophy focuses on backing leaders with high execution conviction, solving a specific problem with commercial viability, and achieving free cash flow within about two years for early-stage companies.
- Berry co-invests through 776's Titans Program with full discretion, having invested in Teal Health, an at-home cervical cancer test, and Reality Defender, a deepfake detection company originally backed by Eric Schmidt.
- She invested in Yann LeCun's world models startup AMI, which aims to give AI an understanding of the physical world, important for robotics and other applications.
- On AI, Berry believes in its transformational promise but criticizes the lack of rigor in measuring ROI on massive capex spending by big tech, noting the difficulty in tying tangible market caps to currently intangible returns.
- She observes that early-stage AI investing shows more discipline compared to the 2021-2022 NFT and web3 bubble, which she finds comforting.
- Berry notes that big corporations like Nvidia and Salesforce are investing balance sheet capital into startups, creating markups that boost earnings, and acquisitions of promising AI startups happen quickly.
- From her time at Wheelhouse, Berry learned the importance of content strategy for consumer businesses, citing Walmart's data-driven ad business that grew to nearly $6.5 billion in revenue and its move to NASDAQ to signal tech focus.
- She warns about dynamic pricing and consumer backlash, suggesting that while agentic AI could benefit consumers by finding better deals, retailers using price discrimination risk alienating customers and prompting regulation.
- Berry credits her high school English literature teacher, Naomi Rich, for training her in inference and structured argumentation, which she equates to writing investment committee memos.
- Mentors including Sanjay Patel, Gail Mandel, and Larry Bossidy shaped her career by emphasizing curiosity, execution, and defining success before starting new ventures.
- Her advice to recent graduates is to get hands-on with AI tools and become the go-to person for adoption within their organizations.
- To her younger self, Berry would stress the value of broader world experience beyond finance, including travel, meeting diverse people, and working inside a company to understand operational accountability.
