# You Ain’t Seen Nothing Yet with Daniel Clifton — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Daniel Clifton, head of policy research at Baird Strategas, joins to break down the post-midterm market shock. Democrats delivered a historic sweep—flipping Texas and many Senate seats—driving a sharp selloff in AI/data center stocks due to local and state-level opposition to data center construction. Despite near-term volatility, Clifton argues historical patterns and potential macro benefits (lower rates, improving deficit) support a positive 12-month outlook, while healthcare and certain tech incumbents emerge as winners.

## Key points
- Democrats scored a historic midterm sweep on par with 1974, flipping the Texas governorship and legislature, gaining Senate seats in Kansas, Iowa, and potentially Alaska and Mississippi, and leaving Republicans with significant losses among non-college-educated white men (down 20 points). This reflects voter backlash against the incumbent party amid economic dissatisfaction and corruption concerns.
- The data center boom faces a major political backlash: local referendums and state-level opposition are blocking projects. In Tuesday's elections, 38 of 40 data-center-related referendums went against the industry, and voters recalled three city commissioners who approved a Meta data center. Virginia's Democratic governor enacted a $600 million tax increase, prompting Blackstone to cancel its largest project and Amazon to cancel four zoned data centers.
- Texas is now the key battleground: Governor-elect Gina Kinojosa is expected to keep the state's moratorium on new data centers in place until the legislature passes taxes and regulations. Texas, along with Ohio, Georgia, and Iowa, accounts for 40% of the U.S. data center pipeline, so decisions there will heavily influence national AI infrastructure buildout.
- The market reaction to the election has been sharp and bifurcated: after an initial relief rally on the peaceful concession, AI and semiconductor stocks sold off. Over the last two days, 20 of 25 semiconductor stocks in a key basket fell 10% or more; NVIDIA entered a bear market (down 21%), and Taiwan Semiconductor fell 9%. However, hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon, and Google were barely down, signaling that existing compute capacity may become more valuable.
- Daniel Clifton argues the sell-off is partly an overreaction, as data center construction won't halt entirely; construction unions support it, and some states like Louisiana and Wisconsin may welcome projects. He compares the panic to the post-2010 municipal bond sell-off that became a buying opportunity.
- Historical precedent is bullish: the S&P 500 has not declined in the 12 months following a midterm election since 1938, with an average return of +14.8%. Clifton notes that sharper declines during the midterm year tend to be followed by larger rallies.
- Data center slowdown could have positive macro effects: lower demand for debt to finance construction may push interest rates down, and reduced fiscal stimulus next year could improve the deficit. Clifton estimates the deficit could improve by $600-700 billion as temporary stimulus rolls off.
- Oil prices are driven by the Strait of Hormuz; if tensions normalize, oil could fall. However, Clifton cautions that Iran may prefer to wait out the Trump administration, and the IRGC sees the U.S. political change as an opportunity to outlast pressure. He remains in the 'higher for longer' oil camp unless there is a concrete plan.
- Healthcare is a major winner from the election: healthcare stocks have outperformed in 12 of the last 14 midterm election years by an average of 10%, and they surged after Tuesday. Democrats are expected to push for drug pricing reforms (e.g., Medicaid drug price 'cram downs'), which could benefit managed care companies like Centene and Molina but pose headline risk for large pharma.
- Texas flipping to Democrats could lead to Medicaid expansion under the Affordable Care Act, which would significantly increase the market share of Medicaid managed care companies, further boosting the sector.
- Kevin Warsh's expected Fed deregulation agenda could lower inflation without aggressive rate hikes by reducing the Fed's balance sheet and freeing bank capital, which would support real estate and other rate-sensitive sectors.
- Government corruption emerged as the top issue in CNBC's All America Economic Survey (36% cited it), and Democrats may prioritize a congressional stock trading ban, with Nancy Pelosi retired. Clifton believes passing such a bill would cement Democratic credibility, though it has repeatedly stalled.
- The AI trade is not dead but is being repriced: companies with existing compute (hyperscalers) could benefit from scarcity, while those relying on new data center construction face pressure. Venture capital, Israel, and Elon Musk are among the biggest losers from the political shift.
