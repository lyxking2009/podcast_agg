# Blunami vs. Space Karen — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
Democrats delivered a historic 'blue tsunami' in the midterms, their best showing since the post-Watergate era, driven by voter anger over affordability, corruption, and perceived indifference from President Trump. Joshua Green of Bloomberg Businessweek breaks down the populist backlash and the coming oversight for big business. Bloomberg's Dana Hull reports from Texas, where Democrat James Tallarico defeated Ken Paxton by 12 points despite Elon Musk spending over $200 million in the state — a race that turned Musk into a liability.

## Key points
- Democrats won their best midterm since the post-Watergate era, flipping House and Senate seats in deep red states like South Carolina and Kansas; Joshua Green calls it a 'blue tsunami' and says the scale exceeded even optimistic Democratic strategists' expectations.
- Voter anger centered on affordability (gas, food, job market) and corruption; a Gallup poll showed 89% of Americans, including majorities of Democrats, Republicans, and independents, see corruption in government as a major problem.
- Joshua Green argues the backlash was not just about specific issues but a sense that Trump governed in the opposite direction of his promises, visibly did not care about voters' pain, and refused to acknowledge their concerns — leading to a massive rebuke.
- Elon Musk spent between $450 and $500 million this cycle, more than any outside donor in history, including over $200 million in Texas alone, primarily through TV ads and a Senate PAC supporting Ken Paxton.
- James Tallarico defeated Ken Paxton by 12 points in the Texas Senate race; Tallarico's stump speech framed the race as David vs. Goliath, attacking Musk as an outsider trying to buy Texas and linking corruption to affordability.
- Dana Hull reports that Tallarico's closing argument in Austin included an inflatable Elon Musk puppet with a check for $215 million labeled 'Elon Space Karen Musk,' highlighting public resentment of Musk's spending and interference.
- Musk moved to Texas in 2020 after California COVID restrictions, expanded SpaceX and Tesla operations, and became central to GOP strategy, but now faces potential Democratic oversight investigations; Texas Democratic Party chair Kendall Scudder warned, 'If you think retribution isn't on the way, you're mistaken.'
- AI companies are rebranding data centers as 'compute campuses' (OpenAI) or 'AI factories' (NVIDIA's Jensen Huang) amid a populist backlash; anti-data center ballot initiatives won 43-to-1 in North Dakota, signaling broad opposition to large tech infrastructure.
- Joshua Green observed Democrats energized low-propensity voters, including in Michigan's Upper Peninsula with Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Abdul El-Sayed, drawing large crowds of formerly disillusioned Democrats and independents.
- Republican Party faces an internal bind: Trump remains dominant, but younger Republicans must decide when to break with him; those who publicly criticized Trump, like Thomas Massey, lost their primaries.
