# ROLLUP: Blue Wave, Black Swan | $6B Binance Heist | French Bank Robbed | ETH Goes Quantum-Proof — Bankless

## TL;DR
Bankless hosts David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams discuss a Democratic blue wave in the U.S. midterms triggering a sharp AI-led sell-off, with the S&P 500 down 4% and Nasdaq down 6% while Bitcoin holds near $76k. They cover the $6.34 billion Binance hack by North Korea's Lazarus group, the largest heist in history, how $2.8 billion was frozen and Binance covered the full loss, and a $130 million French bank hack where stolen fiat was laundered through crypto. They also explore implications of Democrats controlling Congress, ThorChain's role in laundering, Polygon's proposed rollback, and Ethereum DevCon post-quantum updates.

## Key points
- The U.S. midterm elections produced a Democratic 'blue wave,' flipping both the Senate (54 seats) and the House, while markets initially rallied then crashed; by Thursday morning the S&P 500 fell 4% (back to June 2026 levels) and Nasdaq dropped 6% (April 2026 levels) in one of the worst selloffs since 2018, driven by AI infrastructure stocks like NVIDIA (-8.6%), Intel (-7%, 61% off highs), and Micron (-14%, 45% off highs).
- Bitcoin briefly exceeded $80,000 after the midterms but fell to a new local low of $75,500, currently resting near its 50-week moving average at roughly $76,000—a level that historically marks early bull market support; Ethereum and Zcash held up better than Bitcoin on the week, and the market's resilience after the Binance hack is seen as bullish.
- The FBI attributed a $6.34 billion hack of Binance to North Korea's Lazarus group (specifically a sub-team), making it the largest money heist in human history—more than four times the previous record Bybit hack of $1.5 billion; $2.85 billion was frozen/intercepted, including $1.77B USDT, $8.825M USDC, $40M by Ripple, and assets on Arbitrum, while Binance pledged to cover the full $6B using its SAFU insurance fund and balance sheet, publishing proof of reserves.
- The Binance breach was a social engineering attack lasting months: attackers set up a fake job interview with a Binance employee, induced them to download malware disguised as a coding task on a work laptop, then pivoted through the internal network to access private keys; they exploited Ethereum EIP-7702 to approve unauthorized transactions and move funds outside normal withdrawal processes.
- A traditional French bank, Credit Mutuelle (approximately $200B balance sheet, 5.5 million customers), was digitally robbed of about $130 million via a third-party vendor API; the hacker used SWIFT messaging to send fiat to mule accounts at other banks, which then routed funds to crypto exchanges like Kraken, Crypto.com, and OKX to convert into stablecoins and disappear on-chain—ZachXBT first flagged the suspicious on-chain movements on November 2 before the bank noticed.
- ThorChain became a laundering vector for the Binance hack; the FBI explicitly warned ThorChain node operators not to process transactions tied to North Korean wallets, but the node operators refused to censor, maintaining neutrality—unlike Tornado Cash's immutable smart contracts, ThorChain has identifiable validators who can stop the chain, as evidenced by a bug-related halt on October 31.
- Polygon proposed a hard fork to recover frozen POL tokens stolen in the Binance hack; validators already froze 6.5 million POL, and hackers attempted to bribe validators with a 20K transaction fee to process the unfreezing, but no validator defected; this would be the largest chain rollback since The DAO, highlighting a split between immutable chains (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Zcash) and those willing to freeze/reverse transactions.
- With Democrats controlling Congress, hosts debate crypto regulation: they expect Democrats will focus anti-tech efforts on AI/data centers rather than crypto, but warn that Elizabeth Warren and Maxine Waters could still push hostile bills; however, Maxine Waters has already proposed a Crypto Investor Bill of Rights including 24-hour withdrawals, asset segregation, bankruptcy remoteness, and monthly proof of reserves, which hosts view as reasonable.
- At Ethereum DevCon in Mumbai, Justin Drake presented updates to make Ethereum post-quantum sooner, and Vitalik Buterin's keynote outlined current priorities; Layer 2 updates were also discussed, though the episode does not provide detailed technical specifics.
