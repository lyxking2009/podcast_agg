# Lost in Space Game Night — StarTalk with Neil deGrasse Tyson

## TL;DR
Neil deGrasse Tyson hosts a game-night special with co-author Lindsay Nix Walker and Paul to celebrate the book 'Lost in Space: 5,000 Facts to Navigate the Universe.' They compete in trivia, drawing, Yoda readings, taboo, charades, and Play-Doh sculpting, revealing facts such as Alan Shepard's 120-foot lunar golf shot, Uranus's 560 mph winds and three orbits since discovery, and the Voyager golden record's pulsar map that reveals Earth's location. The episode blends humor with scientific insights on topics like the Drake Equation, spiral galaxy density waves, and the demotion of Pluto.

## Key points
- Alan Shepard hit a golf ball only 120 feet on the Moon during Apollo 14, far less than the 350 feet guessed, because the bulky spacesuit restricted his swing and the low gravity made distance perception deceptive.
- Winds at Uranus's equator can reach 560 mph, over four times higher than 120 mph, driven by the planet's extreme axial tilt causing uneven solar heating and strong convection.
- Uranus has completed only three orbits around the Sun since its discovery in 1781, because its orbital period is about 84 years.
- Approximately 11 Earths would fit across Jupiter's diameter, underscoring Jupiter's size; its Great Red Spot alone is larger than Earth.
- Six rhesus monkeys named Albert were launched on American rockets in the 1940s-1950s; none survived, with the last one dying two hours after returning, making Albert I the first mammal in space.
- Spring tides, the highest tides, occur twice a month when the Earth, Moon, and Sun align at full and new moon.
- The asteroid belt contains millions of asteroids plus the dwarf planet Ceres; Ceres was promoted from asteroid to dwarf planet while Pluto was demoted, so they now share the same classification.
- The Voyager Golden Record includes 55 greetings in 55 human languages with humpback whale songs in the background, and its pulsar map acts as a cosmic GPS, revealing Earth's location to any aliens who find it.
- The Pioneer plaque and Voyager records depict the solar system with nine planets, including Pluto, which could mislead aliens if they expect eight planets, but Pluto was still considered a planet when the plaque was made.
- Project Ozma, the first official search for intelligent life, was named after Princess Ozma from L. Frank Baum's Oz books; Frank Drake created it, and his Drake Equation estimates the number of communicative civilizations by multiplying factors like star formation rate and fraction with planets.
- Mendeleev's periodic table predicted missing elements based on periodic properties; technetium was the first element to be artificially created, filling a predicted gap.
- Spiral arms in galaxies like the Milky Way are density waves: gas enters the wave, compresses, and forms new stars, creating a pattern analogous to a traffic jam seen from a helicopter, where individual stars move through but the jam persists.
- Jupiter's gravity may have thrown Ceres into the asteroid belt during the solar system's formation 4.5 billion years ago.
