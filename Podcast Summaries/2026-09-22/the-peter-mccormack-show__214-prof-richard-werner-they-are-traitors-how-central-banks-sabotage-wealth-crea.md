## TL;DR
Professor Richard Werner argues that central bankers are effectively traitors working for globalist interests, deliberately suppressing growth by maintaining centralized banking systems and obscuring the fact that banks create money when they lend. He claims every country can achieve 10% annual growth without inflation or crises by creating many small local banks that lend to small businesses, as demonstrated by Japan, China, and others. Werner explains how credit allocation drives economic outcomes, why the UK’s productivity crisis stems from five dominant banks that ignore small firms, and how central banks and the media collude to block reforms.

## Key points
- Richard Werner states that mainstream economics obscures the power of banks to create money; Paul Samuelson promoted the fractional reserve theory, which Werner calls a half-truth, to mislead the public and protect central banking interests.
- Werner's 2014 empirical test provided first evidence that individual banks create money when they lend, contradicting the still-dominant financial intermediation theory that banks merely lend out existing deposits.
- Double-entry accounting, invented by and for bankers about 800 years ago, hides money creation and fooled economists for centuries into believing banks are just intermediaries.
- Werner argues central bankers are 'traitors' in most countries, working for globalist interests; they deliberately engineer banking crises as 'windows of opportunity' to change ownership structures, as seen in Japan's 20-year recession and the Asian crisis.
- He advocates applying Paul Samuelson's 'revealed preference' principle: ignore central planners' words and watch their actions, which reveal their true intentions.
- Werner claims every country can achieve 10% annual economic growth without inflation or crises; Japan demonstrated 15% growth for decades, doubling national income every 4.5 years after 1960.
- The key mechanism for high growth is decentralizing credit creation: Japan advised Deng Xiaoping in 1978 to create 5,000 small local banks, each with 30 branches and 35 loan officers, resulting in millions of decentralized loan decisions and China's rapid growth.
- The UK's chronic productivity problem stems from banking concentration: five big banks control over 80% of deposits and do not lend to small firms, which employ about 65% of the workforce; the Cowin report identified this in 1918 but nothing changed.
- Small firms' productivity depends on access to local bank credit for technology adoption; in Germany's traditional system of ~1,200 local cooperative and savings banks, small firms could get funding within days, whereas UK small firms cannot.
- Werner's research on 5,000 US banks confirmed that big banks lend to big customers, while only small banks lend to small firms; this explains why banking consolidation suppresses small business and middle-class prosperity.
- Credit allocation determines economic outcomes: bank credit for productive business investment creates growth without inflation or crises; credit for consumption causes consumer price inflation; credit for asset purchases causes asset inflation and banking crises.
- Werner states the Bank of England could engineer 10-12% growth for two decades by establishing many small local banks, but this is blocked because it would decentralize power away from central planners.
- Interest rates are a decoy: they follow economic growth (positively correlated) rather than drive it, so central banks' focus on rate-setting obscures the real tool of credit quantity and allocation.
- Central banks are unnecessary; the US and Scotland historically had strong economies without one, and the Federal Reserve is relatively young; Werner says 'we don't need central banks.'
- Liz Truss was brought down by the Bank of England staging a bond market crisis after she challenged central bank accountability; the media colluded by portraying her negatively, despite her understanding of debt, productivity, and banking.
- The City of London Corporation is a sovereign state with unelected representation in parliament and non-democratic voting, protecting banking interests and hindering reforms like local banking.
- Werner describes the EU as a dictatorship modeled on the Soviet Union: the European Parliament has no power to propose laws; the unelected European Commission holds all lawmaking power; it is moving toward capital controls, including restrictions on non-EU banks offering deposit accounts from January next year.
- He says Germany is still not sovereign due to post-1945 occupation statutes, and the CIA shifts German politics; the AfD is a centrist party, not far-right, and media smears reflect establishment interests.
