# Black October! What Is Happening to the Internet? | Hari Mulackal & Odysseas Lamtzidis — Bankless

## TL;DR
Bankless host David Hoffman talks with security experts Hari Mulackal (Cantina) and Odysseas Lamtzidis (Phylax) about 'Black October,' the recent surge in high-profile cyberattacks. They explain that AI has made hacking scalable and cheap, with attack costs dropping dramatically and the 'zero day clock' now at negative days, meaning defenses are on borrowed time. They recommend companies proactively attack themselves with AI agents, and individuals should audit services, use redundant custodians, and avoid clicking suspicious links.

## Key points
- AI-driven cyber capabilities have surged since early 2024, with both offensive and defensive tools becoming dramatically cheaper and more effective; Hari notes internal efficiency gains made attacks 15x cheaper than in January, and costs are expected to drop another 10x every 6-12 months.
- Hacking has shifted from a bespoke human skill to an industrial, scalable process: attacking a single website now costs about $10 in AI agent compute versus weeks of human effort, enabling criminals to target low-value victims like healthcare providers and dentists.
- The 'zero day clock' has reached negative days, meaning companies are already on borrowed time; if they haven't acted to patch vulnerabilities, they are effectively already compromised.
- Public CVE disclosures are rising exponentially, but the actual attacks lag behind; once a vulnerability is disclosed, agents can immediately weaponize it, leaving no window for patching.
- For the next 12-18 months, offense will dominate defense; companies should adopt AI-powered offensive security agents (like Cantina's Apex) to simulate attacks on themselves and fix findings, raising the attacker's cost from dollars to thousands of dollars.
- Traditional financial institutions and non-crypto companies are more vulnerable than crypto-native firms because they have not operated under constant adversarial pressure; crypto companies like Coinbase are comparatively better prepared.
- Cyber insurance may fail as a safety net: Lloyd's warned in 2024 that LLMs increase cyber risk, yet premiums have been falling, suggesting many carriers could become insolvent.
- If nothing changes, the default trajectory is that most personal data will be exposed and exploited, enabling industrial-scale scams like personalized elder fraud using AI agents with deep knowledge of victims.
- Individuals should audit the services they use, ask companies what they are doing to protect data and money, and move assets if security programs haven't improved; responsibility ultimately lies with builders.
- Practical advice for individuals: avoid clicking suspicious links, consider using multiple custodians or exchanges for redundancy instead of self-custody for the next 12 months, and assume any service could be infiltrated.
- The internet is becoming a 'dark forest' similar to crypto's environment; companies that evolved in that environment have an embedded advantage in defending against AI-scaled attacks.
- Security through obscurity is breaking down in real time; the entire internet now faces the same adversarial conditions that crypto has experienced for years.
