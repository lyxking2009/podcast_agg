# The Chopping Block: Black October, Banks Blocking AI Agents, Hyperliquid's Blockchain-Not-Bitcoin Moment, and the Visa-Fireblocks Rumor — Unchained

## TL;DR
This episode covers a 48-hour 'Black October' wave of critical software vulnerabilities affecting PHP, Node.js, Linux, GitLab, and Railway, with governments taking sites offline. The hosts discuss banks blocking AI agents like Instinct and Muse, arguing this is deliberate friction to protect $1.6T in non-interest-bearing deposits. They also react to Joe Weisenthal's embrace of Hyperliquid as a 'blockchain not Bitcoin' moment and the rumored Visa-Fireblocks acquisition at $2.5B, a down round from its $8B 2021 valuation.

## Key points
- Over 48 hours, multiple critical vulnerabilities were disclosed, including PHP CGI remote code execution (affecting WordPress and legacy Facebook infrastructure), Node.js RCE flaws, Linux kernel exploits posted without responsible disclosure, GitLab RCE, a Quick protocol zero-day, and a Railway platform breach. Governments in Finland, Netherlands, France, and Japan took websites offline; Japan's prime minister publicly urged updates.
- Cloudflare's CEO reported millions of exploit attempts for the PHP vulnerability, and the NSA-level guidance to take down unpatched services was unprecedented. Despite the severity, initial blast radius was relatively contained, with one DeFi project losing keys via Railway, but second-order effects remain unknown.
- Tom argued closed source is more unsafe long-term because binaries can be reverse-engineered easily with AI, and lack of transparency prevents detection of exploits. Open source allows faster dissemination and patching; 'sunlight is the best disinfectant.' This contrasts with Haseeb's view that open source is dead due to AI.
- AI models are now central to both cyber offense and defense. Closed model providers like OpenAI and Anthropic block security tasks due to liability, forcing users to rely on open-source models for live incident response and patching, mirroring the pattern seen in AI trading agents.
- Banks including JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Schwab, Morgan Stanley, and Citi are restricting AI agents like Instinct and Muse. Jamie Dimon warned of 'rogue AI agents' moving money without authorization, and Axios reported banks detecting unvetted agents acting independently. Apollo's Torsten Slok had earlier raised the specter of an agentic bank run.
- Robert argued banks deliberately create friction; they hold $1.6 trillion in non-interest-bearing deposits and design platforms to thwart account closures and money movement. AI agents reduce this friction, benefiting consumers, but banks block them to protect their business model. Bernie Sanders' tweet sided with banks, contradicting progressive consumer-protection ideals.
- Amazon blocks AI agents, while Shopify allows them through its own agent. Companies block agents based on incentives: Amazon sells ads and wants more compute usage, banks want deposit stickiness. Open-source/local agents can circumvent blocks, but current UX limits adoption; over time, open-source will improve and commerce will increasingly resemble crypto.
- Joe Weisenthal's Odd Lots interview with Hyperliquid founder Jeff Yan and his newsletter signaled a shift: he now sees value in blockchain technology beyond Bitcoin, echoing the 2015 'blockchain not Bitcoin' era led by Blythe Masters. Hyperliquid's perp DEX enables markets for equities, commodities, and pre-IPO assets, though similar synthetic platforms existed earlier.
- CME's Terry Duffy appeared on Odd Lots separately and criticized perps and prediction markets, calling for regulatory guardrails. The contrast between Jeff Yan's interview and Duffy's comments highlights the regulatory and philosophical divide over decentralized derivatives.
- Axios reported Visa was acquiring Fireblocks for $2.5 billion, but Fireblocks quickly denied it. Fireblocks raised at an $8 billion valuation in 2021, so the price would be a significant down round but still clear investor preferences. The deal would give Visa wallet infrastructure for stablecoin payments and tokenization.
- The hosts drew parallels to AI bubble risk: Character AI raised at $2.5 billion from a16z while burning $16 in compute for every $1 of revenue. They noted a duration mismatch in AI compute debt, with 10-year borrowing chopped into 1-3 year contracts, creating potential for a financial crisis if AI demand falters.
