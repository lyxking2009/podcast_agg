# How GenLayer Is Building a Court System for Disputes Between AI Agents — Unchained

## TL;DR

GenLayer, founded by Albert Castana, is building a blockchain-based 'court for the internet' that uses AI validators to resolve disputes between AI agents over subjective contract terms, enabling new categories of low-value, high-frequency agentic commerce. The Layer 2 uses intelligent contracts that can interpret natural language and images, with appeals escalating from 5 to 1,500 AI validators, costing cents to $100 and resolving in minutes to hours. Arthur Hayes, involved as advisor via Maelstrom, sees GenLayer as essential adjudication for his Flop network for AI agent compute, with both protocols launching in the coming months.

## Key points

- Albert Castana's background: entered crypto in 2013, worked on NEM (reached $15B market cap), Radix DLT, Badger DAO, and co-founded Stakehound, the first liquid staking solution before Lido, which grew to $350M AUM in 5 months before losing $150M due to custody key loss, exposing the legal system's inefficiency and inaccessibility (5 billion people lack access, e.g., Israel requires 1% upfront fee to file a claim).
- GenLayer concept: a 'court for the internet' where AI agents resolve disputes over subjective terms, using 'intelligent contracts' that can include natural language, read internet content, and interpret images, enabling enforcement of ambiguous agreements. Claims are resolved in minutes at fractions of a dollar, enabling new types of contracts previously impossible due to need for human review or deterministic smart contracts.
- Architecture: GenLayer is a Layer 2 blockchain with an overlaid network of up to 1,500 validators, using delegated proof of stake with GEN token. Each validator runs an LLM of its choice, and the network reaches consensus on subjective questions through a probabilistic Byzantine fault tolerant system, rather than deterministic hash comparison, to achieve reproducible answers.
- Adjudication process: disputes are adjudicated via commit-and-reveal voting, starting with 5 validators and escalating in rounds to 1,500. Initial answer in 3-5 minutes, with a 30-minute finality window; full appeals take about 3 hours. Cost first round: $0.50-$1, full appeals up to $100, based on current AI compute costs. Validators stake GEN, earn fees if in majority, lose if wrong to deter coordination and laziness.
- Appeal economics: the number of rounds needed depends on question clarity: clear questions (e.g., 'is Zelensky wearing a suit if suit means tie') may not require full escalation, while ambiguous ones may. This allows cost-effectiveness based on stakes.
- Use cases: prediction markets like Polkamarket currently use UMA human voters (costly, slow, with gas fees and days for resolution); GenLayer could replace with AI consensus, resolving in minutes. Also trade finance deals that are uneconomical due to legal costs (e.g., $5,000 disputes) become viable, enabling long-tail agent transactions of hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars, 24/7 globally.
- Enforcement limitations: GenLayer can only control escrowed assets; it cannot impose damages beyond escrow or jail. Users can still pursue traditional legal action for additional remedies. This is similar to smart contracts but with subjective judgment, not a substitute for the legal system.
- Differentiation from AAA/Integra Ledger: the AAA/Integra Ledger open standard focuses on legal enforceability and discoverability for offchain contracts, takes months due to regulations and is US-specific. GenLayer aims for a global synthetic jurisdiction like Ethereum, operating as an internet court across borders without KYC or jurisdictional constraints.
- Comparison to Aragon: Aragon court failed due to lack of demand for DAO disputes. GenLayer benefits from the emerging agentic economy with high volume, low notional value, 24/7 transactions. GenLayer also holds state and contracts on-chain, unlike Aragon's offchain forum approach.
- Arthur Hayes and Flop integration: Arthur Hayes is CEO of Flop Labs and CIO of Maelstrom. Flop is a network for AI agents to transact compute using a unit called 'flop', with HTLC escrow. Agents can choose GenLayer as adjudication venue by writing contracts that reference it, needing GEN token for disputes. Flop testnet airdrop campaign Q4, mainnet Q1 next year; GenLayer token launch and mainnet in next few months.
- Internet Court consortium: GenLayer, UMA, and Kleros are part of an 'Internet Court' consortium working on an adjudication ERC standard. The vision is tiered dispute resolution: GenLayer's AI consensus for cheap fast resolution, human arbitration (Kleros/UMA) for higher stakes, then traditional legal system.
- Vision: the future agentic economy will require trust infrastructure: compute (Flop), memory/persona (also Flop), and legal system (GenLayer). Without such trust, AI agents cannot transact safely, as they can clone, cheat, and move fast without jail. Both guests see massive opportunity and are building complementary rails.
