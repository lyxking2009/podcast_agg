# Cloudflare CEO: The Man Deciding The Internet’s Next Business Model — Empire

## TL;DR

Matthew Prince, CEO of Cloudflare, discusses the rapid shift from an advertising-driven internet to a machine-dominated ecosystem where automated traffic has already surpassed human traffic and could reach 1,000x in five years. He outlines Cloudflare's efforts to build infrastructure for AI agents, including efficient isolates and potential micropayment rails capable of 10 million transactions per second, and explains the company's recent 20% layoff of 'measurers' replaced by AI. Prince argues that the next internet business model must compensate content creators and support new entrants, warning of consolidation and global inequality if not designed carefully.

## Key points

- Matthew Prince says Cloudflare's data shows automated (bot/agentic) traffic surpassed human traffic around May 2026, earlier than his earlier predictions of late 2027. He now projects automated traffic could be 1,000 times human traffic within five years, driven by agents, not because humans decline but because bots multiply.
- The historical internet business model has been advertising, with Google as the central architect of the ad ecosystem including search, Analytics, and DoubleClick. Prince credits Google for enabling the web's growth but notes bots do not click ads, so the ad model will break as agents dominate.
- Cloudflare operates a global network in over 350 cities, protecting and accelerating websites; more than 20% of the internet sits behind Cloudflare and almost half of the Fortune 500 uses its services. Prince says it has shifted from CDN/security to a developer platform, with vibe-coding platforms like Lovable, Replit, and OpenAI's Codex preferring Cloudflare for deployment.
- Cloudflare's technical bet is on isolates (the technology behind browser tabs) instead of containers. Prince claims using containers for one agent per knowledge worker would require 40 times the world's CPU production, so isolates enable much faster and cheaper agent deployment.
- In late 2025, Cloudflare embraced AI aggressively, and after a record Q1 2026, it laid off about 20% of staff (~1,100 people), primarily 'measurers'—middle managers, auditors, and compliance roles. Prince says AI's biases are uncorrelated with the organization, providing a fresh set of eyes, and managers can now handle 12 direct reports instead of 6, flattening the org.
- Prince is focused on building a micropayment system for agent-to-content transactions at enormous scale. He estimates 1-10% of Cloudflare's 500 million web transactions per second could be monetized, requiring 10 million financial transactions per second—over 100 times Visa's peak throughput of 80,000 TPS.
- Cloudflare has explored blockchain and crypto rails, including a stablecoin concept called Net Dollar, but found no existing solution that can handle the required volume. The best vendors reach about 2 million TPS, still an order of magnitude too slow; Prince is open to building internally or acquiring a partner.
- Prince highlights the AI labs' fear of Google: OpenAI was founded partly in opposition to Larry Page's view that AI superseding humans is acceptable. Google sees twice as much internet as OpenAI and 4x Anthropic, giving it data advantages; Prince advocates a level playing field so Google cannot leverage yesterday's monopoly for tomorrow's.
- Societally, Prince argues the attention economy engineered by Google and social media optimizes for dopamine or cortisol, fueling polarization. He is optimistic that a shift to rewarding knowledge creation could improve media, but warns of risks: if agents cost ~$1,200/year per US consumer, the 'kid in Sri Lanka' may be locked out, deepening global inequality.
- Prince worries that agents will undermine new business formation because they ignore convenience and emotion, leading to consolidation among incumbents. He proposes using LLMs to mathematically identify top contributors to human knowledge and grant fame-based recognition, akin to Nobel Prizes, to sustain creator incentives.
- Recalling COVID, Prince says internet traffic doubled in two weeks and nearly collapsed; Netflix, Amazon, YouTube and others had to coordinate reducing stream quality. With 1000x traffic predicted, the internet's tubes will break unless efficiency improves or new payment models fund infrastructure.
- Cloudflare is hiring over 1,000 interns in 2026, all AI-native, to reverse-mentor senior teams. Prince remains optimistic about builders and sellers but suggests fields like accounting may be at risk; he predicts enterprise sales will remain human, and agents will handle routine measuring.
- Prince defends his rapid layoff decision, arguing leaders who delay restructuring hurt employees more as job markets worsen. He received death threats but notes Cloudflare's stock recovered; he praises public markets for honesty and long-term orientation, citing Baillie Gifford's 16-year average holding period.
