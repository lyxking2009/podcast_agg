# The New Economics of Crypto Tokens | Austin Barack — Bankless

## TL;DR

Austin Barack, founder of Relayer Capital, discusses his growth-and-value approach to crypto investing, focusing on tokens with real revenue and buybacks like Venice (VVV), Pump, Hyperliquid, and EtherFi. He details his valuation model for VVV, projecting $43.90 based on 2027 burns, and argues that application-layer tokens are undervalued relative to infrastructure. The conversation covers the shift toward applications over infrastructure, the durability of meme coin trading, and EtherFi's evolution into an on-chain neobrokerage.

## Key points

- Austin Barack's Relayer Capital focuses on the intersection of growth and value, now allocating 95% of attention to liquid tokens due to better opportunities, with core themes in crypto-AI and 24/7 trading/tokenization.
- Venice (VVV) is a private, uncensored AI consumer app monetized via subscriptions ($18/$68/$200 monthly) and credit purchases; it programmatically burns tokens for new signups and 5% of credit purchase revenue.
- Venice raised $65 million at a $1 billion valuation (equity and token) in mid-2025, and the team has stated they will return the majority of free cash flow to token holders through burns.
- Barack's valuation model for VVV projects run-rate revenue of $107M annualized as of August 2025, growing to $336M in 2027, with burns rising from $8.3M to $70M, yielding a $43.89 price target at a 50x multiple on projected 2027 token supply (vs. $16 at the time).
- Key model assumptions include $29M of 2027 burns from the unreleased 'Minds' product (AI app store), resubscription burns, and an increase in credit purchase burn rate from 5% to 10% by 2027.
- Venice's growth has exceeded expectations: 4 million historical users, credit purchases launched January 2025 now run-rating $60M ARR, and the token has risen 10x in eight months.
- OpenRouter's $7 billion sale validates multi-model routing; Barack sees Venice as the consumer-level equivalent and argues it supports higher revenue multiples for VVV.
- Pump token trades at 5x buybacks, far below Hyperliquid and Lighter's 30-40x; its revenue is durable like a casino because users accept negative expected value for variance, and pump has committed to 50% revenue buybacks for 12 months.
- Hyperliquid's re-rating came from volume in HIP3 (RWA) markets, but revenue is still dominated by crypto token trading; fees recently jumped to $5M in a single day, up from under $1M daily.
- EtherFi has evolved from liquid restaking to an on-chain neobrokerage: 65% of business now comes from credit card and borrowing products, credit card volume grew 10x year-over-year to $3-4M/day, and it uses an Aave v4 instance with an 80/20 revenue share.
- Barack estimates EtherFi could generate $30M+ in annual buybacks, and at a 30x multiple that implies a token price over $1, double current levels; Blockworks' conservative model projected $21M.
- Applications now generate about two-thirds of total crypto revenue versus execution layers' one-third, and Barack expects applications to reach 90+% share, making application tokens and money the enduring assets.
- Ethereum may have renewed money properties due to Bitcoin concentration risks, and Solana is the chain enabling Pump and most on-chain spot activity but currently generates low revenue.
