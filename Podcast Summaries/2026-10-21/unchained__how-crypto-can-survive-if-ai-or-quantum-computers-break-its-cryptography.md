## TL;DR
Justin Drake's warning that AI may break elliptic curve cryptography before quantum computers has sparked debate on crypto's preparedness. Alex Pruden and Conor Deegan discuss ECDSA's vulnerabilities, the status of AI attacks on cryptographic schemes, and migration strategies like 'bunker mode.' Pruden updates his Q-Day estimate to 2030 at earliest and 2033–2035 as more likely than not, while both guests argue post-quantum and post-AI migrations are identical and inaction is the greatest risk.

## Key points
- Justin Drake's 'bunker mode' proposal calls for controlled migration of assets to addresses whose public keys remain hidden behind a hash, aiming to protect against both quantum computers and advanced AI breaking elliptic curves.
- Alex Pruden, CEO of Project Eleven, updates his Q-Day window: earliest 2030, with 2033–2035 more likely than not for a quantum computer capable of breaking current cryptography.
- Conor Deegan, Project Eleven CTO, states that no cryptographic scheme in production is broken yet, even though AI attacks have defeated the Hawk and McEliece schemes, which are not widely deployed.
- Coinbase's Yehuda Lindell notes that hash functions have historically broken more often than elliptic curves, providing a counterpoint to Drake's emphasis on curve vulnerability.
- NIST still favors lattice-based cryptography among post-quantum candidates due to its balance of security, performance, and maturity compared to curves, isogenies, and hashes.
- Matthew Green's encryption warning highlights that privacy chains like Zcash are particularly exposed because their shielded transactions rely on public keys being exposed, making them vulnerable if ECDSA is broken.
- Alex Pruden cites the Ethereum Classic fork as a precedent for how blockchains could recover from a catastrophic break, suggesting coordinated hard forks or rollbacks may be possible.
- Haseeb Qureshi's concept of 'cryptographic wartime' and Balaji's rollback idea illustrate the industry's consideration of emergency measures, but both guests argue proactive migration is preferable.
- Project Eleven is building crypto-agile custody solutions that can adapt to new cryptographic algorithms and price quantum risk, aiming to reduce exposure before a break occurs.
