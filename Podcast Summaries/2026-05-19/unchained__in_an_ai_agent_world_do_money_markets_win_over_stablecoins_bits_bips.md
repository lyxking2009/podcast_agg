## Key Points

- USDC became Hyperliquid's Aligned Quote Asset (AQA) under the AQAv2 framework, with Coinbase serving as USDC treasury deployer and Circle as the technical infrastructure provider — representing the deepest onchain stablecoin integration to date.
- Hyperliquid's AQAv2 deal allows the protocol to capture up to 90% of reserve yield on approximately $5–5.5 billion in USDC deposits, generating an estimated $135–160 million annually for HYPE token buybacks.
- The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield broke 5% for the first time since 2007–2008, raising the stakes of the stablecoin vs. tokenized money market fund debate: stablecoins yield nothing to the holder, while tokenized MMFs like USYC pass yield to users.
- Gordon Liao (Circle's Chief Economist) argues stablecoins are purpose-built for machine-to-machine payment rails — AI agents need 24/7 settlement finality, programmability, and frictionless micropayments that yielding instruments cannot provide as cleanly.
- The countervailing view from the panel: as yields rise, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding stablecoins grows; tokenized money market funds that combine yield with near-instant redemption may increasingly compete for AI agent treasury allocation.
- Circle's Agent Stack (agent wallets, nanopayments down to one-millionth of a dollar, programmable payment rails) is designed to cement USDC as the default currency for autonomous AI commerce — 99% of AI agent payments on EVM chains already use USDC.
- USDC circulation reached $77 billion (up 28% YoY) with on-chain transaction volume of $21.5 trillion, while the broader stablecoin market cap stands at ~$317 billion — illustrating the scale at which the money market competition plays out.
- The hosts debate whether yield-bearing instruments will eventually displace plain stablecoins for AI agents that can autonomously optimize capital, or whether liquidity and programmability lock-in favors USDC's dominant payment infrastructure.

## Overview

This Bits + Bips episode examines the emerging competition between plain stablecoins and tokenized money market funds as the preferred monetary instrument for AI agents, set against the backdrop of USDC's consolidation as Hyperliquid's core stablecoin infrastructure and the 30-year Treasury yield breaching 5%. Gordon Liao from Circle joins hosts Austin Campbell, Ram Ahluwalia, and Chris Perkins to argue the bull case for stablecoins as the optimal rails for autonomous agent commerce. The panel wrestles with whether rising risk-free yields will erode that advantage by making the zero-yield trade-off increasingly costly for agents sophisticated enough to optimize their own treasury positions.

## Implications

The Hyperliquid-USDC-Coinbase deal shows that protocol-level stablecoin alignment — where reserve yield is recycled back into the ecosystem rather than accruing to the issuer — could become a template that accelerates stablecoin adoption while pressuring issuer margins at Circle and Coinbase. If AI agents become significant holders and transactors of digital cash, the design of their payment stack (yield vs. liquidity) will have macro-scale implications for Treasury market demand, given that stablecoin issuers are already large T-bill buyers. The broader question the episode surfaces is whether programmable, yield-bearing money market tokens will ultimately merge the best properties of both instruments, making the current stablecoin/MMF distinction obsolete.

## Notable Quotes

- **Gordon Liao**: "Stablecoins are the circulatory system for autonomous agents — they need 24/7, programmable rails to stream money around the world, across borders, and that is difficult to do with anything other than a stablecoin."
- **Austin Campbell**: "When the 30-year is at 5%, the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding instrument is real. Agents that can optimize their own treasury will increasingly ask why they are sitting in USDC when they could be in a tokenized fund."
- **Ram Ahluwalia**: "The Hyperliquid deal is a proof of concept — 90% of reserve yield going back to the protocol changes the unit economics of stablecoin issuance and challenges the traditional issuer model."
- **Chris Perkins**: "We are entering an era where software processes are economic actors. The question is not whether stablecoins win, but whether they evolve fast enough to stay relevant as yield-bearing alternatives mature."
- **Gordon Liao**: "Circle's nanopayment infrastructure — gas-free USDC transfers as small as one-millionth of a dollar — is not something you can replicate with a money market redemption cycle, even a fast one."
- **Austin Campbell**: "USYC and instruments like it are trying to have it both ways — yield plus liquidity — and the closer they get to that goal, the more pressure it puts on plain vanilla stablecoins."
