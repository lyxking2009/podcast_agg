## Key Points
- Rick Heitzmann, co-founder and partner at FirstMark Capital, joined hosts Dan Nathan and Guy Adami for lunch at Manhatta, a restaurant 60 stories above NYC's financial district.
- Heitzmann recently joined the New York Yankees ownership group, attributing the invitation to his early investments in StubHub and DraftKings and his advisory roles at MLB, the NFL, and the NBA.
- His investment philosophy centers on identifying 10-year macro trends early — sports betting, digital marketplaces, and now digital health — even when regulatory or reputational headwinds scare off other investors.
- He is bullish on digital health as the biggest, fastest-growing, yet historically technology-lagging sector of the US economy, arguing AI-enabled telemedicine can dramatically cut costs and wait times.
- Heitzmann views prediction markets and sports betting as ultimately self-purifying once proper registration and transparency requirements are enforced, comparing them to the oversight already placed on sports team owners.
- Women's sports — particularly soccer (LAFC/Angel City) and basketball (NY Liberty) — are seen as a major emerging investment opportunity, with merchandise sales cited as the leading indicator of mainstream breakout.
- Restaurant industry headwinds include GLP-1 drug adoption reducing food consumption and younger generations drinking less alcohol, meaning only venues offering a distinctive, high-quality total experience (food + drink + culture) will survive.
- AI is being unfairly vilified by both political parties; Heitzmann plans to brief members of Congress, arguing AI is a tool that improves productivity and healthcare outcomes rather than a job destroyer.

## Overview
Rick Heitzmann, five-time Forbes Midas List honoree and co-founder of FirstMark Capital, sits down with Dan Nathan and Guy Adami for a 21-minute lunch at Manhatta — Danny Meyer's sky-high restaurant atop a Lower Manhattan skyscraper. The conversation spans Heitzmann's path into Yankees ownership, the investment thesis behind backing DraftKings and Airbnb years before they went mainstream, and why digital health is his highest-conviction sector today. The episode blends substantive venture-capital thinking with lighter banter about NYC restaurant culture, jersey-wearing etiquette at sporting events, and women's sports as the next big growth wave.

## Implications
The episode reinforces a consistent FirstMark thesis: regulatory friction and public vilification of a new category (sports betting, telemedicine, AI) are features rather than bugs for contrarian early-stage investors willing to do deep diligence on regulatory risk. Heitzmann's congressional briefing on AI signals that venture capital is increasingly playing a direct role in shaping the legislative environment around emerging technology. The conversation also highlights structural headwinds facing the NYC restaurant industry — GLP-1 adoption and reduced alcohol consumption — suggesting only experiential, high-margin dining concepts can sustain premium valuations.
