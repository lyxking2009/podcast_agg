## Key Points

- Figure became the leading non-bank HELOC lender in the US by using blockchain as its core operating infrastructure, not just as a layer on top of traditional systems — resulting in ~50% EBITDA margins and a "rule of 40" score of 140.
- The real competitive moat at Figure is not its loan origination system (which it gives away free to 380+ partners) but **Figure Connect**, a homogeneous, liquid private credit capital market that functions as a private version of Fannie/Freddie for non-agency loans.
- Blockchain's key value propositions in capital markets are: (1) bilateral settlement that strips out seven intermediaries between buyer and seller, and (2) DeFi lending where the underwriting shifts from credit risk to asset volatility/liquidity risk.
- Figure's on-chain equity platform **Open** allows native public-equity issuance on-chain with 24/7 trading, wallet-based access (no broker required), and direct stock-lending economics that flow back to shareholders instead of prime brokers.
- Mike Cagney argues the crypto industry's biggest unresolved problem is the wallet UX — the current self-custody experience will never attract SoFi/Robinhood users, and Figure is building a new wallet product with an embedded AI agent that handles tax reporting, yield optimization, and cross-collateralized lending.
- Provenance Blockchain's native token (HASH) and Figure's public equity (FGRS) currently coexist, but Cagney says the right end-state is for FGRS to serve as the staking asset for the chain — aligning shareholder and network validator incentives. He is actively working on how to get there.
- The token/labs entity split model (as seen with FTX/Alameda) is structurally untenable for investors. Figure was forced to split and then recombine precisely because public markets would not tolerate the interdependencies.
- Regulatory clarity matters more than benevolent regulators: current exemptive relief can be revoked, but legislation is permanent. Cagney expects a wholesale capital-markets migration on-chain within 10 years, contingent on maintaining a supportive regulatory environment.

## Overview

Mike Cagney, co-founder and executive chairman of Figure, joins Empire host Jason Yanowitz to walk through how Figure evolved from a blockchain-native HELOC lender into a full capital-markets infrastructure company. The conversation covers Figure's Q1 2026 operating metrics (2.9B marketplace volume, 1.6B via Figure Connect, ~50% adjusted EBITDA margins), the mechanics that make blockchain genuinely superior to traditional loan origination and securitization, and Figure's nascent moves into on-chain public equity (Open) and AI-powered wallets. Cagney is an unambiguous believer that blockchain is the most disruptive technology of any lifetime — more disruptive than the internet — and that the industry's central challenge is moving trillions of "Tradfi dollars" onto chain.

## Implications

Figure's results offer the clearest empirical proof point yet that blockchain-native capital markets infrastructure can outperform incumbents on margin and speed, not just ideology, which will accelerate pressure on traditional lenders and clearinghouses. The vision of native on-chain equity with transparent stock-lending economics directly threatens the prime brokerage revenue model and, if successful with enough issuers, could restructure how retail and institutional investors think about custody and yield. Cagney's prediction of a 10-year wholesale migration of capital markets on-chain, while contingent on regulatory permanency, signals that the most consequential blockchain battles will be fought in debt and equity capital markets rather than consumer payments or DeFi speculation.
