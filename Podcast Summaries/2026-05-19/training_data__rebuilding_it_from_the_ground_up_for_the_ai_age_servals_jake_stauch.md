## Key Points

- Serval is building an AI-native IT service management platform — dubbed a "ServiceNow for the AI era" — that automates internal employee support across IT, HR, finance, and legal using natural language instead of traditional ticketing.
- The platform's core innovation is a code generation engine ("vibe coding") that converts plain-English automation descriptions into executable TypeScript, compressing workflow build times from weeks to hours or minutes.
- Serval uses a dual-agent architecture: an admin agent powered by code generation (Anthropic models) for building automations, and a restricted help desk agent (OpenAI models) that can only act through tools admins explicitly approve — a safety mechanism born from an early incident where a work-trial designer accidentally off-boarded the founders.
- Rather than replacing existing systems (ServiceNow, Jira Service Management) immediately, Serval mirrors data bidirectionally, eliminating migration friction and allowing customers to organically discover they prefer Serval's interface.
- Jake's most contrarian product bet: "the product should look like boring old enterprise software, but with unlimited intelligence" — prioritizing enterprise familiarity over flashy AI interfaces.
- Serval reached a $1B valuation within 18 months of founding (April 2024), raising $125M total: $50M Series A from Redpoint (August 2024), $75M Series B from Sequoia (October 2024, offered the day the Series A was announced).
- The company identifies "90% of AI project failure" as stemming not from model capability gaps but from the difficulty of translating business processes to software — their answer is making implementation part of the product itself.
- Hiring philosophy: every candidate must be "better than the median person at the company today" in their function — guarding against the gradual talent dilution that often accompanies fast growth.

## Overview

Jake Stauch, founder and CEO of Serval, joins Pat Grady on Sequoia's Training Data podcast to explain how he is rebuilding enterprise IT infrastructure from the ground up for the AI age. Serval automates the full lifecycle of internal support requests — from password resets to complex cross-system provisioning — by letting IT administrators describe workflows in plain English and generating the underlying code automatically. The 38-minute conversation covers Serval's dual-agent architecture, the deliberate choice to embrace "boring" enterprise software aesthetics, and the operational lessons Jake carried over from five years at Verkada.

## Implications

AI-native platforms like Serval represent a structural threat to legacy ITSM vendors (ServiceNow, Jira Service Management) not through direct replacement but through incremental migration: by mirroring existing systems first, they eliminate the switching-cost moat that has historically protected incumbents. The shift from click-ops to natural-language workflow building also redefines IT roles — professionals move from repetitive ticket handling toward building and maintaining automation portfolios, making "automation literacy" a new baseline IT skill. For enterprise software founders broadly, Serval's traction suggests that AI incumbents are vulnerable precisely where they have the most revenue to protect, and that startups willing to "go hard early" on platform breadth can compress decades of feature development into months.
