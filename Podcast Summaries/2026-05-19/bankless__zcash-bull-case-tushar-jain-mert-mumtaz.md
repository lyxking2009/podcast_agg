## Overview

In this 57-minute episode, Bankless host David Hoffman is joined by Tushar Jain (co-founder, Multicoin Capital) and Mert Mumtaz (founder, Helius Labs) to make the bull case for Zcash as a privacy-first, store-of-value asset. Jain reveals that Multicoin built a significant ZEC position beginning in February 2026, marking a notable reversal from the firm's 2019 stance that privacy was a feature rather than a standalone product. The episode frames Zcash not as a payments network but as a vehicle for private wealth storage — analogous to Bitcoin but with cryptographically enforced financial confidentiality.

The guests argue that a confluence of macro and technical catalysts has rehabilitated the Zcash narrative. On the macro side, growing state surveillance, transparent on-chain activity favoring institutional front-runners, California's proposed wealth seizure legislation, and the migration of financial activity onto public blockchains all increase structural demand for privacy. On the technical side, shielded pool adoption has climbed to roughly 31–32%, Ledger has added support for shielded ZEC transactions, block times are being cut from 75 to 25 seconds, and zero-knowledge proofs have matured into production-ready infrastructure.

Mumtaz contends that privacy has become "the major thing that crypto has forgotten," particularly as institutional adoption pushes activity onto transparent rails where counterparties can monitor positions and front-run trades. Jain distinguishes Zcash from Monero on both cryptographic and brand grounds: ZEC's shielded design provides stronger privacy guarantees than Monero's ring-signature model, while Zcash's positioning as "privacy for the normal person" is more palatable to institutional adopters. Jain also acknowledges the bear case honestly — investors have historically overestimated privacy demand, and prior Zcash cycles were marred by high inflation, poor usability, and weak marketing — but argues those structural flaws have been largely addressed.

## Implications

For investors, the episode frames Zcash as a high-conviction asymmetric bet in a specific niche — private wealth storage — rather than a general-purpose crypto asset. If Jain's reflexivity thesis holds, increasing shielded adoption would strengthen Zcash's privacy product and attract further capital in a self-reinforcing loop. The political backdrop (wealth taxes, surveillance expansion, institutional on-chain transparency) provides a macro narrative that could sustain demand beyond speculative cycles. For builders, the episode signals renewed interest in privacy infrastructure at the protocol and application layer — including ZK-proof tooling, shielded wallet UX, and FHE-enabled private DeFi composability. The Zcash episode also implicitly challenges the broader crypto narrative that transparency is a feature; if privacy becomes a regulatory-viable and institutionally acceptable property, it could shift how future protocols are designed.
