## Key Points
- Markets have continued to rally despite macro headwinds because investor positioning is light ("buses are empty") — few people actually believed in or participated in the post-April 9 rally, leaving room for further upside.
- The fundamental macro framework for the next several years is the bifurcation between China/aligned countries and the West: China is the creditor, the West is the debtor, and the West must rebuild its entire manufacturing stack from scratch.
- China dominates global manufacturing capacity (~55%) and critical supply chains (e.g., ~90%+ of cobalt refining), meaning any decoupling requires enormous Western capital expenditure over years.
- Traders cannot trade individual headlines in this environment — the pace and contradictory nature of news forces participants to take structural long-term positions and ignore short-term noise.
- The AI trade is the dominant equity narrative, with a consensus view that the current run has one to two more years to go, but this is being contested by China's parallel AI development (Huawei AI clusters, DeepSeek, GLM, quantum computing, optical GPUs).
- Japan's role as a stability backstop is crucial: the Bank of Japan has intervened informally to suppress yen weakness and calm rate volatility, acting as a de facto risk-parity stabilizer for global markets.
- UK sovereign debt faces risks from fiscal dominance fears (echoes of the Truss moment), but financial repression — central banks keeping long rates suppressed — is expected to prevail for now.
- Chinese financial assets (equities and government bonds) remain under-owned in global portfolios despite their growing relevance, and there is meaningful room for further adoption.

## Overview
Recorded live in London on May 7, this episode of Odd Lots features Deutsche Bank's Ozan Tarman (Vice Chair of Global Macro) and Aditya Singhal (Head of EM Trading across rates, FX, and credit) discussing why global equity markets have defied macro gravity. The guests frame the current environment through two lenses: the structural US-China economic bifurcation that will define capital flows for years, and the short-term dynamics of AI-driven equity momentum and geopolitical headline risk. Both guests emphasize that the key to navigating this market is taking structural positions rather than reacting to individual news events.

## Implications
The conversation underscores that the post-globalization adjustment is not merely a trade policy debate but a multi-year capital reallocation story — the West must spend trillions to rebuild manufacturing capacity that China currently dominates, which has profound implications for sovereign debt, inflation, and interest rates. The AI narrative propping up equity markets faces a genuine competitive threat from China's parallel ecosystem (Huawei, DeepSeek, optical compute, robotics), meaning the valuation premium embedded in Western AI capex stories could compress faster than consensus expects. Meanwhile, the stability of global risk assets continues to depend on informal coordination between central banks — particularly the Bank of Japan's willingness to act as a volatility suppressor — which is a fragile arrangement that markets are under-appreciating.
