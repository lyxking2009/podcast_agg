## Key Points
- Eid Al-Adha is one of Islam's two major holidays, observed at the end of Dhul Hijjah — the twelfth and holiest month of the Islamic lunar calendar — coinciding with the annual Hajj pilgrimage to Mecca.
- The holiday commemorates the Prophet Ibrahim's (Abraham's) willingness to sacrifice his son in obedience to God, and God's mercy in providing a ram as a substitute — a story shared across Islam, Judaism, and Christianity.
- Dhul Hijjah is considered a sacred month of heightened worship; Muslims worldwide are encouraged to fast (especially on the Day of Arafah), give charity, and recite extra prayers and remembrances even if they are not performing Hajj.
- Eid Al-Adha is celebrated with communal prayer, the ritual sacrifice of an animal (Qurbani), and the distribution of meat to family, neighbors, and those in need — an act emphasizing generosity and social solidarity.
- The Prophet's Last Sermon, delivered during his only Hajj, is a foundational text Muslims often revisit during this season; it emphasizes the equality of all people regardless of race or lineage, and the sanctity of life and property.
- For Muslim families in the West, explaining Eid Al-Adha to non-Muslim neighbors, coworkers, and schools is a recurring challenge — guest Shahnila Ahmad offers frameworks and language to help bridge that gap.
- Shahnila Ahmad, a homeschool parent of four and ICF-certified coach, focuses her work on helping Muslim families raise emotionally resilient children by centering diverse, faith-aligned perspectives in education and parenting.
- The episode is explicitly designed for non-Muslims or those with little prior knowledge, following Wiser World's "history explains headlines" mission of making unfamiliar cultural and religious contexts accessible.

## Overview
Host Alli Roper invites Shahnila Ahmad — homeschool parent, parent mentor, and ICF-certified professional coach behind the platform @BraveMuslimParents — to walk listeners through the holy month of Dhul Hijjah and the holiday of Eid Al-Adha from the ground up. The episode is framed as a primer for people who know little to nothing about these Islamic observances, consistent with Wiser World's broader mission of turning complex cultural and historical contexts into clear, approachable explanations for busy adults. Ahmad draws on her experience translating Islamic practices for broader audiences as a Muslim parent in the West.

The conversation covers the spiritual significance of Dhul Hijjah, the story behind Eid Al-Adha, how Muslims around the world celebrate — including prayer, Qurbani (ritual sacrifice), and communal giving — and how to understand and explain the holiday across cultural lines.
