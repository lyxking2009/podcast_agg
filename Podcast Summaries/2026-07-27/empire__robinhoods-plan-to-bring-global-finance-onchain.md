# Robinhood's Plan to Bring Global Finance Onchain | Johann Kerbrat

**Show:** Empire
**Date:** 2026-07-27

## Key Points
- Robinhood Chain launched July 1st, 2026 — reached ~750M market cap, ~500M TVL, and 105M transactions in 3 weeks
- Robinhood has 11 business lines each generating 9-figure revenue, providing diversification when crypto sentiment is low
- The crypto-as-a-service business (white-label platform) serves institutions like BBVA in Europe
- Robinhood chose to build an L2 on Ethereum rather than an L1, prioritizing decentralization and security of the Ethereum base layer
- The vision is convergence — tokenized assets on chain could enable global exchange connectivity, using portfolio as collateral across jurisdictions
- Bitstamp acquisition brought institutional exchange capabilities; staking, lending, and trading fees are the main crypto revenue sub-items
- Robinhood Chain overtook Base for daily active users in the first weeks after launch
- The team focuses on having an ecosystem ready at launch — partnerships with Slider (perps), Arcus (spot), RWA stock tokenization (~19M TVL)

## Overview
Jason and the Blockworks team interview Johann Kerbrat, GM of Crypto at Robinhood, about the launch of Robinhood Chain and the company's broader crypto strategy. Kerbrat explains the evolution from simple Bitcoin/Ethereum trading in 2018 to building a full L2 chain on Ethereum. The conversation covers Robinhood's 11 diversified business lines, the chain's impressive early metrics (750M market cap, 500M TVL, 105M transactions in 3 weeks), and the long-term vision of convergence between TradFi and on-chain finance. Key design decisions included building on Ethereum as an L2, launching with a ready ecosystem of partners, and enabling stock tokenization as RWA.

## Implications
Robinhood Chain's rapid traction signals growing demand for regulated on-chain finance. The L2-on-Ethereum choice validates Ethereum's security model for institutional use cases. The crypto-as-a-service model could become a significant revenue driver as more traditional institutions want crypto exposure without building infrastructure. The convergence thesis — where all assets eventually trade on-chain with global liquidity — remains the long-term vision but is now being actively built rather than just theorized.
