# Jensen Huang: The Mindset That Built NVIDIA

**Show:** Y Combinator Startup Podcast
**Date:** 2026-07-27

## Key Points
- NVIDIA started with the wrong technology — the algorithm they chose for 3D graphics was 'exactly wrong,' and they had to pivot
- The Sega story: Jensen went to Japan and told the CEO their $12M contract wouldn't work because the technology was flawed, but asked for the money anyway — 'What you're telling me is what I contracted you to do, you can't do, but you would like all the money' — and got $5M that kept NVIDIA alive
- NVIDIA's core philosophy: it's not about building a great chip, it's about 'accelerating an algorithm domain'
- The AlexNet moment: Jensen saw deep learning not as just a computer vision breakthrough but as a 'universal function approximator' — this realization drove NVIDIA's pivot ~15 years ago
- Great companies are built on 'a unique perspective about the world that you deeply believe in' — not just the right technology or market
- Jensen's management approach: starts with curiosity, goes deep into papers and talks directly to scientists, then shares insights to empower the organization
- The five-layer cake: processor, middleware, algorithms, applications, and the entire industrial stack — Jensen imagined reinventing all of it ~15 years ago
- Sega sold their NVIDIA investment the moment NVIDIA went public at $300M valuation in 1999 — now north of a trillion dollars
- First principles thinking: 'If this, then what? If this can get better, then so what?' — asking basic questions about impactful observations

## Overview
At Startup School 2026 at Chase Center, Garry Tan interviews Jensen Huang, founder and CEO of NVIDIA. Jensen tells the origin story: NVIDIA started with a fundamentally wrong 3D graphics algorithm, had to go to Japan and ask Sega for money on a contract they couldn't fulfill, and pivoted to discover accelerated computing. The conversation covers Jensen's realization that deep learning was a 'universal function approximator' (~15 years ago), his management philosophy of going deep into the weeds and empowering teams with insights, and his framework for building great companies: a unique perspective about the world that you deeply believe in, where pursuing that vision is hard to do. Jensen emphasizes that NVIDIA's success came from asking 'if this, then what?' about foundational algorithms, not from chip design per se.

## Implications
Jensen's 'universal function approximator' framing from 15 years ago explains NVIDIA's sustained AI dominance — they saw the implications before anyone else. The Sega anecdote illustrates a counterintuitive startup lesson: radical honesty about failure can build more trust than pretending things are fine. The 'accelerating an algorithm domain' framework (not 'building chips') is the strategic insight that separates NVIDIA from every other chip company. For founders: the emphasis on first-principles curiosity over management technique suggests that the best companies are led by people who go deep into the technical details, not just delegate.
