## Key Points

1. Ned Naylor-Leyland frames gold investing as 'being short the behavior of your government' — if you lack confidence that governments will maintain fiscal discipline and give real returns on cash, gold and silver are the rational alternative.
2. Gold and silver producers have 'never been cheaper than they are today, ever' — despite rising metal prices, there has been negative float in the space, with companies generating 50% free cash flow margins and double or triple the free cash flow of tech companies.
3. The Trump Fort Knox audit relates to a longstanding gold community suspicion that gold was moved elsewhere and Fort Knox is 'a bit of a shell game.' If Trump opens it and proves gold is missing, it could trigger a chain reaction where wealthy individuals demand their physical gold, driving prices significantly higher.
4. The ECB has published research notes warning about the implications of gold reserve audits, suggesting the institutional establishment recognizes this risk.
5. The case for precious metals is fundamentally about monetary history — 'the history of money tends to suggest one thing, which is you're better off owning gold and/or silver versus your local currency.'
6. Mining producers represent a unique value proposition: the most profitable companies in the world by free cash flow margins, yet trading at historically low valuations relative to their earnings power.
7. The discussion connects fiscal policy, monetary history, and geopolitical risk into a coherent thesis for precious metals allocation.

## Overview

Wilfred Frost interviews Ned Naylor-Leyland, newly crowned Investment Week Precious Metals Manager of the Year and head of Jupiter's Gold & Silver Strategy, about the case for gold and silver in the current environment. Ned frames precious metals investing as being 'short the behavior of your government' and argues that gold producers have never been cheaper relative to their free cash flow generation. The conversation covers the Trump Fort Knox audit implications, ECB research on gold reserves, the historical case for metals over fiat currencies, and why mining companies offer 50% free cash flow margins at historically low valuations.

## Implications

For investors: the gold/silver thesis is framed not as speculation but as a rational response to fiscal and monetary policy history. Mining producers offer an unusual combination of historically high profitability and historically low valuations. The Fort Knox audit angle — if it reveals missing gold — could be a catalyst for significant price moves in physical metals.
