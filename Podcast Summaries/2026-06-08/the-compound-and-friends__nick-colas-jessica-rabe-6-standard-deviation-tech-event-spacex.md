# Nick Colas and Jessica Rabe on the 6 Standard Deviation Tech Event, How SpaceX Will Tell Us if It's 1997 or 1999

## Key Points

- Nick Colas and Jessica Rabe of DataTrek Research join Josh Brown and Michael Batnick for a "What Did We Learn" episode analyzing one of the most extreme stretches of tech outperformance in recent market history.
- The tech outperformance is described as a "6 standard deviation event" — a statistical rarity that raises questions about whether the current environment is more analogous to 1997 (early in the tech bubble, with years of gains still ahead) or 1999 (near the peak, with a crash imminent).
- SpaceX's upcoming potential IPO is positioned as a key signal: if SpaceX goes public and sustains high valuations, it suggests we're in a 1997-like environment; if it marks a peak of private market euphoria, the 1999 analogy becomes more apt.
- The DataTrek co-founders bring their signature data-driven approach to contextualizing the current market environment within historical frameworks.
- Discussion covers tech sector valuations, the role of mega-cap stocks in driving index performance, and whether current extremes are sustainable or mean-reverting.

## Overview

In this "What Did We Learn" episode, Josh Brown and Michael Batnick are joined by DataTrek Research co-founders Nick Colas and Jessica Rabe to analyze a historic stretch of tech outperformance. The conversation frames the current market through the lens of a 6 standard deviation event — a rarity that demands historical comparison. The central question: is this 1997 (early innings of a longer rally) or 1999 (the final euphoric stretch before a crash)? SpaceX's potential IPO serves as a barometer for where we are in the cycle. The discussion draws on DataTrek's data-driven research to contextualize tech valuations, index concentration, and investor positioning.

## Implications

- A 6 standard deviation tech outperformance event is historically rare and unsustainable — investors should prepare for either continued momentum (1997 scenario) or a sharp reversal (1999 scenario).
- SpaceX's IPO timing and reception could serve as a real-time indicator of market cycle positioning.
- Index concentration in mega-cap tech remains a key risk factor — the S&P 500's dependence on a handful of names mirrors late-1990s dynamics.
