# Charley Ellis on How America Actually Got Built (Investing in America Series) | #633

## Key Points

- Charley Ellis, founder of Greenwich Associates and longtime member of Yale's investment committee, reflects on his new book *Great American Investments: A History of the Bold Initiatives that Shaped a Nation*, covering 14 audacious public investments from the Louisiana Purchase to the Marshall Plan.
- Each transformative American project — from the Louisiana Purchase to Social Security to the Alaska Purchase — came down to one or two obsessed individuals who pushed through political resistance and public skepticism.
- Ellis wrote the first major book on share repurchases 50 years ago, when the idea was so foreign that Goldman Sachs mailed it to 1,000 corporations as a "legitimizer."
- The lunch with Sandy Gottesman in the early 1970s that led Ellis to buy Berkshire Hathaway at $700 a share — and hold it ever since — is one of the most memorable investment decisions of his career.
- Frances Perkins, FDR's Secretary of Labor, was the driving force behind Social Security, muscling it into law against significant political opposition.
- Alaska turned out to be the bargain of the century — purchased for $7.2 million in 1867, it has generated trillions in natural resource value.
- Ellis discusses the current state of investing, behavioral economics, and advice for young investors emphasizing long-term diversified index fund strategies.

## Overview

Meb Faber interviews Charley Ellis as part of the "Investing in America" series tied to Faber's upcoming coffee table book. Ellis, with 60+ years of investment wisdom and 20+ books to his name, walks through his latest work on 14 bold American public investments. The conversation spans the Louisiana Purchase, Social Security's origins through Frances Perkins, the Alaska bargain, and the pattern of obsessed individuals driving transformative projects. Ellis also shares his personal investing journey, including buying Berkshire Hathaway at $700 per share after a pivotal lunch with Sandy Gottesman, and his philosophy on long-term index investing.
