# NBA Finals Bonus: MAI Capital's Joe McLean on Being the NBA's 'Money Whisperer'

## Key Points

- Joe McLean began as a professional and college basketball player (4 years at Arizona, Division I) before becoming the financial advisor — or "money whisperer" — to virtually every major professional athlete across basketball, football, hockey, baseball, golf, and NASCAR.
- The core framework for athlete financial management uses a "bucket" approach: the first bucket is aggressive saving (a substantial percentage of income), the second bucket is growth/investing for the future, and only then comes the entrepreneurial "fun money" bucket.
- College athletes now earning significant money through NIL (Name, Image, Likeness) deals face the same financial literacy gaps that pro athletes have historically struggled with — many come from backgrounds with no exposure to wealth management.
- The psychology of sudden wealth is a recurring theme: athletes signing $100M+ contracts often have zero financial education and are surrounded by entourages with competing financial interests.
- McLean emphasizes that the principles he teaches athletes — save first, invest for growth, then explore entrepreneurial ventures — are directly applicable to everyday investors regardless of income level.
- The conversation explores how the NBA's financial ecosystem has evolved, including salary structures, endorsement economics, and the growing importance of financial literacy programs for young athletes.

## Overview

Barry Ritholtz interviews Joe McLean, managing partner at MAI Capital, who has become the go-to financial advisor for professional athletes across every major sport. The conversation traces McLean's journey from Division I basketball at Arizona to the financial advisory world, where he applies a disciplined "bucket" framework to help athletes manage sudden wealth. They discuss the psychological challenges of going from modest backgrounds to multi-million-dollar contracts, the explosion of NIL money in college athletics, and why the same principles of saving and disciplined investing apply equally to the average listener. The episode is timed to coincide with the NBA Finals, highlighting the intersection of sports and finance.

## Implications

- Financial literacy programs for young athletes (both college NIL earners and pro rookies) represent a growing advisory niche.
- The "bucket" framework — save aggressively first, invest for growth second, entrepreneurial ventures last — is a transferable model for any windfall scenario (inheritance, startup exit, etc.).
- The NIL era is creating a new generation of young wealth holders who need the same financial guidance previously reserved for pro athletes.
