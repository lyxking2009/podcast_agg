# Talk Your Book: Diversification in Private Assets

## Key Points

- Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson are joined by Danielle Singer and Ben Linder from Invesco to discuss investing in private markets and the role of private assets in portfolio construction.
- The episode explores how advisors should think about carving out allocations to private assets within diversified portfolios, comparing the roles of private and public equity.
- The illiquidity premium in private assets is framed as something to embrace rather than work around — investors who can tolerate lock-up periods are compensated with higher expected returns.
- Private credit is discussed in context, addressing common concerns and headlines about the asset class and providing perspective on risk-adjusted returns.
- The conversation covers accessing infrastructure and real assets through evergreen structures, which provide continuous exposure without the traditional fund lifecycle constraints.
- The episode is sponsored by Invesco and positions private assets as an increasingly important component of diversified portfolios for both institutional and individual investors.

## Overview

In this "Talk Your Book" episode, hosts Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson welcome Invesco's Danielle Singer and Ben Linder to discuss the growing role of private assets in portfolio construction. The conversation covers the comparison between private and public equity, the strategic embrace of the illiquidity premium, contextualizing private credit risk beyond headlines, and accessing infrastructure through evergreen investment structures. The episode emphasizes that private assets are becoming more accessible to a broader range of investors and that advisors need frameworks for incorporating them into diversified portfolios.

## Implications

- The illiquidity premium in private assets represents a genuine source of excess return for investors with longer time horizons and stable cash flow needs.
- Evergreen structures for infrastructure and real assets are democratizing access to asset classes previously reserved for institutional investors.
- Advisors need to develop clear frameworks for allocating to private assets — the "how much" and "which type" questions are becoming increasingly important as the asset class matures.

## Notable Quotes
