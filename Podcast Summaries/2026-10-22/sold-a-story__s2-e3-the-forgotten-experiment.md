# S2 E3: The Forgotten Experiment — Sold a Story

## TL;DR
The speaker recounts learning about Project Follow Through, a 10-year federally funded study that tested 22 teaching models and found Direct Instruction outperformed all others on every measure, including self-confidence. Despite a re-evaluation confirming the results, rival program developers rejected the findings and the government continued funding unsuccessful models. The speaker argues this reveals a systemic preference for discovery-based education over evidence-based practice and that educators are never taught about the study.

## Key points
- The speaker had never heard of Project Follow Through, a massive federally funded educational study designed to determine the most effective teaching methods for low-income students, and learned about it from Sold a Story S2 E3.
- Project Follow Through involved 22 different instructional models, ranging from academically focused direct instruction to more discovery-oriented learning experiences, and was implemented across many school districts with diverse student populations over roughly 10 years.
- Results showed that Direct Instruction outperformed all other models on every single measure, including not only academic outcomes but also measures of self-confidence and self-assurance, indicating students knew they had acquired skills.
- Despite the clear findings, developers of the other 21 models rejected the results; a re-evaluation still confirmed Direct Instruction's superiority, yet the federal government gave additional money to those unsuccessful programs to give them more time.
- The speaker argues that the failure to widely disseminate Direct Instruction reflects a conflict between evidence and beliefs about what education should look like, with American society preferring discovery and exploration even when data says otherwise.
- The speaker is concerned that strong educational research findings like Project Follow Through do not translate into meaningful changes in school systems, noting the study is not taught in teacher preparation, graduate school, or professional development.
- The speaker suggests that if more educators knew about Project Follow Through, more would question current policies, curricula, and practices that are not evidence-based, and hints that this ignorance may be by design.
