# The Recommendation Engine for Commodity Traders — Anekha Sokhal, Moshi — Latent Space: The AI Engineer Podcast

## TL;DR
Anekha Sokhal, founder of Moshi, discusses building real-time insights for commodity traders that processes vast data types including structured, unstructured, and private data, using machine learning and a recommendation engine to surface relevant information. Drawing on her background as a derivatives trader at JP Morgan and ML research with Stability AI and NASA, she explains how Moshi filters trusted sources, provides confidence scores, and integrates private data via vision language models. She also shares feedback from the AI Engineer conference, including inbound from traders and overwhelmed AI engineers, and suggests efficient group meetings.

## Key points
- Moshi is building real-time insight software for commodity and energy traders, processing structured data (futures time series, technical indicators), fundamentals, unstructured news, X/Twitter, podcasts, and private data (analyst preferences, internal reports) to push relevant information to users. It claims to learn 300 new things every 4 seconds and provide LLM answers in 30 seconds that would otherwise take days.
- Anekha Sokhal's background includes a MORSE degree (mathematics, operational research, statistics, economics) at Warwick, derivatives trading at JP Morgan on emerging markets then commodities, a side-hustled computer vision startup, a Fulbright data scholarship (one per UK), and ML research with ICLR, Stability AI, and NASA before founding Moshi.
- Moshi differentiates from Bloomberg by combining a recommendation system similar to Netflix/Claude: if a trader looks at one thing, it surfaces second and third order impacts across a connected data graph, providing push-based delivery rather than a news stream.
- For handling noisy X/Twitter data, Moshi filters for trusted industry voices (suppliers, facility status updates), provides confidence scores, and highlights divergent sources across Reuters, Bloomberg, and WSJ as potential trading opportunities.
- For private/user data, Moshi uses a vision language model to parse messy PDFs uploaded to a private cloud version, avoiding third-party processing due to data privacy concerns. The team has NASA background and builds models in-house rather than off-the-shelf.
- After her talk at AI Engineer conference, inbound came from traders at funds, consulting firms, and AI engineers feeling overwhelmed by management expecting one person to replace a whole team. She extended her NY stay to Monday, scheduled Houston meetings, and will host a webinar to handle volume.
- On hiring, Moshi posted one job for 4 days and received 2,500 applicants, leading them to close it. Current priority is client acquisition, not hiring; hiring noise includes visa issues and ineligible candidates.
- She suggested efficient group meetings (one-to-many) like finance roundtables to handle conference inbound, and praised AIE as the best organized conference, including a speaker lounge with amenities sponsored by Cognition.
