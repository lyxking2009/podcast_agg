## Key Points
- Consumer crypto has persistently failed mainstream users due to high friction — managing wallets, gas fees, bridges, and multiple chains are all barriers Fomo is designed to eliminate
- Fomo's core product is a social feed where users see others' real-time trades, can copy them, and build a public on-chain identity and reputation track record
- The platform enables onboarding, funding, and first token purchase in under 30 seconds, with sign-in via Google or Apple and funding via Apple Pay, debit card, or bank transfer
- Fomo raised a $75M Series B (June 2026) led by Index Ventures with participation from Union Square Ventures and Benchmark, valuing the company at $550M with only 17 employees
- Key metrics at time of episode: 625,000+ total signups, ~30% conversion to active traders (~200,000 active), $4B in trading volume, 110M social interactions, ~3,500 new users onboarding daily
- 70,000 users entered via traditional fiat onramps rather than crypto rails, demonstrating reach beyond crypto-native audiences
- Fomo added perpetual futures in June 2026, with plans to add equities, derivatives, and prediction markets as assets tokenize on-chain
- The co-founders' long-term goal is explicitly not to be seen as a crypto app but as the largest trading app in the world
- The 'exit-liquidity problem' — traders pump conviction publicly then exit as followers arrive — is mitigated by radical transparency so bad actors lose followers over time
- Creator-driven distribution (influencers and top traders) is a key user acquisition strategy, making social features a defensible competitive moat
- Competitive landscape includes Robinhood Social, Bullpen, Legend Trade, and paste.trade; Fomo distinguishes itself with deeper on-chain social infrastructure
- Co-founders Paul Erlanger, Se Yong Park, and Prashan Dharmasena all came from dYdX and originally met at Deutsche Bank, giving the team both TradFi and DeFi depth

## Overview
Empire host Jason Yanowitz sits down with Fomo co-founders Paul Erlanger and Se Yong Park to discuss why consumer crypto has repeatedly failed mainstream users and how Fomo is trying to fix that by combining on-chain trading with a social feed. The episode covers Fomo's recent $75M Series B raise at a $550M valuation, its 625K+ user base, and its vision to eventually become the largest trading app in the world by expanding beyond crypto into stocks and other tokenized assets. The conversation spans product philosophy, user acquisition, fee structures, copy trading, and how transparent on-chain data creates a new kind of social accountability.

## Implications
Fomo represents a structural bet that the next wave of crypto adoption will be social and consumer-first rather than technical or institutional, and that on-chain transparency — where trades are publicly verifiable — can substitute for the trust mechanisms that regulated brokerages provide. If successful, the model could meaningfully compress the cost of financial consumer acquisition through viral copy-trading mechanics, ultimately blurring the line between social media and financial services in the same way Robinhood blurred the line between investing and gaming.
