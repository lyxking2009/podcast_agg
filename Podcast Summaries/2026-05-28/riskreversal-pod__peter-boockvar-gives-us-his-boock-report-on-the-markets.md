# Peter Boockvar Gives Us His "Boock Report" On The Markets

## Key Points
- Markets since early April have been driven by exactly two narratives: the war ending and the AI trade — oil at $100 and the 10-year at 4.5% have both been ignored because they don't challenge those two stories.
- The commodity bull market is structural: even if the war ends tomorrow, oil is unlikely to drop below $85 due to strategic reserve rebuilding, semiconductor double/triple ordering, and underlying supply constraints.
- New Fed Chair Kevin Walsh may prioritize balance sheet reduction over rate cuts — shrinking the Fed balance sheet below $6 trillion would surprise the long end of the yield curve.
- Boockvar's base case is zero rate cuts in 2026; stagflation (above 3.5% inflation, potentially above 5% unemployment) is his most likely year-end economic scenario.
- Micron is trading at ~9x price-to-sales — historically extreme for a cyclical company; Boockvar cautions against PE multiples at cyclical peaks and flags 2027-2028 supply risk from current double/triple ordering.
- Auto delinquencies 90+ days past due hit 5.6% in Q1 2026, surpassing 2008 highs; Walmart reports average fill-up dropped below 10 gallons as gasoline hit $4.50/gallon — consumer stress at the bottom of the K.
- China has become a genuine technology competitor: it leads in EVs, solar, robotics, automation; it is closing on chips; it can offer AI models globally at 75% of U.S. provider costs. Nvidia has essentially zero China market share.
- Gold's recent weakness is largely a liquidity phenomenon (Turkey selling reserves, real-rate algorithms) rather than a fundamental shift — central bank demand and de-dollarization trend remain intact.
- Japan remains interesting: BOJ likely hikes in June, yen likely rallies, and a new governance push pressures companies to invest excess cash rather than just return it — a different regime from the buyback/dividend-focused prior period.
- Housing is in a structural recession: NAHB estimates the housing ecosystem at 15-18% of U.S. GDP; transaction pace is at 30-year lows; the only real solutions are local permitting reform and lower home prices.

## Overview
Peter Boockvar, CIO at 1Point BFG Wealth Partners and author of The Boock Report on Substack, joined Danny Moses in person for a wide-ranging markets discussion covering macro, sector stress, geopolitics, and event contract trading. The conversation opens with Boockvar's simple framework for the recent market rally: since early April, when the White House signaled intent to end the war, exactly two things have mattered — the war-ending trade and the AI infrastructure trade. Every other data point (oil at $100, the 10-year above 4.5%, Walmart consumer stress comments, auto delinquencies at all-time highs) has been shrugged off because nothing challenged those two narratives.

Boockvar argues this complacency will be tested by a commodity bull market that predates the current conflict and will outlast it. Strategic reserve rebuilding, semiconductor hoarding (CDW reported customers pulling forward orders ahead of memory price increases), and structural supply constraints mean oil likely stays above $85 even post-peace. Combined with a Fed that is constrained from responding — Walsh may surprise with balance sheet reduction rather than rate cuts — the inflation picture remains complicated well into 2027.

The China discussion is the sharpest section of the episode. Boockvar argues U.S. policy achieved the opposite of its intent: by threatening ZTE and Huawei in 2018, the U.S. incentivized China to build an entirely self-sufficient technology ecosystem. China now leads in EVs, solar, automation, and robotics; it is narrowing the chip gap; and it can offer AI inference at a significant discount to U.S. providers for emerging market customers. Nvidia's China market share is essentially zero. The competitive consequences for U.S. AI terminal value assumptions are being ignored by markets laser-focused on the current domestic buildout cycle.

## Implications
If Boockvar's framework is correct — that markets have priced in war-end and AI perfection while ignoring commodity inflation, consumer stress, and the Fed's constrained position — the setup for second-half 2026 is more fragile than equity valuations suggest. The specific risks are non-linear: Walsh surprising with balance sheet reduction, oil staying elevated post-war, and auto delinquency contagion into consumer discretionary are individually plausible and potentially reinforcing.

China's competitive positioning in AI and technology infrastructure is the most underappreciated long-term risk Boockvar raises. The global AI model market will face pricing pressure from multiple directions, compressing the terminal value assumptions currently embedded in U.S. AI stock prices. Investors focused solely on the domestic buildout narrative are not adequately pricing this.
