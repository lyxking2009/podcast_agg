# The Chopping Block: Ethereum's Identity Crisis, Apostates Speak Out

## Key Points
- David Hoffman, Bankless co-founder, announced he sold all of his ETH, marking a symbolic end to the "ETH is money" thesis he championed
- At least eight Ethereum Foundation senior members departed in 2025–2026, including Tim Beiko, Carl Beekhuizen, Josh Stark, Julian Ma, Barnaby Buswell, and Trent Van Epps
- Haseeb Qureshi proposed the "Microsoft of Crypto" thesis: Ethereum is entrenched and enterprise-beloved but slow-moving, potentially fumbling new categories as Microsoft fumbled mobile
- Max Resnick argued Solana has better technological trajectories and developer adoption, especially for on-chain securities and post-quantum cryptography readiness
- Tarun Chitra raised concern that Ethereum no longer inspires younger developers — the ecosystem is "selling the olds, not the youngs"
- Regulatory clarity is attracting "weak crypto" (enterprise blockchain) rather than Ethereum's core "strong crypto" identity
- Layer-two fee compression has gutted ETH value capture: "layer two margins are at 98%" and the fat protocol thesis is being displaced by fat app dynamics
- Vitalik Buterin's outsized influence is seen as preventing the decentralized leadership succession Ethereum needs
- A proposed "second foundation" for adoption-focused work was discussed but noted as lacking EF legitimacy — compared to "making a new government for the U.S."
- Protocol ossification is increasingly viewed as a safety feature rather than a path to competitive progress

## Overview
This Chopping Block episode served as an extended post-mortem on Ethereum's identity and competitive position, with Bankless co-founder David Hoffman appearing as a guest alongside regular hosts Haseeb Qureshi, Tarun Chitra, and Tom Schmidt, plus Anza lead economist Max Resnick. The episode opened with Hoffman's dramatic disclosure that he no longer holds any ETH, framing the conversation as a genuine reckoning rather than routine punditry. Hoffman explained that while the "ETH is money" thesis achieved meaningful success since 2020, structural obstacles — chiefly the layer-two fee compression problem and the rise of fat-app dynamics — have stalled momentum for ETH the asset even as Ethereum the network may continue to grow.

The mass departure of senior Ethereum Foundation researchers and contributors provided the backdrop for the "apostates" framing. The panel catalogued the exits of Tim Beiko, Carl Beekhuizen, Josh Stark, Julian Ma, Trent Van Epps, Barnaby Buswell, and others, noting that five of the departures occurred in May 2026 alone. Vitalik Buterin responded by clarifying that the EF's mission is "making Ethereum impressive" through technology, not driving asset price appreciation — a statement the panel read as philosophically coherent but strategically tone-deaf given the competitive environment.

The discussion repeatedly circled back to identity fragmentation. Ethereum has cycled through "world computer," "ultrasound money," and "sanctuary tech" narratives without consolidating on a single compelling thesis. Max Resnick argued this narrative whiplash, combined with a lack of meaningful technological progress relative to competitors, has made it harder to recruit new builders. Haseeb's Microsoft analogy offered a more optimistic read: Microsoft lost mobile and the web but remained deeply embedded in enterprise infrastructure and eventually reinvented itself. The question is whether there is a "Satya Nadella" figure capable of executing that transformation for Ethereum — and the panel was not confident one exists or would be permitted to lead.

## Implications
If the Ethereum Foundation's mission is genuinely confined to protocol correctness and censorship resistance, the ecosystem faces a coordination gap: no organization has the legitimacy and resources to drive adoption-focused work at scale. The proposed "second foundation" addresses this conceptually, but without the EF's imprimatur it risks fragmentation. Ethereum's near-term fate may hinge less on any single technical upgrade and more on whether the community can resolve this governance vacuum before competitors like Solana consolidate the developer and institutional pipelines that Ethereum built.

The Bankless co-founder's ETH sale carries symbolic weight beyond the individual position. When the most prominent ETH-native media franchise publicly abandons the asset thesis, it signals that the narrative infrastructure sustaining retail demand is under stress. Combined with EF departures and layer-two fee compression, the episode maps a scenario where Ethereum's network utility grows while ETH the asset struggles to translate that growth into price appreciation.
