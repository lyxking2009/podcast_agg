# Building an AI Guardian for Enterprise with Onyx Security CEO Maxim Bar Kogan

## Key Points
- Onyx Security launched in March 2026 with $40 million from Conviction Partners and Cyberstarts after 18 months in stealth — reaching 70 employees and Fortune 500 customers before any public announcement.
- The founding question: "When something smarter than you is responsible for the things you depend on, how do you stay in control?"
- Existing enterprise security tools were built for deterministic software and human workflows — they are fundamentally insufficient for non-deterministic AI agents operating autonomously across systems and data.
- Onyx's platform discovers all AI assets across an enterprise (agents, models, AI-powered apps) including unsanctioned "shadow AI," then enforces governance policies at runtime.
- The Guardian Agent is a supervisory AI that monitors other agents in real time and can block unsafe actions, require human approval, narrow an agent's scope, or redirect it before anything reaches a downstream system.
- At launch, Onyx reported securing 137,000+ agents, covering 593,000+ employees, and analyzing 10 million+ agent sessions.
- The platform covers observability, security, governance, orchestration, and ROI measurement in a single control plane — replacing a fragmented stack of point solutions.
- Maxim Bar Kogan is a Unit 8200 veteran who won first prize in Israel's 2008 CodeGuru competition; co-founder Gil Elbaz previously led agentic AI research at Nvidia.
- Sarah Guo's Conviction Partners being both investor and No Priors co-host means this episode is Onyx's highest-visibility founder platform since launch.
- Shadow AI (agents deployed without IT or security awareness) is the enterprise version of shadow IT — but with far greater blast radius because agents can take consequential actions across systems.

## Overview
Maxim Bar Kogan joined No Priors co-host Sarah Guo to discuss the founding of Onyx Security and the company's thesis that the agentic era has created a fundamental governance gap in enterprise security. The conversation begins with Bar Kogan's central provocation: as AI agents take over tasks previously done by employees — accessing sensitive data, triggering workflows, making consequential decisions — the enterprise has lost visibility into what is actually happening inside its own walls. Traditional security tools were designed for deterministic software that follows predictable paths; AI agents operating on language models do not behave predictably, and existing infrastructure was not built for that.

Onyx's solution is a control plane sitting between the enterprise and its AI agents — a supervisory layer that discovers what agents exist (including those deployed without IT approval), enforces identity and data-access policies, and monitors behavior continuously. The Guardian Agent, the platform's flagship capability, acts as an AI-native overseer: it watches other agents and intervenes in real time by blocking, redirecting, or escalating to humans when behavior falls outside defined boundaries. This is meaningfully different from perimeter security or post-hoc auditing.

Bar Kogan's background gives Onyx credibility on the security side — Unit 8200, Israel's elite intelligence unit, has produced much of the global cybersecurity startup ecosystem. Co-founder Gil Elbaz brings AI depth from his time leading agentic research at Nvidia's CTO office. The combination positions Onyx as one of the few teams that can credibly build at the intersection of security operations and AI systems engineering. The episode also touches on market timing: enterprises are deploying agents faster than any governance framework can track, creating an urgency that allowed Onyx to reach Fortune 500 deployments before its public launch.

## Implications
The emergence of AI agent governance as a distinct security category represents a significant market shift and opportunity. As enterprises move from AI assistants to autonomous agents with write access to production systems, the blast radius of a compromised or misbehaving agent expands dramatically. Onyx's framing — that existing security tools are categorically insufficient for non-deterministic AI — points to a wave of security stack rearchitecting at large enterprises.

For AI builders and platform teams, the episode underscores that agent governance cannot be an afterthought. Companies that embed governance infrastructure into agent frameworks from the start will have a structural advantage in enterprise sales, where security reviews are increasingly a deployment gating mechanism. The race between agent deployment speed and governance tooling maturity is the central tension Bar Kogan is betting on.
