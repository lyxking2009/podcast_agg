# Tokenizing Latin America: Six Currencies, One Financial System | Sebastian Serrano — Bankless

## TL;DR
Sebastian Serrano, co-founder and CEO of Latin American crypto fintech Ripio, joins David Hoffman to argue that the next phase of stablecoin growth is not more dollar tokens but local-currency tokens. The case: people need to pay bills, borrow and settle contracts in their own currency, and a dollar stablecoin does not solve that.

## Key points
- Serrano's claim is that local-currency stablecoins - 'wrapped fiat' - are the next large market, because the dollar token solves savings and remittances but not domestic payments, payroll and credit.
- Ripio's moat is regulatory rather than technical: a decade of licence-building across six Latin American countries, which is why it can issue and redeem local-currency tokens with banking rails behind them.
- Its business is increasingly B2B 'crypto-as-a-service', providing the crypto and stablecoin plumbing behind Mercado Libre and Argentine brokers rather than serving only retail users.
- The 'six currencies, one financial system' framing: each local token needs on/off-ramps, FX and liquidity, and the interesting economics arrive when those corridors connect so value can move across borders without touching the dollar.
- Cross-border payments and remittances are the beachhead use case; DeFi lending against local-currency collateral and FX/carry trades are the extension.
- Multi-chain EVM deployment is treated as table stakes - the constraint is licences and liquidity, not the chain.
- Velocity and float are the open questions: if local tokens are used for payments rather than holding, the float is smaller and the business looks more like payments infrastructure than like a stablecoin issuer.
- Hoffman's pushback thread: dollar stablecoins already have network effects and liquidity; local-currency tokens must win on utility for people who are paid and taxed in pesos, reais or bolivars.
