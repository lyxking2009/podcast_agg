# This Is How New York City Takes Out Its Trash — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Tracy Alloway and Joe Weisenthal spend an hour inside New York City's Department of Sanitation with Deputy Commissioner Joshua Goodman, treating trash collection as a logistics and industrial-policy problem rather than a political one. The through-line: New York is mid-way through the largest change to residential waste handling in decades - on-street containerization - and the constraints are trucks, bins, curb space and compliance rather than ideology.

## Key points
- Goodman rejects the famous LaGuardia line that there is no Democratic or Republican way to pick up the garbage: any large public-space logistical program is inevitably shaped by ideology, and the binary framing badly undersells the planning required.
- Tracy describes the DSNY as one of the last services untouched by neoliberalism - a fully public, in-house operation - which is what makes it an interesting case study in state capacity.
- New York's core problem is structural: there are no driveways or garages, so bags go on the curb, and cars, bikes and scaffolding sit between the truck and the waste. Containerization is the response.
- The city is running the first on-street containerization program in North America, rolling out wheeled bins (including the large 'Empire' bins for bigger buildings) with rear-tipper compatibility.
- Because no residential automated side-loader existed that was suitable for New York's streets, DSNY had to help design a new truck. Industry experts estimated five years to design from scratch, and European trucks could not simply be imported because of different emissions standards.
- For small buildings workers can tip bins manually or use a rear tipper; for large buildings the whole collection workflow and truck design had to change.
- Enforcement is part of the rollout: from September, buildings that leave trash on the ground rather than in containers are subject to summonses - a behaviour-change lever as much as a sanitation rule.
- Goodman frames the work as long-horizon capital planning in a system where the public notices only failure: if the trash is not picked up, it is not picked up.
