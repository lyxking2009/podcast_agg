# #228 - Rupert Lowe - Is Britain Going Bankrupt? — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR
Rupert Lowe, MP for Great Yarmouth and leader of Restore Britain - the party he founded after leaving Reform UK - returns with a blunt fiscal diagnosis: Britain is bankrupt, the state no longer serves the people who pay for it, and none of the main parties will admit how bad the numbers are.

## Key points
- Lowe's central claim is arithmetic rather than ideological: the debt burden works out at roughly GBP 100,000 of legacy borrowing per household being handed to the next generation, and no party's manifesto acknowledges the scale.
- He argues the state has grown into a force bigger than any elected government - an apparatus with its own institutional interests that survives changes of administration and frustrates ministerial direction.
- On the inquiry and the 'secret deal' accusations: Lowe addresses being summoned, the media's treatment of Restore Britain, and his account of the fallout with Reform UK and an olive branch that went nowhere.
- Policy specifics he puts on the table: tax, energy costs, the triple lock and welfare - with welfare reform framed as the only line item large enough to change the fiscal trajectory.
- He makes the case that the electorate still does not understand the scale of the debt, which is why fiscal honesty is politically punished and why the adjustment, when it comes, will be abrupt rather than chosen.
- On Europe: he argues Andy Burnham may make rejoining the EU his last throw of the dice, and predicts the euro cannot survive in its current form.
- Framing of the episode: the diagnosis is 'the state is bigger than the government', and the bet is that the Conservative party cannot be the vehicle for the correction - hence Restore Britain.
- McCormack pushes on the mechanics - what Restore would actually cut, in what order, and what happens to the bond market if it does not.
