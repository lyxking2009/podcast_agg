# Why Are Employers Dropping GLP-1 Coverage? — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR

Bloomberg Businessweek’s workplace reporter Taylor Nicole Rogers explains the unprecedented step employers are taking to contain health-care inflation: carving out an entire drug category — GLP-1s — from coverage, leaving roughly one in ten Americans managing obesity and diabetes to pay out of pocket or switch to compounded alternatives.

## Key points

- This is the first time an entire category of drugs has been carved out of employer coverage: economists Rogers spoke to said usually when one drug is too expensive employers offer an alternative — this time workers are simply told they are out of luck.
- The cost driver: an employer can pay $500–$700 per month for a single employee’s GLP-1 treatment, at a moment when about 1 in 10 Americans are on these drugs and employers are already straining to fund health benefits.
- Scale of employer health spend: roughly $18,000–$19,000 per employee on average, with the average price increase now about 6.7% — so a $100,000 salary actually costs about $120,000-plus once health benefits and taxes are included.
- ACA marketplace buyers already absorbed this pain a year earlier when tax subsidies expired; employer-insured workers are now getting a taste of the same dynamic because health-care spending is up across the board.
- One worker Rogers interviewed paid $25 a month under her employer plan; after coverage was dropped she pays about $300 a month for a compounded version — cheaper, but not FDA-approved. Branded drugs with manufacturer affordability programmes still run upwards of $300 a month in most cases.
- The prevention math is unsettled: Bank of America and others say they are happy to spend tens of millions hoping to avoid future spending, but a study out last year found employers were spending more because patients return for follow-up monitoring, erasing the avoided-cost savings.
- The human cost is stark: in union surveys, one member said they were choosing between groceries for the family and staying on their medicine; another said they might take a spouse or child off the plan to stay on the drug.
- GLP-1s are not the only budget-breaker — expensive immunotherapies and cancer treatments can raise premiums if just one or two employees are on them, which is why workers ask why GLP-1s became the corner drug to crack down on.
- No employer has adopted the obvious compromise — a separate GLP-1 add-on plan, like dental or vision — because in this job market employers do not feel they need creative retention tools.
