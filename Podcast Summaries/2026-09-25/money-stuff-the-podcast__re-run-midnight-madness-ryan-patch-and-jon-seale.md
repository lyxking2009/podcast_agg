# Re-run: Midnight Madness: Ryan Patch and Jon Seale — Money Stuff: The Podcast

## TL;DR

A re-run of Matt Levine and Katie Greifeld’s May 2025 conversation with Ryan Patch and Jon Seale, who run Midnight Madness — the overnight puzzle hunt that sweeps New York’s financial district — re-aired ahead of the October 10 event. It is a craft interview about designing puzzles hard enough to feel transformative but not annoying enough to make teams rage-quit.

## Key points

- Midnight Madness is a location-based overnight puzzle hunt: teams travel from place to place, solve a complex puzzle that yields a keyword or the next location, and repeat — described as ‘the Amazing Race plus an escape room, run overnight at 3am’.
- Patch and Seale are outside hires into the Midnight Madness tradition — they had never played it and are not from the finance industry, so they had to reverse-engineer what counts as a puzzle in this format.
- Their working definition of a puzzle: it must involve a logical leap — a mental connection between two different types of information. A ‘task’, by contrast, is just an action you complete, which is what separates puzzle hunts from scavenger hunts.
- Worked example from a past event (designed by Josh Stabiner): a rap sheet that looks like a police description of seven suspects, each with what appears to be a fingerprint — the fingerprint is actually a topographic map, and the seven suspects encode the seven highest peaks on each continent, yielding a final keyword.
- On difficulty calibration, which the hosts call the number-one skill of a puzzle designer: aim for the moment where teams are ‘just about to quit’ and then get the spark — the result should feel, in hindsight, like the only possible answer.
- Patch’s framing of that payoff is ‘second-degree fun’: miserable in the moment, pleasurable in retrospect — the design problem is manufacturing that specific arc at scale.
- The re-run is timed to the next Midnight Madness, on October 10.
