# Why So Many Public Companies Are Saying No to Tokenized Stocks — Unchained

## TL;DR
Matt Hougan of Bitwise explains why dozens of public companies are opting out of tokenized stock trading: corporate committees see downside risk without upside, so they let others pioneer. He argues tokenization will ultimately happen from the edges—international investors and 24/7 traders—and discusses the NYSE-Avalanche deal, ICE's multiple blockchain bets, Hyperliquid's Amazon-like expansion, and an optimistic outlook for regulatory convergence.

## Key points
- Public companies are rejecting tokenized stock trading because corporate committees exist to say no; they can identify downside risks like pricing anomalies and lawsuits, but see no upside to being a pioneer, and opting out is costless since other pioneers will do the dirty work.
- Long-term, all stocks will be tokenized, trade 24/7/365, and settle on public blockchains, but adoption will start at the edges—people internationally who lack good access to US stocks and traders wanting 24/7 or offshore execution—not mainstream US retail investors.
- The NYSE selected Avalanche as the ledger for its upcoming 24/7 tokenized stock venue expected online later this year; Matt Hougan praised the Avalanche team as one of the hardest working and most business-focused in crypto.
- Regulatory clarity from the SEC is directionally sufficient—equal rights, dividends, and simple vehicles—so the main remaining factor is passage of time and pioneers demonstrating benefits, not waiting for a specific regulatory event.
- The NYSE-Avalanche deal is positive for the AVAX token because continued business wins keep Avalanche in the cohort of assets likely to perform well in a broad crypto bull market, unlike some alt L1s that risk being cast aside.
- ICE is placing multiple on-chain bets—NYSE-Avalanche, OKX ICE on X Layer, Trade XYZ, and Hyperliquid—because it is technology-forward and sees crypto, tokenization, and stablecoins as the future of finance; multiple shots on goal are smart corporate strategy given market uncertainty.
- Hyperliquid is compared to Amazon: it started with crypto-based perpetuals and is expanding into real-world assets, prediction markets, and an open-source platform, building a financial super app and juggernaut with ambition to become one of the largest financial entities in the world.
- On the risk of a US-only vs. rest-of-world financial internet split, Hougan is optimistic that permissioned pools, automated compliance, and privacy-preserving identity will create a largely frictionless global system, with only a tiny piracy corner, though legislative action on DeFi protocols remains uncertain.
- Institutional interest in Trade XYZ is high only among the top 1% of tech-forward wealth managers; the other 99% cannot distinguish Trade XYZ from Hyperliquid, but the cutting edge sees it as one of the most exciting developments in the world.
