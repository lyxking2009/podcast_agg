## TL;DR
Brad Gerstner, founder of Altimeter Capital, and Geoffrey Canada, founder of Harlem Children's Zone, discuss Invest America, the program that now provides nearly 70 million children with savings accounts and gives every American newborn $1,000 in the S&P 500. The initial challenge was that free money is surprisingly hard to give away, with many families not claiming accounts. Gerstner and Canada argue that even small nest eggs, with early compounding, can rebuild faith in capitalism and form the basis of a new social contract in an AI-driven economy where every child owns a piece of the upside.

## Key points
- Brad Gerstner first pitched Invest America to Patrick O'Shaughnessy in 2020; Patrick initially thought it was a fool's errand, but six years later nearly 70 million children have accounts in their name.
- Geoffrey Canada spent decades helping disadvantaged kids get a great education through Harlem Children's Zone, but he argues that education alone isn't enough to achieve the American dream without savings.
- Every American newborn receives $1,000 in the S&P 500 through Invest America, but the current challenge is getting families to claim their accounts because free money is surprisingly hard to give away.
- Gerstner and Canada believe that even a small nest egg, when given a head start on compounding, can help rebuild faith in capitalism.
- As AI reshapes the economy, they view these children's savings accounts as the basis of a new social contract built on ownership and participation, where every child owns a piece of the upside.
- The episode covers the mechanics of how the accounts work, the difficulty of giving money away, winning over both political parties, program durability, financial education at scale, and lessons from Geoffrey Canada's 50 years fighting poverty.
