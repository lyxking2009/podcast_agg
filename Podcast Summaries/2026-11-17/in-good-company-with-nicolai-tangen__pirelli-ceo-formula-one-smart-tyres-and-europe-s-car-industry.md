# Pirelli CEO: Formula One, Smart Tyres and Europe’s Car Industry — In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen

## TL;DR
Andrea Casalucci, CEO of Pirelli, discusses the centrality of tires to vehicle safety and performance, from Formula One to EVs, and Pirelli's high-performance strategy. He explains how F1 serves as a virtual-development laboratory, the company's "cyber tire" sensor technology for autonomous driving, and its response to Chinese competition and Europe's industrial-policy gaps. He also outlines a $1B US plant expansion and leadership lessons centered on passion and trust.

## Key points
- Tires are the sole contact point between vehicle and road, making them critical for safety, braking distance, handling, and comfort; EVs add weight, torque, and silence, increasing demands for load index, grip, and noise reduction.
- Pirelli uses virtualization, mathematical modeling, and AI to design tire patterns and compounds, reducing time-to-market by 30% and producing physical prototypes only after simulations meet carmaker targets.
- Tire production involves chemicals and compounds, building a green cover, curing in presses to 'cook' the tire, and finishing, with increasing connectivity and automation across all stages.
- Natural rubber is sourced from equatorial regions, so European tire manufacturing depends on imports of this key raw material.
- Pirelli supplies high-end car brands including Ferrari, Lamborghini, Bentley, McLaren, Aston Martin, Porsche, Rolls-Royce, and Maserati, competes in 350 motorsport events annually, has been sole Formula 1 tire supplier since 2011, and will become sole MotoGP supplier next year.
- Pirelli's strategy focuses exclusively on consumer tires for high-value segments, with 82% of turnover from high-performance tires (close to 90% in Europe) to protect against low-cost Asian competition.
- Formula One acts as an 'open air laboratory': extreme time-to-market pressure and limited physical testing forced Pirelli to develop tires virtually, and that know-how transferred to everyday tire development.
- Formula One tires must balance durability and performance varying by circuit, with safety paramount; a fresh tire is about predictability rather than simply being faster.
- The 'cyber tire' uses embedded sensors to collect data on asphalt conditions, driving style, and tire state, then algorithms convert them into information to assist braking systems and improve safety and comfort.
- Cyber tire is already on the market for high-performance cars from McLaren, Aston Martin, and Pagani, and is essential for autonomous driving where a grip estimator provides real-time data for software-based steering and braking decisions.
- Europe's car industry retains strengths—100% of prestige car brands manufacture in Europe, with leading design, vehicle dynamics, and quality—but must accelerate innovation and factory automation, digitization, and electrification to compete with Chinese premium makers.
- Europe set electrification targets via the Green Deal but lacked a supporting industrial policy: incentives, charging infrastructure, and clarity; Norway achieved over 90% EV new registrations due to consistent policy, unlike Europe's confusion over technological neutrality.
- Chinese tire competition is tougher for European manufacturers, who face imported raw materials, duties, and trade-down from Chinese brands; Pirelli responded by focusing on high-performance tires where competition is lower and price power higher.
- Pirelli's China operations evolved from a low-cost manufacturing hub into an R&D hub; Chinese premium carmakers demand low rolling resistance at low temperatures, comfort, and very fast time-to-market, prompting Pirelli to adapt development processes.
- In supplier tenders, European suppliers took two months to return an NDA while a Chinese supplier returned a physical prototype, illustrating cultural speed driven by lean organizations, lack of legacy, result-driven mindset, and fast decision-making.
- The US remains the largest high-value tire market at roughly 40% of the global high-value segment; Pirelli is investing to scale its Rome, Georgia plant from under 500,000 to 6 million tires annually in 7 years to improve resilience against tariffs, exchange rates, and logistics.
- Casalucci joined Pirelli as an apprentice in 2002 and became CEO in 2023; his leadership principles emphasize trust, listening, collaborative decision-making, and learning from mistakes.
- He advises young people to be passionate and learn from mistakes; personally he aims to balance work with family, after previously over-prioritizing work.
