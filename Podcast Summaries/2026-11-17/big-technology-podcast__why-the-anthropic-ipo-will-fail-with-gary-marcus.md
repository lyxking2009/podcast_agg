# Why The Anthropic IPO Will Fail — With Gary Marcus — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Gary Marcus argues that Anthropic's IPO valuation of up to $1.4 trillion is unsustainable because the company has never been profitable, carries nearly $900 billion in future liabilities, and faces slowing revenue growth and intense competition. He credits Anthropic for adopting neurosymbolic AI in Claude Code but says the economics remain poor, predicting eventual bankruptcy or acquisition at a discount. Host Alex Kantrowitz pushes back with oversubscription reports, compute efficiency gains, and product expansion, but Marcus remains bearish.

## Key points
- Gary Marcus calls Anthropic a bad business: despite rapid revenue growth, it has never made a profit and has nearly $900 billion in future liabilities, making a $1.4 trillion valuation 'lunacy' and likely to end in bankruptcy or acquisition at a discount.
- Anthropic's quarterly revenue growth collapsed from 147% in Q2 2025 to 47% in Q3 2025, as the token maxing fad faded and Chinese open-weight competitors like DeepSeek exerted price pressure, undermining projections of continued exponential growth.
- Marcus argues that the cost of improving AI models is rising exponentially while capability gains diminish; each incremental benchmark improvement now costs billions more, and Anthropic's only niche is top-end models for hard problems, which is a narrow and moving target.
- Market sentiment toward AI has cooled: OpenAI's last raise came in below ask at a $1.25 trillion valuation instead of $1.4 trillion, SoftBank posted a $7 billion loss, and Nvidia underperformed Krispy Kreme over the past year.
- Anthropic has concentration risk: five customers made up 19% of revenue, and major clients like Microsoft, Meta, and Goldman Sachs are pulling back or restricting usage, which undermines the rocket-ship growth needed to cover liabilities.
- Marcus acknowledges that Anthropic has abandoned pure scaling and embraced neurosymbolic AI, as seen in Claude Code's use of conditionals, loops, tools, and external verification, marking a victory for his long-held position but not fixing the business model.
- Host counterpoint: Bloomberg reports the Anthropic IPO is multiple times oversubscribed, and Nvidia plans a $10 billion investment, suggesting strong demand despite fundamental concerns.
- Anthropic's compute efficiency improved dramatically, from $0.40 of revenue per $1 of compute invested in Q1 2025 to $1.72 in Q3 2026, but Marcus argues competitors achieve similar efficiencies, so prices fall and profits remain elusive.
- Marcus says Chinese open-weight models have been aided by distillation and Jensen Huang's advocacy against export controls; however, Huang's scandal may empower China hawks, potentially restricting Chinese model access in the US and reducing competitive pressure on Anthropic.
- Anthropic's customers with over $100,000 in annual revenue grew from 950 to 11,900 in a year, indicating broadening enterprise adoption, but Marcus counters that many studies show lack of ROI and large customers are cutting back.
- Anthropic is expanding from a model provider to a product company with Claude Code, Co-work, Design, and new Sheets, directly competing with Microsoft and Google; Marcus says products must be hallucination-free and rivals may replicate them quickly.
- Marcus predicts AGI will not arrive this decade, citing missing core discoveries around world models and continuous learning, and warns that AI safety has made no substantive progress, which should delay AGI pursuit.
