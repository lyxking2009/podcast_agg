# Damn it, Jensen! With JC Parets — The Compound and Friends


## TL;DR
JC Parets joins The Compound to break down Nvidia's 23% crash after CEO Jensen Huang stepped down amid an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Despite the scandal, Parets and Josh Brown argue the broad market remains bullish, with sector rotation, international all-time highs, and extreme bearish sentiment signaling further upside. They highlight opportunities in AMD, housing stocks, and beaten-down precious metals, while using historical magazine covers as contrarian signals.

## Key points
- Nvidia stock is down 23% in 10 days, worst 10-day drop in four years, putting it in the bottom 2% of all rolling 10-day periods since IPO; it has lost around $1 trillion in market cap, falling below $4 trillion valuation.
- Jensen Huang has stepped down as CEO amid revelations of an affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy; concerns include potential blackmail and unauthorized chip sales to China, with 18% of Nvidia's fiscal 2025 sales coming from Singapore, up from 8.5%.
- Josh Brown holds Nvidia due to extremely low cost basis after 12 years, while Stephanie Link sold citing opportunity cost (stock could be trapped for months), and Malcolm sold because political investigations could drag on for a year; all reasons are valid given different time horizons.
- The stock now trades at 10x forward earnings, less than half the market multiple, with expected earnings growth of 95% this year and 70% next year; Josh argues there is no 'Jensen premium' because the stock has been cheap relative to tech peers for three years.
- JC Parets notes that Nvidia's addition to the Dow at $5 trillion market cap, Jensen's autographing women's chests, and Economist cover 'The Sorcerer of Silicon' were classic late-cycle signals; he also highlights individual investor sentiment at 51.4% bears as contrarian bullish.
- Sector rotation is the lifeblood of a bull market: over last six weeks, tech fell 12% while staples, healthcare, consumer discretionary, financials, and materials rose; previously tech was the best performer, showing rotation back and forth.
- AMD has outperformed Nvidia dramatically over 12 months, up 123% versus Nvidia's 15%, and has become a trillion-dollar stock; Broadcom and AMD rose 5% on the day of Nvidia's drop, indicating investors want AI exposure but shifting away from Nvidia.
- Consumer discretionary stocks are at all-time highs when including Apple, Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Delta, United, Walmart, etc.; JC argues the official index excludes Apple, which is the biggest consumer product, so his custom index better reflects consumer strength.
- Existing home sales fell to a 16-year low in October, down 5.5% monthly and 8.8% year-over-year, yet homebuilder stocks like PulteGroup and Toll Brothers are near all-time highs; JC suggests if they break out, a housing boom may be starting despite bearish sentiment.
- The S&P bank index has broken above its 2007 pre-GFC highs, a 20-year milestone; JC says the bear market case requires this chart to fail, as banks are a forward-looking indicator.
- Precious metals are crashing: gold down almost a third since January, silver cut in half, palladium crashing; JC sees this as a generational opportunity, noting silver's long-term base breakout retest and gold stocks like Newmont breaking out.
- Historical magazine covers serve as contrarian indicators: examples include The Economist 'How low can it go?' on dollar at 1995 bottom, 'The world over a barrel' on oil at 1979 top, and Bitcoin magazine cover at 2021 top; JC brought physical copies as props.
- European and international markets are at all-time highs: FTSE 350, Germany DAX, Euro Stoxx 600, Switzerland, Sweden, Poland, Italy, Ireland, Spain, Greece, Canada, and Australia; indicating global bull market breadth.
