# Special Report: The Fall Of Jensen Huang & What Comes Next — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang was ousted after revelations of at least three extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Wang, a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, and another with an employee, alongside evidence he allegedly helped funnel restricted H100 and H200 chips to China via Singapore-based MegaSpeed. The board named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO and began a succession search. The episode examines national-security, legal, competitive, and market implications, including Nvidia's eroding CUDA moat and Anthropic's rapid onboarding of AMD GPUs.

## Key points
- Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Wang, identified by some US officials as a Chinese intelligence asset, and another with a direct-report employee. The board installed CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and launched a search considering internal and external candidates.
- Alice Wang allegedly helped move 240,000 banned NVIDIA AI GPUs, mostly H100 and H200, into China during a three-year relationship with Huang, involving over $7.5 billion in imports. Reports indicate she operated from Singapore through MegaSpeed International and would intervene to resolve supply-chain delays by citing her close ties to NVIDIA.
- Nvidia altered its geographic revenue reporting methodology in October 2025 after blacklisting MegaSpeed, switching from customer headquarters to billing location. This reclassified the bulk of Singapore billed revenue as US-related while revising China and Taiwan revenue higher, raising concerns about a cover-up of export-control circumvention.
- Will DePue, a former OpenAI employee, published forensic evidence that top Chinese AI labs DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax, and Ant Group built their newest models on Nvidia's most advanced Blackwell chips, which are barred from export to China, indicating widespread circumvention of US export controls.
- Legal and political accountability is likely to continue despite Huang's resignation. The episode notes potential violations of the Espionage Act and federal trade restrictions, with bipartisan criticism from figures like Elizabeth Warren and Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and the possibility of congressional hearings and federal investigations.
- Nvidia's business faced headwinds even before the scandal: major cloud providers were reportedly running away from Nvidia and planning alternative chips. Jensen Huang himself described committed chip wafer and memory supplies coming at him 'like an avalanche' in an internal meeting, suggesting demand may not be infinite.
- Anthropic's IPO roadshow revealed that with its AI model Mythos, the company onboarded AMD GPUs in about three days, compared to the previous six to twelve months required. This suggests Nvidia's CUDA software moat may be eroding as AI accelerates cross-platform compatibility.
- Huang's ouster may benefit US AI labs OpenAI and Anthropic by removing the most effective advocate for shipping advanced chips to China and potentially reducing Chinese AI competitiveness. The episode notes Chinese models accounted for 57-67% of OpenRouter usage in a September week, and limiting chip flows could shift that balance.
- Nvidia faces succession uncertainty. Huang had no formal succession plan and ran a flat structure with 60 direct reports. External candidates like Arm CEO Rene Haas have been floated, but the episode suggests the complexity of the scandal may make external hires hesitant, possibly leading to an internal promotion like Ian Buck.
- The stock market reaction has been mixed: Nvidia's stock was heavily hit during the week, but the S&P 500 was up, indicating the broader market has not fully priced in the implications. The coming Anthropic IPO is expected to provide a short-term boost, but the longer-term effects on the AI trade remain uncertain.
