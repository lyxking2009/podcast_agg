# Investing in Financial Security: Masters in Business with Salim Ramji — Masters in Business


## TL;DR
Salim Ramji, CEO of Vanguard, discusses his path from law and consulting to leading Vanguard, emphasizing the firm's client-owned structure, relentless fee cuts, and cultural faithfulness. He highlights Vanguard's technology overhaul, fixed income capabilities, and the launch of a revived Flagship advisor service. Ramji also details the Altruist acquisition as a solution to the advice access gap, and outlines Vanguard's cautious, cost-focused approach to private markets.

## Key points
- Ramji's upbringing in an immigrant family running a grocery store shaped his understanding of financial security; he experienced both having it and its absence, which now informs his mission at Vanguard.
- He began his career as a lawyer at Clifford Chance in London and Hong Kong, then spent about 12 years at McKinsey advising financial institutions before joining BlackRock in 2014 as head of corporate strategy.
- At BlackRock, Ramji eventually led U.S. Wealth Advisory and then iShares and Index Investing, overseeing roughly $3 trillion, where he learned the importance of maintaining investment resiliency during market dislocations like March 2020.
- As the first outsider CEO of Vanguard, he spent his first months meeting crew across sites, discovering a pervasive but unwritten culture; for the 50th anniversary, Vanguard engaged 4,000 crew to distill cultural tenets and behaviors from Bogle's memos.
- Ramji cut fees on 53 Vanguard funds, creating about $250 million in annual investor savings; Vanguard's asset-weighted average expense ratio is approximately 0.06% on nearly $12.5 trillion in client assets, enabled by the client-owned mutual structure.
- He discovered Vanguard's active fixed income performance was extraordinary and that the firm started active fixed income in 1981; he allocated more marketing and client resources to highlight low-cost active fixed income alongside index offerings.
- Vanguard completed a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar technology migration from legacy mainframes to a unified cloud-native platform for self-directed clients, improving resilience and enabling AI, web, and mobile enhancements.
- Vanguard relaunched its Flagship advisor service on October 15, providing dedicated advisors and better cash rates, and reinvested in licensed professionals for non-flagship clients to improve client service after past outages.
- Ramji emphasized the advice access gap: only one in five Americans has access to a fee-based advisor, and the ratio worsens as advisors retire; Vanguard's research (Advisor's Alpha) suggests a good fiduciary advisor can add 200-250 basis points annually by preventing behavioral mistakes.
- The Altruist acquisition (Vanguard was a Series A investor) is intended to close the advice gap by using Altruist's technology, including Hazel, to help independent advisors serve more clients; Ramji sees it as a rare cultural and strategic fit, not a departure from Vanguard's organic growth.
- Vanguard applies a 'does it load' test to new products, comparing them to the efficient frontier of index 60/40, 70/30, or 80/20 portfolios; most products fail, but private markets can load for high-net-worth investors if fees are low and transparency is high.
- Ramji reiterated Vanguard's cash flow test: investments must generate cash flow or have the prospect of it, excluding crypto, meme stocks, prediction markets, and gold/commodities, which he considers speculation masquerading as investment.
