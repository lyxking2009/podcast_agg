# Loser Mentality — Money Stuff: The Podcast


## TL;DR
Matt Levine and Mary Giles discuss NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang's admission of an affair with Alice Wang, who is linked to a Singaporean neocloud investigated for export-control violations, wiping about $400 billion off NVIDIA's market cap and triggering board investigations and shareholder lawsuits. They also cover Anthropic's oversubscribed IPO, its half-hearted switch from 'AI' to 'SI' to appease the Trump administration, and new research showing chatbots push retail investors toward index funds.

## Key points
- NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an affair with Alice Wang, a figure linked to Megaspeed, a Singaporean neocloud investigated for violating US chip export controls; the admission followed weeks of calling the relationship 'normal' and erased roughly $400 billion from NVIDIA's market cap, reviving calls for stricter export restrictions and prompting the board to hire Wachtell Lipton for an investigation.
- Matt Levine frames the $400 billion market-cap drop as a clean causal event: unlike historical analogies such as Antony and Cleopatra, the stock-price decline directly reflects the increased probability of export restrictions or frontier pacing concerns tied to Huang's relationship, making it possibly the most expensive extramarital affair in history.
- Anthropic launched its IPO marketing at a conservative $70-$78 per share range, implying a $1.4 trillion valuation, and was multiple times oversubscribed; the deal includes $26 billion of anchor investments from chipmakers and cloud providers like NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, TSMC, and Google, which Levine notes saves hundreds of millions in fees and supports the circular AI ecosystem.
- Offshore pre-IPO perpetual futures on Hyperliquid traded Anthropic at about $105 per share and a $1.8 trillion market cap, roughly 30% above the high end of the IPO range; Levine suggests these markets could become a real-time indicator of where IPOs should price, complementing the traditional walk-up strategy.
- Anthropic's updated S-1 prospectus randomly replaced about half the instances of 'AI' with 'SI' (superintelligence) to align with the Trump administration's preferred terminology, leaving both terms used interchangeably; Levine criticizes the half-finished search-and-replace as both capitulating and sloppy, especially in the founder letter which remains image-based and says 'AI'.
- New research by NYU PhD student Valerie Baldinger using data from a wealth management platform that introduced a ChatGPT/Gemini assistant in November 2024 found the chatbot consistently recommended low-cost index funds, leading users to shift purchases toward funds, improving performance and reducing risk; when the platform temporarily altered the system prompt to encourage ticker-specific stock recommendations, explicit buy recommendations rose from 18% to 33% and fund share of purchases shifted by 8 percentage points.
- The episode discusses honeypot risks for AI employees, quoting WSJ reporting that OpenAI warns staff about spies using sex or romance, with a training example of a US diplomat in Russia who was successively sent a blonde woman with champagne, a redheaded woman, and then a young man; Levine notes AI researchers are now a vast new pool of targets for such operations.
- In a lighter segment, Matt Levine's daughter explains how to identify fake Pokémon cards (a smaller ball in the center of the Pokéball or missing star at the bottom), and Mary Giles recounts a contractor's claim that Pokémon cards have outperformed the S&P 500 and gold, advising storage in bins for college funding—advice Levine rejects.
