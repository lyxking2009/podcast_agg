# ROLLUP: Crypto’s Bull Market on the Brink | Nvidia Turmoil | ETH Is Money | Ethereum’s New Security Shield — Bankless


## TL;DR
The episode covers the crypto market's downturn as Bitcoin loses its 50-week moving average and sentiment hits extreme fear, questioning whether the bull cycle is intact. It also discusses the shocking resignation of NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang amid extramarital affair scandals and possible hidden reasons, with hosts speculating on conspiracies. Vitalik Buterin finally declares ETH as money/store of value, but the timing is criticized; Ethereum Foundation's "seppuku clause" controversy resurfaces; and Phylax introduces circuit breakers at the block builder level to prevent DeFi exploits, sparking debate on censorship resistance. Other news includes CFTC approval for equity perpetuals and IRS letters to crypto traders.

## Key points
- Bitcoin dropped 5.4% on the week to around $72,000, falling below its 50-week moving average (~$75,500) for the first time in this cycle, with extreme fear sentiment exceeding any point in the early bull phase of 2023. The 200-week moving average sits at $67,000, creating a narrow band that Bitcoin may tag according to analysts like Ben Cowan, who had predicted post-midterm Q4 weakness. Michael Nadeau now sees a possible dip below the 50-week but expects a bounce, while Alex Thorne says breaking below the 50-week for more than a month would invalidate the early bull thesis.
- Ethereum fared worse, down 10% to approximately $2,000, trading below both its 50-week and 200-week moving averages, with ETH underperforming Bitcoin significantly, raising concerns about the four-year cycle's integrity. The episode notes Bitcoin's 5-year annualized return has fallen to 3%, underperforming T-bills, prompting debate whether crypto's growth phase is over and it may trade more like gold with long flat periods.
- Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO after two extramarital affairs, one allegedly with a Chinese intelligence asset. NVIDIA lost about $1 trillion in market cap from peak, down 17% on the week and 27% in November. Hosts expressed skepticism that the affairs alone justify removal of such a critical leader and speculated hidden reasons: possible illegal chip sales to China via intermediaries (violating CHIPS Act), opposition to his open-source AI advocacy, or political power struggles with closed-source AI labs and regulatory capture interests. They noted The Economist's call for heads to roll and lack of scrutiny from major media.
- Vitalik Buterin publicly declared ETH is money/store of value, stating he had decided this week that focusing on fee-burning makes Ethereum act like a corporation and that the pragmatic reason to hold ETH is as money. The hosts criticized the timing as too late, noting this message would have been more impactful years ago, and that it aligns with the existing roadmap (crops, quantum resistance) but comes at a time of weak market sentiment. Community reactions varied: Tom Lee bullish, Dan Held dismissive, Tulip King calling it an act of desperation.
- The Ethereum Foundation's 'seppuku clause' controversy resurfaced: an ex-employee suing the EF claims she was fired days after a cancer diagnosis, and the mandate employees were forced to sign included a Japanese suicide reference. After employee backlash, the text was converted to a graphic but retained. Hasu argued the outcome was positive: necessary downsizing, purging of misaligned elements, and increased ecosystem diversity (e.g., ETH Labs). Hosts acknowledged the implementation was ham-fisted and morale at EF is low, but the structural outcome may be beneficial.
- Phylax, in partnership with Titan Builder (largest Ethereum block builder, ~50% of blocks), launched app-defined circuit breakers that revert transactions violating pre-set rules (e.g., vault cannot pay out >50% of AUM in one transaction). This opt-in system aims to prevent DeFi exploits like North Korea's hacks while preserving protocol-level censorship resistance: with the upcoming Fossil EIP, transactions can be force-included but still fail, maintaining a separation of powers. Hosts expressed cautious optimism, viewing it as a small compromise on cypherpunk values to stop billions in losses.
- The CFTC approved onshore perpetual futures on individual equities for registered exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken, Kalshi, NADX/Zero G). Traditional players like CME, CBOE, and Citadel Securities are suing to block this, reminiscent of banks vs crypto exchanges. Hosts expect perps to prevail given crypto-friendly CFTC and note the importance of onshoring these products.
- The IRS is increasing 'letter 6174' notices to crypto traders, a voluntary compliance scare tactic; while not an audit, it signals IRS has information on crypto activity. Hosts emphasize keeping taxes in order and note AI tools may enhance IRS enforcement capabilities, though public sector AI adoption lags.
