# Don Wilson on the GPU Market's Trillion Dollar Question — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Don Wilson, founder and CEO of DRW, discusses the development of GPU futures markets for compute, emphasizing the need for risk management amid volatile GPU prices and the potential to lower neoclouds' cost of capital. He notes the CFTC comment period is open, with futures possibly listing after the election, and addresses concerns about index robustness and the distinction between perpetuals and swaps. Wilson also shares views on NVIDIA's future and the growing ecosystem of open-source model providers.

## Key points
- GPU futures are essential risk management tools for neoclouds. They would allow cloud providers to hedge future compute revenue, especially when selling to less creditworthy customers or offering on-demand compute. This could reduce their cost of capital significantly by enabling them to sell futures strips against deployed GPUs.
- Regulatory approval for GPU futures is pending. The CFTC extended the comment period; if no action is taken, futures could list on November 9, after the U.S. election. Some theories for delay include concerns that certain indices may not be robust enough.
- Silicon Data's index focuses on on-demand compute from neoclouds, of which there are many, rather than hyperscalers. Don Wilson claims the index is designed to be IOSCO compliant, a standard he says other providers may not even be aware of.
- GPU prices are volatile. H100 on-demand prices declined for months, then surged after Anthropic's new model release, driven by inference demand. Recent B200, B300, and H100 prices have seen another upward surge, highlighting the need for hedging instruments.
- Wilson argues that perpetual futures are not swaps and serve useful economic purposes. They could improve efficiency for ETFs (e.g., oil ETFs) and democratize access to income from hard-to-borrow stocks, which retail investors currently miss out on.
- NVIDIA's dominance may be challenged as the market grows, but Wilson is confident the company will continue to grow. The ecosystem of open-source model providers (like Fireworks and Together) is already rich enough to support a compute futures market.
