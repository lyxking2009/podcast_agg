# Is Wearable Tech a Red Flag? — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
In this episode, the hosts discuss Bob Garry, a Tulane virologist whose NIH funding was cut after he co-authored a paper arguing COVID-19 likely originated naturally, not from a lab leak; he now faces an empty lab but is pitching his research on economic grounds. They also cover Apple's upcoming product blitz under new CEO John Ternus, including foldable iPhones, smart home displays, and AirPods with depth-map cameras, amid a backlash against wearable tech like Meta's smart glasses. Finally, they examine legal challenges to prediction markets, with a Ninth Circuit ruling against Kalshi's sports contracts and George Santos receiving a lifetime ban.

## Key points
- Bob Garry, a Tulane University virologist with nearly 50 years of experience, laid groundwork for rapid COVID tests but became a target after co-writing a March 2020 paper arguing SARS-CoV-2 likely emerged naturally, not from a lab leak.
- His NIH funding through the Centers for Research in Emerging Infectious Diseases (CREED) was cut about two years ago after the Trump administration deemed his work unsafe, leaving his New Orleans lab empty despite prior $9 million in government support.
- Garry was investigated by Congress and accused of helping cover up a lab-leak origin; he received death threats, but he maintains that changing his views as new evidence emerged reflects the scientific method, not dishonesty.
- Key evidence cited by lab-leak proponents includes private messages where Garry and colleagues initially expressed openness to a lab leak before later publishing a paper rejecting it—a shift scientists say is normal iterative science.
- To secure renewed NIH funding, Garry is now pitching his research on economic grounds, arguing that addressing diseases like Lassa fever (which he is developing a monoclonal antibody therapy for) protects U.S. mining interests in West Africa where rare earths are mined.
- Jason Gale, Bloomberg global health editor, says the pandemic damaged public trust in science because people misunderstood science as iterative; he argues that despite better tools like rapid tests, mRNA, and wastewater surveillance, misinformation and politicization mean the next pandemic response will be severely hampered.
- Apple has appointed John Ternus as new CEO (Tim Cook remains involved) and is poised for its most prolific product cycle ever: Mark Gurman predicts nearly 10 major new product categories in the next 2-3 years, versus only three (Apple Watch, AirPods, Vision Pro) in Cook's 15-year tenure.
- Upcoming Apple products include a foldable iPhone, a 7-inch smart home display with facial recognition, a touchscreen MacBook, new AirPods, iPhone 18 Pro models, and an iPad mini with OLED; later products include AirPods with cameras, smart glasses, and a 20th-anniversary iPhone redesign.
- Apple's AirPods with cameras use depth-map sensing rather than optical recording, so they cannot capture actual video or color images; they are designed to give Siri visual context, like identifying items in a pantry, without storing footage.
- Mark Gurman argues that public backlash against Meta's smart glasses (bars banning them, Lorde calling them 'not sexy') is overblown for Apple, which will add privacy lights and controls; he contends the real AI danger is models creating their own models within 1-2 years, which he says needs urgent regulation.
- The Ninth Circuit Court ruled that Kalshi's sports prediction contracts are legally bets, not derivatives, meaning Kalshi can no longer offer sports trading in Nevada and other Ninth Circuit states; Kalshi plans to appeal, likely to the Supreme Court.
- George Santos was banned for life from Kalshi and suspended three years from trading futures by the CFTC after allegedly placing a bet on his own attendance at the State of the Union and then not showing up to manipulate the market.
- The CFTC and courts are debating whether prediction markets on sports have legitimate economic hedging value; if sports betting is removed from platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket, it would eliminate a huge portion of their trading volume.
