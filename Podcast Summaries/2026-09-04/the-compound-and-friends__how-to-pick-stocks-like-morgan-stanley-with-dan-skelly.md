# How to Pick Stocks Like Morgan Stanley With Dan Skelly — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Dan Skelly, portfolio manager at Morgan Stanley, discusses the current market resilience, driven by strong earnings growth (Q2 S&P 500 +28% y/y, median +14%) and AI-driven productivity. He explains how Morgan Stanley's wealth business grew from 8% to 60% of revenues under James Gorman's strategy, and argues that the recent rotation from AI capex winners to other sectors is healthy for the secular bull market. Skelly also cautions about small-cap risks and potential AI capex double ordering, but remains optimistic about long-term American innovation.

## Key points
- Morgan Stanley's wealth management now contributes about 60% of firm revenues, up from 8% in 2005, driven by strategic moves such as acquiring Smith Barney, buying E*TRADE, and leveraging the Morgan Stanley at Work platform as a lead generator.
- Dan Skelly attributes resilient S&P 500 earnings to a paradigm-shifted economy: services over goods, K-shaped income distribution, and AI supercycle; Q2 index earnings grew 28% y/y while median company grew 14%.
- Tariff-related price increases were largely passed through to consumers and not given back, creating a synthetic operating leverage boost for average companies; Skelly cites his landscaper taking up prices 15-20% and never lowering them.
- AI is boosting productivity on top of existing labor forces, with companies in a 'no hiring, no firing' zone; Skelly expects a labor lever to be pulled in the next 12-24 months, further expanding margins without headcount growth.
- The market rotation from AI capex winners like semis into healthcare, financials, and small caps is considered healthy because it prevents euphoria and extends the duration of the secular bull market.
- Small caps face unique risks: higher interest rates disproportionately hurt lower-quality companies, and AI could disintermediate some small-cap industries while lack of capital prevents them from adopting AI, creating a left-tail risk.
- AI infrastructure spending is underpinned by credible, cash-rich hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, etc.), unlike 2000 when customers were speculative dot-coms; however, Skelly warns of potential double ordering and deployment constraints in data center buildout.
- The high yield corporate spread has remained stable while triple C spreads have widened, indicating stress in the lowest-quality credits but strength in energy and materials sectors, which benefit from AI buildout and geopolitical factors.
- Short positions on Nasdaq 100 futures have surged 35% since mid-June to near record levels, reflecting significant bearishness that could fuel further upside if earnings continue to beat.
- Skelly sees the possibility of a pull forward of data center construction ahead of the 2028 election, which could create a later slowdown, analogous to Y2K capex pull-forward and subsequent hangover.
