# From CEO to Startups: Masters in Business with Former Vanguard CEO Bill McNabb — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Former Vanguard CEO Bill McNabb reflects on his 30-year career, including leading the firm through the 2008 financial crisis and its expansion into ETFs, target date funds, and the advisor channel. He discusses his post-Vanguard roles on the boards of IBM and UnitedHealth, and his work advising fintech startups like Altruist, Vanilla, and Finney, emphasizing the hybrid future of human advisors augmented by technology. McNabb also shares leadership lessons from Jack Bogle and Jack Brennan, and warns about rising leverage in markets.

## Key points
- Bill McNabb joined Vanguard in 1986 when it managed $15 billion and later became CEO in August 2008, weeks before Lehman Brothers collapsed; by retirement Vanguard surpassed $5 trillion under management, now about $15 trillion.
- McNabb attributes his path to luck and mentors; leadership lessons from coaching rowing included 'we vs I' and leading by example, reinforced by Vanguard's Jack Brennan.
- Indexing's rise was driven by both psychological factors (scandals, crashes) and math: after-tax index funds beat 90% of active equity funds over any rolling 10-year period, and arithmetic shows active managers as a group must match market before costs.
- During the 2008 crisis, McNabb and Vanguard leadership met twice daily, assured investors the system would survive, and announced no layoffs, redeploying employees to improve service and client education; this boosted morale and client trust.
- Post-crisis, Vanguard pivoted to the independent advisor channel and ETFs; Vanguard was a serious bidder for iShares but backed away because regulators wanted pairing with Barclays institutional business, which BlackRock acquired. Vanguard then went all-in on ETFs and serving advisors.
- Using Jim Collins' frameworks, Vanguard refined its mission to 'take a stand for investors, treat them fairly, and give them the best chance for investment success,' leading to target date funds as 401k defaults and automatic enrollment/escalation, influenced by Richard Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi's research.
- McNabb's relationship with Jack Bogle was demanding but formative; Bogle sent a 20-page memo after McNabb became CEO, and while initially skeptical of ETFs and international investing, Bogle eventually accepted Vanguard's approach.
- On succession, McNabb cites Jack Brennan's principle that after 10-12 years, people stop questioning the CEO; McNabb stepped down at 60 with a strong team and planned transition with the board.
- Post-Vanguard, McNabb co-authored a governance book with Ram Charan and Dennis Carey, led NACD's blue ribbon commission, and serves on the boards of IBM and UnitedHealth, focusing on talent, strategy, and risk while respecting the management-governance line.
- At IBM, McNabb supports the pivot to hybrid cloud and AI under CEO Arvind Krishna, bringing shareholder and venture perspectives to board discussions, including quantum computing where a fellow director with computer science background can engage research team.
- UnitedHealth faced multiple external shocks (CEO Brian Thompson's murder, guidance issues, leadership change); board pivoted quickly and welcomed Stephen Hemsley's return as CEO, demonstrating need for board agility.
- McNabb is senior advisor at Venrock, working with partner Nick Beim to vet startups; he advises Altruist founder Jason Wenk, helping scale the custodian that became third-largest for RIAs by digitizing custody and adding AI-driven tax planning (Hazel), passing cost savings to advisors.
- Vanilla, a software platform for estate planning, automates balance sheets and family trees; Vanguard was an early investor and pilot user, and McNabb says it dramatically improves high-net-worth planning conversations saving clients millions.
- Finney uses AI to match prospects and clients for RIAs, and its pricing aligns with success rather than seat licenses, analogous to Vanguard's performance-based fees for active equity managers.
- McNabb is also involved with Moment (fixed income trading innovation from ex-Citadel team) and Sillow (litigation technology combining legal and engineering skills).
- On future of advice, McNabb predicts a spectrum from fully automated to human-led, but bulk of investors will want human advisors augmented by technology; technology can free advisors to serve more clients (e.g., 300 vs 100) and improve personalization.
- McNabb warns about over-gamification and day trading (retail options volumes surging), and about rising leverage in hyperscalers and private credit that nobody is talking about.
- Advice to graduates: apply the hedgehog concept (passion, world-class ability, economic engine); consider venture or wealth management/starting an advisory firm; maintain long-term discipline and pay attention to contrarian signals.
