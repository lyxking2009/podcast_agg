# Paul Kedrosky: Nvidia Is Now the AI Bubble's Single Point of Failure — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Paul Kedrosky argues that the current AI investment boom is a historic bubble at the intersection of four forces—technology, credit, public policy, and real estate—driven by data center buildout and Nvidia's central role. He contends that Nvidia has become a single point of failure, funding customers and absorbing surplus GPUs, while large language models are rapidly commoditizing and their prices are in hyperdeflation, making the economics of heavily indebted hyperscalers and neoclouds unsustainable.

## Key points
- AI capital spending now accounts for 30-60% of US GDP growth, with data center spending growing about 70% per year and displacing nearly all other commercial fixed investment, making it the largest share of non-residential fixed investment in US history.
- The current bubble is unusual because it sits at the intersection of four forces: a powerful technology story, easy credit, public policy (US-China existential competition), and real estate speculation (powered shells and energy-secured sites), which multiply rather than add.
- Historically, similar surges in non-residential investment—canals, railways, rural electrification, fiber optics, WWII rearmament—followed a predictable pattern: a compelling narrative, capital inflow, overbuilding, and eventual overcapacity, with about 50% of railway tracks eventually abandoned.
- More than half of hyperscalers' data center spending is now funded externally through SPVs and off-balance-sheet vehicles, shifting from cash-flow-based to debt-based financing, and this is turning prime borrowers into subprime borrowers as capital markets repeat historical patterns.
- Nvidia announced a $500 billion funding pool with Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, KKR, and Goldman Sachs' David Solomon, providing up to 25% of the capital, which Kedrosky interprets as a defensive move to support its ecosystem and used GPU prices, making Nvidia a single point of failure.
- Large language models are rapidly commoditizing: capability differences are shrinking, and per-token prices for frontier models are still 5-50 times higher than Chinese open-source models, but that gap is collapsing as models converge, setting up a fall to marginal cost.
- AI tokens are a hyperdeflationary commodity, with prices falling 50-80% per year due to semiconductors, software, and engineering improvements; to maintain revenue, a company would need to sell 500-1000% more tokens each year just to stay in place.
- Anthropic's revenue is reportedly $9 billion last year, running at a $65 billion annual pace now, and projected to reach $200 billion by end of 2027, but Kedrosky doubts such growth is sustainable given model commoditization and the atypical nature of early programmer adopters.
- Programming is an atypical and expansive use case for LLMs (short prompts produce many tokens), whereas most office applications are compressive (large documents summarized), so the last four years of token demand are not representative of the next four, undermining capacity buildout assumptions.
- Neoclouds like CoreWeave are the most vulnerable because they are marginal suppliers with heavy debt and dependent on a customer base that is shifting from expansive programming workloads to compressive applications, making them likely to be pushed out when the market consolidates.
- Microsoft and Amazon are better positioned because they can integrate forward as neutral hosts of any model, offering reliability, security, and enterprise integration, rather than trying to build proprietary models that are increasingly indistinguishable from competitors.
- Google's new TPUs compress key-value sequences by a factor of 6-8, dramatically reducing memory requirements, and Chinese memory manufacturers plan to add capacity at Samsung-like volumes by 2028, signaling future HBM oversupply and undermining Nvidia's current memory advantage.
- The SOX semiconductor index is down about 25%, Micron's HBM supply-demand balance went from 100 to 1200 in a year then declined about 25%, and Nvidia trades at 17.5 times earnings despite beating estimates, reflecting the market's discounting of future oversupply and commoditization.
