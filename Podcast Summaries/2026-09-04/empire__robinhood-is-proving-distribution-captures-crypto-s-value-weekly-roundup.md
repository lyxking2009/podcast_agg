# Robinhood Is Proving Distribution Captures Crypto’s Value | Weekly Roundup — Empire

## TL;DR
This Empire weekly roundup examines how Robinhood's new chain is capturing massive fee revenue from memecoins and tokenized stock-backed tokens, with one-day fees annualizing to $1.7B. The hosts debate whether these tokenized equities are a net positive or a regulatory risk, and they discuss Ethereum L1's shrinking fee capture relative to L2s like Arbitrum. The episode also covers Athena's neo-bank launch, remittance fintech Felix Pago, and Hyperliquid's reported partnership with Kraken's Bitnomial.

## Key points
- Robinhood Chain generated $4.7 million in fees in a single day, annualizing to nearly $1.7 billion in revenue; if current activity continues, Robinhood could double its topline and triple profits, with the stock up over 15% and nearly doubled from its low of $68 to around a $100 billion valuation.
- Meme coins on Robinhood Chain are increasingly backed by tokenized stocks, such as Pawns (launchpad token, ~$500 million market cap), ArtificialIs (Nvidia-backed, ~$240 million), Marscoin (SpaceX), Memory Moo (Micron), and Boner (HIMS), creating a new paradigm where speculative activity ties directly to equity derivatives.
- The tokenized stock meme coin mechanism uses an AMM pool with authorized participants minting new shares during market hours to maintain a one-token-to-one-share peg, but off-hours dislocation occurs because APs cannot mint, leading to extremes like HIMS implying a $100+ price over a weekend when the stock closed at around $20 on Friday.
- One host argues these tokenized stock meme coins are a huge net negative because they could be classified as unregulated equity derivatives, triggering KYC/AML and DCM registration requirements, and may draw regulatory scrutiny for potential market manipulation similar to the GameStop short squeeze.
- The other host counters that meme coins are stress-testing infrastructure for tokenized stocks, analogous to how Solana's meme coin surge improved its rails, and that LP pools could bring market makers and market structure to support tokenized equities, eventually enabling NASDAQ-like trading on-chain.
- Market makers cannot hedge 24/7 tokenized equities over weekends, so they avoid holding volatile inventory; exchanges may temporarily hold inventory to bootstrap liquidity, but this does not scale, and 24/5 trading may arrive within nine months while weekend trading remains far off.
- Ethereum L1's fee capture from Robinhood Chain fell from 0.5% to 0.14% of fees between August 22 and August 30, highlighting how L2s like Arbitrum capture the majority of fee revenue, with Robinhood Chain's run-rate implying Arbitrum could earn $100-200 million in fees versus Ethereum's negligible share.
- A comparison of valuations—Robinhood at $120 billion, Arbitrum at $1 billion, and Ethereum at $300 billion—led the hosts to agree that ETH is not the best trade among the three, and the contested proposal EIP-559 to increase Ethereum L1 fees is expected to be a major community debate in the next 6-12 months.
- Athena launched Athena Pay, a neo-bank offering zero FX fee markup, 5% cash back on a Rain-issued card, 6% on daily balances, free on/off ramps, and multicurrency accounts; Athena has diversified its USD backing beyond the basis trade into equities, BlackRock BUIDL treasuries, Janus Henderson CLOs, and collateralized lending with FalconX.
- Neo-bank competition is fierce, with Cast, Etherfi, Plasma, RedotPay, Arc, and Felix Pago all targeting crypto-native travelers and global citizens; customer acquisition costs remain high, and fintech investors are skeptical, preferring to pile into winners like Revolut rather than back early-stage direct-to-consumer fintech.
- Felix Pago raised $200 million (equity $85 million, debt $116 million from General Catalyst) for its WhatsApp-based remittance product, using stablecoins and partnering with NuBank for deposit growth; remittance is seen as a wedge to build a deeper wallet and deposit relationship, though unit economics are currently negative.
- Remittance industry dynamics show Remitly up 51% in six months to a $5.6 billion market cap, while Western Union is down 30%; take rates vary widely by corridor, with digital corridors heavily compressed and cash corridors retaining higher fees, and scale in fulfillment networks remains critical.
- Hyperliquid is reportedly in talks with Payward (Kraken's parent) to use Bitnomial's regulated DCM and DCO to offer segregated on-chain markets with KYC/AML for US participants, potentially boosting liquidity and volume on Hyperliquid.
