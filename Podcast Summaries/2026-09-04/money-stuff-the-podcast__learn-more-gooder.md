# Learn More Gooder — Money Stuff: The Podcast

## TL;DR
Matt Levine and Mary Childs discuss how AI could make consumer financial options like mortgage refinancing and savings yields optimally exercised, repricing mortgages and other products; the FTC's complaint against Amazon over a 'soft reserve' phantom bid in ad auctions; and an ETF dividend-avoidance trade using IVV and VOO. They also explore how AI threatens professional services firms' apprenticeship model.

## Key points
- Morgan Stanley research reported by Bloomberg suggests AI tools will make mortgage refinancing much easier, potentially reducing the process from two weeks to 10 minutes, leading borrowers to exercise prepayment options more optimally when rates fall.
- Fabrice Touré (formerly of Goldman Sachs, now at Baruch) co-authored 'Refinancing Frictions, Mortgage Pricing, and Redistribution,' which finds that many policies that increase refinancing frequency also raise equilibrium mortgage rates and reduce mortgage credit access.
- Because mortgage investors currently benefit from borrowers not refinancing optimally, if AI reduces friction, mortgage-backed securities would need repricing; Morgan Stanley estimates mortgage rates could rise 10–20 basis points for everyone.
- The same dynamic applies to other consumer finance options: credit card rewards and high-yield savings accounts rely on consumer inattention; AI agents that automatically check and switch accounts could erode those margins.
- Amazon is accused by the FTC and 22 state attorneys general of deceiving advertisers by claiming second-price auction mechanics while actually using a 'soft reserve' phantom bid that makes the winning bidder pay its own bid in many cases.
- Amazon's defense is that advertisers don't rely on simplified auction descriptions because they care about return on ad spend, not auction mechanics, and that the ad algorithm optimizes for relevance and other factors, making it a black box.
- Art auctions use 'chandelier bidding,' where auctioneers bid on behalf of the seller up to an undisclosed reserve price; New York requires disclosures in catalogs, making the practice more transparent than Amazon's soft reserve.
- Foreign investors running cash-and-carry trades on the S&P 500 avoid dividend withholding tax by switching between nearly identical ETFs IVV (BlackRock) and VOO (Vanguard), which pay dividends about a week apart, capturing the dividend via price changes with minimal risk.
- The ETF dividend swap works because there are two identical economic assets with staggered dividend dates; hedge funds like Marshall Weiss (as reported) are cited as doing this, not retail investors who would face capital gains taxes.
- AI pressure on law firms is undermining the hourly billing model, where partners leverage associates' billable hours; if AI handles grunt work, firms may need to switch to value-based pricing, and junior associates may lose training opportunities.
- The apprenticeship model in professional services is at risk: if partners can leave to boutiques with AI agents and no associates, large firms may struggle to train future partners and maintain their model.
