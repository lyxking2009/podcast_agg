# One Weird Trick to Get Your Credit Card Charge Reversed? Fraud. — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
In this episode of The Big Take, reporter Amanda Mall discusses the surge in credit card chargebacks, which rose 29% in the U.S. from 2021 to 2025. She explains that while some disputes are legitimate, many are 'friendly fraud'—unintentional disputes due to confusing billing descriptors or forgotten subscriptions—or intentional fraud, where consumers abuse the system to get free items, often encouraged by social media. The trend reflects growing distrust between consumers and merchants, fueled by opaque subscription models and declining customer service.

## Key points
- Chargeback rates in the U.S. rose 29% from 2021 to 2025, and 46% globally, driven partly by increased card-not-present transactions and consumer disputes.
- Many chargebacks are 'friendly fraud,' where consumers dispute legitimate charges because they don't recognize billing descriptors from app stores, Uber, or subscription services that often design terms to be missed.
- Banks often refund small-dollar disputes without investigation due to the high cost of labor, which encourages more chargebacks.
- Intentional fraud is growing, especially among younger consumers who learn tactics on TikTok, using chargebacks punitively for slow shipping or poor service.
- Filing a false chargeback is illegal (claiming fraud when none occurred) and can lead to credit card closure, credit score damage, bans from merchants like airlines or Amazon, and possibly law enforcement referral.
- For merchants, chargebacks cost a $15 penalty per dispute plus lost revenue and product; they hire third-party AI firms to fight disputes, but high chargeback rates can increase transaction fees and require larger cash reserves.
- Small businesses are hit hardest by chargebacks, as they face immediate penalties even if they win a dispute.
- Banks use technology to triage disputes, approving or denying en masse, and may close accounts of repeat fraudsters, while payment processors drop high-chargeback merchants.
- The chargeback surge signals eroded trust in the economy, as consumers feel scammed by hidden subscription fees and unhelpful AI customer service, prompting them to retaliate by disputing charges.
