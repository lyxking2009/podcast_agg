# Sam MacPherson on Why Spark Benefited So Much From the KelpDAO Hack — Unchained

## TL;DR
Sam MacPherson, co-founder of Spark, discusses how the DeFi protocol built on Sky (formerly MakerDAO) survived the KelpDAO hack and grew its TVL. Spark’s conservative risk approach—tight rate limits, triple-redundant oracles, and minimal collateral sets—shielded it while others suffered. MacPherson also shares views on AI-driven security threats, stablecoin yield wars, the subDAO governance model, and Spark’s expansion into institutional lending and cross-chain liquidity.

## Key points
- Spark is a DeFi protocol built on Sky (previously MakerDAO), functioning like a commercial bank that draws liquidity from Sky’s central bank-like reserves and deploys it into crypto-backed lending, real-world assets, and other yield strategies.
- Spark’s main products include an on-chain lending market (Spark Lend), a yield-generating vault (Spark Savings), institutional Bitcoin-backed lending via Anchorage, and a prime brokerage (Spark Prime) for hedge funds.
- The KelpDAO hack in April 2024 caused many DeFi protocols to suffer losses, but Spark’s TVL in ETH rose ~50% (and at one point 80%) because it had exited the vulnerable rsETH asset months earlier and maintained conservative risk controls.
- Spark had already off-boarded rsETH in January 2024 as part of a general risk reduction strategy, never used it in high-leverage ‘efficiency mode,’ and kept LTVs at 60-70%, which would have limited damage even if the asset remained listed.
- Spark’s lending market uses tight rate limits that slow down large withdrawals, making it unattractive for hackers who need quick exits, and it relies on a triple-redundant oracle setup (Chainlink, Redstone, Chronicle) to prevent price manipulation.
- The protocol’s risk-averse DNA stems from MakerDAO’s historical ethos of compounding growth slowly by avoiding hype cycles and focusing on long-term stability, which has helped Sky maintain over $10 billion in stablecoin deposits.
- Governance decisions like asset off-boarding follow a multi-stage process taking about a month, including risk council reviews and Sky’s final approval, while emergency multisigs can pause the protocol within hours to address imminent threats.
- MacPherson believes AI poses both offensive and defensive potentials for DeFi; smart contracts are already kept minimal and audited, and AI tools are improving enough to complement human auditors, moving toward formal verification for higher security.
- The stablecoin landscape is seeing a trend where exchanges and fintechs (e.g., Robinhood, Stripe) launch their own chains and stablecoins to capture net interest margins, and Spark provides liquidity and yield infrastructure to help newer stablecoins like USDG scale.
- Spark’s SPK token has governance and staking utility, and despite being near all-time lows, the protocol generates revenue and profits, with excess profits planned for token buybacks as voted by governance.
- Spark’s institutional lending product has grown to $250 million in loans since its late-2024 launch, with a pipeline targeting $1 billion by year-end, and its USDT-based Spark Savings is becoming one of the most liquid USDT yield products.
