# #200 - Sebastian Thrun - The Singularity Is Here & We Are Completely Unprepared — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR
Sebastian Thrun, creator of Waymo's self-driving cars, discusses exponential tech progress ('the singularity'), where AI, robotics, and autonomous vehicles will radically reshape society within years. He highlights Waymo's 100x safety improvement over humans, the inevitability of job disruption, and the cultural divide between Silicon Valley's optimistic rule-breaking and Europe's regulatory caution. Thrun advocates for embracing failure, lifelong learning, and redistributing productivity gains to ensure widespread benefit.

## Key points
- Waymo self-driving cars are approximately 100 times safer than human drivers when measuring accidents causing bodily harm, according to an insurance company estimate. The fleet learns collectively from every mistake, improving faster than any human driver population.
- Globally, 1.2 million people die annually in traffic accidents. Thrun was motivated by losing a childhood friend to a crash, seeing the technology as a preventable solution that will save countless lives without recipients ever knowing.
- The 'singularity' describes an exponential expansion of technological capability where the pace of change doubles faster and faster, making it impossible to predict outcomes even five years ahead. Ray Kurzweil foresaw this decades ago.
- Silicon Valley thrives on a culture of optimistic rule-breaking and hands-off regulation. Unlike Europe, which preemptively regulates worst-case scenarios, the US typically reacts after failures, allowing rapid innovation.
- Flying cars (eVTOLs) are already deployed en masse in Ukraine for military logistics, but passenger services face regulatory hurdles in the US. China has leapfrogged both in commercial flying taxi operations.
- Large language models like Claude now write better code than many human software engineers, threatening high-paying jobs. This commoditization extends to law, copywriting, and other professions, forcing a rethink of career security.
- Foundation model companies (OpenAI, Anthropic) are 'big amoebas' that can absorb vertical SaaS startups, eroding traditional competitive moats. Investors are over-indexing on these trillion-dollar-scale opportunities.
- Europe's fragmented regulations and cultural aversion to wealth creation stifle technology firms. A United States of Europe could rival the US and China, but current trends move toward further fragmentation.
- Success traits: grit (finish what you start), curiosity (lifelong learning), and empathy (empower others). The biggest mistake is not learning from errors; effective people never repeat the same mistake twice.
- Productivity gains from AI require societal consensus on redistribution. Example: using freed-up resources to hire more teachers, shifting from 1:30 to 1:6 student-teacher ratios, improving education universally.
