# Peter Steinberger: "Fun Is Velocity" — Y Combinator Startup Podcast

## TL;DR
Peter Steinberger shares the story of building OpenClaw, an open-source AI agent, from a personal annoyance to a viral phenomenon. He details the challenges of rapid growth, security pressure, feature bloat, and burnout, offering lessons on maintaining fun as the key to velocity, the importance of personal brand, and staying focused. The talk covers his recovery, the role of community, and a vision for proactive, multimodal agents.

## Key points
- OpenClaw started because Steinberger was annoyed there was no seamless way to send prompts from his phone to his computer; he built a WhatsApp relay in an hour, which evolved into an agent that felt like AGI.
- The project gained product-market fit when non-technical friends demanded to use it, leading to a Discord launch that accidentally went viral after he left the agent running overnight, resulting in 800+ messages.
- Within eight months, OpenClaw attracted over 18,000 unique issue/PR contributors, 111,000 total interactions, and nearly 3,000 committers, with Jensen Huang calling it one of the most successful open-source projects.
- Intense pressure from security researchers forced a shift to hardening code with sandboxing, allow lists, and vet protocols, but most users didn't prioritize security, and the extra complexity slowed feature development.
- Feature creep led to 9,500 configuration options, making the software nearly impossible to fully test or evolve; Steinberger learned that every feature carries long-term maintenance costs.
- Anthropic’s sudden deprecation of a subscription model taught him that a dependency’s business model is your business model, and prompted a multi-model strategy.
- Burnout caused him to stop using his own product and turn it into a chore; he regained momentum after restructuring help from Nvidia and reconnecting with the joy of building for himself.
- He emphasized that 'fun is velocity': weeks spent enjoying development led to visible product improvements, while weeks spent on obligation only yielded configuration tweaks.
- Key advice: build what you personally want, cultivate your personal brand early, stay focused, and ignore hype cycles and obituaries.
- He now treats code review as risk management, trusting gut instinct on what needs deep inspection, and uses AI sub-agents for testing and orchestration.
- The largest bottleneck in agentic infrastructure is managing compute across sessions, especially when needing macOS environments or distributing loads across machines.
- He would have been less stressed about security researchers and clearer about security boundaries from the start, rather than trying to address every report.
- Open-source projects should maintain a vision.md to stay opinionated and resist merging every pull request, even popular ones, if they pull the project off course.
