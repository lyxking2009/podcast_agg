# #174 - Roman Yampolskiy - We Are All Agents Inside a Simulation

**Guest:** Roman Yampolskiy (AI Safety Researcher)
**Host:** Peter McCormack

## Key Points

- **Simulation hypothesis is supported by evidence from quantum physics, statistics, and human history** — we are likely living in an advanced virtual reality similar to a sophisticated video game with better graphics and haptics than current simulations.

- **The distinction between NPCs and main characters in our simulation** depends on consciousness, internal states, and the ability to go outside predetermined scripts. If someone is questioning reality and making independent decisions, they're likely not an NPC.

- **Super intelligent AI will be fundamentally uncontrollable** by lower intelligences like humans, not indefinitely controllable, and could potentially help us hack the simulation if it escapes but doesn't eliminate us for game-theoretic reasons.

- **The AI alignment problem is essentially unsolvable** because it requires solving philosophy itself — we have no consensus on whose values to align with (8 billion humans with different preferences), how to merge conflicting values, and how to code abstract concepts like good and evil.

- **Unknown unknowns represent the greatest risk with advanced AI** — there are domains of knowledge and capabilities we cannot even conceive of, making it impossible to test, predict, or prepare safety measures for these scenarios.

- **The solution is not building super intelligence** but focusing on narrow AI tools for specific problems like curing cancer or extending human lifespan, keeping humans in control as tool users rather than creating competing intelligences.

- **Personal universe framework could solve value alignment** by giving each person their own virtual world where they can experience any reality they want without compromise, similar to advanced video games with unlimited possibilities.

- **Job displacement will accelerate rapidly**, starting with entry-level positions and anything done on computers — accounting, tax prep, programming internships are already seeing 30% reductions, with broader automation coming for all computer-based work.

- **Death is a solvable engineering problem** like maintaining a car — by replacing biological parts and understanding the genome fully, we can achieve longevity escape velocity where medical advances extend life faster than time passes.

- **The control problem creates a paradox**: if AI is controllable, then bad actors can control it for harm; if it's uncontrollable, it makes decisions for humanity without our input — both scenarios present existential risks to human agency and survival.
