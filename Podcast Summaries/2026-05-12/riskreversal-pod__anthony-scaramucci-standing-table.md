# Anthony Scaramucci at Hunt & Fish Club Restaurant | Standing Table Episode #1

**Guest:** Anthony Scaramucci
**Hosts:** Dan Nathan and Guy Adami (RiskReversal Media)

## Key Points

- **Scaramucci's working-class Italian roots shaped his values** of hard work, integrity, and celebrating others' success. He was raised by strict Italian Catholic women who taught traditional values of sharing and playing nice, while his father instilled the principle of paying bills and living within means.

- **The wealth gap in America has reached dangerous levels**, with median house prices at $432,000 requiring $160,000 income while median income is only $84,000. Half of Americans can't afford a median-priced home, leading to angry populism on both left and right as people struggle with basic needs.

- **Inflation represents a regressive tax that destroys purchasing power** for working-class families who lack assets. While Wall Street and asset owners are protected, hourly wage workers have lost 30% of their purchasing power since January 2020, fundamentally stealing from their life savings.

- **The American economic system broke due to two key factors**: globalization that benefited shareholders while hurting working-class jobs (as protesters warned in 1999 Seattle WTO meetings), and political duopoly that serves big business rather than ordinary citizens through gerrymandering and third-party exclusion.

- **Bitcoin represents a technological evolution of money rather than speculation**. Like all money throughout history (from gold coins to cotton-linen bills), its value comes from trust and acceptance as a medium of exchange, but Bitcoin offers transparency, decentralization, and protection from government debasement.

- **Wall Street culture differs fundamentally from Washington politics** — "we are front stabbers" versus backstabbers. Political establishment rejected Scaramucci's direct communication style during his White House tenure because it bypassed traditional power structures and spoke directly to Americans.

- **Success requires maintaining perspective and gratitude** regardless of circumstances. Scaramucci advocates writing down 10 things you're grateful for each morning, maintaining optimism even during public humiliation, job loss, or other setbacks.

- **Cultural differences between old-money establishment and working-class newcomers create barriers** on Wall Street. Scaramucci had to learn proper dress codes and social customs, moving from polyester suits to J. Press clothing to fit Goldman Sachs culture.

- **Restaurant ownership through Hunt & Fish Club represents more than business** — it's about creating community, hospitality, and maintaining connections to New York's cultural heritage while building inclusive workplace culture with extensive internship programs.

## Overview

This conversation at Hunt & Fish Club reveals Anthony Scaramucci as more than his famous 11-day White House tenure, showcasing his journey from working-class Long Island roots to Wall Street success while maintaining perspective on economic inequality. The discussion opens with personal anecdotes about his strict Italian Catholic upbringing and cultural adjustment to Goldman Sachs, then evolves into serious economic analysis of inflation's regressive impact and the housing affordability crisis affecting half of Americans. Scaramucci articulates how globalization and political duopoly created current wealth disparities, arguing that angry populism results from legitimate economic grievances rather than mere political manipulation. The conversation culminates in his philosophy of gratitude and direct communication, contrasting Wall Street's "front-stabbing" culture with Washington's backstabbing politics, while discussing Bitcoin as monetary technology and hinting at potential political aspirations.

## Implications

For founders and business leaders, this conversation highlights the critical importance of understanding macroeconomic forces affecting different socioeconomic classes when building companies and making strategic decisions. Scaramucci's analysis suggests that current inflation and wealth inequality trends will continue driving political instability and social unrest, requiring business leaders to consider how their decisions impact working-class employees and customers.
