# How Long Will the AI Boom Continue? The #1 Question for Crypto Investors

**Guest:** Michael Nadeau (The DeFi Report)
**Host:** Ryan Sean Adams

## Key Points

- **Crypto has never been more correlated to the NASDAQ than in 2026**, with Bitcoin showing the highest correlation to traditional markets, making crypto vulnerable to AI boom/bust cycles. The QQQ is up 25% since March/April lows, pulling crypto prices upward alongside tech stocks.

- **Current market valuations suggest we're in bubble territory** using multiple metrics: Schiller PE ratio at 42 (only exceeded by dot-com peak of 44), forward earnings growth at 27.7% (matching 1999 levels), and AI companies representing 40% of S&P 500 concentration similar to historical bubble peaks.

- **The AI boom follows Carlota Perez's technology revolution framework** with clear eruption (ChatGPT late 2022) and frenzy phases (2024-2025). We're likely in late-stage frenzy, but timeline uncertainty means we could be in 1998, 1999, or 2000 equivalent — potentially seeing more 30% moves before ultimate collapse.

- **AI money flows mirror dot-com bubble mechanics**: enterprise demand flows to model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic), then to hyperscalers (Google, Amazon), then to chip manufacturers (Nvidia), creating a similar overinvestment pattern that historically leads to oversupply and correction.

- **Anthropic's revenue growth exemplifies the boom's scale**, rising from $10 million in December 2022 to $45 billion annualized by May 2026 on logarithmic scale, yet profitability remains questionable while driving massive infrastructure spending throughout the AI stack.

- **Market positioning shows extreme bullish sentiment** with retail call options hitting 9 million contracts (vs 6 million at 2021 peak), hedges coming off, and low VIX indicating complacency. Only 4 of MAG7 stocks back at all-time highs despite broad index recovery, suggesting weakening leadership.

- **Historic analysis shows 26%+ NASDAQ moves in 28 days occurred only 8 times since 1971**, with three happening during dot-com (1998, 1999, 2000), suggesting either mean reversion rally or late-cycle meltup dynamics currently at play.

- **Investment strategy under bubble conditions requires understanding that price leads fundamentals**, not vice versa. Even strong earnings (27.7% growth) and margin improvements (15.3%, highest since 2004) don't prevent bubble collapse when psychological sentiment shifts.

## Overview

The episode explores whether we're experiencing an AI bubble comparable to the 2000 dot-com crash and its implications for crypto investors. Ryan and Michael Nadeau examine extensive market data showing that crypto has reached unprecedented correlation with NASDAQ, making crypto performance dependent on AI boom sustainability. They present compelling evidence of bubble conditions through multiple metrics: Schiller PE ratios near historical highs, extreme market concentration, and earnings growth matching 1999 levels. Using Carlota Perez's technology revolution framework, they position the current AI boom in late-stage frenzy, but acknowledge the impossibility of timing the peak. The conversation traces AI money flows from enterprise demand through model providers to infrastructure, drawing parallels to dot-com overinvestment patterns. While acknowledging strong fundamental metrics like earnings growth and margin improvements, they emphasize that price typically leads fundamentals in bubble cycles. The discussion concludes with practical positioning strategies, noting that understanding the crypto-NASDAQ relationship is crucial for navigation, while maintaining that crypto markets may eventually decouple and operate independently from traditional finance manipulation and policy interventions.

## Implications

For crypto investors, the unprecedented correlation with traditional markets means portfolio diversification benefits have largely disappeared during this cycle. Founders and engineers building in crypto should prepare for potential severe correction if AI bubble bursts, as crypto could be dragged down despite fundamental technological progress. Product managers should consider that current favorable funding and user acquisition costs may not persist through a broader market correction. The analysis suggests maintaining significant cash positions and avoiding overexposure during late-cycle conditions, while recognizing that timing the peak is impossible. For technical builders, the silver lining is that crypto's eventual decoupling from traditional finance could restore its role as an independent, manipulation-resistant market.
