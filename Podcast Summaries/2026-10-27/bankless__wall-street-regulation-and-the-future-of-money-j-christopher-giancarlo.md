# Wall Street, Regulation, and the Future of Money | J. Christopher Giancarlo — Bankless

## TL;DR
J. Christopher Giancarlo, former CFTC chairman and 'Crypto Dad,' discusses his new book and the evolution of crypto regulation. He argues that U.S. regulators failed to provide clarity, especially under Gary Gensler's 'rogue' SEC, and that political hostility emerged after Sam Bankman-Fried. Giancarlo predicts full tokenization of finance by 2040 and stresses that privacy and freedom must be embedded in the digital dollar to avoid surveillance-state outcomes.

## Key points
- Giancarlo's new book 'The New Adventures of Crypto Dad' (subtitled 'The Quest for Financial Freedom') aims to explain that crypto is a whole new architecture of finance, not just an asset class, and to argue for a smart regulatory framework that fosters innovation while preventing fraud and manipulation. He wrote for a broad audience, not just crypto aficionados.
- In a 2018 Senate hearing, Giancarlo urged senators not to dismiss crypto; he went from 1,500 to 45,000 Twitter followers and was nicknamed 'Crypto Dad.' He laments that despite his plea, Congress failed to pass a regulatory clarity bill, with the House passing but Senate stalling.
- Giancarlo attributes Democratic hostility to crypto largely to Elizabeth Warren and bank lobbying, and to a political shift after Sam Bankman-Fried's campaign contributions; he says Gary Gensler's SEC became 'rogue' and abused enforcement powers such as Wells notices and debanking to suppress the industry.
- As CFTC chairman, Giancarlo approved the first Bitcoin futures, which drew anger from Wall Street banks who took out ads and lobbied Congress; he defends the CFTC's market expertise and says the Bitcoin futures market is now deep, liquid, transparent, and well-policed against fraud and manipulation.
- On perpetuals, Giancarlo argues they are not novel; the CFTC never banned them but required retail trading on fully licensed exchanges. He praises current CFTC Chairman Mike Selig for trying to bring perp markets onshore to avoid offshore development like the Eurodollar market caused by bad policy.
- He highlights his relationship with Mike Selig, whom he hired as a law clerk, later recruited to Wilkie Farr & Gallagher, and supported for CFTC chairman. Selig shares Giancarlo's view that digital assets are a new architecture of finance and that innovation should happen in the U.S. to support American capital formation and risk hedging.
- Drawing on his experience at GFI Group during 2008, Giancarlo explains that the Federal Reserve feared Lehman Brothers failure would cause $400 billion in gross payouts, but later research by CFTC chief economist Bruce Tuchman showed net payouts would have been under $9 billion; the crisis was caused by opacity, and blockchain transparency could prevent similar events.
- He predicts that by 2040 the entire financial system will be moved onto digital rails, distributed ledger technology, and tokenization. He cites experiments like DTCC's tokenized collateral project with over 50 financial institutions as early steps, and expects generational demand to drive adoption.
- Giancarlo identifies privacy as the most fundamental issue, warning that current financial surveillance under the Bank Secrecy Act and the third-party doctrine has created a system closer to China's than Americans admit. He criticizes the Genius Act for imposing surveillance on stablecoins and argues the dollar should be an instrument of freedom with a proper balance between privacy and law enforcement.
- He says the U.S. has an opportunity to be the big winner in the 21st century if it encodes democratic values like privacy into digital assets, rather than following China's control-oriented digital yuan or Europe's surveillance-oriented digital euro; he co-founded the Digital Dollar Project to advocate for these values.
