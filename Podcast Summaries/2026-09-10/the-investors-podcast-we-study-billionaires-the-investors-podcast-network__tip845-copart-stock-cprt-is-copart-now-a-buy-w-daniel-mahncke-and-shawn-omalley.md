# TIP845: Copart Stock (CPRT): Is Copart Now a Buy? w/ Daniel Mahncke & Shawn O'Malley — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)  - The Investor’s Podcast Network

## TL;DR

TIP845 revisits Copart, the online salvage-auction monopoly-adjacent business that turns totaled cars into a marketplace connecting insurers with more than a million buyers in 190 countries. The question of the episode is whether a 40%-plus drawdown is an opportunity or a warning: US insurance volumes have fallen for four straight quarters, and Progressive now steers three-quarters of its salvage to rival IAA.

## Key points

- Copart owns over 250 salvage yards outright — land that is increasingly difficult to permit — and compounded EPS at more than 20% a year for a decade, the foundation of its long run as a compounder.
- The stock is down more than 40% from its highs, prompting the central question of whether the volume decline is cyclical or structural.
- The cyclical explanation: premiums rose roughly 50% in three years, so Americans dropped insurance coverage, reducing the pool of insured vehicles that become salvage.
- The structural threat: Progressive has become America's largest auto insurer while sending three-quarters of its salvage to IAA, Copart's main competitor — a channel shift that could permanently impair Copart's volume share.
- Founder-era CEO Jay Adair returned after four years away, a governance signal the hosts weigh carefully.
- Copart bought back $1.6 billion of its own stock after five years of doing nothing, a capital-allocation shift that management presumably views as an expression of confidence at current prices.
- A rumored acquisition of CCC — the software layer for insurance claims and repair estimates — is discussed as a possible vertical move deeper into the insurance workflow.
- The hosts frame the whole question through the lens of what actually broke: is the market mispricing a cyclical air pocket, or correctly discounting a durable share loss to a larger, better-distributed competitor?
