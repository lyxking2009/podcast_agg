# The Chopping Block: FOMO's Co-Founder Defends the Memecoin Trenches, Hunter Biden's $LAPTOP, and AMC vs Robinhood — Unchained

## TL;DR

The Chopping Block invites Paul Erlanger, co-founder of FOMO, to answer the panel's own criticism from a prior episode that they were dinosaurs about memecoins. Erlanger concedes nothing about the asset class and instead reframes FOMO's ambition: onchain trading as the biggest revolution in finance since computerization, with memecoins as the current, noisy stress test of infrastructure that eventually carries tokenized equities and a social graph of all finance.

## Key points

- Erlanger opens by acknowledging the panel's earlier skepticism and jokes that the hosts need to be brought 'back to the modern age' — framing the episode as an attempt to articulate FOMO's brand ethos and long-term vision rather than defend memecoins as an end state.
- His core thesis: onchain is the largest revolution in the financial industry since trading was computerized in the 1970s, and most teams are mistakenly building skeuomorphic apps (essentially 'Twitter on chain') instead of leaning into the real advantages — global distribution and radical transparency.
- Tokenized equities are the next step: distribution of real-world assets to a global audience is the use case, and memecoins are what currently stress-test the infrastructure, liquidity and market makers that tokenized stocks will need.
- FOMO's product insight is that onchain's transparency is crippled by terrible interfaces: everything is public but obfuscated behind alphanumeric addresses and clunky block explorers, so the opportunity is an experience easy enough for anyone to onboard with identity expressed through natural language rather than hex.
- Erlanger's personal history is the credibility pitch: a builder who worked on institutional business at dYdX alongside co-founder 'Se', and who found the old onboarding path — three days from fiat, separate EOA wallets, slippage settings, sandwich risk — a barrier the industry has not solved.
- The panel is explicit that airdrops are largely pointless as a mechanism, and that unnamed trading firms extract value from retail in DeFi — an argument used to justify a FOMO-style integrated consumer product.
- The episode ends on the panel's recurring motif of academic credit and 'I found this band before they were cool' status games, with Tarun joking that the same dynamic shows up in who gets credit for first inventing a thing.
