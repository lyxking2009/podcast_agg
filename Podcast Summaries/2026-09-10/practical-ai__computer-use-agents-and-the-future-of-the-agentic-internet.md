# Computer-Use Agents and the Future of the Agentic Internet — Practical AI

## TL;DR

Chris Benson interviews Demetrios Brinkmann, head of developer experience at the Agentic AI Foundation and founder of the MLOps community, about computer-use agents. Demetrios shares how agents now handle multi-step tasks like checking multiple email and Slack accounts, booking flights, filling forms, and even managing LinkedIn outreach, without needing APIs. He discusses the future of agentic internet, including potential shifts in e-commerce, payments, and enterprise adoption, as well as challenges with current coding harnesses like Claude Code.

## Key points

- Demetrios Brinkmann's MLOps community has merged into the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, rebranding as the Agentic AI community. The foundation has grown to over 250 member companies, 80+ local chapters, 250 community leads, and received over 1,000 ambassador applications since the beginning of the year.
- Computer-use agents, such as OpenAI's computer-use skill in model 5.6, now perform complex multi-step tasks without APIs. Demetrios uses them to check three or four Gmail accounts and five Slack workspaces for daily briefings, fill out forms automatically using stored personal information, and book flights, with the user manually approving payment.
- Automated workflows can be triggered by events. Demetrios set up an agent to monitor his inbox for flight check-in emails, automatically check him in, and send the boarding pass to his phone. He also used computer-use to update his German driver's license appointment three times, generating calendar invites and listing required documents.
- For LinkedIn outreach, Demetrios instructed computer-use to analyze his DMs. LinkedIn's data export process takes 48 hours, so the agent set reminders and checked email periodically. Once the data arrived, the agent analyzed conversations, created a shortlist of 500 contacts, and generated personalized media kits and websites for each person to promote a 36-hour livestream event.
- B2B adoption of computer-use agents faces security concerns. Enterprise environments require role-based access control and audit trails, but the ease of use in consumer applications is building pressure for similar capabilities in workplace software.
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) was donated to the Linux Foundation and became a neutral standard under the Agentic AI Foundation, with over 250 member companies. MCP allows agents to access systems, but it is still maturing; computer-use can bypass the need for MCP servers for many tasks, especially where APIs are unavailable.
- Coding harnesses are increasingly blurred with models. Claude Code harness has experienced issues with recent models (e.g., Fable and Opus 5), with users frustrated by verbose output and janky behavior. Lab-provided harnesses like Claude Code are optimized for their own models, while open-source harnesses such as Pi or Hermes may perform differently on other models.
- Skills like 'Bro' (which forces the model to explain simply) and 'Show Me' (which adds visuals) help mitigate verbose outputs and improve the user experience when working with coding agents.
- The future of e-commerce may shift to chat-based shopping where agents handle purchases directly. Merchants may need to build agent-enticing interfaces, potentially disintermediating traditional web stores. Payment processors are exploring agentic payments, raising questions about the role of platforms like Shopify or Amazon.
- Consumers may increasingly rely on agents that learn their preferences and handle shopping autonomously. However, if platforms like OpenAI become marketplaces, there could be conflicts of interest if they prioritize their own offerings over outside merchants.
