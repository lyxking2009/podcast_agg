# Coinbase’s Everything Exchange: Agentic Finance, Stablecoins, and Tokenization with CEO Brian Armstrong — No Priors: Artificial Intelligence | Technology | Startups

## TL;DR

Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong joins No Priors to outline the 'everything exchange' — stocks, commodities, crypto, perpetuals and prediction markets in one venue with cross-margin — and to argue that the biggest new business in crypto is stablecoin payments. The most forward-looking segment is agentic finance: AI advisors for ordinary users, and bank accounts for the agents themselves, since 76% of agentic commerce transactions are under 30 cents and card rails cannot serve them.

## Key points

- Coinbase's core strategy is consolidating every asset class — stocks, commodities, crypto, derivatives, perpetual futures and prediction markets — into a single venue with good liquidity and cross-margin, enabled by improving regulatory clarity across more jurisdictions.
- Stablecoin payments are described as the fastest-growing line: growth continues even when Bitcoin prices fall, and the pitch is moving money anywhere in the world in under a second for under a cent.
- Agentic finance ('AI-fi') is the third pillar. Coinbase has shipped an AI advisor so that retail users can get the kind of portfolio guidance previously reserved for wealthy clients — dollar-cost averaging, tax-loss harvesting, buying dips, earning yield on idle cash — through conversation.
- Armstrong's most quotable claim is that AI agents need their own bank accounts: Coinbase has built one-command tools so an agent can provision its own financial account. The rationale is economic — about 76% of agentic commerce transactions observed are under 30 cents, while card interchange starts around 30 cents flat plus a percentage, making sub-dollar payments uneconomic on traditional rails.
- He expects more agents than humans and, eventually, an agentic economy larger than the human economy — the design goal is that AIs are not 'unbanked.'
- Armstrong also co-founded New Limit, a longevity biotechnology company doing high-throughput screening to understand aging; the episode treats Coinbase and New Limit as a pair of bets on chain-based finance and on biology.
- On policy, Armstrong argues the US lacks experimentation sandboxes for financial innovation, and that special economic zones ('freedom cities') modeled on Shenzhen, Singapore and Dubai — and in effect on American federalism — would drive growth. Without a sandbox, entrepreneurs cannot iterate.
- Regulatory clarity is framed as the unlock for listing more products in more countries, with the everything exchange as 'bread and butter' and stablecoin payments as the next big emerging area.
