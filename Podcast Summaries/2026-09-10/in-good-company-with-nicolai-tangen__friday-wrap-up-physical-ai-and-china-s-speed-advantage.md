# Friday Wrap-up: Physical AI and China's Speed Advantage — In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen

## TL;DR

This Friday wrap-up discusses XPeng as a 'physical AI' company and the rapid advancement of autonomous driving and humanoid robotics, driven by China's deep supply chain and intense competition. The hosts highlight China's speed in EV production and technology adoption, contrasting it with Europe's slow response, and discuss implications for global markets and the fund's strategic focus on performance, speed, and ambition.

## Key points

- XPeng's Brian Zhu describes the company as a 'physical AI company', reflecting the increasing importance of software and chips in vehicles, covering driving, entertainment, and self-driving systems.
- Fully autonomous Level 4 vehicles (including no steering wheel) are expected within our lifetime, with a timeline in China: hands-off by 2028, eyes-off by 2030, and mind-off by 2035, enabling passengers to sleep during transit and transforming urban living.
- The supply chain for self-driving cars significantly overlaps with humanoid robotics, including sensors, actuators, software, and chips, giving China an advantage in both sectors due to its deep and wide supply chain.
- China's EV market is highly competitive with around 60 producers and roughly 500 new models launched recently, making exported Chinese vehicles extremely strong on both technology and pricing.
- China's domestic market is crucial for XPeng: Norway's EV sales were 98.7% of all car sales in August, but China's new car sales are 60% EV, providing a massive home market to refine products.
- Volkswagen Group announced elimination of 50,000 jobs and cutting half of its vehicle offerings, illustrating the pressure traditional automakers face from Chinese competition.
- Chinese exports increased 25%, with 50% of that increase coming from technology and AI-related exports, showing the impact of AI integration across industries.
- Europe has lost the first four layers of the AI tech stack (energy, chips, cloud, models) and has an opportunity primarily in the application layer, but there is little political will to drive it.
- Xiaomi went from no car business to a fully fledged car company in three years, whereas in Europe it takes three to five years just to develop a new model from an existing car company.
- The fund's strategic direction emphasizes performance, speed, and ambition; high ambition is necessary because even if you fail, you achieve great things, whereas low ambition leads to achieving nothing.
- Michael Moritz, original Sequoia partner, warned that Asian technology is becoming so powerful that it is competing out European car manufacturers, with implications for economic growth, unemployment, and political landscape; embedding AI in Europe is urgent to maintain democratic standards.
- The fund held a Tech Day in London, showcasing use cases from Oslo, Singapore, and New York, and launched an agent factory; already more than half of employees have built their own agents.
