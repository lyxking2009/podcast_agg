## Key Points
- Listen Labs automates the entire customer research pipeline — from crafting questions to conducting voice interviews to generating analysis and presentations — completing thousands of concurrent interviews in hours instead of weeks.
- People tend to be more honest with AI interviewers than human ones because the AI is non-judgmental, never makes respondents feel rushed, and reduces the social-desirability bias that plagues traditional moderated research.
- One year after launch, Listen serves hundreds of organizations including Microsoft, Google, NBC Universal, P&G, Anthropic, Cursor, and Cognition, and has conducted over 300,000 interviews.
- Interview transcripts — capturing stated motivations and intent — prove more predictive of customer behavior than traditional behavioral data sources such as credit card transactions or usage logs.
- Listen Labs validates its methodology through rigorous back-testing: running simulations against known outcomes to confirm which research questions yield reliable, actionable predictions.
- Eighty percent of the company's engineering resources are devoted to audience construction — ensuring the right respondents are reached, not merely any respondents, which is what makes research valid.
- Wahlforss and co-founder Florian Juengermann met at Harvard, first built a viral AI avatar app that gained 20,000 users on day one, then built an early Listen prototype to understand those users — and that internal tool became the company.
- The core thesis is that as AI reduces the cost of building software, the scarce resource shifts from engineering capacity to customer understanding: knowing what to build, for whom, and why becomes the primary competitive moat.
- Sequoia led both the seed and Series A rounds and uses Listen internally for industry research and customer due diligence, giving the partnership a direct product-feedback dimension.

## Overview
Alfred Wahlforss, co-founder and CEO of Listen Labs, discusses how his company built an AI agent that conducts thousands of simultaneous customer interviews drawn from a panel of 30 million people across 200+ countries. The episode explores the surprising finding that people are more honest with AI interviewers than human ones, and how deep customer understanding is becoming the scarce competitive advantage as AI lowers the cost of building software.

## Implications
As AI continues to commoditize software development, the ability to rapidly and reliably understand customer needs at scale may become the defining competitive differentiator for product companies. Listen Labs represents a bet that the research layer — historically slow, expensive, and hard to scale — can be fully automated without sacrificing depth or validity.
