# The SpaceX IPO, Fable 5, AI Capex Update & Market Check w/ Gavin Baker, Andrew Fox & Clark Tang

## TL;DR
SpaceX IPO at $135/share values the company at $1.77 trillion — the largest private-to-public transition in history. Goldman and JPMorgan project $160B revenue by 2028.

## Key Points

1. SpaceX IPO at $135/share values the company at $1.77 trillion — the largest private-to-public transition in history. Goldman and JPMorgan project $160B revenue by 2028.
2. Starlink + direct-to-cell creates a telecom moat. SpaceX's broadband constellation is orders of magnitude larger than competitors, with direct-to-cell enabling mobile connectivity without special hardware.
3. "Elon Web Services" — SpaceX is becoming an AI compute hyperscaler. xAI's Colossus cluster, Starship's rapid reusability, and orbital data centers position SpaceX as a new kind of cloud/AI infrastructure provider.
4. Orbital compute economics: $5B per gigawatt in space vs. $25B on the ground. Space-based data centers could eventually offer 5x cost advantage for certain workloads.
5. Fable 5 and the model race: frontier models (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI) capture 90% of AI revenue. Open source narrows the gap but doesn't close it. The "snapshot benchmark" problem — static benchmarks don't capture real-world agent performance.
6. AI CapEx disconnect: $1.5T in planned CapEx vs. $300B in AI revenue. The math works if AI becomes a general-purpose technology like electricity — the question is timing and adoption curves.
7. Nvidia vs. ASICs: GPUs remain dominant for training and flexible inference. Custom ASICs (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium) gain share for specific inference workloads but don't replace the GPU ecosystem.
8. The next $1 trillion companies: three candidates could reach $1T in half the time it took FAANG. SpaceX, xAI, and one unnamed contender discussed.

## Overview

Brad Gerstner hosts Gavin Baker (Atreides Management), Andrew Fox, and Clark Tang for a wide-ranging discussion on the SpaceX IPO, AI infrastructure, and the model race. The conversation opens with SpaceX's IPO mechanics and valuation ($1.77T at $135/share), then pivots to the broader AI compute thesis — SpaceX as "Elon Web Services" combining Starlink, xAI, and potential orbital data centers. The group debates whether SpaceX can 8x revenue in 4 years, post-IPO drawdown risks, and how to size the position. They then shift to the model race: Fable 5, Mythos, ChatGPT 5.5, and why frontier models capture 90% of AI revenue. The episode closes with a CapEx reality check — $1.5T in planned spend vs. $300B in revenue — and whether the math works if AI becomes a general-purpose technology.

## Implications

For investors: SpaceX represents a unique convergence of space infrastructure, telecom (Starlink), and AI compute. The IPO creates a liquid vehicle for exposure to all three mega-trends. The AI CapEx discussion suggests the market may be underestimating the duration and scale of the buildout — infrastructure plays (Nvidia, power, cooling) benefit even if specific AI applications disappoint. The frontier vs. open source dynamic favors companies with both model and distribution advantages.
