# What Is Bitcoin Telling Us? | Michael Howell, Macro Analyst — Bankless

## TL;DR
Michael Howell discusses his macro liquidity framework, arguing that Bitcoin may signal an earlier-than-expected turn in global liquidity if it holds above $70,000, though this is not his base case. He explains that rising bond yields reflect strong nominal GDP growth rather than fiscal deterioration, and that the Fed and Treasury are actively managing bank reserves and collateral to reduce volatility. Howell maintains that the speculation phase is ending and turbulence likely looms, but Federal Reserve and Treasury interventions could shorten the downturn.

## Key points
- Michael Howell previously predicted liquidity growth peaking in late 2025 and crypto being late cycle, which proved correct; he now sees speculation era ending and turbulence approaching, with global liquidity bottom expected in second half of 2027.
- Bitcoin may be an early warning of the liquidity tide turning if it holds above $70,000; this could indicate Fed/Treasury interventions causing shorter, shallower turbulence and earlier rebound, but Howell says this is not his base case and requires monitoring.
- Federal Reserve under Chair Kevin Walsh is managing bank reserves and repo collateral markets, not just Fed funds rate; after slashing reserves a year ago caused repo spread spike, Fed introduced Reserve Management Purchases (RMP), another form of QE, and bank reserves have recently flatlined.
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is not trying to push down yields but to reduce market volatility; he is retiring off-the-run treasuries at 60 cents on the dollar and replacing with shorter-duration debt, aiming for low bond volatility and ample reserves.
- Long-duration Treasury yields are rising because of strong nominal GDP growth, not fiscal deterioration; US term premia are low/flat compared to Japan, Britain, France, and yield increases are driven by higher rate expectations, which track NGDP growth closely.
- Liquidity is tightening because the economy is growing fast, not because central banks are tightening aggressively; evidence includes flattening yield curve, widening credit spreads, and Russell 2000 likely in bear market, while Fed tightening is at half pace.
- Higher bond yields will increase US debt service costs, accelerating future debasement; government is issuing more short-duration bills, which lowers average duration and expands liquidity, a positive for Bitcoin over time.
- Howell disagrees with Ray Dalio's view that 2027 will be bad for bonds due to supply/demand and loss of safe-haven status; Howell argues markets have no alternative to US Treasuries given their size and liquidity, and bonds could serve as safe haven in a liquidity downswing.
- The liquidity cycle and real economy are anti-cyclical; liquidity leads the real economy by about 18 months, and currently the liquidity cycle is down while the real economy is accelerating, consistent with late speculation phase.
- Asset allocation across Howell's four regimes: in turbulence, own bond duration and credits; in rebound, equities and credits; in calm, equities and commodities; in speculation, commodities and top-slice equities. For crypto, Bitcoin as monetary inflation hedge should outperform altcoins in downswing, while altcoins outperform in upswing.
- The speculation phase normally lasts 9-12 months, and while PE multiples are compressing as expected, an anomaly is strong earnings growth, especially in technology, which has extended equity market outperformance; AI capex and fiscal deficit are key drivers.
- Rising yields could stress AI funding; CoreWeave debt scare saw bond investors pricing 50% default probability over five years, and as liquidity tide goes out, high-yield borrowers may get squeezed; but government debt could be the asset to own in financial stress.
- AI is a major technological revolution comparable to internal combustion engine, but financial markets may not benefit proportionally; Global Crossing fiber optic boom led to 80% price collapse and Chapter 11, yet fiber optic still benefited economy.
