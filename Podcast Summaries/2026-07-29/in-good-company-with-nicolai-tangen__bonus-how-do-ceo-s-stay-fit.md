# Bonus: How do CEO's stay fit?

**Show:** In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen
**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Transcript source:** web

## Key Points
- This bonus episode compiles Nicolai Tangen's recurring 'how do you live' questions into a single reel, gathering fitness, sleep, nutrition and stress-management habits from fifteen past guests.
- Vas Narasimhan (Novartis CEO) follows a coach-taught framework of mindset, movement, nutrition and recovery, is a lifelong vegetarian who drinks two scoops of whey protein daily, and chases 7+ hours of sleep.
- Bob Iger (former Disney CEO) works out roughly an hour every morning without exception, calling it necessary 'for sanity and vanity,' and is in bed by 10-11pm.
- Stefano Domenicali (Formula One CEO) runs 8-10km before sunrise every day as uninterrupted thinking time.
- Tan Su Shan (DBS Bank Singapore CEO) credits meditation, learned after a personal crisis in her twenties, plus yoga and long runs.
- Author Dan Pink describes the 'scientifically optimal nap': drink coffee immediately before a 25-minute nap so caffeine kicks in right as you wake.
- Jack Dorsey eats one meal a day, has done 10-day silent meditation retreats annually, and now meditates about an hour every morning.
- Jane Fraser (Citigroup CEO) targets 7 hours of sleep and uses deliberate deep-breathing as meditation.
- Ari Emanuel (TKO CEO) wakes at 4:35am, works out 90 minutes, does a daily sauna plus ice bath, and runs quarterly 3-day fasts.
- Magnus Carlsen says 'if your body is fit, your mind is fit,' relying on golf, padel and weekly strength training.
- Tangen closes by sharing his own routine: gratitude practice, a cold fjord swim, sauna, and a 20-minute afternoon nap.

## Overview
This bonus episode departs from the show's usual one-guest format to compile answers to a question Nicolai Tangen asks almost every guest: how do you get up, sleep, exercise and switch off? He stitches together clips from fifteen chief executives, an investor, an author and a world chess champion. The reel moves from structured routines (Narasimhan's framework, Fraser's breathing-based meditation) through extreme discipline (Dorsey's one-meal-a-day eating, Emanuel's sauna-and-ice-bath ritual and quarterly fasts) to gentler practices (trail running, journaling, guitar, Dan Pink's engineered nap). Common threads: nearly everyone treats sleep as an actively managed lever, several use physical discomfort as deliberate training for mental toughness, and multiple guests reject the idea that overwork is a prerequisite for high performance. Tangen ends by revealing his own routine.

## Implications
The episode illustrates a broader normalization among elite business leaders of talking openly about physical and mental self-maintenance as a professional tool, linking nervous-system regulation and discomfort tolerance to better business judgment. Several guests push back against always-on hustle culture. For NBIM's audience, the format also serves a relationship-building purpose by aggregating personal, humanizing details from CEOs of companies NBIM holds as a major shareholder.
