## Key Points
- A wave of crypto wind-downs hit the same week: BitMEX is shutting down after 11 years, alongside BitMart, Movement Labs, and Balancer Labs — yet guest Nemil Dalal says he's 'never been more bullish.'
- BitMEX's core innovation (perps) has simply been absorbed industry-wide — the panel frames these closures as consolidation and maturation rather than failure of the underlying ideas.
- Growing regulatory clarity (CLARITY Act progress, SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce's statements) is reducing the need for governance tokens/DAO wrappers that existed to route around legal ambiguity.
- The episode responds to a viral tweet claiming almost every failed crypto idea (NFTs, creator tokens, DAOs, gaming, on-chain reputation) will eventually work like Bitcoin did; Haseeb Qureshi calls this 'toxic positivity.'
- Haseeb's core thesis: crypto is not an 'everything technology' like the internet — it's an 'everything financial technology'; ideas far from money/finance keep failing.
- Base's consumer pivot is cited as a case study: Coinbase's Jesse Pollak acknowledged the Base app's creator-token/social strategy was a distraction and is refocusing on real-world-asset/financial use cases.
- On-chain credit and reputation keep failing structurally: blockchain addresses are pseudonymous and unenforceable, so working on-chain credit systems end up reaching off-chain for KYC/identity.
- Nemil's YC framework for evaluating comeback ideas is 'why now' — what has concretely changed — citing Instacart vs. Webvan as the model.
- The group debates AI-agent payments (x402): whether machine-to-machine micropayments create decentralized rails or just recreate centralized bulk-licensing deals.
- Haseeb's big-picture argument: decentralization is not a natural end-state of technology — markets default toward centralization; only sustained user demand keeps a system decentralized.

## Overview
Hosts Haseeb Qureshi, Tom Schmidt, and Tarun Chitra are joined by Nemil Dalal, a Visiting Partner at Y Combinator and former Coinbase executive, on The Chopping Block. The episode opens against a backdrop of crypto wind-downs (BitMEX, BitMart, Movement Labs, Balancer Labs), which the group reads as maturation rather than failure since core innovations like perps have been absorbed by mainstream exchanges. The bulk of the episode debates a viral tweet arguing nearly every failed crypto idea will eventually work like Bitcoin did — Haseeb pushes back hard, arguing crypto is fundamentally a money/finance technology, not an everything technology, while Nemil is more sympathetic but insists on a 'why now' test. The conversation covers Coinbase's Base app pivot away from consumer/creator-token experiments, structural reasons on-chain credit keeps failing (pseudonymity, unenforceability), and AI-agent micropayments (x402) possibly recreating centralization rather than decentralizing. The episode closes on Dalal's framing that decentralization requires sustained demand to persist rather than being inevitable.

## Implications
The episode captures a maturing-industry mood: crypto's narrative is shifting from 'every experiment eventually vindicates itself' toward a demand-driven framework for evaluating ideas, with regulatory clarity reducing the incentive to wrap products in DAOs purely for legal cover. Capital is more likely to reward infrastructure and B2B plays than consumer social/creator-token experiments, and launching a token pre-PMF is increasingly viewed as a red flag. The AI-agent payments discussion signals an emerging battleground over whether the AI/crypto intersection produces genuinely decentralized infrastructure or relocates centralization into a new technical wrapper.
