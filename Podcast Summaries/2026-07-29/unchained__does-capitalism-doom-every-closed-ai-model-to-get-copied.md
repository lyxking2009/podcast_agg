# Does Capitalism Doom Every Closed AI Model to Get Copied?

**Show:** Unchained
**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Transcript source:** web_unchained

## Key Points
- NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang broke years of silence on X to publish an open letter arguing that open-weight AI models are a national asset, not just a business choice.
- Within two days of the letter, the list of signatory companies doubled to roughly 50, but Anthropic and Amazon have still not signed on.
- Nick Carter argued the government doesn't owe large AI labs a guaranteed business model.
- An Anthropic researcher publicly needled Huang by asking why NVIDIA doesn't open-source CUDA if openness is so virtuous; Andrew Ng called that comparison a false equivalence.
- Bloomberg's Joe Weisenthal questioned whether any of the open-letter posturing amounts to more than virtue signaling.
- Guest Lorenzo Valente (ARK Invest) argued cheap open models aren't actually cheap once costs are measured per completed task rather than per raw parameter or license.
- Valente also asked why the U.S. has no policy answer to Chinese lab DeepSeek reportedly raising at a $70 billion valuation.
- Co-host Ram Ahluwalia closed with a historical analogy: the Wright brothers held foundational aviation patents but watched Boeing and Lockheed Martin build the entire aerospace industry on top without collecting meaningful royalties.
- The episode frames the core tension as whether capitalism's competitive dynamics make it structurally inevitable that closed AI models get replicated or commoditized by open alternatives.

## Overview
This short clip from Bits + Bips (a markets-and-macro show under the Unchained network) is built around NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang's sudden entry into the open-vs-closed AI debate via his first-ever post on X, framing open-weight AI models as a national asset. Host Austin Campbell walks co-hosts Ram Ahluwalia and Chris Perkins, along with guest Lorenzo Valente of ARK Invest, through the argument that broke out beneath the letter — from Nick Carter's claim the government doesn't owe closed-model labs a business model, to an Anthropic researcher's CUDA counter-jab, to Weisenthal's skepticism about corporate virtue signaling. Valente presses on why 'cheap' open models aren't actually cheap per task, and why the U.S. lacks a policy response to DeepSeek's reported $70B valuation. Ahluwalia closes with the Wright brothers analogy, implicitly suggesting closed AI labs could suffer the same fate — pioneering the technology but ceding most of the long-run value to downstream builders.

## Implications
Framing open models as a 'national asset' recasts AI openness as a competitiveness and national-security issue rather than a purely commercial decision, which could shape U.S. government approaches to export controls, subsidies, and antitrust scrutiny of closed labs versus open-weight challengers. The Wright brothers analogy underscores a risk that pioneering closed-model labs could cede most of the long-run economic value of their innovation to downstream integrators — directly relevant to how investors value AI-model companies versus AI-infrastructure companies.
