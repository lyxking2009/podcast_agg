## Overview
In this episode of RiskReversal Pod, host Dan Nathan sits down with Adam Nelson, a fintech-focused partner at FirstMark Capital, to unpack how stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets are reshaping banking and payments. The conversation frames the GENIUS Act as a watershed moment that gave stablecoin issuers regulatory clarity in the US, accelerating institutional adoption, and points to Circle's net income rivaling major banks as evidence of how lucrative stablecoin issuance has become. From there, the discussion turns to how incumbents Visa and Mastercard are responding to the competitive threat that stablecoin rails pose to their card-network business models. Nelson discusses the infrastructure layer being built around stablecoins, citing FirstMark portfolio companies Rain and Velocity, and the two use these examples to illustrate why traditional banking moats are increasingly vulnerable to faster, always-on, blockchain-based alternatives. The episode wraps with a broader look at adjacent areas of on-chain finance growth, including Hyperliquid, perpetual futures, and prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket.

## Implications
The episode's throughline is that stablecoins and tokenization are no longer a crypto-native niche but a direct structural challenge to incumbent financial infrastructure: card networks, correspondent banking, and treasury services. Regulatory clarity from the GENIUS Act appears to be the catalyst unlocking mainstream and institutional capital into this space. For VCs like FirstMark, this creates an investable infrastructure layer analogous to earlier fintech-rail buildouts, suggesting continued venture capital flow into stablecoin and tokenization infrastructure through 2026 and beyond.
