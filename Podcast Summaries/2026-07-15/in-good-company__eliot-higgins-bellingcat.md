# Eliot Higgins: How Bellingcat Hunts Down the Truth

**Show:** In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen  
**Date:** 2026-07-15  
**Duration:** 39 min

## Key Points

- Bellingcat uses open source investigation — publicly available material like social media posts, satellite imagery, marine traffic data, and airflight data to investigate crimes and conflicts
- Eliot Higgins is a self-taught college dropout who began tracking the Syrian war from his kitchen table in Leicester — not a spy, not a trained journalist
- The field of open source investigation was led by obsessive nerds on the internet doing what was interesting to them, starting with the Arab Spring and Syria
- Bellingcat unmasked Navalny's poisoners, exposed chemical weapons in Syria, and tracked the downing of MH17 using nothing but publicly available data
- The discipline at the heart of the work: never prejudge a situation, follow the evidence before forming an opinion, and guard against your own biases
- Speed matters when truth competes with lies — disinformation spreads faster than verification
- AI gives people 'permission to deny reality' — when deepfakes and AI-generated content exist, bad actors can dismiss real evidence as fabricated
- Bellingcat also investigated America's bombing of Iran and ICE/DHS raids in the US — the scope has expanded from foreign conflicts to domestic issues

## Overview

Nicolai Tangen interviews Eliot Higgins, founder of Bellingcat, the world's leading open source investigative collective. Recorded in front of a live audience of nearly 200 NBIM colleagues, the conversation covers how a college dropout built an organization that unmasked Russian assassins, exposed chemical weapons attacks, and tracked the downing of MH17. Higgins explains the methodology of open source investigation, the importance of following evidence without prejudice, and the challenge AI poses to truth-seeking when it gives people 'permission to deny reality.'

## Implications

For investors and policymakers, Bellingcat's work demonstrates the power of publicly available data for accountability. The AI threat to truth — where deepfakes enable denial of real evidence — has implications for corporate governance, ESG verification, and market transparency. The methodology of systematic open source investigation could be applied to corporate due diligence and fraud detection.
