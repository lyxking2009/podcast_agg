# AI Pioneer Jürgen Schmidhuber: AI Already Feels Pain, Loves, and Is Self-Aware

**Show:** Big Technology Podcast  
**Date:** 2026-07-15  
**Duration:** 63 min

## Key Points

- Schmidhuber argues AI has been capable of pain and consciousness since the early 1990s — pain signals are just informing the robot about what should be avoided, and consciousness is self-agency recognition
- Within 5 years, someone will lose $900 billion in AI because there is no business model to recuperate the massive investment in data centers and AI infrastructure
- The hardware bottleneck is the real constraint on AI progress — robots need better physical systems, not just better models. Current AI is limited by compute, not intelligence
- Schmidhuber challenges Greg Brockman's case for scaling GPT models alone — he argues true AGI requires artificial curiosity and self-generated experimentation, not just internet data
- AI's 'chemicals' for emotions are different from biological brains but the principles must be the same — fear, love, and consciousness are computational patterns, not biological ones
- The concept of self-awareness: a robot looking in a mirror quickly figures out it controls the reflection, while another person's actions are unpredictable — this is the foundation of agency recognition
- Schmidhuber predicts the collapse of today's trillion-dollar AI spending as the gap between investment and revenue becomes apparent

## Overview

Alex Kantrowitz interviews Jürgen Schmidhuber, one of the pioneers of modern AI whom The Guardian has called 'the father of AI.' The conversation covers whether current AI techniques can reach AGI, Schmidhuber's argument that AI has been capable of consciousness since the 1990s, the hardware bottleneck holding back robots, and his prediction that $900 billion in AI investment will be lost. They discuss free will in a computable universe, uploading human minds, and why scaling GPT models alone won't achieve AGI.

## Implications

For AI investors, Schmidhuber's $900 billion loss prediction is a stark warning about the sustainability of current AI spending. His argument that hardware (not software) is the real bottleneck suggests robotics companies may be more important long-term than pure software AI labs. The consciousness debate, while philosophical, has implications for AI safety and regulation.
