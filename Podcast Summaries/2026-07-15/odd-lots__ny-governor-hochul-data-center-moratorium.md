# NY Governor Kathy Hochul on Her One Year Data Center Moratorium

**Show:** Odd Lots  
**Date:** 2026-07-15  
**Duration:** 42 min

## Key Points

- New York State became the first state to institute a formal data center moratorium, at least for one year — Governor Kathy Hochul announced the policy
- The moratorium comes amid growing anti-data center politics that crosses party lines — one of the rare areas of bipartisan agreement
- Maine attempted a similar moratorium but it was vetoed by the governor, making New York's move a potential template for other states
- The AI industry faces a paradox: there's talk of compute scarcity, yet data center backlash is growing. Some states still offer tax breaks to attract data centers
- Data center moratoriums could impact the compute supply chain for AI training and inference, potentially slowing AI development in affected states
- The conversation explores the tension between energy consumption, local community impacts, and the economic benefits of data center development
- Hochul's moratorium is framed as a pause for planning rather than a permanent ban — the goal is to understand impacts before allowing further construction

## Overview

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway discuss New York's groundbreaking data center moratorium with Governor Kathy Hochul. The episode explores the growing bipartisan backlash against data center construction, the tension between AI compute needs and local community concerns, and whether other states will follow New York's lead. The conversation touches on energy consumption, tax incentives, and the paradox of compute scarcity coexisting with data center opposition.

## Implications

For AI infrastructure investors, this moratorium signals increasing regulatory risk around data center construction. The bipartisan nature of the opposition means this isn't a partisan issue — it could spread to other states. Companies with significant data center plans in New York or other states facing similar pressure may need to adjust their expansion timelines.
