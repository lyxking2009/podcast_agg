# At The Money: When Should Do-It-Yourself Investors Fire Themselves?

**Show:** Masters in Business  
**Date:** 2026-07-15  
**Duration:** 18 min

## Key Points

- DIY investors in the FIRE movement often start with a 'why pay someone else' mentality that works well during the accumulation phase when mistakes are cheap and time horizon is long
- The accumulation phase is relatively easy to manage yourself — index investing, regular contributions, and staying the course. The risk of messing up is low when you're young
- The real complexity comes during decumulation — when you need to draw down assets, manage tax implications, and plan for healthcare costs in retirement
- Financial advice should be holistic, not just about net worth targets — it should encompass family, travel, career, and life goals (the 'balcony view' of your life)
- Red flags for DIY investors: concentrating risk in career + business + investments simultaneously, overconfidence after a long bull market, and inability to control emotions during drawdowns
- The biopsychosocial model from medicine applies to financial planning — understanding how money fits into your broader life environment
- Even experienced investors benefit from a 'financial checkup' similar to medical checkups — periodic professional review of assumptions and plans

## Overview

Barry Ritholtz interviews Dr. Jordan Grummet (Doc G), a physician turned financial independence advocate, about when DIY investors should bring in professional help. The conversation explores the FIRE movement's origins, the difference between accumulation and decumulation phases, and the warning signs that an investor has become overconfident. Doc G argues that while young investors in the accumulation phase can mostly manage on their own, the complexity of retirement planning — tax optimization, healthcare costs, withdrawal strategies — often warrants professional guidance. The episode emphasizes that financial planning should be holistic, encompassing life goals beyond just net worth targets.

## Implications

For DIY investors approaching or in retirement, this episode makes a strong case for periodic professional review. The distinction between accumulation (simple) and decumulation (complex) is crucial — many investors who built wealth successfully on their own may struggle with the withdrawal phase. The 'balcony view' concept is useful for framing financial planning conversations with advisors.

## Notable Quotes

- "When you are young and you're in the accumulation phase, it's almost hard to mess up" — Doc G
