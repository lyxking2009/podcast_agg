# 28. 女性与叙事：谁在讲述，又为了什么而讲述？

## Key Points

- Narrative authority — who has the right to tell stories about women — is the central question of feminist narratology; "the voice carries form; authority is the purpose"
- Contemporary films including 《给外婆的信》(Letters to My Grandmother) and 《主君的太阳》(The Master's Sun) illustrate how individual and social narratives interweave in women's storytelling
- Male narrative mediation has historically shaped how women's stories are framed, even in ostensibly feminist or sympathetic texts
- Wang Anyi's novels and the French documentary *Delfina and Carol: The Rebellious Muse* show how women reconstruct historical experience and navigate the power vacuums created by new media
- Women's trauma narratives differ structurally from traditional heroic journeys — they resist and challenge the dominant narrative arc built around growth, triumph, and resolution
- Web fiction and podcasts offer alternative narrative structures that bypass traditional publishing gatekeepers, substantially expanding the space for women's self-authored storytelling in China

## Overview

In this nearly three-hour episode, the hosts of 西西弗高速 examine narrative as a cultural form through a feminist lens, asking who tells women's stories and why. Rather than approaching from formal literary theory, the discussion uses contemporary films, Wang Anyi's novels, and the French documentary *Delfina and Carol: The Rebellious Muse* to investigate the elements of narrative authority and mediation. The episode moves through three thematic areas: contemporary voices (how individual and social narratives interweave in recent films), historical exploration (how women reconstruct historical experience), and new media possibilities (how web fiction and podcasting create alternative structures for women's voices outside traditional publishing mechanisms). The discussion spans feminist narratology, the construction of female mythology, and the structural differences between women's trauma narratives and the conventional heroic journey arc.

## Implications

- Digital platforms and new media fundamentally alter the gatekeeping dynamics that have historically controlled women's narrative authority — the barriers to self-publishing and self-broadcasting have collapsed
- Feminist narratology provides analytical tools that can reveal hidden power structures in even sympathetic or progressive storytelling, making it a practical critical lens rather than merely an academic one
- The rise of web fiction and podcast culture in China represents a significant structural shift in who controls cultural narrative production, with implications for representation and the diversity of stories reaching mass audiences
- The structural difference between women's trauma narratives and the heroic journey arc points to a mismatch between dominant storytelling frameworks and lived female experience — a gap that new media forms may be better positioned to close
