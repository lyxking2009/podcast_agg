# Money Stuff: The Podcast — Frisky Dentists

## Key Points

- GameStop's takeover bid for eBay appears to be Ryan Cohen's convoluted job application to become eBay's CEO rather than a serious acquisition attempt. The deal structure is flawed - GameStop lacks both sufficient cash ($20B needed) and authorized shares for the stock portion.
- Apollo is launching MAPS (Multi Asset Prime Securities), essentially CLOs for private credit that securitize direct loans rather than syndicated bank loans. This represents Apollo's push to make private credit more liquid and tradeable, similar to the broadly syndicated loan market.
- Anthropic and OpenAI issued stern warnings against secondary market stock sales through SPVs and forward contracts, threatening legal action and claiming such transfers violate company restrictions. This could affect billions in purported AI company exposure through secondary markets.
- Goldman Sachs president John Waldron described investment banks as 'human assembly lines' facing automation, suggesting AI will replace repetitive tasks currently done by junior analysts while potentially creating new high-level oversight roles.
- Apollo's push for daily pricing in private credit is seen as a stepping stone toward establishing an active trading market, moving away from the traditional relationship-driven, hold-to-maturity model of private lending.
- The GameStop-eBay situation highlights how stock-for-stock deals differ from cash deals in that target shareholders must evaluate the acquirer's management capabilities, not just the price offered.

## Overview

Matt Levine and Katie Greifeld dissect the week's most intriguing financial stories, starting with GameStop's bizarre takeover attempt of eBay that appears more like CEO Ryan Cohen's job application than a serious acquisition. They explore how the deal's structural problems - lacking both cash and authorized shares - reveal it as performance rather than substance. The conversation shifts to Apollo's new securitization product MAPS, which repackages private credit loans similarly to CLOs, representing a broader trend toward making private credit more liquid and tradeable. They discuss warnings from Anthropic and OpenAI about secondary market stock sales through SPVs, which could invalidate billions in AI exposure. The episode concludes with Goldman Sachs' description of investment banks as 'human assembly lines' facing AI automation, raising questions about the future of junior banking roles.

## Implications

For founders and PMs, the AI company warnings signal increased scrutiny of secondary market liquidity and potential legal risks for employees selling equity. Engineers should note the banking automation trends as AI increasingly handles routine analytical work. The private credit securitization trend suggests continued institutional appetite for alternative credit products with better liquidity features. Investors should be wary of secondary market AI exposures that may not be legally valid.
