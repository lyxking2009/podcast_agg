# Uneasy Money: Is Anthropic's IPO a 'Must Buy' or an Existential Gamble? — Unchained

## TL;DR
Anthropic is pricing its IPO with 825 million Class A shares at $70–$78, raising $64 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation, with up to 20% going to retail. Hosts K Ark, Taylor Monahan, and Austin Griffith debate whether the IPO is a 'must buy' or an existential gamble, weighing Anthropic's dominance in AI (Opus 5.5) against internal governance risks and rising competition from Google, Meta, and OpenAI. They also cover a week of major outages (Microsoft, FAA, Apple) and the Optimizely supply-chain hack, then shift to security advice for crypto teams: prioritize continuous monitoring, AI-assisted audits, and autonomous circuit breakers over relying solely on one-off human audits.

## Key points
- Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares priced between $70 and $78, aiming to raise $64 billion in cash at a $1.4 trillion valuation, with up to 20% of shares allocated to retail investors; trading is expected to start Thursday on NASDAQ.
- Hosts describe Anthropic as the 'front frog' in AI, noting its Opus 5.5 model dominated for about six weeks, and even after newer models like Fable 5.2, Astra 6.2, and Google's Carbon emerged, they found Opus 5.5 still acceptable—a first in the current AI race.
- The biggest risks to Anthropic, according to co-host Taylor Monahan, are internal governance and culture—'governance nonsense and EA craziness'—rather than the existential AI risk listed in its disclosures; hosts fear Anthropic could 'shoot themselves in the foot or the face.'
- For investors to 'make bank' on the IPO, Anthropic would need to reach roughly a $5 trillion market cap (a clean 4x from the $1.4T IPO valuation), which is possible but requires multiple factors to align; some are pattern matching the SpaceX IPO for a post-listing dip buying opportunity.
- A series of high-profile outages occurred during the week: Microsoft suffered two separate outages, the FAA went down due to 'frequencies stopping working' (its first major grounding since 2023, with the prior event being 9/11), and Apple—normally reliable—also experienced a rare outage.
- The Optimizely supply-chain hack involved attackers injecting a JavaScript skimmer into a plugin used by Ledger, Zoom, HSBC, BBC, and an estimated 160 million websites, yet the attackers extracted only about $7,000, which hosts likened to the 2020 Twitter hack where teenagers made ~$160,000 despite full access to major accounts.
- AI is now advanced enough to exploit human-written code vulnerabilities, leading to a transition period where AI can write better code than humans, but 'normies' still overwhelmingly prefer human-written software for critical systems like flight tracking, surgery, and autonomous vehicles.
- Taylor Monahan recommends crypto teams shift from one-off audits to continuous monitoring and detection: use agent-based anomaly detection, red team their own Web2 infrastructure, and design autonomous kill switches or circuit breakers that activate on predefined invariants (e.g., $200 million never leaves a contract) to prevent catastrophic loss.
- Austin Griffith cautions that the '$1 audit' is still a meme: pointing Mythos at a smart contract is not yet on par with a $50,000 human audit because audit firms combine experienced humans, their own tools, and Mythos, and he expects a long transition before AI-only audits match that level.
- The hosts discuss The DAO hack as a lesson in circuit breakers: the 28-day time lock allowed white-hat intervention, and they argue that immutable code plus predefined kill switches or upgrade paths is now preferable to pure immutability, as long as no single human has unilateral kill power.
