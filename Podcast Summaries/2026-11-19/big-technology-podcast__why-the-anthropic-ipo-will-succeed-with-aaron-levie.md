# Why The Anthropic IPO Will Succeed — With Aaron Levie — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Aaron Levie, CEO of Box, makes the bull case for Anthropic's public-market debut, arguing that enterprise AI adoption is still in its early stages and will expand far beyond coding into cybersecurity, legal, manufacturing, and other trillion-dollar TAMs. He highlights Anthropic's revenue growth from $1.4B in Q3 2025 to $17B in Q3 2026, a jump from 950 to 11,900 customers spending over $100K annually, and inference revenue per compute dollar rising from $0.40 to $1.72. Levie contends frontier models will retain value as orchestrators even as cheaper open-weight models proliferate, and he addresses bear concerns about debt, token maxing, and diminishing returns, arguing the scale of demand supports growth despite acknowledged duration risks.

## Key points
- Anthropic's revenue grew roughly 12x from $1.4 billion in Q3 2025 to $17 billion in Q3 2026, a growth rate Aaron Levie calls unprecedented but cautions against expecting another 12x in the next 12 months.
- The number of Anthropic customers spending $100,000 or more annually jumped from 950 in September 2025 to 11,900 at the time of recording, which Levie sees as evidence of broad-based enterprise utility and real adoption.
- Anthropic's revenue per dollar of compute spend improved from $0.40 in Q1 2025 to $1.72, indicating that inference has become profitable and the company can generate cash despite expensive training.
- Levie argues AI adoption remains in the early phases, with coding currently the dominant use case but large adjacent TAMs emerging in cybersecurity, legal work, manufacturing, product design, sales, and life sciences, all of which will require vastly more tokens.
- Agentic workflows break the historical link between human time and output; Levie describes giving an agent a goal and a budget, such as $20,000 for life-sciences research or $5 million for enterprise security, leading to uncapped token consumption.
- Frontier models will remain economically valuable as orchestrators: enterprises will use the most capable models for reasoning, tool use, and decision-making while routing bulk processing to cheaper open-weight models, so total token spend and frontier spend both increase (a Jevons paradox effect).
- Levie reports hands-on testing of Anthropic's Fable 5.2 and Google's Gemini Carbon shows a step-change in reasoning and product prototyping; models can now research 20 companies and synthesize designs no individual product manager could produce, indicating generalization beyond narrow coding.
- Responding to bear concerns about Anthropic's $900 billion in future liabilities, Levie says the debt question is downstream of growth expectations: if the company generates hundreds of billions in annual revenue, the commitments are manageable, and if not, it blows up.
- On the revenue growth slowdown attributed to token maxing (sequential growth of 147% from Q1 to Q2, then 47% from Q2 to Q3), Levie dismisses token maxing as a short-lived phenomenon not central to Anthropic's business.
- On diminishing returns and narrower task applicability, Levie contends post-training costs are in millions to billions, not hundreds of billions per domain; he lists multiple trillion-dollar TAMs (cyber, legal, manufacturing, bio, product design) where capturing 10-20% would support frontier labs, and cites cybersecurity public market cap of roughly half a trillion dollars as evidence of the opportunity.
- Levie acknowledges a possible duration mismatch where adoption takes longer than capital markets allow and money runs out, but argues current usage and compute limits show strong demand; he sees the lag between Silicon Valley and the rest of the world as opportunity rather than fatal risk.
- Beyond Anthropic, Levie announced Box Architect, an agent that can autonomously design entire enterprise workflows and contract management systems using Box primitives, and Box Industries for vertical solutions in banking, public sector, and media/entertainment.
- Levie says he personally participated in SpaceX and would similarly buy Anthropic shares, emphasizing this is not investment advice.
