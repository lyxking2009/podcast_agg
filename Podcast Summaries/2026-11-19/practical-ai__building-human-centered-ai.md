# Building Human-Centered AI — Practical AI

## TL;DR
Dr. Gautham Pallapa, Principal Director for AI and Innovation at Scotiabank, discusses how the bank is adopting generative and agentic AI while maintaining human-centered values. He shares the 'Invaluable' framework, developed from over 600 hours of executive workshops and 400+ interviews, for identifying value, defining success, and leveraging AI. Pallapa emphasizes that happy people are productive people, technology is rooted in empathy, and the ultimate goal is to improve human quality of life, cautioning against shoehorning AI without clear value.

## Key points
- Gautham Pallapa is Principal Director for AI and Innovation at Scotiabank, a top-five Canadian bank with operations across North America and EMEA, and author of the book 'Invaluable'. He holds a PhD in machine learning and AI and previously worked at Pivotal/VMware on Tanzu, Kubernetes, and cloud-native application development.
- Banking has traditionally been risk-averse due to compliance, asset protection, and a low risk appetite, but COVID-19 forced digital transformation as customers now expect mobile-first banking; banks are investing in generative AI and agentic systems to improve customer experience and speed to market.
- To select AI opportunities, Pallapa advises first identifying organizational value and defining measurable success, then asking how AI can help; he prioritizes low-hanging fruit like customer service agents and compliance support, while avoiding fully autonomous financial transactions until trust in agents is established.
- The 'Invaluable' framework, based on over 600 hours of executive workshops, 400+ interviews, and coaching 500 companies, guides organizations through eight steps: identify value, define success, measure success, establish priorities, leverage AI, optimize processes, overlay risk, and deliver value.
- Pallapa's core belief is that 'happy people are productive people' and that technology is rooted in empathy; AI should reduce pain for customers and employees, shifting humans from low-value repetitive tasks like coding and documentation to high-value activities involving creativity, context, and intent.
- He educates teams that AI agents are not truly intelligent but nondeterministic probabilistic models that evaluate risk-reward functions; agents lack real-world context, street smarts, and the ability to handle conflicting information, so human oversight and trust-building remain essential.
- At Scotiabank, Pallapa rebooted the internship program to operate like a startup: interns form stable teams, pitch for venture funding from executives, and are discouraged from 'vibe coding'; the emphasis is on software engineering over coding, with humans acting as system architects who guide agent teams.
- Pallapa experiments with multi-agent patterns including swarms, orchestrator patterns, and balanced teams of agents (e.g., BA agent, design agent, testing agent) to handle developer experience tasks like converting user stories into designs, rapid prototyping, and iterating with customers.
- He shared five agentic AI patterns at MCP Dev Summit and stresses that swarms are not a universal solution; organizations should mirror high-performing human teams, use shared memory and guardrails, and employ evaluation juries to guide agent output and prevent hallucinations.
- For the future, Pallapa warns against shoehorning AI into everything and urges organizations to ask 'Do we really need AI?'; the ultimate goal is to use technology to improve human quality of life, increase global value delivery, and improve the happiness index across the world.
