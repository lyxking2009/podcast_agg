## TL;DR
Daniel Mahncke and Shawn O'Malley analyze Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL), which missed AI in 2025 but became the best-performing Mag7 stock and most valuable company, closing a record fiscal year with revenue up 15% to $479 billion. They highlight how Tim Cook turned Apple into a toll road: Services is a $109 billion business with mid-70s gross margins generating over 40% of gross profit, and more than $850 billion in buybacks cut share count by a third. The toll faces pressure from courts pushing App Store fees toward 15% and Services growth slowing to 10%, while Apple's AI strategy—own the device, rent the model—raises the question of whether the phone remains the interface for AI or agents diminish the app economy; they discuss valuation and what they would pay.

## Key points
- In 2025, Apple was widely seen as the company that missed AI, but it has since become the best-performing Mag7 stock and reclaimed the title of the world's most valuable company.
- Apple closed a record fiscal year with revenue up 15% to $479 billion, driven by the strongest iPhone cycle in years.
- Apple spends about $13 billion annually on capex, while Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta are spending around $600 billion combined this year.
- The stock now trades at close to 40 times earnings, compared to roughly 12 times a decade ago.
- Services has grown into a $109 billion business with mid-70s gross margins and contributes over 40% of Apple's gross profit; more than $850 billion in buybacks have reduced the share count by a third.
- The services toll is under pressure: courts are pushing App Store fees toward 15%, and Services growth slowed to 10% last quarter.
- Google pays Apple $20 billion per year for search, while Apple pays Google only about $1 billion for the Gemini models behind the new Siri.
- Apple's AI strategy is the opposite of Meta's and Google's—own the device, rent the model—and the key question is whether the phone remains the interface for AI or whether agents make the app economy and Apple's cut matter less.
