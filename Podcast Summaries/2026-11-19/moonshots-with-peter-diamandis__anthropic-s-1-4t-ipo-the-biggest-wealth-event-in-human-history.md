# Anthropic’s $1.4T IPO: The Biggest Wealth Event in Human History? — Moonshots with Peter Diamandis

## TL;DR
Anthropic's IPO at a $1.4T valuation and the resulting concentration of AI wealth in San Francisco dominate this episode, alongside NVIDIA's massive earnings and the fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation. The hosts also break down recent FAA, bank, and Microsoft outages as symptoms of fragile legacy infrastructure, celebrate SpaceX catching a Starship with Mechazilla, and analyze escalating US-China chip restrictions. They close with takes on GPT-6.2 and AMA questions about agents, education, and recycling.

## Key points
- Anthropic begins trading under ticker ANT with an offering of 825 million shares marketed at $70–$78, raising up to $64B and valuing the company at $1.4T, still below SpaceX's $85.7B raise in June; Q3 revenue was $17B with an $81B annualized run rate as of Oct 31, and seven companies including Google, NVIDIA, AMD, and Microsoft committed $26B in non-voting shares.
- The average Anthropic employee holds shares worth $72M at the IPO midpoint, while the average OpenAI insider's stake is worth almost $126M based on recent valuation; San Francisco, with 0.1% of world population, now absorbs more than a third of global VC, minting thousands of fortunes over $10M.
- NVIDIA reported Q3 revenue of $115.8B, up 103% YoY, with data center revenue of $107B and net income of $65B, yet the stock fell 1.5%; interim CEO Colette Kress guided Q4 revenue to $130B, as the board rejects Jensen Huang's offer to return amid concerns about no succession plan and governance failures.
- The hosts discuss how Jensen Huang's resignation differs from Sam Altman's OpenAI ouster: Jensen quit voluntarily after surrounding himself with family and ignoring succession planning, and time passing makes it harder for him to return; predictions range from activist investors intervening to no return due to espionage concerns.
- In one week, Microsoft Outlook/Exchange logged ~130,000 reports across 74 countries, the FAA issued a 4+ hour nationwide ground stop with 12,462 flights canceled, three major banks experienced service disruptions, and a confirmed Optimizely script injection hit 178 organizations including CNN, Zoom, and HSBC.
- The hosts argue these outages reveal single points of failure and legacy hardware/software that AI now makes cheap to exploit; cyber offense is automated and parallel while defense remains human-in-the-loop, creating a dangerous asymmetry that requires a broad 'great refactoring' of embedded systems.
- SpaceX's Ship 42 achieved the first-ever Mechazilla catch of a Starship upper stage after 22.5 hours in orbit and deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites; Elon Musk now projects propellant costs of $500K per flight and 200-ton payloads, dropping per-person orbit costs below $1,000.
- Peter Diamandis calculates that with fully reusable Starship, fuel at 25% of operating cost yields total flight costs of $4–8M and $27–53/kg, a ~1,000-fold reduction from the Space Shuttle's $54,000/kg; Steve Jurvetson's chart shows the steep decline in cost per kg across Falcon and Starship.
- The US and nine partners signed the San Francisco Declaration with five working groups on policy, adoption, supply chain, safe harbor, and security; Commerce is preparing an interim rule requiring licenses for advanced AI chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil to close China backdoors.
- The White House is weighing a rule requiring US companies to disclose use of Chinese AI models; Airbnb, for example, uses Alibaba's Quen through approved US providers, while hosts debate whether labels or bans are effective, noting open-weight models can be retrained at one-tenth to one-hundredth the cost.
- SpaceX AI completed Mini-Hard, part of Colossus II, in about five months, with Gun Range targeted for four months compared to industry's 8–12 months; Semi-Analysis credits Tesla's modular electrical and cooling gear and parallel construction, giving an organizational velocity advantage.
- OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Soul and Astra, with Astra being the first release model to meet OpenAI's high threshold for AI self-improvement; Alex states his preference remains Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 for overall balance, while noting leaderboard shifts in math benchmarks.
- On audience questions: agents will first shift customer service from supervising tasks to outcomes; education should focus on mindset, purpose, and entrepreneurship; one AI can directly adjust another's weights via weight-space programming; and robotics plus AI sorting could make recycling economically viable.
