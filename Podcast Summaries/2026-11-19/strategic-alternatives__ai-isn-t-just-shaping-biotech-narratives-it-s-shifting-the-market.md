# AI isn’t just shaping biotech narratives, it’s shifting the market — Strategic Alternatives

## TL;DR
RBC analysts Rishi Jaluria and Leonid Timashev discuss their report 'Does Healthcare Dream of Electric Sheep?' showing that since late March through August, biotech market performance has become inversely correlated with AI and semiconductor trades due to defensive capital rotation. They identify AI's greatest biotech impact in early drug discovery and regulatory document generation, but human verification and fixed FDA timelines limit acceleration. Key signals for biotech investors include AI compute scaling, open-weight model adoption, geopolitical supply-chain risk, cybersecurity, and CapEx—each with quantified 5–25% potential biotech moves—underscoring the need to monitor cross-sector capital flows.

## Key points
- The RBC Imagine report 'Does Healthcare Dream of Electric Sheep?' by Rishi Jaluria and Leonid Timashev examined the relationship between AI and biotech market performance, motivated by index moves suggesting AI impacts were underappreciated by biotech investors focused on interest rates and government policy.
- Historically the AI-biotech correlation was positive, but from March 30 to August 10, biotech rallied while semiconductor and AI names declined, creating an inverse correlation that the authors attribute to fund flows out of AI and into biotech rather than traditional biotech drivers such as M&A, FDA flexibility, policy stability, or drug pricing.
- The inverse relationship is strongest among large-cap biotech names because they are more liquid and viewed as defensive hedges against AI/semiconductor volatility, whereas smaller-cap biotech shows a less significant correlation; whether the dynamic is temporary or structural depends on AI scalability, biotech's defensive status, and interest-rate/policy impacts.
- Biotech including pharma accounts for about 12% of the S&P 500 compared to AI at roughly 50%, so outflows from AI into biotech are more acute and identifiable; much of the biotech inflow may reflect investors defending against volatility rather than confidence in biotech-specific clinical or regulatory catalysts.
- Rishi Jaluria explains that when the AI trade comes under pressure, capital exits high-volatility tech and rotates into more defensive, less AI-exposed sectors like biotech, potentially creating a feedback loop where AI weakness reinforces biotech inflows and strengthens the inverse correlation.
- Leonid Timashev identifies the greatest AI impact in early drug development, including accelerating molecule discovery, novel chemistry, hypothesis generation from large biological and chemical datasets, and compiling large FDA regulatory submissions, a task well suited to large language models.
- AI's ability to accelerate biotech innovation is constrained by the need for human verification of underlying science and by fixed regulatory timelines—such as FDA requirements for one year of animal toxicity or six months of data—that cannot be shortened by AI.
- The report's first AI risk signal is diminishing returns to compute scaling for large language models: if hyperscaler performance metrics show diminishing returns, biotech could rise 15-20%; if scaling continues to improve and commercial adoption grows, that could drive a 15-25% biotech contraction via capital rotation back to AI.
- A second signal is enterprise AI adoption and the threat from open-weight/open-source models to token-based pricing: aggressive open-weight adoption could drive 15-20% biotech upside, stabilization around 35% open-weight market share would have only incremental impact, and regulatory restrictions that reduce open-weight usage could trigger 15-25% capital rotation back out of biotech.
- Additional signals include geopolitical risks to AI supply chains (resolution could pressure biotech 5-10%, prolonged disruption modestly benefits biotech), cybersecurity and regulatory risks (rising AI incidents could create 18-20% biotech upside), and AI CapEx changes (a slowdown supports biotech 10-15%, while accelerated AI investment pressures biotech 5-10%).
- The episode concludes that investors must think laterally across sectors: RBC's 'The Great Recalibration' found AI has concentrated returns and pushed dispersion to historical highs, with power flowing through data, talent, capital, culture, and trust, meaning biotech cannot be assessed solely through a healthcare lens if half of healthcare price action is explained by AI fundamentals.
