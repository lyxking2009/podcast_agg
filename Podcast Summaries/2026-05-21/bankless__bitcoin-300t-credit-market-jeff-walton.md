## Key Points

- The global fixed-income credit market is approximately $300 trillion — the largest capital pool in the world. Even a 1% Bitcoin allocation would represent $3 trillion in new demand for Bitcoin-backed products.
- Bitcoin-backed credit instruments are a new asset class: structured as perpetual preferred stocks backed by Bitcoin on corporate balance sheets, paying regular yield with no fixed repayment date, and marked to market 24/7.
- Strategy (MicroStrategy) pioneered the category with STRF (paying ~9% yield, 8x overcollateralized) and STRC (a high-yield savings account alternative targeting stable $100 principal with dynamic interest rate mechanisms).
- Strive's SATA is transitioning to 13% APR paid every business day starting June 16, 2026 — approximately 250 dividend payments per year, up from 12, dramatically increasing income frequency for holders.
- The digital credit market reached approximately $10 billion in issuance in under one year, making it one of the fastest-growing product categories in capital markets history.
- Walton rejects the "Ponzi" critique: traditional corporate bonds are backed by uncertain future revenue streams; Bitcoin treasury credit is backed by a scarce, permanently-held, non-depreciating asset with continuous price discovery.
- Risk is "mispriced globally," according to Walton — traditional equities rely on speculative future cash flows, while Bitcoin treasury companies leverage capital already on-hand, creating a fundamentally different risk profile.
- Bitcoin-backed credit offers higher yields than comparable traditional fixed income (9–13% vs. ~5.5% conventional bonds) with superior collateralization and real-time transparency.
- Walton's True North initiative within Strive focuses on building a peer group of Bitcoin treasury adopters and developing structured credit and collateralized finance products.
- If the thesis holds, Bitcoin's TAM expands dramatically beyond "digital gold" — from competing with gold's ~$13 trillion market cap to potentially accessing a meaningful slice of the $300 trillion credit market.

## Overview

In this episode, Bankless host David Hoffman sits down with Jeff Walton, Chief Risk Officer at Strive and founder of True North (a Strive subsidiary), to unpack Bitcoin's emerging role as the collateral backbone of a new global credit market. The central thesis: Bitcoin-backed credit instruments are disrupting the $300 trillion fixed-income market — the largest pool of capital on earth — by offering superior yield, transparency, and collateralization versus traditional debt securities.

Walton argues that every company will eventually hold Bitcoin on its balance sheet, and that the first movers — Strategy (MicroStrategy) and Strive — are pioneering a new class of financial instrument: perpetual preferred stocks and structured credit products backed by Bitcoin rather than future cash flows. Products like Strategy's STRF, STRC, and Strive's SATA are already generating yields in the 9–13% range, with SATA announcing a transition to 13% APR paid every business day beginning June 16, 2026. This new category reached approximately $10 billion in issuance in under a year, described as "the second fastest product launch in capital markets history."

Walton directly addresses critics who call these structures a "Ponzi," arguing the framing misunderstands the balance sheet: unlike traditional corporate bonds backed by uncertain future cash flows, Bitcoin treasury credit is backed by an asset that is permanently held, 8x overcollateralized in some structures, and mark-to-market 24/7. The conversation explores how this new financial layer could expand Bitcoin's total addressable market far beyond the gold comparison, potentially drawing trillions in capital from yield-seeking fixed-income investors.

## Implications

For investors, the emergence of Bitcoin-backed credit products fundamentally changes the yield opportunity set around Bitcoin exposure. Rather than simply holding BTC and hoping for price appreciation, investors can now access 9–13% yields backed by Bitcoin collateral — yields that exceed most traditional fixed-income alternatives while offering the collateral strength of the hardest asset in the world. For builders, this creates an entirely new financial infrastructure layer to build on: Bitcoin-native yield curves, collateralized lending, and structured products that could attract institutional fixed-income capital that has historically been unable to justify direct Bitcoin exposure. The key risk is reflexivity: if Bitcoin's price declines sharply, the collateral value of these products decreases simultaneously. Walton's counter is that overcollateralization ratios (8x in STRF's case) provide substantial buffers, but whether these hold in a true bear market remains untested at scale.
