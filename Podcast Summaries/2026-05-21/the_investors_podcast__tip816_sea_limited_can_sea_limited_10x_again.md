## Key Points
- Sea Limited operates three integrated divisions: Shopee (e-commerce), Garena (gaming), and Monee (digital fintech/lending), serving 700 million people across Southeast Asia plus Taiwan and Brazil
- Shopee has become the dominant e-commerce platform across Southeast Asia, but faces intensifying competition from TikTok Shop, which is gaining market share in markets like the Philippines and Malaysia
- Garena's gaming profits (anchored by the globally popular Free Fire) historically subsidized and funded Shopee and Monee's aggressive expansion — a distinctive model where gaming bankrolled marketplace growth
- Monee (fintech) is compared directly to Mercado Libre's Mercado Pago: Monee embeds credit products (SPayLater, BNPL) into the Shopee checkout flow, acquiring credit customers at near-zero marginal cost versus standalone neobanks
- Sea's Brazil expansion pits Shopee head-to-head against Mercado Libre on its home turf, with Shopee Mall GMV in Brazil more than doubling year-over-year and Monee's Brazil loan book crossing $1 billion in size
- Q1 2026 financials showed strong across-the-board growth: total GAAP revenue up 46.6% YoY to $7.1B, Shopee GMV of $37.3B (+~30%), Monee revenue +57.8% to $1.2B, Garena bookings +20.1%
- The episode's central debate: whether Shopee's competitive flywheel is strengthening or being eroded by TikTok Shop, and whether the Brazil investment represents the next growth chapter or a costly strategic distraction
- Hosts evaluate whether Sea Limited merits inclusion in The Intrinsic Value Portfolio, alongside prior positions in Amazon, Coupang, and Mercado Libre
- Shopee's four competitive moats identified: intangible assets (brand/product), network effects (buyer-seller flywheel), cost advantage (SPX Express logistics network delivering 50%+ of orders), and efficient scale in a winner-takes-most market
- SEA e-commerce TAM is projected to grow from $185B (2025) to $359B (2030), with digital payments and digital lending offering additional long-term runway

## Overview
Daniel Mahncke and Shawn O'Malley conduct a deep-dive analysis of Sea Limited (NYSE: SE), Southeast Asia's largest marketplace company, examining whether the business can replicate its historic 10x return. The episode follows the hosts' prior deep dives into Amazon, Coupang, and Mercado Libre, and situates Sea Limited as a direct competitor to both Mercado Libre (in Brazil) and Grab (in Southeast Asia). The discussion centers on two core investment questions: whether Shopee can maintain regional e-commerce dominance against a rapidly growing TikTok Shop without margin compression, and whether Sea's aggressive Brazil expansion into Mercado Libre's home market is a legitimate next growth chapter or an expensive distraction.

The hosts trace Sea Limited's origins and business structure — where Garena's gaming revenues (built on Free Fire, one of the world's most downloaded mobile games) funded the capital-intensive buildout of Shopee and Monee across markets that were largely unbanked and underserved by logistics. They analyze Shopee's unit economics improvement (take rate expanding to 13.3% record highs, logistics costs per order falling from -$0.66 in 2020 to $0.05 today), the competitive dynamics against TikTok Shop, and the strength and durability of Sea's moat across its three business segments.

## Implications
Sea Limited represents a compelling but contested case study in whether dominant e-commerce platforms built in emerging markets — funded by adjacent cash-generating businesses — can sustain 10x growth trajectories even as they face well-resourced new entrants like TikTok Shop and expand into structurally competitive markets like Brazil. The episode underscores a broader investment theme: that Southeast Asia's 700-million-person market, still in the early innings of e-commerce adoption and smartphone penetration, may offer growth runways that dwarf even Latin America, but that navigating competitive pressures from global tech giants (ByteDance/TikTok) requires a durable platform flywheel that goes beyond scale alone.
