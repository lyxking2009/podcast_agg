## Key Points
- Rupert Lowe argues that Britain's state has become the enemy of its own people, with power systematically transferred from elected MPs to unelected bureaucrats and administrators since the Blair era.
- The Independent Complaints and Grievance Scheme (ICGS) within Parliament has been deliberately placed outside parliamentary oversight, granting it immunity from legal challenge and enabling it to suppress inconvenient political speech.
- Lowe believes the rule of law in Britain has broken down — courts are "woke," judges are not impartial, and civil litigation is no longer a reliable recourse for ordinary citizens.
- The DEI and "woke virus," imported from America and the EU, has permeated the civil service, police, schools, social services, and parliament itself.
- Mass immigration — both legal and illegal — has damaged social cohesion, particularly because many newcomers do not integrate, and the justice system applies different standards based on cultural background.
- The private sector is dying under the weight of Labour's workers' rights legislation and tax complexity, while the government now accounts for over 50% of GDP.
- His party, Restore Britain, achieved a significant by-election result in Great Yarmouth (46% of the vote vs. Reform's 19.5%), demonstrating that mobilizing apathetic non-voters is a viable path to winning.
- Lowe sees AI as a force that will eliminate the lawyer and accountant class he labels "parasites on productive Britain."
- He is an Austrian school economist who wants to abolish inheritance tax, simplify the 22,000-page tax code, revive the City of London, and reward enterprise over rent-seeking.
- Lowe draws a parallel between Britain's decline and Argentina's century of Fabianism, and is in contact with Milei's office as a model for economic reform.

## Overview
Rupert Lowe, founder of Restore Britain and independent MP, joins Peter McCormack for a wide-ranging conversation about the systematic dismantling of British parliamentary democracy. Lowe contends that power has been deliberately transferred from elected representatives to unelected administrators, a partisan media, and a "woke" judiciary, making the state itself the primary threat to individual liberty. He argues that the Fabian-dominated Labour front bench, combined with decades of EU integration, has imposed a creeping socialist agenda that is strangling the private sector, suppressing free speech in parliament, and corrupting the rule of law.

Against this backdrop, Lowe outlines his strategy for Restore Britain: fielding candidates with real-world experience who are loyal to their constituents rather than a party, mobilizing the millions of politically disengaged voters, and publishing policy papers on energy, economics, and financial regulation. The Great Yarmouth by-election — where Restore Britain achieved 46% of the vote with a 60% surge in turnout over the 2021 local elections — is presented as proof of concept for this grassroots model.

## Implications
If Lowe's diagnosis is correct — that parliamentary sovereignty has been hollowed out, the judiciary compromised, and the private sector near collapse — then conventional electoral politics may be insufficient to reverse Britain's trajectory without simultaneous institutional and legal reform. The success of Restore Britain in mobilizing previously disengaged voters suggests that there is a large latent constituency for a non-party-aligned, economically liberal, and culturally conservative alternative, which could reshape the British political landscape ahead of the next general election.
