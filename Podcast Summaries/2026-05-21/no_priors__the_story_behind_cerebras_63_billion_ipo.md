## Key Points
- Cerebras builds wafer-scale AI chips — dinner-plate-sized processors (~46,000 sq mm) versus the postage-stamp-sized GPUs from Nvidia and AMD — achieving 15–20x faster inference speeds across all model sizes
- The company nearly ran out of money during 2017–2019, spending ~$8 million per month and incinerating nearly $200 million before solving the packaging challenges for the wafer-scale chip in July 2019
- Demand for Cerebras' technology was almost nonexistent until AI models became practically useful in everyday work (~2025), at which point speed became a critical competitive differentiator
- Strategic partnerships with sovereign wealth funds (e.g., G42) provided crucial intermediate revenue that allowed Cerebras to scale manufacturing and battle-test the technology before mainstream adoption exploded
- Cerebras signed a landmark $20 billion deal with OpenAI (term sheet signed Christmas Eve 2025, completed in four and a half weeks) and a deployment agreement with AWS in March 2026
- The company went public in May 2026 at approximately a $63 billion market cap, raising $5.55 billion in the largest U.S. IPO of the year; Feldman's stake is worth ~$3.2 billion
- Cerebras has grown to 8,850 employees and carries north of $20 billion in backlog
- Internal adoption of AI-generated coding drove per-engineer token spending from near zero to $25,000–$30,000/month in eight months, with aligned engineers seeing ~10x productivity gains
- Feldman views speed as enabling fundamentally new business models rather than merely incrementally improving existing ones, analogizing it to how faster internet transformed Netflix from a DVD delivery service into a content studio

## Overview
Andrew Feldman, co-founder and CEO of Cerebras Systems, joins No Priors hosts Sarah Guo and Elad Gil to discuss the story behind Cerebras' blockbuster $63 billion IPO. Feldman traces the company's journey from a contrarian bet on wafer-scale chip architecture in 2017, through a near-death phase of burning $8 million a month with no customers, to the breakthrough moment in 2019 and the explosion in demand that followed as large AI models became indispensable in daily work. The conversation covers the strategic role of sovereign wealth partnerships, the landmark OpenAI deal, and the cultural challenge of maintaining a fearless engineering mindset while scaling to nearly 9,000 employees.

## Implications
Cerebras' IPO signals that differentiated AI infrastructure — not just model-layer companies — can command massive valuations, and that the market is beginning to seriously price in the inference-speed race. Feldman's framing of speed as a business-model enabler (rather than a spec improvement) suggests the next wave of AI applications may be as structurally different from today's as streaming video was from DVD rental.

## Notable Quotes

> "Between 2017 and 2019, we spent $8 million monthly without success, but each failure taught us something valuable."

> "We'd rather fail pursuing the extraordinary than succeed in the ordinary."

> "We incinerated nearly $200 million trying to solve one technical problem."

> "Just stood in the lab and stared at it" — Feldman on the July 2019 moment the packaged wafer-scale chip first ran correctly, calling it "one of the greatest moments of my life."
