# Ada Palmer – Machiavelli is the most misunderstood thinker of all time

**Date:** 2026-06-16

**Transcript source:** web

## Key Points

1. Machiavelli is not the amoral villain of popular imagination — he was a deeply patriotic Florentine diplomat whose works were a desperate job application to his torturers and a manual for stabilizing his beloved republic.
2. Renaissance Italy was a volatile landscape of competing city-states where governments lacked legitimacy. Florence relied on diplomacy, bribes, and cultural patronage as cheaper alternatives to military conflict.
3. The patronage system — loyalty to persons and families, not abstract states — was the fundamental glue of society, affecting everything from justice to military loyalty.
4. After the Medici tortured and exiled Machiavelli, he remained in the countryside to serve Florence rather than fleeing. The Prince was written as a private guide for the Medici, not for general publication.
5. The 'fear vs. love' doctrine: better to be feared than loved, but crucially must avoid being hated. Fear provides stable obedience; love is fickle.
6. Half of all outcomes depend on luck (fortuna). A leader can do everything right and still fail. We should judge actions by their most probable outcomes.
7. Cesare Borgia was Machiavelli's archetypal effective ruler — conquered territories by eliminating rival nobles, then imposed neutral justice making him popular with common people.
8. Machiavelli analyzed religion as a tool for social stability, arguing ancient Roman religion was better for fostering patriotism than Christianity, which encouraged passivity.
9. Florence's investment in art was soft power diplomacy — showcasing high-tech cultural achievement to intimidate rivals as a cheaper alternative to unwinnable wars.
10. Copyright emerged directly from the Inquisition's censorship system — to get a license to print, authors had to submit works for approval, receiving a monopoly in return.

## Overview

Historian Ada Palmer joins Dwarkesh Patel for a deep dive into Niccolò Machiavelli — arguing he's the most misunderstood thinker in history. Palmer reframes Machiavelli not as the architect of amoral power politics, but as one of history's greatest patriots who sacrificed career and comfort for Florence. The conversation covers Renaissance Italian politics, the patronage system, Machiavelli's relationship with Cesare Borgia, his analysis of religion as a tool of statecraft, and how Florence used art as soft power diplomacy. Palmer also traces the surprising journey of The Prince from private memo to banned book to the birth of copyright law.

## Implications

For anyone in leadership, politics, or strategy: Machiavelli's actual insights are about institutional stability, the dangers of mercenary loyalty, and the role of fortune in outcomes — far more nuanced than the popular 'ends justify the means' caricature. The patronage system dynamics he described still echo in modern organizational politics.
