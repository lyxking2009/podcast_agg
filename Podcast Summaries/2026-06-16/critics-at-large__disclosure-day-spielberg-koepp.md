# Disclosure Day with Steven Spielberg & David Koepp

**Show:** Critics at Large (The New Yorker)  
**Date:** 2026-06-16  
**Duration:** 55 min  
**Transcript source:** web

## Key Points

1. Disclosure Day is Steven Spielberg's new alien thriller — described as a 'spiritual sequel' to Close Encounters of the Third Kind, featuring Emily Blunt, Josh O'Connor, and Colin Firth.
2. Screenwriter David Koepp required an unprecedented 42 drafts for the script — a personal record. Spielberg was 'more exacting than I've ever seen him' due to deep familiarity with the alien genre.
3. The film is a '70s-style paranoid thriller about cybersecurity expert Daniel Kellner (Josh O'Connor) attempting to blow the whistle on Wardex, a clandestine government agency concealing evidence of alien life.
4. The New Yorker's Justin Chang calls it technologically proficient but overly familiar — 'blurs the line between phoning home and phoning it in,' with diminishing returns from retreading Spielberg's own classics.
5. Koepp's career spans $2.97B+ domestic box office: Jurassic Park, Spider-Man (2002), Mission: Impossible, War of the Worlds. Disclosure Day is his 7th script for Spielberg.
6. The film's theological subplot — a nun arguing extraterrestrial life confirms God's infinite nature — is called 'the script's most topical and intellectually provocative thread.'
7. Real-world parallels undercut the film: Obama and Trump's UFO statements and Pentagon UAP releases have 'let the air out of the movie's tires.'
8. Koepp's creative process: parks ideas in files, gives them 24 hours to a week to develop. If they stick, they get their own file. His office has index cards everywhere.
9. Early success (Jurassic Park at age 30) was overwhelming: 'It doesn't give you the grit... And it doesn't necessarily endear you to your friends.'
10. Koepp is extremely busy: Disclosure Day is his 5th film in ~18 months (Presence, Black Bag, Jurassic World: Rebirth, Cold Storage).

## Overview

The Critics at Large podcast discusses Steven Spielberg's Disclosure Day, featuring analysis of the film and a Vanity Fair interview with screenwriter David Koepp. The episode covers the film's 42-draft development process, its place in Spielberg's alien filmography, the theological subplot about extraterrestrial life and religion, and Koepp's four-decade career writing for Hollywood's biggest directors. The New Yorker review calls it proficient but self-repetitive, while Koepp reflects on failure, craft, and writing movies he'd want to see 'with a burrito in hand.'

## Implications

For filmmakers and writers: the 42-draft process shows that even veteran collaborations can demand unprecedented iteration. The film's theological thread about aliens and religion represents Spielberg's most intellectually ambitious alien work. Koepp's philosophy of embracing failure as defining is a masterclass in creative resilience.

## Notable Quotes

> Though Disclosure Day teems with intelligent life, it also blurs the line — not the one separating us from them but the one between phoning home and phoning it in. — Justin Chang, The New Yorker

> He's a good collaborator because he listens as much to me as I do to him... willing to rework a script including and often through principal photography. — David Koepp on Spielberg

> I think they're out there. I think maybe we're looking for the wrong things... Who's to say they're not here now, but in forms that we can't perceive? — David Koepp on aliens

> A failure is what defines you. How do you get up after you get punched in the face? — David Koepp

> Thinking up stories and spending a day in this office, solving a problem, writing something you think is funny or exciting? That's priceless. — David Koepp

> I write movies I'd like to see in a matinee showing, with a burrito in hand. — David Koepp
