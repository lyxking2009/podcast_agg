# Simulating Humans at Scale: Simile's Joon Sung Park

**Show:** Training Data (Sequoia Capital)  
**Date:** 2026-06-16  
**Duration:** 39 min  
**Transcript source:** web

## Key Points

1. Joon Sung Park (CEO, Simile) argues that leading AI models (GPT-4, Claude) are optimized to be rational, but humans are inherently irrational, subjective, and inconsistent — creating a fundamental simulation gap.
2. The 'Say-Do Gap': what people post online differs from their real actions. Training on internet text does not yield accurate behavioral simulation.
3. Simile's models predict human behavior 85% as accurately as people replicate their own choices (accounting for self-retest variability).
4. Total Variation Distance (TVD) measures simulated vs. real response distributions. A TVD of less than 0.15 is considered strong evidence for business decision-making.
5. Simile partners with Gallup to recruit representative samples. Participants provide rich narrative data (e.g., 'Tell me the story of your life') to capture long-tail information beyond demographics.
6. Each real person's data constructs a 'digital double' — a unique generative agent carrying their behavioral signals.
7. The architectural analogy: today's models are like CPUs (superhuman, single-threaded); Simile's model is like a GPU — not superhuman, but as human as possible, running many in parallel.
8. CVS is a flagship enterprise partner. Expansion path: simulated focus groups → testing 1,000 ideas across 1,000 subpopulations → longitudinal product experiences → multi-agent simulations.
9. Grand vision: simulation as a 'measurement instrument for social science' — a 'Hubble Telescope for human society' to model bank runs, monetary systems, collective action failures.
10. Key defense: the say-do gap is structural. Behavioral data will remain the key differentiator even if frontier labs close the irrationality gap.

## Overview

Sequoia Capital's Training Data podcast features Joon Sung Park, CEO of Simile and Stanford PhD who pioneered generative agents. Park argues that today's AI models are too rational to simulate real human behavior and presents Simile's approach: building 'digital doubles' from rich behavioral data that predict human choices with 85% accuracy. The conversation covers the say-do gap, Total Variation Distance as a validation metric, enterprise applications with CVS, and the grand vision of simulation as a 'Hubble Telescope for human society' — enabling new questions in economics, climate, and politics.

## Implications

For AI builders and market researchers: the say-do gap is a structural problem that internet-text training alone can't solve. Behavioral data (not just text) is the key differentiator. TVD < 0.15 as a decision-making threshold offers a concrete validation standard. For enterprises: simulated focus groups and multi-agent simulations could replace expensive traditional market research at 100x the scale.

## Notable Quotes
