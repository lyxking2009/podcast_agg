## Key Points

1. Kareem Amin is co-founder and CEO of Clay, one of the fastest-growing software companies valued at over $4 billion, helping companies find and reach customers at scale.
2. Clay's organizational philosophy centers on three statues: truth, courage, and justice — which Amin uses as practical guides for decision-making, not abstract values.
3. The conversation explores sales as a craft that rewards a lifetime of practice, and what it means to create from a place of wholeness rather than lack.
4. Amin discusses why some companies are better off dying than scaling — not every startup should pursue growth at all costs.
5. The 'Day Five Insight' and running a startup unusually — Amin's non-standard approach to company building.
6. Learning from magicians about making people feel something new — applying performance craft to product design.
7. Music's role in Amin's life and how it informs his creative process.
8. All problems are communication problems — a key lesson from scaling Clay.
9. The 'death doula' concept applied to company building — knowing when to let things go.
10. Jony Ive and Steve Jobs as case studies in introspection and role of design in company building.

## Overview

Patrick O'Shaughnessy interviews Kareem Amin, co-founder and CEO of Clay, about his unusual approach to company building. The conversation covers Amin's philosophical framework of truth, courage, and justice as practical business tools, sales as a lifetime craft, creating from wholeness rather than lack, and why some companies are better off dying than scaling. Amin shares insights from magicians, music, and the concept of a 'death doula' applied to company building.

## Implications

For founders and operators: Amin's framework of truth/courage/justice offers a practical alternative to typical startup playbooks. His willingness to let companies die rather than force scaling challenges the growth-at-all-costs mentality. Clay's $4B+ valuation validates that unconventional approaches can work.
