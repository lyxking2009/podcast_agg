# Invest Like the Best — Krishna Rao - Anthropic's CFO on Compute, Scaling to $30B ARR, and the Returns to Frontier Intelligence

## Key Points

- Compute is the foundational resource ('canvas') for everything Anthropic builds — procurement and allocation are the CFO's central challenges, managed via daily reallocation meetings across model training, internal tooling, and customer inference.
- Anthropic operates three chip platforms fungibly — AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, and NVIDIA GPUs — giving flexibility to shift workloads based on availability, cost, and performance.
- The 'Cone of Uncertainty' framework helps manage the enormous range of possible compute demand outcomes in a hyper-growth environment where revenue doubled from $9B to an estimated $30B run rate in one year.
- Returns to frontier intelligence are increasing, not diminishing — especially in enterprise where customers show very high willingness to pay for coding agents, document analysis, and other high-value use cases.
- Anthropic deliberately builds its own products (Claude Code) alongside the API — to dogfood models, improve product quality, and capture value at the application layer where model and product are increasingly coupled.
- Pricing for Anthropic's models has remained stable despite rapid improvement, thanks to efficiency gains offsetting higher compute costs and a strategic choice not to race to the bottom on price.
- Krishna's own finance team uses Claude internally for forecasting, scenario analysis, and data synthesis — demonstrating AI transformation of knowledge work inside the company itself.
- Anthropic has retention numbers unmatched by other AI labs, built on a culture of intellectual honesty, safety research, and deep collaboration.
- The next big frontier is AI systems acting as autonomous collaborators (virtual collaborators) — not just tools but teammates, with Anthropic investing in multi-agent workflows.
- Biggest risks: geopolitical chip export controls, energy constraints for data centers, and alignment/safety challenges as models become more powerful.

## Overview

Patrick O'Shaughnessy sits down with Anthropic CFO Krishna Rao for a deep dive into the financial and operational engine behind one of the fastest-growing AI companies in history. Rao frames compute as Anthropic's lifeblood — the 'canvas' on which everything from model training to customer inference is built. He walks through the daily operational rhythm of reallocating compute across three fungible chip platforms (AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs), and introduces the 'Cone of Uncertainty' as the framework for navigating the immense forecasting challenge of a business that has gone from $250M to an estimated $30B run rate in just two years. The conversation covers why returns to frontier intelligence are actually increasing (not diminishing) with scale, especially in enterprise applications where customers pay premium prices for coding and analysis capabilities. Rao explains Anthropic's controversial decision to build consumer products like Claude Code alongside the API — a bet that the line between platform and application is blurring, and that building your own products makes your models better. He also touches on Jevons Paradox for labor: as AI gets cheaper, demand for human workers may actually increase as more tasks become economical to automate. The episode closes with Rao's view on the biggest risks — geopolitics, energy, and alignment — and his excitement about AI's potential to transform healthcare and scientific discovery.

## Implications

For founders and investors: The 'Cone of Uncertainty' is a critical framework for any hyper-growth AI company — forecasting demand 3-6 months out in a market that doubles yearly requires operational discipline, not just financial modeling. For engineers: Anthropic's decision to build products like Claude Code alongside the API signals that AI labs see the application layer as a strategic imperative, not just a sideline. For PMs: The blurring line between platform and application means the winning AI companies will be those that deeply integrate model development with product experience. For finance/ops: The fungible chip strategy (multi-cloud, multi-architecture) is becoming table stakes — single-cloud AI risks are too high. For everyone: Jevons Paradox suggests AI creates more jobs than it eliminates, but the nature of work shifts toward higher-judgment tasks augmented by AI tools.
