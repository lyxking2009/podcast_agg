# An Economy So Good No One Can Live In It with Jill Schlesinger⁠ — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Jill Schlesinger discusses the disconnect between strong economic indicators—record-low jobless claims, falling bank charge-offs, and a rising stock market—and widespread consumer anxiety, particularly among younger generations facing high housing costs, expensive entertainment, and social media-driven comparison. She criticizes media negativity and the AI hype cycle, noting that while AI spending surges, efficiency and ROI concerns are emerging; political risks to data centers are manageable. She also highlights the importance of human attention in financial planning and promotes her new podcast Money Moves.

## Key points
- U.S. consumer overall healthy: Bank of America net charge-offs declining; initial jobless claims at 194,000, fourth lowest since 1960s; bank executives consistently report consumers are okay.
- Affordability issues real: heating oil prices up but only ~3% of U.S. households use oil heat, so impact limited; $8 water at movie theater and $600 concert tickets exemplify price gouging for scarce experiences.
- Wealth and generational divide: older homeowners with assets are doing well, but young adults struggle with housing costs and rent, often living with family; parents worry about kids' job prospects.
- Jill criticizes media negativity: after missing 2008 crisis, financial media overcorrected and spent years looking for next crisis; algorithms reward negative headlines, creating doom loop.
- AI media coverage overwhelmingly negative: headlines about security risks, school concerns, and who pays for buildout; Jill says regulation unlikely until something bad happens, consistent with U.S. history of reacting to disasters.
- Jill experimented with AI chatbots for financial planning: advice not bad but just pulls from articles; people increasingly rely on it, but human attention is irreplaceable in wealth management.
- Meta earnings: stock fell 8% after hours; first negative free cash flow quarter since 2012; ad impressions up 10% year-over-year, average price per ad up 14%, showing AI ad improvement; Zuckerberg plans new share class to maintain control while raising capital.
- Data center politics: Stephanie Roth of Wolfe Research says regulatory risks are concentrated at state/local level, mostly adding costs and timelines, not reducing investment; Texas governor race biggest sentiment risk.
- Jill sees no imminent recession: no over-leveraged banks like 2008; AI capex funded by professional investors and overseas billionaires; potential risks like France debt but not cascading.
- Financial planning industry: planning software solved for a generation, but hard part is attention; AI can scale advisory firms but cannot replace human client relationships.
- Jill's new podcast Money Moves launched June 30, aimed at younger listeners, with segments like TikTok takedown and calls from "jillionaires."
