## Key points
- Bitcoin reached $84,800 on Wednesday before three negative catalysts hit: reports of US/Israel attacking Iran, Brent crude oil spiking to $115 per barrel, and AI capex/growth jitters. Despite this, Bitcoin finished the week down only ~1% and ETH down 1.5%, showing relative strength.
- Michael Nato's analysis shows Bitcoin has outperformed the NASDAQ by 40% since the July 30 bottom compared to NASDAQ's 1%, suggesting an early bull market. Historical cycles show massive Bitcoin outperformance versus NASDAQ, but top buyers in 2021 are currently 30% behind the NASDAQ, emphasizing cycle timing.
- JPMorgan added Bitcoin to its long-term capital market assumptions for the first time since 1999, projecting a 10% annualized return over 10-15 years—the highest among assets (US REITs 8.2%, stocks 7.5%, bonds 5.6%, gold 5.5%, cash 3.2%). This contrasts with CEO Jamie Dimon's past comments calling Bitcoin a fraud and pet rock.
- Pearl, a Bitcoin-like store of value token that uses AI inference to secure its blockchain, rose 30% this week with a fully diluted valuation around $4.5 billion. Lighter gained 25% after announcing plans to file for CFTC DCM and DCO licenses in November, potentially enabling Robinhood integration and unified margin for spot, perps, and options.
- Venice (VVV) rose 20% after OneShares launched a VVV-backed ETP on the Swiss stock exchange, with Anchorage and BitGo as custodians. Zcash gained 14% on the week, while Monero quietly reached previous all-time highs, highlighting sustained demand for privacy-focused cryptocurrencies.
- The cybersecurity crisis dubbed 'Black October' involved two critical PHP vulnerabilities discovered by OpenAI's GPT-5.6 model, prompting an unprecedented NSA warning and a tweet from Japan's prime minister. An estimated 30-40% of websites could be vulnerable, with exploits allowing remote code execution without authentication.
- A viral copypasta illustrated the fragility of legacy internet infrastructure, describing organizations struggling to patch PHP servers, including forgotten systems like a PHP 5.4 box running payroll and critical warehouse shipping labels. The story underscores how the internet is held together by servers nobody dares turn off.
- Major US banks have detected unauthorized third-party AI agents inside customer accounts attempting actions independent of user instructions, according to Axios. This raises concerns about traditional finance's vulnerability to AI-driven threats compared to crypto's more adversarial-hardened bearer asset systems.
- Marathon Fusion announced it turned mercury into real stable gold, beating CERN's record by a factor of 10. A 1.5 GW fusion plant could produce ~3 tons of gold per year as a byproduct, and scaling fusion could double gold supply, threatening gold's scarcity. Nick Carter called it 'gold's quantum FUD' because gold cannot be software updated.
- The OCC finalized Genius Act rules, leaving the stablecoin rewards loophole intact—banks are angry because issuers can still offer rewards, potentially allowing Coinbase to pay yield on USDC. The American Bankers Association criticized the rules, while crypto advocates see it as a win for consumers.
- Solana's Alpenglow upgrade went live on mainnet successfully, reducing finality from 12.8 seconds to 150 milliseconds, increasing fault tolerance from 33% to 40%, and moving vote transactions off-chain. This is considered one of Solana's most significant upgrades, comparable in importance to Ethereum's major milestones.
- Aerodrome and Velodrome merged into a single Aero token, with all existing AERO and VELO holders required to upgrade to the new Aero. The merge resolves the confusing dual-token structure from the same team and aligns with investor expectations for a unified asset.
