# Let Them Eat Steak — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
The episode covers two major economic stories: the soaring price of beef due to drought-driven supply shortages, and signs of strain in the AI market as CoreWeave's troubles raise fears of a bubble. Dina Shanker explains how beef has become a luxury item amid political backlash, while Sarah Fryer discusses CoreWeave's risky financing and the upcoming Anthropic IPO. ## Key points
- Beef prices have surged dramatically: steak is up 81% since January 2017 and ground beef is up 95% over the same period, making beef increasingly unaffordable for ordinary consumers.
- The US cattle herd is at its smallest in 75 years, driven by prolonged drought that has limited grass and feed, causing ranchers to hesitate to expand despite high prices and low feed costs.
- Beef is becoming a luxury item, exemplified by Poppy Steak in Miami Beach and Las Vegas, which sells a $1,000 beef case and a $95 baked potato; its regulars reportedly include Jared Kushner and Ivanka Trump.
- Political backlash erupted after Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith suggested consumers choose 'other proteins'; her opponent Scott Collum ran an ad mocking the comment, reflecting the emotional attachment Americans have to beef.
- The Trump administration announced plans to import 300,000 metric tons of lean ground beef product, mainly from Brazil, to mix with fattier domestic beef, but the move caused cattle futures to fall and created uncertainty among ranchers.
- Fast food chains are shifting toward chicken: McDonald's now sells more chicken than beef, while Arby's has brought back steak nuggets due to high demand, showing the pressure on beef-centric menus.
- CoreWeave's stock has fallen about 30% in a week and its bonds suggest default risk; the CEO's statement that the company expects 'unlimited' access to capital and 'all the money in the world' alarmed investors.
- The neocloud business model relies on circular financing: NVIDIA and other big tech companies provide funds to customers to buy their own chips, creating systemic risk if demand falters.
- Anthropic's anticipated IPO could reveal crucial details about future liabilities, entanglements with Amazon and Google, and even existential risk, as the company uses the controversial 'annualized run rate' metric.
- The documentary 'You Can See Everything' by Nathan Fielder about Elizabeth Holmes earned $15 million in its opening weekend on 855 screens, comparable to 'Borat' and 'Fahrenheit 9/11', and explores the blurred line between self-deception and fraud in startup culture.
