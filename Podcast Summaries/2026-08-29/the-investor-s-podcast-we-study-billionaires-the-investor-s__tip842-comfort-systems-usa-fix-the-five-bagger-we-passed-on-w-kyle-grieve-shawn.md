# TIP842: Comfort Systems USA (FIX): The Five-Bagger We Passed On w/ Kyle Grieve & Shawn O'Malley — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)  - The Investor’s Podcast Network

## TL;DR
The episode revisits Comfort Systems USA after Shawn O'Malley passed on it at a fair value of $320; the stock is now around $1,800, a five-bagger driven by explosive AI data center spending. Hosts Kyle Grieve and Shawn O'Malley analyze the business's dramatic growth: backlog up over 8x since 2020, electrical segment revenue +81%, operating margins expanded to 16.5%, and return on incremental invested capital nearly 60%. They update intrinsic value to ~$1,600, acknowledge wide range of outcomes, and decide to remain on the sidelines despite FOMO, concluding the original pass was defensible.

## Key points
- Shawn O'Malley originally pitched Comfort Systems USA with a fair value of about $320 per share and passed, waiting for an entry below $300. The stock now trades near $1,800, a five-bagger, prompting a review of whether the decision reflected a flawed process or the natural outcome of a disciplined framework.
- Comfort Systems is a Houston-based mechanical, electrical, and plumbing contractor that operates as a serial acquirer. It has over $10 billion in revenue, more than 23,000 employees, and a market cap exceeding $60 billion. Historically, it compounded revenue at 13% and EPS at 22% annually for two decades; in the last 10 years, revenue growth increased to 21% and EPS growth to 37%.
- AI data center spending transformed the business. Backlog grew over 8 times since 2020 and more than doubled between 2024 and 2025. Technology-related revenue rose from 30% of total revenue in 2024 to 56% in the first half of 2026. The electrical segment grew 81% year-over-year and now contributes nearly 29% of revenue, up from 0.4% in 2017.
- Organic growth (same-store sales) reached 26% in fiscal 2025, far above the historical 3-4%. The backlog increased by $1.6 billion in the latest quarter, with $1.4 billion from same-store growth. The industrial segment, which includes technology, grew 74% and represents about three-quarters of first-half 2026 revenue.
- Operating margins expanded from roughly 10.5% at the time of the original pitch to 16.5% today. Net income nearly tripled compared to 2024 and is up almost 10 times from 2020. However, hosts question the sustainability of these margins given the cyclical nature of AI CAPEX and data center spending.
- The price-to-earnings multiple expanded from the mid-30s to about 43 times, but the 5x share price gain was driven primarily by earnings growth. Shawn's updated model, assuming 13% average revenue growth and a lower exit multiple, yields a fair value of about $1,600, slightly below the current price, indicating limited margin of safety.
- Surety bonding capacity gives Comfort Systems a widening competitive advantage. With a strong balance sheet, the company can secure bonds for large projects, such as one worth $470 million, out of a total backlog of $24 billion across 8,500 projects. This acts as a cornered resource that smaller competitors cannot match.
- Return on incremental invested capital improved from over 25% historically to nearly 60% on a trailing twelve-month basis, leading to a compounding rate of about 39%. Acquisition pace increased: since start of 2025, Comfort completed six deals totaling over $540 million, funded mostly from cash, at an average multiple of about 9 times operating earnings.
- Management is viewed as high-quality. CEO Brian Lane handed the president title to Trent McKenna in December 2025, signaling succession planning. The incentive plan ties bonuses to public EPS and free cash flow targets; management earned the maximum 200% of target for three consecutive years. Insider ownership declined from 1.8% to 1.2% since 2024.
- Key risks include the cyclicality of data center and chip manufacturing spending, evidence of potential bubble conditions such as SEC loosening of asset-backed securities rules for data centers, and the fact that backlog is not contractually guaranteed revenue. Hosts believe a downturn could erase much of the recent earnings gains.
- Despite acknowledging the business quality and the pain of missing a five-bagger, both hosts conclude that staying on the sidelines is the right decision given the wide range of outcomes and their lack of conviction on AI cycle duration. They prefer to wait for a more favorable risk-reward setup rather than chase the stock.
