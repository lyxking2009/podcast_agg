# Max Junestrand: You Need The Willingness To Learn Faster Than Anyone Else — Y Combinator Startup Podcast

## TL;DR
Max Junestrand shares Legora's journey from initial YC rejection to becoming a legal AI operating system used by over 3% of the world's lawyers, growing from $1M to $100M ARR in 18 months and scaling to 750+ people. Key lessons include betting on model improvements over fine-tuning, enforcing a six-month sales freeze to perfect product reliability, and building a competitive 'LFG' culture that blends US ambition with Swedish work ethic. The talk also covers raising $35M from Benchmark and Redpoint, hiring for growth trajectory over logos, and a notable Jude Law marketing campaign.

## Key points
- Legora began as Judelica in 2020, founded by a lawyer, a physicist, an engineer, and a psychologist before GPT existed; they used early Google BERT models to explore summarization of court cases for law students.
- Max met co-founders August and Siga at a volleyball game in the Swedish archipelago; after seeing a GPT-3.5 demo that explained stock option agreements, Max dropped out of his master's program to join because the opportunity cost of not building became too large.
- Early go-to-market involved cold emailing lawyers and offering to pay their hourly rate for lunch to learn about legal practice areas; many lawyers declined payment or even paid for lunch, helping the team build domain understanding.
- Legora's first YC application in May 2023 was rejected; when asked what type of lawyers they served, Max answered 'What do you mean? Are there different type of lawyers?', causing partner Tom Bloomfield to laugh, but the team persevered and was accepted two months later under a new name and platform.
- After YC acceptance, the team moved into the offices of Manheimr Swartling, the largest law firm in the Nordics, working in a windowless conference room where the AC turned off at 5pm; an engineer would fan the door for oxygen, and those conditions became a symbol of early hustle.
- During YC, Legora grew from zero to $1 million ARR; Max handled sales calls to Europe between 1am and 10am using a ring light, demonstrating the cross-timezone grind required.
- Legora raised $9.51 million from Benchmark after lining up 80 investor meetings in a week and a half; Redpoint led a pre-letter Series A three weeks later, leaving the company with $35 million in the bank.
- A board decision to freeze sales for six months was made because lawyers only give one chance; after fixing product reliability and platform adaptability, the company began its growth curve from $1 million to $100 million ARR.
- In October 2024, Legora wrote the 'Legora product manifesto' (referred to as Leia product manifesto) to focus a 25-person team on core product priorities; at the time they had $1.3 million ARR while competitors had 10x revenue but only one feature each.
- Legora's cultural values are 'Lean in, fight for excellence, and grow together,' abbreviated LFG; the explicit profanity signals the intensity and self-selects candidates who fit the culture.
- Hiring shifted away from resumes with prestigious logos and toward candidates with high growth trajectories; top seller today is 23 years old with no prior sales background and has sold over $10 million of Legora.
- Max interviewed every candidate outside engineering until the company reached 500 people, then moved to interviewing directors and above, embedding cultural fit and self-selection.
- The Swedish 'Jantelagen' (law of Jante) humility was balanced with US, European, and Asian cultural elements to build an ambitious global company; the company runs as if there is no place for number two.
- Legora partnered with actor Jude Law for a marketing campaign after persistent outreach; Law agreed on condition he choose his own script writer and cinematographer, bringing an SNL script director and the cinematographer from Oppenheimer, which boosted brand recognition beyond legal circles.
- The product is evolving from reactive agents that follow prompts to proactive agents triggered by context, such as automatically routing contracts to agents or organizing data rooms and producing due diligence reports.
- Legora remains model-agnostic, having early bet that foundation models would improve rather than investing in fine-tuning; they recently released the Legora Bench benchmark and found that Grok (SpaceX) performed surprisingly well on their benchmarks, though data processing agreements prevented immediate customer offering.
- Domain expertise was not required to build Legora; the team learned the legal market by spending extensive time with lawyers, and a Swedish VC who passed on the pre-seed due to lack of lawyers later regretted it.
- Storytelling is a critical CEO skill; Max emphasized that you must sell the company to yourself, employees, investors, and customers, and that ambition can be learned by surrounding yourself with ambitious peers.
