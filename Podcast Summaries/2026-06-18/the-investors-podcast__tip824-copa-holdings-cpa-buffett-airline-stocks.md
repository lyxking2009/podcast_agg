## Key Points

- Copa Holdings (CPA) is a Panama-based airline with unique structural advantages
- Its hub-and-spoke model centered in Panama efficiently serves 85 destinations
- Fleet simplicity: operates only single-aisle Boeing 737s
- Industry-leading cost base: ex-fuel CASM below 6 cents (matched only by Ryanair and Wizz Air)
- 99.8% flight completion rate creates a structural cost advantage
- 38-year-old CEO enforces discipline: no European expansion, no growth-at-any-price
- Buffett's continued airline investments suggest best-in-class operators can succeed
- The central question: are Copa's advantages genuinely uncopiable or just temporary?

## Overview

Daniel Mahncke and Shawn O'Malley take a deep dive into Copa Holdings (CPA), examining whether this Panama-based airline is a structural exception to the typical "airline curse" that destroys value. Copa's investment case rests on hard-to-replicate advantages: a geographically optimal hub, fleet simplicity, industry-leading costs, and operational excellence. The hosts assess whether these advantages are durable and whether Copa qualifies for their Intrinsic Value Portfolio.

## Implications

- Copa may represent a rare, profitable outlier in an otherwise capital-destructive sector
- Its geographic hub advantage and cost structure create meaningful competitive moats
- The market may be undervaluing Copa by lumping it with the broader airline sector
- Buffett's continued airline investments suggest sector-level pessimism may be overdone
