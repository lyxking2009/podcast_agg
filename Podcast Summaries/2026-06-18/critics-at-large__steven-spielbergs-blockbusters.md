## Overview

On this episode of Critics at Large, Vinson Cunningham, Naomi Fry, and Alexandra Schwartz trace how Steven Spielberg inaugurated the modern blockbuster with "Jaws" in 1975. They work through his filmography — Close Encounters, E.T., Jurassic Park — culminating in his new release "Disclosure Day." The hosts argue that while other directors share Spielberg's capacity for spectacle, his appeal lies in his profound earnestness and ability to bring humanity to the fore in extreme circumstances.

## Implications

- Spielberg's return to blockbuster form with "Disclosure Day" may reinvigorate theatrical cinema
- The blockbuster model he created remains the dominant commercial filmmaking paradigm
- His emphasis on human emotion within spectacle is a template for commercially successful filmmaking

## Notable Quotes

- "What Spielberg is so good at is bringing the human to the fore in these extreme, sci-fi circumstances. And *that's* what makes a great blockbuster." — Alexandra Schwartz
