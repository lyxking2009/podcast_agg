## Key Points

- Federal government forced Anthropic's Fable 5 offline with no transparent process or legal authority cited
- Jessi Brooks argues this is fundamentally a crypto story: "what began as a cybersecurity story is really a chokepoint story"
- The government is effectively inventing "KYC for AI" — setting a precedent for centralized intervention
- SEC proposed repealing Rules 611 and 610E of Reg NMS, pillars of U.S. equity market structure
- Vy Le argues the Reg NMS repeal is crypto's "real opening" — DEXes shouldn't fit outdated centralized rules
- Proposed replacement uses crypto-native solutions: Solver Auctions and Cryptographic Attestations
- CFTC released a 300-page proposed rule on political event contracts
- Central question: "When Washington moves this fast and quietly, is crypto the cautionary tale or the fix?" 

## Overview

This DEX in the City episode analyzes two major U.S. regulatory actions with crypto implications. The forced shutdown of Anthropic's Fable 5 AI model — with no statutory authority or public comment — is framed as a "chokepoint story" that crypto has seen before. Simultaneously, the SEC's proposal to repeal Reg NMS rules creates an opportunity for crypto-native market structure solutions like Solver Auctions and Cryptographic Attestations.

## Implications

- AI export controls could extend to APIs, DeFi protocols, and blockchain front-ends
- The Reg NMS repeal could be a "generational unlock for tokenized equities and DeFi"
- Crypto-native solutions (Solver Auctions, Cryptographic Attestations) may replace traditional market structure
- National security is becoming a card to regulate emerging tech sectors including crypto
- The 1990s "encryption wars" parallel suggests these battles will be long-fought
