# Hot Market Summer

**Show:** Everybody's Business  
**Guests:** Max Chafkin, Stacy Vanek Smith, David Papadopoulos, Amanda Mull  
**Date:** 2026-06-19  
**Transcript source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points

- Ice cream prices have surged to $10-12 per cone in NYC due to food inflation, rising diesel/fuel costs for trucks, and cold storage energy prices — a microcosm of cumulative 20-30% inflation over five years
- The 'luxuriization' of consumer markets: budget ice cream chains (Carvel, Baskin Robbins) are closing while premium shops (Van Leeuwin, Jenny's) are expanding, as ice cream becomes a 'small luxury experience'
- Kevin Warsh's first press conference as Fed Chair was emphatically hawkish — he repeatedly stressed the Fed has 'failed on inflation' and is committed to price stability, while Trump argues oil price drops will naturally bring inflation down
- The peptide craze: GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic/Mounjaro) spawned a gray market of unregulated peptides, with telehealth platforms and biohackers injecting compounds with minimal human testing (e.g., BPC-157 for muscle healing)
- RFK Jr. has instructed FDA to reconsider banning ~20 peptides, arguing harm reduction via regulated compounding pharmacies is better than people importing raw powder from China
- A ZipRecruiter study found ~1/3 of workers now hold multiple jobs to make ends meet, as inflation (4%+) outpaces wage growth (3%)
- Cape Verde tied Spain 0-0 in a massive World Cup upset; one player was recruited via LinkedIn DMs from Ireland

## Overview

A wide-ranging summer episode covering ice cream inflation as an economic microcosm, the exploding gray-market peptide industry, new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish debut, and a Cape Verde World Cup upset story. The hosts explore how cumulative inflation has reshaped consumer behavior (luxuriization), the regulatory chaos around peptides under RFK Jr., and the growing divide between the SpaceX/AI economy and workers juggling multiple jobs.

## Implications

Warsh's hawkish stance signals continued rate pressure despite Trump's calls for cuts. The peptide market represents a regulatory vacuum that could reshape pharmaceutical policy. Consumer 'trade-down to luxury' behavior is a new inflation-era pattern worth monitoring.
