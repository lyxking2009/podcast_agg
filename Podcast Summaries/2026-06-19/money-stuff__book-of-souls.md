# Book of Souls

**Show:** Money Stuff: The Podcast  
**Guests:** Matt Levine, Katie Greifeld  
**Date:** 2026-06-19  
**Transcript source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points

- New Yorker profile of Ken Griffin reveals Citadel's model: not stock-picking but repeatable 'quality of earnings' businesses — market-making, convertible arbitrage, liquidity provision services that used to be done by investment banks
- Citadel has constant high turnover; one employee kept a 'book of souls' about departed colleagues. The firm recreates portfolios from scratch rather than maintaining them, requiring constant personnel churn
- Bridgewater under CEO Nir Bardea is transitioning from a 'strange giant institution' to a smaller, higher-performance hedge fund — but outside investors who bought GP stakes for steady management fees are unhappy with variable performance fees
- Two of Bridgewater's seven institutional owners have sold shares back at a discount; Texas Teacher Retirement System also looking to sell
- SEC's proposed elimination of Regulation NMS trade-through rules: major unlock for tokenized stocks on crypto exchanges, since NMS doesn't work with blockchain settlement times
- Alex Thorn (Galaxy) called the NMS reform 'one of the biggest unlocks yet for tokenized stocks'
- Bill Ackman's Pershing Square structure: public shareholders get preferred returns on the first ~5% of performance (like management fees), employees get the upside tail — turning performance fees into something more like stable revenue

## Overview

Matt Levine and Katie Greifeld dissect the Ken Griffin/Citadel model revealed in a New Yorker profile, contrasting it with Bridgewater's ongoing restructuring under new leadership. The episode explores how multistrategy hedge funds function more like service businesses than investment firms, the tension between management fee stability and performance fee variability, and how SEC NMS reform could unlock tokenized stock trading on crypto exchanges.

## Implications

The Citadel model (repeatable fee-for-service businesses) is becoming the dominant hedge fund paradigm. Bridgewater's GP stake investors are learning that management company valuations depend on AUM stability. NMS reform is a major structural catalyst for crypto-tradfi convergence.
