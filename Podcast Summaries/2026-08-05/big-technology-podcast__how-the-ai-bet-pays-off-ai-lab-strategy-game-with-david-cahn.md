# How The AI Bet Pays Off + AI Lab Strategy Game — With David Cahn — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Sequoia partner David Cahn returns to Big Technology to update his AI ROI math: cumulative AI infrastructure CapEx since ChatGPT is roughly $3 trillion, requiring an equivalent multiple in lifetime revenue to pay back, and he argues 'nothing short of AGI' justifies spending at this scale. He and host Alex Kantrowitz then play a 'strategy game,' rating each major AI player's resource-allocation strategy: Anthropic (corner AI talent to win), OpenAI (all-in aggression on AGI), Google (TPU advantage undercut by insular execution), Meta (mercenary-army talent buying), Microsoft (patient hedge that wins either way), Amazon and Apple (do-little/do-nothing approaches), Nvidia (Jensen Huang's ecosystem-first strategy), and SpaceX (Elon Musk's mission-driven pivot to neocloud). The conversation closes with Cahn's reflections on whether AI is filling a spiritual void left by declining religion in the West.

## Key points
- Cahn's cumulative AI infrastructure CapEx tally since ChatGPT's launch: $200B (2023), $600B (2024), $850B (2025), and $1.5 trillion (2026) — summing to roughly $3 trillion that eventually needs to be paid back via revenue.
- Cahn's payback framework: for every $1 spent on a GPU, roughly $1 more is spent on energy/data-center costs, and builders need a 50% margin, implying each year's GPU CapEx requires about 4x that amount in lifetime revenue to be repaid.
- Cahn argues 'nothing short of AGI' can justify current and proposed CapEx levels; he sees a bifurcated outcome — either AGI arrives and pays back the investment many times over, or a financial reckoning follows.
- OpenAI and Anthropic together now generate over $100 billion in combined revenue, up from about $12 billion when Cahn published his original 2024 post.
- Anthropic's strategy, per Cahn: 'corner the world's talent in AI and win' — a consistent, AGI-focused strategy that lost far fewer people than rivals during Meta's 2025 superintelligence-lab poaching spree.
- Microsoft's strategy, per Cahn: a hedge that wins in multiple scenarios — Satya Nadella secured a large equity stake in OpenAI (Cahn estimates ~27%) and can profit either if frontier labs win or if models commoditize via Microsoft's distribution advantage.
- Nvidia's strategy, per Cahn: 'ecosystem first' — Jensen Huang keeps prices restrained and invests broadly in the ecosystem because his goal is for the overall AI market to succeed.
- SpaceX's strategy, per Cahn: after initially trying to build its own frontier model (Grok), it pivoted to becoming an AI/neocloud provider; Musk is framed as mission-driven rather than profit-driven.
- Cahn closes with a personal reflection connecting the AI buildout to spirituality, invoking the 'Tower of Babel' framing and questioning whether AI could partially refill a societal need for transcendence left by religion's decline in the West.
