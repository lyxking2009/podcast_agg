# BONUS: Future Standard President & CIO Mike Kelly — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Mike Kelly, President and CIO of Future Standard (formerly Franklin Square/FS Investments, now a $90 billion alternatives platform), traces his path from cold-calling Lee Cooperman to co-building FrontPoint Partners and transforming Franklin Square into a diversified multi-strategy alternatives platform. He argues private credit is not a bubble, having grown in lockstep with the US economy, and predicts the term 'alternatives' will eventually disappear, replaced by a growth/income/real-assets portfolio framework.

## Key points
- Kelly grew up in Queens, son of an NYPD cop; he taught himself investing as a teenager and kept the notebook of stock research he started at the time.
- After Cornell and Stanford GSB, Kelly cold-called hedge fund managers from a payphone; Lee Cooperman hired Kelly to work for free at Omega Advisors after Kelly said he was 'poor, hungry, and driven.'
- From Omega, Kelly moved to Tiger Management under Julian Robertson, then helped found FrontPoint Partners in 2000, later sold to Morgan Stanley.
- At ORIX Asset Management he led the acquisition of Robeco (a $300+ billion asset manager) from Rabobank, the largest deal in ORIX's history.
- He joined Franklin Square as President/CIO, pitching founder Michael Forman on building in-house investment capabilities rather than just reselling Blackstone-subadvised strategies; under Kelly the firm evolved into Future Standard, now managing over $90 billion.
- Kelly disputes that private credit is a bubble: the sector has grown in lockstep with an economy that expanded from roughly $12-13 trillion pre-GFC to $30 trillion today, with market share shifting from banks to asset managers post-Dodd-Frank.
- He estimates that even a severe stress scenario in large-cap software-sector credit would only shave about one point of annual return, calling this normal sector weakness, not a systemic crisis.
- Kelly rejects the standard 60/40 portfolio framework, proposing instead three buckets — growth, income, and real assets — arguing private equity behaves more like public equity risk-wise than like real estate credit.
- He cites a stat that 88% of surveyed financial advisors plan to allocate client capital to private market strategies, though many remain at 0% today, indicating the space is still in early adoption.
