# Are Perpetuals Swaps or Futures? The CME Picks a Fight

## Key Points
- **CME sued the CFTC** over its approval of Kalshi's perpetual futures as futures contracts, arguing they meet the Dodd-Frank definition of swaps
- **Joint CFTC/SEC request for comment** issued the same day — seeking to clarify definitions of "swaps," "security-based swaps," and novel products like perpetual futures
- **Core legal question:** Should perpetual futures (where parties exchange ongoing payments) be classified as swaps or futures?
- **CME CEO Terrence Duffy:** "Under the Dodd-Frank Act, it clearly defines what a swap is and what a future is, and when there's two parties exchanging payments to each other, that's deemed a swap"
- **CFTC plans to seek dismissal** of the lawsuit, characterizing it as contrary to the Trump administration's pro-innovation agenda
- **60-day public comment period** after Federal Register publication
- **Stakes:** The resolution determines which regulator (CFTC or SEC) oversees perpetual futures and prediction-market contracts

## Overview
Two parallel and conflicting actions occurred regarding derivatives definitions: the CFTC and SEC issued a joint request for public comment on clarifying swaps definitions, while CME Group simultaneously filed a lawsuit against the CFTC over its approval of Kalshi's perpetual futures as futures rather than swaps. The episode covers this regulatory clash and its implications for the U.S. derivatives market, including who gets to compete and under what rules.

## Implications
- The outcome will determine whether new market entrants (like Kalshi) can offer perpetual futures under CFTC jurisdiction or face stricter swap regulations
- CME's lawsuit signals that incumbent exchanges see perpetual futures as a competitive threat
- The joint CFTC/SEC comment period suggests regulators are trying to get ahead of the litigation
- This is the most significant derivatives classification dispute since Dodd-Frank

## Notable Quotes

- "Today's joint request for public comment presents an opportunity to address longstanding ambiguities within Title VII of Dodd-Frank that have stifled fair competition and responsible innovation." — CFTC Chairman Michael Selig
