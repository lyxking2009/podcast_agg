# Meet The VCs Digging for Gold in the Gutter with Dan Teran & James Gettinger

## Key Points
- **Market volatility signals:** SOXX hit all-time high then fell 7%, KOSPI dropped 10% overnight — raises concerns about "global snowball effect"
- **Meme stock dynamics** noted in names like Intel; heavy AI capital raising with push-outs in data-center build plans
- **Consumer strain** evident in retailer and restaurant commentary
- **Micron (MU) earnings** highlighted for extreme implied moves; hosts skeptical that "dirt cheap" valuation offsets cyclicality
- **Gutter Capital closed $75M Fund III** — concentrated, hands-on portfolio strategy
- **Elbow Grease accelerator** in NYC (second iteration) — puts 15 startups in one building
- **Dan Teran** founded and sold Managed by Q to WeWork; these experiences shaped Gutter's operational approach
- **James Gettinger** spent a decade as a profitable professional gambler, informing his quantitative investing style
- **Critique of current VC:** Venture has become increasingly consensus-driven; rising seed valuations make early fundraising harder for nontraditional founders
- **Gutter's thesis:** Earlier-stage bets, community/mentorship on Canal Street, AI-enabled application-layer companies

## Overview
Dan Nathan and Guy Adami open with market analysis — heightened volatility in NASDAQ and semiconductors, consumer strain signals, and Micron's upcoming earnings. They then welcome Gutter Capital co-founders Dan Teran and James Gettinger for a deep dive into their venture approach. The pair discuss closing their $75M Fund III, running the Elbow Grease accelerator, and their thesis on AI-enabled application-layer companies. The conversation covers Teran's experience selling Managed by Q to WeWork, Gettinger's professional gambling background, and their critique of the increasingly consensus-driven VC landscape.

## Implications
- The SOXX/KOSPI volatility may signal broader market fragility
- AI capital raising fatigue could create opportunities for contrarian VCs
- Concentrated, hands-on VC strategies may outperform spray-and-pray approaches
- Application-layer AI companies may offer better risk-adjusted returns than frontier model bets
