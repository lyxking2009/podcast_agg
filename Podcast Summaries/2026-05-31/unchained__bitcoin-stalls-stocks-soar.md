## Overview
In this episode of Bits + Bips: The Interview, host Steve Ehrlich (Head of Research at SharpLink) speaks with Steve Sosnick, Chief Strategist at Interactive Brokers, about the divergence between soaring equity markets and a stagnant Bitcoin amid a complex macro backdrop. The conversation covers the Iran ceasefire "ratchet effect" driving stocks higher despite persistent geopolitical risk, the uncomfortable parallels between today's AI infrastructure buildout and the 1999 bandwidth boom that preceded widespread telecom bankruptcies, and why core PCE hitting a multi-year high has flipped the Fed's posture from cutting to potentially hiking. Sosnick argues that Bitcoin is losing its competition for speculative capital to AI stocks, upcoming mega-IPOs from SpaceX, OpenAI and Anthropic, and even a focused DRAM memory chip ETF that rivaled IBIT's record launch. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh enters a politically fraught environment with a skeptical FOMC and an inflation picture worsened by Gulf supply disruptions, making his first policy moves highly uncertain.

## Implications
The episode highlights a structural challenge for Bitcoin during this cycle: the same retail and institutional infrastructure that supercharged crypto adoption via ETFs has also normalized it as "just another risk-on sector," making it vulnerable to rotation whenever a more exciting narrative emerges. The AI capex boom is absorbing the speculative capital that might otherwise flow into crypto, and unless Bitcoin reasserts a clear narrative — digital gold, inflation hedge, or uncorrelated asset — it risks being treated as a second-tier momentum trade. Meanwhile, the Fed's constrained ability to cut rates (due to persistent inflation from the Gulf and a hawkish FOMC consensus) removes a key tailwind that drove crypto's prior bull run.
