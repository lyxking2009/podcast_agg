# Uneasy Money: Why Ethereum's 'Ultrasound Money' Meme Finally Ran Out of Road — Unchained

## TL;DR
Kain Warwick, Austin Griffith, and Greg Marcu discuss the Ethereum Foundation's Sepaku lawsuit and the 'suicide pact' clause, arguing that focusing on it is a distraction from real issues. They explore why Ethereum's 'ultrasound money' meme faded as Vitalik Buterin emphasized intrinsic value, and examine tensions between core developers and app developers over gas repricing. The episode also covers Ledger's signing SDK telemetry concerns and MegaETH's pivot to building a blockchain-based AI inference marketplace to exploit API vs subscription arbitrage.

## Key points
- Kain Warwick argues that the EF Sepaku lawsuit's focus on the 'suicide pact' clause is a distraction; anyone who prioritizes removing it is not someone the foundation should want, and the clause can be seen as a metaphorical samurai honor code, not making light of suicide.
- Warwick separates the suicide clause from the cancer-related firing, stating that while you shouldn't fire someone for having cancer, you are allowed to fire someone who is problematic even if they have cancer, and conflating the two is misleading.
- Greg Marcu notes that the EF document is silly but acceptable as a metaphorical statement, and that many good people left the EF after organizational changes, leaving a less serious org; Vitalik's stance against 'shenanigans' is justified because foundations attract people causing mischief.
- EIP-1559 significantly improved transaction UX by reducing failures and smoothing fee estimation, but the burn mechanism was an unintended addition that led to the 'ultrasound money' meme, which became the dominant narrative around ETH's value.
- Vitalik Buterin's recent comments signal a capitulation on the ultrasound money meme, urging the community to focus on Ethereum's intrinsic properties rather than memetic extrinsic valuation, but the ecosystem lacks credible researchers to articulate ETH's value to Wall Street.
- Core developers are facing backlash from application developers over gas repricing changes (e.g., increasing S store costs), as core devs often do not fully test impact on existing smart contracts, leading to breakages.
- Greg Marcu explains that core devs are overwhelmed by a flood of EIPs (10 per day), forced to relitigate dead proposals, and may not actively use the chain, causing blind spots; they rely on testnets but full state testing is nearly impossible due to off-chain dependencies.
- Past gas repricing and protocol changes have caused significant issues, including Greg Marcu breaking web3.js and bricking apps after EIP-1559, and the 1inch gas token leading to S store repricing and uncovering the Osaka bug.
- Ledger's signing SDK sends transaction metadata to their server after signing, which is unnecessary and potentially exposes user address and behavior; their CTO's explanation that devices lack memory for clear signing is questionable given ample alternatives.
- Clear signing for hardware wallets remains incomplete; dynamic contracts (proxies, diamond patterns) require trusted server lookups, but Ledger's telemetry approach is risky and contrasts with better models like Grid Plus.
- The future of crypto UX is likely passkey-based multisig wallets with daily limits, social recovery, and embedded in applications, rather than standalone hardware wallets; users are accustomed to cumbersome banking security, so adoption may be easier than expected.
- MegaETH is pivoting to an AI inference marketplace to exploit the massive arbitrage between API subscription costs and usage, using blockchain to create a permissionless two-sided market; Venice is an early example, and the subsidy-driven market will inevitably be arbed away.
