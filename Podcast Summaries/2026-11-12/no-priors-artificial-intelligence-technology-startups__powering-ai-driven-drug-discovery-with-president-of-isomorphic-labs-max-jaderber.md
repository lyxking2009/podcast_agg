# Powering AI-Driven Drug Discovery with President of Isomorphic Labs Max Jaderberg — No Priors: Artificial Intelligence | Technology | Startups


## TL;DR
Max Jaderberg, President and founding team member of Isomorphic Labs, discusses how the company is using AI to transform drug discovery. He explains that traditional pharma R&D is empirical, expensive, and slow, with costs rising to $1.5 billion per drug and timelines stretching to 15 years. Isomorphic aims to move drug discovery in silico by building AI models with experimental accuracy, reducing real-world iteration and improving clinical success rates.

## Key points
- Isomorphic Labs, backed by Alphabet, was founded in 2021 with the mission to solve all disease. Max Jaderberg, a deep learning pioneer who sold a computer vision startup to DeepMind in 2014 and worked on AlphaGo and AlphaStar, is president and part of the founding team.
- Pharma productivity has stagnated: annual new drug approvals are roughly flat despite a 10-20x explosion in scientific knowledge and new modalities. Cost per drug has risen from a few hundred million dollars to $1.5 billion, and development time has increased from 8-9 to 15 years.
- The core problem is that drug discovery remains an empirical, trial-and-error process constrained by low-throughput real-world experiments. Low-hanging fruit medicines have been found, and remaining diseases require solving highly complex biological and molecular design spaces.
- Isomorphic's goal is to bring drug discovery onto a computer by building AI models that achieve 'experimental accuracy' for structure prediction, molecular interactions, and toxicity, thereby reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world synthesis and assays.
- AlphaFold served as the proof of concept that deep learning can generalize meaningfully in biology. Isomorphic is now developing a suite of predictive models across drug design, disease biology, and clinical safety to compound learnings across programs.
- Most clinical failures occur because toxicity and off-target effects are only discovered when drugs reach patients. Current animal models are only about 30% predictive of human clinical toxicity, creating a huge opportunity for AI models trained on human-relevant in vitro and organoid data.
- Isomorphic's business model is to use its AI drug design engine internally to create medicines, either wholly owned or through partnerships with pharma and biotech. They do not license the models or sell tokens.
- Internal drug discovery focuses on oncology, immunology, and inflammation, prioritizing root-node mechanisms like RAS mutations (e.g., Revolution Medicines' KRAS inhibitor for pancreatic cancer) that could impact many patients across different indications.
- Hiring combines core AI researchers with domain experts in biology and chemistry at roughly a 5:1 ratio. The company emphasizes building a cross-disciplinary culture to create a new type of drug discovery scientist who understands both deep learning and life sciences.
- Isomorphic generates much of its own proprietary data rather than relying on biased public or pharma repositories. This approach has led to promising data scaling laws and better model generalization.
- Combining drugs into cocktails is currently limited by toxicity and drug-drug interactions. Max argues that better molecular engineering could enable safe combinations that modulate multiple targets and pathways simultaneously, improving treatment of complex diseases like cancer.
- On regulation, Max stresses patient safety but notes that risk appetite varies by indication—late-stage cancer patients accept more risk than those with chronic low-level inflammation. AI-designed molecules with superior safety profiles may eventually shift regulatory frameworks.
- Max predicts that within five years the base tools for predictive understanding of disease biology will step-change in a massive way. While clinical trials are still years away, Isomorphic is building its clinical development team to prepare for first-in-human studies.
- On longevity, removing cancer and heart disease would extend average lifespan by 5-7 years, but healthspan—quality of life in later years—is equally important. Isomorphic's platform could address aging-related degradation by targeting root causes and improving molecular precision.
