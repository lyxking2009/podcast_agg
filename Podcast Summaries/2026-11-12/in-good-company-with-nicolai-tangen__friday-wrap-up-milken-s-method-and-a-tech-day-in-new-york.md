# Friday Wrap-Up: Milken’s Method and a Tech Day in New York — In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen

## TL;DR
In this Friday wrap-up, Nicolai Tangen and Marte Skahr discuss their interview with Mike Milken, who pioneered the high-yield bond market and uses Socratic questioning to change minds. They reflect on investor psychology, the need to challenge biases, and current market volatility, particularly tech corrections tied to data center delays. Tangen also highlights how many companies are underinvesting in AI, creating risks of obsolescence and private equity takeovers, and recaps the fund's global Tech Day initiative.

## Key points
- Mike Milken pioneered the high-yield (junk) bond market, which enabled consolidation of telecom, media, and cable industries and supported founders like Rupert Murdoch; more than 80 firms trace their origins to people trained under him.
- Milken's core insight was questioning assumed safety: assets perceived as safe are often riskier than thought, while pooled risky assets can be less risky and pay more even after accounting for higher losses.
- Milken changes minds by asking simple multiple-choice questions rather than raising his voice, allowing people to reach conclusions themselves; this is powerful because people's opinions often become entrenched like religion.
- As investors, overcoming pre-held biases is among the most difficult tasks; one should daily ask, 'Why do we have this position? Would I buy it from scratch today?'
- Extensive interview preparation—reading relevant book chapters, listening to prior interviews, and soliciting questions from experts—reduces regret over missed questions; the art is balancing forward movement with follow-up drilling.
- Markets have been volatile since the midterms, with tech sector correction driven by potential delays in data center build-outs.
- The U.S. demonstrates greater agility than Europe, shrugging off losses after the financial crisis and moving on quickly, whereas Europe remained stuck with unresolved losses.
- Many companies are underinvesting in AI adoption and integration; a gulf is opening between AI-modern firms and old-fashioned ones, and private equity players are acquiring old businesses to replace labor with AI (e.g., SoftBank targeting a guarding company).
- The fund runs global Tech Days across Oslo, London, Singapore, and New York, sharing best practices and user cases; the New York office currently operates 16 AI agents, with an upcoming podcast featuring Greta on technology efficiency.
- Favorite books: Mike Milken loved the almanac for facts; Nikolaj Tangen favored The Three Musketeers and The Count of Monte Cristo; Marte Skahr loved The Brothers Lionheart by Astrid Lindgren and named her son Jonathan after a character.
