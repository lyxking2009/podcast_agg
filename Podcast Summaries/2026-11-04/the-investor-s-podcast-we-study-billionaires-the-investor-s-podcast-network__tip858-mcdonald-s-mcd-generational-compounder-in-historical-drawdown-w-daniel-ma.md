## Key points
- McDonald's functions primarily as a real estate company: it oversees roughly 46,000 restaurants generating $139 billion in annual systemwide sales, with about 95% run by franchisees who paid McDonald's approximately $10.4 billion in rent and $6 billion in royalties last year, meaning about 91% of restaurant-level profit comes from rent and royalties rather than burger sales.
- Ray Kroc, after taking over McDonald's, partnered with Sonneborn to develop the real estate model that underpins the company's profitability by owning the land and leasing it to franchisees.
- Bill Ackman proposed a refranchising plan in 2005 to unlock value, but management rejected it; the strategy was later implemented a decade later under CEO Steve Easterbrook, who was subsequently fired and returned $105 million in compensation.
- Franchisees are currently pushing back on costs and support, leading McDonald's to announce roughly $8.5 billion in rent relief and remodel support at its September investor day.
- The hosts examine whether GLP-1 weight-loss drugs could pose a terminal risk to McDonald's demand, analyzing potential long-term impacts on consumption.
- They build a valuation model to determine whether McDonald's stock is worth its current price and whether it deserves inclusion in The Intrinsic Value Portfolio.
