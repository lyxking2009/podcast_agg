# The Chopping Block: Bitget's 387 Million Dollar Hack, Kalshi's Cooked Perps Volume, and Agentic Bank Runs — Unchained

## TL;DR
The hosts discuss three major topics: the $387M Bitget hack attributed to North Korea's Lazarus Group, and the debate over cross-chain protocols like Thorchain versus Near Intents in handling stolen funds; the Kalshi perps volume controversy where an analyst exposed inflated volumes driven by market maker incentives; and the concept of agentic bank runs where AI agents could optimize away consumer laziness. They also touch on staking risk after the Consensus validator hack.

## Key points
- Bitget was hacked for $387 million, likely by North Korea's Lazarus Group. The exchange was solvent, using its $460 million insurance fund to fully cover losses, and withdrawals resumed quickly.
- Stolen funds were moved from stablecoins to Ethereum to avoid freezes, then routed through Thorchain for cross-chain swaps. Thorchain was criticized for being too permissive, while Near Intents' Shield protocol froze $500k and refused $100M of suspicious assets.
- The hosts debated the spectrum of decentralization vs. censorship. Robert argued for two extremes: fully neutral infrastructure like Uniswap or actively policing systems like centralized exchanges, with no middle ground. Tarun countered that Near's programmatic anomaly detection is a balanced approach and a fuller conception of property rights.
- Consensus, a major Ethereum validator, had its staking node compromised. The attacker exited the stake, causing temporary loss of rewards (~200 ETH) but no principal loss. This raised questions about the 'risk-free' nature of staking.
- Robert estimated the staking risk premium at around 5 basis points, far below the 35 basis points CDS on US Treasuries. He suggested staking providers could offer fixed yield insurance products.
- Analyst Benny exposed Kalshi's inflated perps volume, showing that market maker incentives led to wash trading-like behavior. Kalshi had huge volume relative to tiny open interest. The company ended its liquidity incentives program, and the CFTC is reportedly investigating.
- The hosts discussed market maker rebates on exchanges, noting that they are common but become problematic when they create a false impression of traction. Kalshi's rebates were likely tied to equity, encouraging market makers to cross-trade.
- Apollo's slide suggested AI agents like Muse and Hermes could cause 'agentic bank runs' by moving deposits to higher-yield products. The hosts largely dismissed the suddenness, arguing it would be gradual as consumers are slow to adopt agents, but acknowledged that agents could accelerate SVB-style runs once adopted.
- Steve shared a personal anecdote: using Muse to review health insurance claims, he found $1,700 in overcharges and recovered $200 so far, illustrating the potential of agents to erode consumer laziness.
