# ROLLUP: Uptober Green Light? | Robinhood Goes All-In | $400M Bitget Hack | Prediction Markets to SCOTUS

## Overview

Bankless's weekly ROLLUP with Ryan Sean Adams and David Hoffman, recovered via web fallback after the Flightcast RSS feed returned its intermittent 167-byte error. Bitcoin closed both its weekly and monthly candles above the May high, breaking the bearish market structure and prompting the hosts to ask whether this is a green light for 'Uptober'. Against that, the bond market is doing something uncomfortable: long yields accelerated higher even on a better-than-expected CPI print, with the 30-year reaching its highest level since 2002 and the 10-year back above 5%. The rest of the episode covers Robinhood's HOOD Summit announcements, the $400M Bitget hack and NEAR's decision to refuse the funds, prediction markets heading to the Supreme Court, and Apollo's warning about agentic bank runs. Full transcript published on bankless.com.

## Key Points

- Market structure: Bitcoin's weekly and monthly candles closed above the previous May high, which the hosts read as breaking the bearish structure and opening a runway for the rest of October.
- Ryan cites quant Michael Nadeau's confirmation - a second weekly close above the 50-week moving average (76.8K) and a close above 84.5K - as the early-bull confirmation.
- The paradox: CPI came in better than expected at 3.0% annualised versus roughly 3.3% expected, with July revised down - yet yields rose instead of fell, and accelerated faster than in any of the prior four weeks.
- The 30-year reached 5.64%, its highest since 2002; the 10-year returned to 5.3%. David: 'yield's going crazy.'
- Ben Hunt's warning is quoted: the move in the 10- and 30-year is not oil- or Iran-related (oil was down on the week), and 'something in the financial world is going to break.'
- The hosts push back on alarmism, citing Jim Bianco: 5.6% is a historically normal yield, and anything below 2% was the real anomaly. The concern is the speed of the move, not the level.
- Altcoin froth had gotten extreme - Zcash, Morpho and Uni down double digits while the same names had run 50-300% - which the hosts read as healthy mean reversion, with 96% of the top 50 beating Bitcoin over 30 days.
- Robinhood's HOOD Summit: expanded agent capabilities, the rollout of US perpetuals, a new social feed, and a short-term blow to Lighter.
- Bitget's $400M hack: the funds ran through THORChain while NEAR's Shield refused to route them, which the show treats as a live test of what 'decentralised' commits you to.
- Prediction markets: US courts are increasingly siding with state gaming laws over prediction markets, and the fight is heading to the Supreme Court.
- Apollo's agentic bank-run thesis is covered alongside Ethereum's 'cryptographic world computer' framing.

## Implications

The ROLLUP's central tension is worth taking seriously because it is not a crypto-only story: bonds are repricing upward on a good inflation print, which is not what a standard rate model predicts, and the hosts land on velocity rather than level as the risk - anything leveraged to the long end can be wrong-footed by the speed of the move even if 5.6% is a historically ordinary number. For crypto specifically, the structural break above the May high is being read as cycle confirmation, but the same rising-rate environment that makes risk assets expensive is the stated threat to it. The Bitget/NEAR episode is the more durable thread: a chain chose to refuse tainted funds, which converts 'decentralisation' from a slogan into a governance decision with legal consequences.
