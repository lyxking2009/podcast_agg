# Reintegration With the Default World

## Overview

Matt Levine, guest-hosting with Mary Childs while Katie Greifeld is on parental leave, works through two threads. The first is the IRS crackdown on 351-exchange ETFs - the structure that lets an investor contribute an appreciated stock portfolio into an ETF and then trade inside it tax-free by combining a 351 contribution with the ETF's in-kind heartbeat trades. Levine explains why each leg is independently non-taxable, why combining them means nobody would ever pay tax on stock trades, and why this week's revenue ruling recharacterises the combined transaction under substance-over-form. The second thread is agentic bank runs: what happens to a banking system built on customer inertia when AI agents start optimising their owners' cash into higher-yielding accounts.

## Key Points

- The 351-exchange ETF structure works because of two separately non-taxable legs: contributing appreciated stock to an ETF in exchange for ETF shares (changing the form of ownership), and the ETF's in-kind heartbeat trades with authorised participants.
- Combined, those two legs let a large investor rebalance a stock portfolio indefinitely without ever incurring capital gains tax - and the wealth threshold was falling, which is why the industry was extrapolating to a world where nobody pays tax on stock trades.
- This week's IRS revenue ruling says that if you contribute appreciated stock to an ETF and, as part of the same plan, immediately heartbeat it out for a diversified portfolio, the transaction gets recharacterised as a sale and taxed.
- Levine's analogy is the 1031 like-kind real-estate exchange: both are 'aggressive settings of the batteries' that are nonetheless written into the tax code, and both work because they have a well-organised constituency.
- The open question left by the ruling is timing - nobody knows how long you would need to wait between contribution and heartbeat for the structure to survive.
- The controlling doctrine is substance over form: tax law routinely accepts each step of a plan individually while refusing the combination when it is 'too cute'.
- Agentic bank runs: as AI agents get deployed to consumer accounts, one of the first things an agent will optimise is idle cash earning 0.01% in checking - the whole point being that a large part of the banking system is predicated on people not doing that.
- Levine's counterweight: if everyone optimally moved cash and refinanced mortgages, the likely consequence is not a collapsed banking system but repricing - you would pay a higher rate for the option, just as mortgage rates would be higher if everyone refi'd optimally.
- The episode name-checks the move towards narrow banking as the structural answer, alongside the viral 'agentic bank run' framing (which Levine notes he did not start).
- A running gag that carries a real point: podcast admissions create intent, which is exactly the kind of evidence that defeats a substance-over-form tax structure.

## Implications

Two things worth carrying forward. First, the ETF tax ruling is a reminder that tax arbitrage is doctrinally limited by substance-over-form, not by the technical validity of each step - and that the boundary is drawn in practice by the size and political organisation of the constituency, which is why 1031 survives and this did not. Second, the agentic bank run framing is the sharper idea: banks earn from depositor inertia, and agent-mediated optimisation of cash and refinancing choices converts a slow, sticky deposit base into a fast, rate-sensitive one. That is a deposit-beta story before it is a crisis story.
