# Is Gen Z Completely Cooked? With Ed Elson — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Josh Brown and Michael Batnick interview Ed Elson about AI hype, market structure, and Gen Z's economic prospects. They dissect the White House AI summit, where tech CEOs were pressed on safety, and debate whether AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic are overvalued given massive losses ($8B operating loss for OpenAI). Elson presents stark statistics about Gen Z—such as one in five having no friends and a third living with parents—arguing the generation faces unprecedented financial and social hurdles, while the hosts push back on generational pessimism.

## Key points
- Tech now accounts for 55% of all US capital spending, up from 15% in 1960, and could reach 80%, signaling an unprecedented tech investment cycle.
- Ed Elson argues the conversation around AI safety is 'the dumbest conversation of the year' because claims like Anthropic's 10% extinction risk are not backed by evidence; he calls for subpoenas and formal investigations to verify such claims.
- The White House AI meeting was performative: tech leaders (Jensen Huang, Dario Amodei, Sam Altman, etc.) gave 'hostage statements' under pressure from Trump, with no substantive regulation announced.
- Trump's four-point AI safety framework includes internal controls, internal oversight teams, external auditors, and an independent board committee, but Elson criticizes it as industry-written self-regulation.
- OpenAI posted $8 billion in operating losses while Anthropic has significant customer concentration; Elson warns this is a '$2 trillion venture bet' and private market valuations are not based on price discovery.
- Public AI-related stocks trade at more modest valuations; Nvidia at 16x forward earnings is not in bubble territory, but private AI labs may be, suggesting a 'pocket bubble' rather than broad market bubble.
- Elson is excited about AI in biotech and medical discovery (e.g., protein design) but wants to see at least one frontier AI company become profitable to prove unit economics.
- Autonomous vehicles like Waymo could prevent ~20,000 annual US deaths from drunk/distracted driving; Elson is bullish on Waymo but skeptical of Tesla's robotaxi promises.
- Gen Z faces severe economic challenges: median home price is >7x annual income (vs 3x for grandparents), a third live with parents, half don't want children, and half say socialism is better, reflecting financial nihilism.
- One in five Gen Zers report having no friends; loneliness is a major issue, and young people spend 118 days per year on phones, leaving limited time for in-person socializing.
- Many Gen Z investors are drawn to risky assets: a fifth have crypto as their only investment, and a quarter of young men equate sports betting with investing.
- Elson advocates for policy solutions: build more housing, support 401(k) for all (Trump accounts), oppose reckless fiscal policies like the proposed $1.3 trillion dividend, and address rising national debt.
- On education, test scores are declining, and the gap between top and bottom students is widening; Elson argues AI should be used as a tutor to reduce inequality, not as a crutch that replaces learning.
