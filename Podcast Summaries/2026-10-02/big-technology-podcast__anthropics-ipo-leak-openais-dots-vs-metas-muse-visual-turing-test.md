# Anthropic's IPO Leak, OpenAI's Dots vs. Meta's Muse, Visual Turing Test

## Overview

Big Technology Podcast's Friday edition, with host Alex Kantrowitz joined by Ranjan Roy of Margins. The anchor segment is the leaked Anthropic pre-IPO S-1: revenue, losses, and a $518 billion, largely non-cancellable compute commitment that Kantrowitz frames as a collective "leap of faith" the whole market is taking. That leads into a discussion of record-low S&P 500 breadth and how a wobble in the AI narrative could cascade through hyperscaler earnings. The second half covers OpenAI's new always-on agent Dots (Roy's hands-on review, and why he nearly deleted Meta's Muse) versus Meta's Muse/Muse for Enterprise, Google's Gemini Spark and Instinct, and the debate over whether frontier intelligence is required for these tasks. It closes on Tavus's "Griffin" lifelike video-call avatar and whether the visual Turing test has effectively been passed.

## Key Points

- Reuters leaked essentially the whole Anthropic S-1: revenue grew from $400M in 2024 to $4.6B in 2025, with a claimed $10B ARR at year-end; the reported ~$42B net loss is really ~$8B after ~$34B of accounting treatment (mark-to-market), i.e. Anthropic spent roughly $2 for every $1 of revenue.
- The striking number is the commitment: Anthropic told investors it plans to spend $518B over the next decade on compute and infrastructure, and 80% of that is binding/non-cancellable - against a most recent reportable year of ~$4B revenue.
- Compute commitments run to Alphabet ($11B), Amazon ($110B) and Microsoft ($31.4B) - the same companies that are investors, creating circular-financing risk. Roy argues they would likely rescue Anthropic rather than let it fail.
- Risk factors: nearly a quarter of Anthropic's revenue came from two customers last year, and many of its largest clients are not locked into long-term contracts (customers can cut spending at will, even as Anthropic's own commitments cannot be cancelled).
- Market concentration: the S&P 500 is at its worst breadth since the dot-com bubble - the median stock is ~16% below its 52-week high while the index is up ~13% year to date. Nvidia added more than $1T in market cap in 2026, and roughly half of S&P EPS growth comes from AI investment (Goldman). Peter Schiff: the index is ~0.7% below its high yet 430 stocks are ~27% below theirs, i.e. ~86% of stocks in a bear market - breadth this bad previously only in 1973 and 1990-2000, both followed by ~50% crashes.
- Kantrowitz's thesis is "as Anthropic goes, so goes AI": SpaceX set the precedent that a huge IPO can trade up and then fade without a collapse, but Anthropic carries structural risk (frontier models competing with cheaper standard models, token-cost-conscious customers) and could take the index down 5-10% (up to ~20% from highs) if the narrative breaks.
- OpenAI's Dots: an always-on personal agent announced at DevDay, competing with Meta's Muse, Google's Gemini Spark and Instinct, priced at ~$100/mo (Pro). A shaky demo, and CNBC's Sarah Friar misnamed the product "Muse" on air - while Meta's stock rose on the read-through that Dots is directly competitive.
- Roy's Dots review: he gave it control of his laptop and browser; it picked up his writing voice from past emails, drafted replies, sent invoices, booked an El Salvador trip (hotels, rental car, itinerary), emailed a rental agency about off-road access, and signed a Web Summit speaker release. Verdict: impressive; he nearly deleted Muse.
- Frontier vs. non-frontier: Roy argues most of these agent tasks (email, invoicing, travel) do not require frontier intelligence and enterprises are shifting spend toward open-weight and in-house models (per Ramp spending data); the open-weight lag has compressed from 12-18 months to 3-6. This is also a core Anthropic risk.
- Differentiation among these converging agents will come down to trust - and to not overreaching, as when Muse pushed Roy an unsolicited news alert (a California AG subpoena to OpenAI) unprompted.
- Visual Turing test: Tavus previewed "Griffin," a lifelike avatar that can hear, see, speak and react on a live video call; nearly half of participants in a short company test believed they were talking to a human. Latency is the main tell. Both hosts agree the visual Turing test has essentially been passed, and flag the scam/pig-butchering implications ("Shrimp Jesus" with a face).

## Implications

The episode's most useful frame for operators is the mismatch between locked-in obligations and unlocked customers: Anthropic is contractually committed to ~$400B+ of non-cancellable spend while its own largest clients can walk, and a quarter of revenue sits with two customers. The market-concentration data is the practical macro read - index-level returns are carried by a handful of AI names even as the median stock is in a bear market, so AI-narrative risk is now broad-market risk. On the product side, the Dots review is a concrete signal that mainstream "always-on" agents are already good enough for real work tasks without frontier models, which cuts against the frontier-margin story underpinning the AI trade.
