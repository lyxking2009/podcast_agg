# Robert Friedland on the World's Monumental Shortage of Copper — Odd Lots

## TL;DR

Odd Lots goes deep on copper with Robert Friedland, the mining entrepreneur behind Ivanhoe Mines, as the metal sits near record highs around $14,875 a ton. The puzzle the hosts set up: enormous long-term demand from data centers and grid electrification, yet producers hesitate to build, and 2026 may bring the first annual decline in copper production since 2017.

## Key points

- Copper is near record highs but badly behaved: the price has often lagged the bullish narrative, and the volatility that accompanies new highs is precisely what makes producers reluctant to expand — the classic supercycle trap.
- Tracy Alloway cites BNP research suggesting 2026 could see the first decline in copper production since 2017, an extraordinary outcome in a market everyone describes as structurally short.
- The supercycle mechanism is laid out: volatility deters investment, underinvestment creates scarcity, scarcity lifts prices, high prices trigger simultaneous expansion, expansion creates a glut, and shareholders punish the companies that overbuilt — so memory and caution persist across cycles.
- Data centers, electrification and grid infrastructure are the demand pillars; the hosts note resource nationalism and infrastructure spending as amplifiers, making copper the center of the story rather than oil.
- A tangent on copper's ubiquity: Peter Schiff's tweet noting that at $6.85 a pound, a pre-1982 penny has a melt value of about 4.5 cents — which the hosts note is also illegal to do, and logistically absurd to scale.
- Recycling is a material part of copper supply and competes with new mine production, meaning the supply response is not only about new mines but about scrap economics.
- Friedland, described as someone 'right in it,' is the guest bringing an operator's view of exploration, permitting timelines, and why the industry cannot simply turn on production when the price signals say to.
- The hosts close by noting there was little to add after Friedland's answers — a rare concession on a show that usually presses guests hard.
