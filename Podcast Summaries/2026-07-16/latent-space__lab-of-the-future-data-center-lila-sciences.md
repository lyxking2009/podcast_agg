# The Lab of the Future Should Feel Like a Data Center

**Show:** Latent Space: The AI Engineer Podcast  
**Date:** 2026-07-16  
**Duration:** 1:41:00

## Key Points

- Lila Sciences proposes treating the physical laboratory as a data center: instruments become nodes on a graph, a magnetically levitating transport layer ("PCI bus" for lab hardware) connects them, and an orchestration layer analogous to a Slurm queue schedules experiments
- Lila's core thesis: the scientific method is the last untapped internet-scale dataset; they treat reinforcement learning (RL) as a data generation mechanism with nature itself as the verifier
- Andy Beam (CTO) and Rafa Gómez-Bombarelli (CSO, Physical Sciences) discuss how their rebuilt gas sorption measurement runs approximately 2,500× faster than conventional approaches
- The company's AI model ("the brain") is paired with proprietary AI Science Factory instruments ("the body"), enabling autonomous hypothesis generation, experiment design, execution, and learning
- Lila ranked #25 on the 2026 CNBC Disruptor 50 and announced a partnership with NVIDIA BioNeMo Agent Toolkit
- Use cases span advanced materials, therapeutics, chemicals, energy/environment, aerospace & defense, and biotech
- Lila's CEO Geoffrey von Maltzahn frames the company's moment as a "Deep Blue moment" for AI in scientific discovery

## Overview

Latent Space hosts sit down with Andy Beam (CTO) and Rafa Gómez-Bombarelli (CSO, Physical Sciences) from Lila Sciences to explore what it looks like to build an AI-run laboratory from the ground up. Lila's argument is architectural: physical labs have historically been organized around human workflows, but if you redesign them around data generation and machine learning, you get compounding returns on scientific progress. The episode details how Lila engineers instruments as composable units, uses a novel magnetically levitating transport system to move samples between stations, and orchestrates everything with software inspired by compute cluster management. The conversation also covers how RL can work in the physical world when nature provides direct feedback on experimental outcomes — a fundamentally different environment from language model training where human preferences dominate.

## Implications

The "lab as data center" framing has significant implications for how AI companies approach scientific R&D. If Lila's approach generalizes, the bottleneck on drug discovery and materials science shifts from scientific intuition to compute and experimental throughput — a dynamic that strongly favors well-funded AI-native companies over legacy pharmaceutical and chemicals players. The 2,500× acceleration in gas sorption measurement suggests that the gains are not incremental; certain tasks may be orders-of-magnitude faster in the autonomous lab paradigm. The partnership with NVIDIA BioNeMo also suggests that the AI science infrastructure stack is beginning to consolidate around a few key platforms, similar to how cloud computing consolidated around AWS, Azure, and GCP.
