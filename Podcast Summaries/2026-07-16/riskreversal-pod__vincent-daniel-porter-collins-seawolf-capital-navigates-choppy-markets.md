## Key Points

- The Federal Reserve is described as being "in a box," caught between cooling inflation, softness in housing, and ballooning government interest costs — making any clear rate path difficult to signal.
- Oil prices remain elevated due to war-driven supply disruptions and a tight refined-product market; Collins and Daniel see continued energy price pressure as a structural, not cyclical, feature.
- Gold has pulled back sharply from a $5,500 peak to approximately $4,000, a move attributed to prior overbuying by central banks and Chinese institutions; the pair discuss whether the dip is a buying opportunity.
- AI's CapEx cycle is a double-edged sword: near-term margin benefits accrue to a handful of infrastructure and semiconductor names, but Daniel and Collins flag future unemployment risks as a longer-run headwind to consumer spending.
- Q2 earnings are the immediate catalyst being watched; the hosts stress that top-line revenue guidance will matter more than beats on already-lowered estimates in this environment.
- Middle East tensions are keeping a risk premium embedded in oil and acting as a drag on broader equity sentiment, though markets have so far proved resilient.
- Seawolf's framework leans on identifying "debasement" themes — assets that benefit from fiscal excess and an accommodating Fed — including precious metals and select commodity names.
- Kalshi event contracts are used as a precision tool to express theses on Fed decisions, oil price levels, and election outcomes without taking outright directional equity risk.
- Midterm election implications are beginning to be priced; the team references Brazil election odds as a case study in using prediction markets for macro positioning.
- The conversation closes with Open Championship golf picks at Royal Birkdale, a recurring lighter segment on the pod.

## Overview

Danny Moses hosts Vincent Daniel and Porter Collins of Seawolf Capital for their quarterly appearance on the RiskReversal Pod. The conversation navigates the core tension in markets right now: an equity tape that has remained resilient despite a Federal Reserve that is effectively paralyzed, elevated energy prices driven by geopolitical disruption, and an AI enthusiasm that may be creating future macro risks even as it lifts current margins. Daniel and Collins share how Seawolf is positioning across precious metals, energy, and event contracts to express its macro views in a market where traditional fundamental signals are noisy.

The duo's edge has historically come from a "debasement" framework — the thesis that fiscal excess and Fed accommodation create long-duration tailwinds for hard assets and select commodities. With gold having corrected sharply from its highs, they examine whether the macro setup that drove Seawolf's strong 2025 performance (a 44% gain) is still intact or whether market structure has shifted enough to demand a different playbook.

## Implications

For investors, the episode's core message is that the "easy money" in gold and simple debasement trades may be over for the near term after the steep correction from $5,500, but the structural argument — fiscal largesse, a captive Fed, and sovereign buying — has not changed, suggesting pullbacks remain opportunities rather than trend reversals. The Fed-in-a-box framing is particularly relevant: if the central bank cannot cut (inflation) or hike (housing and fiscal costs), volatility will stay elevated and the range of macro outcomes widens, which favors hedged, event-driven positioning such as Kalshi contracts over naked directional bets. On AI, the signal to watch is whether CapEx spending by hyperscalers translates into durable demand for the broader economy or becomes a concentrated, over-invested theme susceptible to sharp multiple compression.
