## Key Points

- NEAR Intents has become infrastructure enabling seamless asset trading across 35+ blockchains, positioning itself as crypto's defragmentation solution
- Most new stablecoins will function as backend accounting tools rather than independent branded currencies competing for wallet share
- MiCA (EU regulatory framework) forced Binance and Bybit into compliance challenges, paradoxically driving users toward on-chain alternatives and strengthening decentralized finance
- "Confidential Intents" — a new privacy feature using trusted execution environments — was recently launched to shield transaction details from counterparties and observers
- Tokenized real-world assets (stocks, securities) represent a major new asset class driving integration demand for NEAR Intents
- NEAR captures value via 10–20 basis points protocol fees on swaps, with proceeds directed toward token buybacks via the NEAR house of stake
- NEAR AI builds privacy-preserving AI infrastructure aimed at enterprises concerned about exposing sensitive business data to model developers

## Overview

Host David Hoffman sits down with Kendall Cole, Co-founder of Proximity Labs and core contributor to the NEAR ecosystem, to discuss how NEAR Intents has grown into a multi-chain interoperability layer used by wallets, applications, and chains across the industry. The conversation traces the evolution of chain abstraction from a niche idea to real infrastructure: users increasingly want to think in terms of assets rather than which specific blockchain holds them, and NEAR Intents fills that gap by providing a unified solver network. Cole explains that the crypto business model ultimately comes down to exchange volume regardless of whether a company is a DEX, lending protocol, or infrastructure layer — volume is the north star.

## Implications

The proliferation of stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets is converging with multi-chain infrastructure. If NEAR Intents becomes the default settlement layer for cross-chain asset movement, it stands to capture significant fee revenue at scale, and its token buyback mechanism aligns protocol success with token appreciation. The regulatory angle is notable: MiCA's restrictive approach appears to have inadvertently accelerated the decentralized alternatives it sought to constrain, raising broader questions about how national/regional regulation shapes the competitive landscape in crypto. Enterprise adoption of privacy-preserving AI through NEAR AI also hints at a potential second revenue stream for the ecosystem beyond DeFi.
