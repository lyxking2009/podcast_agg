# TIP848: Meta (META): What the Market Misses? w/ Daniel Mahncke & Shawn O'Malley — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)  - The Investor's Podcast Network

## TL;DR

Daniel Mahncke and Shawn O'Malley revisit Meta nine months after their first deep dive, asking whether the spending that has crushed the stock is buying a real third platform or repeating the metaverse mistake. The numbers are stark — ad revenue up 27% to roughly a $240 billion run rate, but operating margins down from 43% to 31%, quarterly free cash flow down from $8.5 billion to $784 million, and capex guidance of $130-145 billion this year — so the episode is really a valuation exercise about whether Meta's AI infrastructure, glasses, agent and cloud optionality justify the burn. Mahncke rebuilds the model from scratch and decides whether META finally earns a place in The Intrinsic Value Portfolio.

## Key points

- Meta reaches about 3.6 billion people every day — over 40% of the world's population — across Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, and just grew ad revenue 27% to nearly a $240 billion annual run rate.
- The stock trades more than 20% below its highs even without an advertising downturn, which is the puzzle the episode sets out to explain. Mahncke notes he started building a personal position not long before recording.
- Margin and cash-flow compression: operating margins fell from 43% to 31% in a single year and quarterly free cash flow collapsed from $8.5 billion to $784 million, while capex guidance sits at $130-145 billion this year with analysts pencilling in around $200 billion for next year.
- Zuckerberg announced on Threads that Meta intends to build AI infrastructure at scale — tens of gigawatts of data-centre capacity this decade, extending to hundreds of gigawatts over the following decades. The hosts note a single gigawatt is roughly the output of a nuclear reactor.
- Meta hired Dina Powell McCormick, a former government official, as president and vice chair explicitly to handle government partnerships and to finance the data-centre buildout. Mahncke reads this as evidence of the scale of the capital allocation: when your capex plans are so large that you need a dedicated federal-government diplomat, the numbers are serious.
- Data centres have become politically controversial where they were previously uncontroversial. Mahncke cites a Pew survey in which about half of Americans say they are more nervous than excited about AI, roughly 40% are equally excited and concerned, and only about 10% are more excited. He also relays a family example: a relative's land spent years in court seeking approval for a large data centre and the deal recently fell through — sentiment shifts are now producing real legal obstacles for big tech.
- Meta's $17 billion settlement with 47 states over teen safety is covered, alongside what the regulatory risk means for Meta's future.
- The hosts examine the special purpose vehicles that keep tens of billions of dollars of data-centre debt off Meta's balance sheet.
- The central strategic question: is this Mark Zuckerberg's third failed attempt to turn Meta into a platform — after the FarmVille-era Facebook apps and a roughly $100 billion metaverse spend — or does AI finally make the vision real?
- Four routes to that optionality are discussed: AI glasses, a personal agent, a budding cloud business, and an advertising engine where every pixel becomes sellable inventory. The hosts cover Meta's cloud business announcement as one of the biggest changes since the January episode.
- The optionality of WhatsApp is given its own segment, as is why AI could finally make Meta's glasses work.
- The episode contrasts Meta with Apple, which is quietly taking the opposite side of Meta's bet, and asks why Wall Street rewards Google for the same capex it punishes Meta for.
- Mahncke rebuilds the valuation model from scratch, discusses what the stock is actually worth, and decides whether META gets added to The Intrinsic Value Portfolio. The episode references Ben Thompson's Stratechery research and Zuckerberg's Wall Street Journal article 'The AI Future is for Everyone'.
