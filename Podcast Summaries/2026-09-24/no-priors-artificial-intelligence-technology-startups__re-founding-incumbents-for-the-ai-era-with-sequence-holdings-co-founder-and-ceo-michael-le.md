# Re-Founding Incumbents for the AI Era with Sequence Holdings Co-Founder and CEO Michael Lee — No Priors: Artificial Intelligence | Technology | Startups

## TL;DR

Michael Lee, co-founder and CEO of Sequence Holdings, makes the case to Sarah Guo that legacy incumbents get transformed from the inside out rather than replaced. Sequence is a permanent holding company that buys established real-economy businesses and re-founds them with frontier engineering — roughly one deal a year — most visibly through its $7.7 billion take-private of Baldwin alongside the Dell Family Office. Lee's thesis is that traditional consulting and software sales both fail at real enterprise AI transformation, because neither changes how the business actually operates.

## Key points

- Sequence Holdings positions itself as a permanent holding company that partners with established businesses core to the real economy and reimagines their operations through frontier technology.
- The interview's framing question is whether AI can transform legacy incumbents rather than replace them. Lee argues holding-company structures plus deep engineering integration are how market leaders get reshaped from the inside out.
- Sequence details the $7.7 billion take-private transaction of Baldwin alongside Dell Family Office (DFO), presented as the proof case for the model.
- Scarcity is deliberate: Sequence aimed at one deal a year. Lee says the firm's job is to do a single transaction and get it right, which is the anti-thesis of fund-style deal volume.
- The episode argues traditional consulting models and software sales both fall short for genuine enterprise AI transformation — neither rewires operations, they sell deliverables and licences.
- Segment on why a holding company rather than a fund: permanent capital and control let Sequence make operational changes that a time-limited fund structure with minority positions cannot.
- Segment on recruiting top engineers — a central constraint for a firm whose thesis is applying frontier engineering to unglamorous businesses.
- Worked example on community banking: Sequence made an investment in BankSouth, a roughly $1.6 billion-asset community bank headquartered in Greensboro, Georgia. A team of Sequence engineers with experience at Palantir, Scale AI and Apple works side by side with the bank's team to build and deploy AI-powered tools. The stated approach is human-first: employees remain in control of every decision and AI is a tool that helps bankers do more rather than a replacement for the people customers know.
- Lee joined BankSouth's board of directors as part of the partnership, bringing his financial services and technology background to the bank's governance.
- Segment on why Sequence bought an insurance brokerage, extending the same 'apply frontier engineering to a durable real-economy business' logic.
- The Atlas Platform is discussed as the internal technology layer Sequence builds and reuses across its holdings, which is what makes operational re-founding repeatable rather than bespoke.
- Segment on the limitations of traditional private equity, and on what Sequence looks for in management teams — the implication being that operator quality is the binding constraint on whether technology investments translate into results.
- Founder lessons and the story of the Dell partnership close the episode, covering how the DFO relationship was built.
- Lee's prior background: eight years at Lone Pine Capital, where he was managing director and head of private investments, before roles at Apollo Global Management and Goldman Sachs.
