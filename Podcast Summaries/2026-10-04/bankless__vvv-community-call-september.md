# $VVV Community Call | September — Bankless

## TL;DR

In September, Venice's daily LLM token processing accelerated from 100 billion to 250 billion, reinforcing its growth as a privacy-focused AI platform. The call covered VVV emission reductions to 2 million annually, a DIEM supply target rise to 40,000, and expansion of programmatic burns, while also addressing major AI privacy controversies from OpenAI and Anthropic. New products like Minds and in-house data centers signal Venice's bet on combinatorial specialized models and improved margins.

## Key points

- Venice's daily LLM token processing grew from 100 billion on June 25 to 250 billion on September 16, with the growth curve bending upward, indicating accelerating adoption. John calls this chart 'the best chart in crypto' as it represents the fundamental business metric driving revenue and burns.
- VVV emissions were reduced from 3 million to 2.5 million annually and will drop to 2 million on Thursday; John suggests further reductions may be needed to make VVV a deflationary asset, with more communication likely after mid-October.
- The DIEM supply target increased to 40,000, the sixth consecutive weekly increase. The team is monitoring staking, locking, and burning behavior, noting that VVV price rallies can create buy demand for DIEM as users unlock stakes, acting as a natural pendulum.
- Discretionary burns are funded from Venice's free cash flow after expenses and programmatic burns, executed as a continuous TWAP. For the first time, programmatic burns have eclipsed discretionary burns, a positive sign for tokenomics decentralization.
- Programmatic subscription burns were adjusted in April to stratify by tier, doubling Pro burns to $2, with higher tiers burning more. This improves on-chain data readability and aligns burn amounts with subscription value.
- OpenAI faced accusations that it accessed mathematicians' work via Codex to solve the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem. OpenAI couldn't rule out that user data was used in training, highlighting the structural lack of privacy in frontier AI labs.
- Anthropic changed its zero data retention policy to a 30-day retention for 'safety,' even for enterprise workspaces. John argues this likely serves training data needs more than safety, as labs are in a prisoner's dilemma to gather data.
- Venice's Minds product (beta) allows creators to combine multiple models and agents for specific tasks, betting that specialized combinatorial models will outperform a single super-model for user-specific needs.
- Venice is building its own data center, expected online late October or November, which should improve margins and potentially increase VVV burns through greater free cash flow.
- Venice collaborated with Moonpay on the Lumara Film Festival, receiving over 700 AI-generated short film submissions, demonstrating the democratization of film production through AI video tools.
- Venice integrates Nier for top-tier TEE and end-to-end encrypted inference models, offering cryptographic proof of privacy for users who require the highest level of data protection.
- A federal judge ordered OpenAI to turn over 20 million anonymized ChatGPT logs in a copyright case, showing the legal risks of centralized AI data retention and reinforcing Venice's no-data-storage advantage.
