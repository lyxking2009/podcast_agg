## TL;DR
Paul Collier argues that Britain's economic stagnation stems from over-centralization of power in Whitehall and the Treasury, which starves local areas of funding and autonomy. He contends that capitalism has been broken by incentives that divert investment away from productive businesses, with only 4% of UK savings staying in the country, and that energy costs and housing unaffordability harm the next generation. Collier proposes breaking up Treasury power, rebuilding local economies, and shifting taxes from enterprise to land to restore hope.

## Key points
- Whitehall's centralization makes even brilliant people useless, as power is concentrated in the Treasury, leaving towns and cities without money or freedom to shape their futures.
- The civil service has grown by 25%, yet only 4% of UK savings stay invested in Britain, indicating capital is not flowing to productive local businesses.
- Central planning always fails because it cannot capture local knowledge; Britain is far too centralized compared to successful economies.
- The loss of local banks has removed a key source of small-business lending and community investment.
- Energy costs are killing the economy, and the minimum wage is set too high, reducing employment opportunities for low-skilled workers, especially in poorer regions.
- Collier argues for taxing land rather than enterprise to shift incentives toward productive investment and away from speculation.
- Sheffield is now poorer than East Germany, highlighting the depth of regional decline in northern England.
- Brexit was a mutiny against London, reflecting widespread frustration with metropolitan elites and over-centralization.
- The right wing lacks figures who understand these structural economic problems; the military is more decentralized and effective than the bloated civil service.
- Collier advocates devolving power only to stable regions that cannot secede, to avoid fragmentation.
