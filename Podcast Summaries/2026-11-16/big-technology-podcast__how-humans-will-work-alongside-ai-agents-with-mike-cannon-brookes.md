# How Humans Will Work Alongside AI Agents — With Mike Cannon-Brookes — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Atlassian CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes argues that AI agents are already changing work by embedding into business processes, delivering modest efficiency gains and quality improvements but not yet massive productivity jumps. He contends that jobs will evolve rather than disappear, and that the key bottleneck is design and experience, not model capability, with Atlassian's Amp protocol and teamwork graph enabling multi-agent collaboration. Cannon-Brookes dismisses headless software as 'brainless' and emphasizes that the differentiator will be the harness, context, and interface layers built atop increasingly commoditized models.

## Key points
- Work is changing as companies embed AI agents into business processes, yielding modest efficiency gains, quality improvements, and early creativity growth, but efficiency gains are arguably less than hoped.
- Cannon-Brookes believes AI will be a net job creator; he likens the shift to bank branches, which did not disappear but changed function, and says job roles will evolve rather than vanish, so companies should focus on how to change jobs before competitors do.
- The critical bottleneck for realizing AI value is design and experience, not model capability; interfaces and workflows must change to enable humans and agents to collaborate intuitively.
- Atlassian recently launched 'Amp,' an agentic multiplayer protocol, to bring agents into Jira, Confluence, and Loom via MCP and A2A standards, aiming to integrate agents in familiar human-centric interfaces.
- Cannon-Brookes identifies four human-agent collaboration modalities: chat (one-on-one question/answer), delegation (agent acts on your behalf), collaborative multiplayer (multiple agents and humans editing together), and pairing (working side-by-side on same outcome).
- Atlassian's 'teamwork graph' is an enterprise context graph developed over eight years, now with roughly a thousand people, mapping relationships across knowledge, work, communications, code, physical assets, and data to provide context for agents.
- The teamwork graph learns and compounds: after each agent session, it extracts key memories and linked objects, feeding them into subsequent sessions to improve answer quality, reduce token usage, and speed outcomes.
- Models are not fully commoditized but have different strengths and cost profiles; Atlassian uses 'model blending' to route each question to the best model, avoiding the expensive and slower practice of always using the latest model.
- The differentiator in AI products will be the harness, context layer, interfaces, and application stack built atop models, not the models themselves, since model progress is broadly available to all vendors.
- Cannon-Brookes dismisses 'headless' software as 'brainless,' arguing that abandoning UI design for CLI-only agent access is a path to death because most users will not work from command lines and need visual, intuitive interfaces.
- The future of jobs involves focusing on desired outcomes over tasks, using taste and judgment, verifying AI outputs, and moving to higher abstraction with goal-seeking loops, but this must be packaged into everyday interfaces.
