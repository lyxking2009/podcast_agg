# The global bond markets: A new era — Strategic Alternatives

## TL;DR
In 2026, global investment-grade bond issuance hit all-time records, with $5 trillion raised year-to-date, up about 20% from 2025, driven by AI/infrastructure financing, M&A, and refinancing. Despite elevated rates, issuers remained active and strategic, and Europe's role grew with record reverse Yankee issuance. Looking to 2027, conditions are expected to stay supportive but with more credit differentiation, capacity constraints in tech, and increased diversification across currencies.

## Key points
- Global investment-grade bond issuance set all-time records in 2026: investment-grade financials and corporates raised $5 trillion year-to-date, approximately 20% higher than 2025, with two months of the year remaining.
- Only half of the issuance growth was attributable to AI and infrastructure financing; the rest came from robust M&A financing, banks funding healthy loan growth, and corporates refinancing about twice as much as they did a decade ago.
- Despite expectations of falling rates at the start of 2026, the year ended in a higher rate environment, yet issuers did not retreat—they accepted a longer-term structural shift and adopted more strategic, less complacent market access.
- In technology, media, and telecom, hyperscaler supply dominated: in a $2 trillion total issuance year, ex-financials volume was $1.1 trillion, and tech alone accounted for over $300 billion, about 30% of that total.
- To fund massive CapEx budgets, tech companies are using diverse funding sources beyond debt, including equity, common or mandatory leasing, and customer prepayments, demonstrating increased creativity in capital raising.
- U.S. industrial bond supply increased 31% in 2026 versus full-year 2025, driven by higher CapEx and heavy M&A as investment-grade corporates add capabilities to participate directly in AI and infrastructure buildout globally.
- In healthcare, M&A remained the primary issuance driver, comprising 62% of U.S. dollar supply year-to-date, a trend expected to continue into 2027.
- For 2027, investment-grade conditions are expected to remain supportive but with greater credit differentiation, moving from a record year to normalization; new issue concessions for TMT widened from an average of 5 bps to 20 bps and utilities from 3 bps to 10 bps by October.
- Structural tailwinds persist: hyperscalers are far from finished, and European utilities face hundreds of billions of euros in network upgrade funding; the top 10 utilities have forward CapEx plans of approximately €430 billion, up 50%, which will require bond market refinancing.
- High yields and steep curves are concentrating issuance in shorter maturities; about one-third of euro supply in 2026 was 5 years or less, while only 15% was 10 years and longer, with similar trends in dollars, adding to capacity and crowding-out dynamics.
- Credit spreads decompressed late in 2026 from record tights, and hyperscaler pricing is distorting credit indices, dragging wider spreads for other issuers; in Europe, hyperscalers' cost of access is impacting index composition.
- Tech issuers, particularly hyperscalers, will prioritize first-mover advantage to get ahead of competing supply and will issue into markets with more attractive conditions as index concentration issues arise in smaller markets.
- Industrials and healthcare sectors have benefited from the hyperscaler/utility supply wave: despite wider spreads in those sectors, single-A industrial and healthcare spreads showed little movement, and borrowers maintained a front-footed approach to refinancing and M&A financing.
- Europe is becoming a strategically important capital source, with euro volume growth compounding and large benchmark deals from Schneider Electric (€6 billion) and others like AT&T, Orange, Stellantis, SAP, and TenneT (around €4 billion each), supported by persistent foreign investor inflows over three years.
- Reverse Yankee issuance (non-European borrowers in euros) reached an all-time record of 28% of euro supply YTD and surpassed €100 billion, exceeding the previous record set in 2019, and RBC led inaugural euro transactions for several reverse Yankees.
- French fiscal concerns are a key risk for 2027: the French election in April is focusing attention on public spending and social unrest, with the OAT-Bund spread widening from 40 bps at the start of 2026 to as high as 200 bps, highlighting European system stress.
- Corporate funding strategies increasingly emphasize diversification and efficiency: borrowers are maximizing net investment hedge capacity across currencies, using U.S. private placements to access foreign currencies without registration, and avoiding over-reliance on any single market.
- Hyperscalers are accessing non-traditional bond markets at record scale, including the largest Canadian dollar deal (Amazon), largest Australian dollar deal (Alphabet), and significant yen and Swiss franc issuance, which offer billions in capacity and competitive pricing versus USD.
- Tech companies historically cash-generative now face negative free cash flow due to ballooning CapEx, leading to quarterly/annual issuance plans, creative financing such as chip financing, leasing, and data center funding, and rating pressure; Meta was put on negative outlook by S&P due to increased lease obligations.
- AI investment is already a capacity constraint: new issue concessions for jumbo dollar deals rose from 0-5 bps to 15-20 bps or more, many recent deals show no secondary market performance, and each new issue reprices the secondary curve, forcing diversification across currencies and asset classes.
- For industrial and healthcare borrowers, strategic M&A drives financing agendas, with record multiples paid for assets to define roles in infrastructure buildout; companies are using bridge facilities even for single-A and double-A borrowers and considering hybrid capital, though supply is not yet meaningful.
- Practical recommendations for 2027 include placing all global markets on the radar, establishing relative value frameworks, maintaining proactive investor marketing, setting up issuance programs for operational flexibility, and averaging in spreads/yields across currencies using layered issuance strategies.
