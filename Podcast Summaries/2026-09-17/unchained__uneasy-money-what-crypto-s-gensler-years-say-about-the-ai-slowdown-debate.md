# Uneasy Money: What Crypto's Gensler Years Say About the AI Slowdown Debate — Unchained

## TL;DR

Kain Warwick and Taylor Monahan are joined by Tommy Shaughnessy of Delphi Ventures to work through a dead CLARITY Act, the SEC and CFTC stepping into the vacuum, and what the crypto industry's lost regulatory years say about today's argument over slowing AI down.

## Key points

- The episode opens on the AI framing: if we put all of the agents' thinking into a tiny cube, the problem is that the cubes are portable and can go anywhere — the tension between capability and containment.
- That leads to the hosts' framing of the stakes: humanity spent 10,000 years slowly fumbling towards progress, and has now invented something that might get there in 10 years while being far smarter than us.
- On CLARITY, the headline is that it did not even reach 50 votes — a sharper failure than expected given the last-minute optimism that swept through crypto circles.
- The hosts note the scale of the sunk cost, with listeners pointing out that vast sums were spent on lobbying for a bill that could not clear a procedural hurdle.
- Tommy's read is that the process is structurally broken rather than merely unlucky — in his telling, senators with no context for the technology were voting on its future, and his first proposed remedy is simply voting out everyone who voted against it.
- The group then pivots to the SEC and CFTC, which they expect to push as much enabling rulemaking as they can now that legislation has failed.
- Kain flags the uncomfortable irony of depending on agency rulemaking: the same discretionary power that produced bad rules under the Gensler-era SEC is what would now produce good ones, which he calls a necessary short-term fix but not a sustainable strategy.
- The conversation closes on governance oddities at the state level and on the difficulty of distinguishing genuine decentralised projects from well-marketed ones.
