# David Rosenberg: "The Fed Just Made a Policy Mistake" — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR

David Rosenberg argues the Federal Reserve's 25 basis point rate hike is a policy mistake, tightening into a supply-driven oil price shock rather than demand-led inflation. He contends the economy is weaker than the Fed portrays, with slowing nominal wage growth and a labor market that is not tight, and warns this could trigger a significant stock market correction due to extreme valuations, concentration, and rising recession risk. Rosenberg recommends de-risking portfolios, having already raised cash to 10% and planning to increase it further.

## Key points

- The Federal Reserve raised the fed funds rate by 25 basis points with a unanimous vote, as 93% priced by markets, but signaled a more hawkish path than expected; futures now price three additional hikes next year versus the Fed's own projection of one.
- Rosenberg contends the Fed is mistakenly tightening into a cost shock: oil prices have risen about 40%, but this is not demand-led or wage-led inflation; nominal wage growth has slowed to 3% year-over-year (lowest in five years), real wages are contracting, and hiring and quit rates are declining, indicating a labor market that is far from tight.
- The Fed's emphasis on 'relative prices' implies it will attempt to offset higher energy costs by deflating the rest of the economy, which Rosenberg argues can only be achieved by inducing a recession.
- The economy is being supported by two narrow props: an AI-driven data construction and capex boom (data construction spending up 70% year-over-year while the rest of commercial construction is down 7%) and the equity wealth effect on consumer spending, with household net worth-to-income at a record high and a 90% correlation to consumption.
- Market risks are acute: the top 10 stocks comprise over 40% of S&P 500 market cap (vs. under 30% at the 2000 peak), eight of the ten are tech names, sector correlations are extremely high except healthcare and staples, and equity mutual funds hold only 1% cash while households have 72% of financial assets in equities.
- Credit markets are flashing warning signs: private credit default rates have risen above 6% for the first time, Oracle's CDS spreads have blown out, and the semiconductor index (SOX) has entered a bear market, historically a leading indicator for broader market declines.
- Rosenberg recommends de-risking: he raised his model portfolio cash to 10% last week and plans to double it; he suggests healthcare and staples as the only sectors with low correlation to the tech trade.
- He draws a parallel to 2008, when Fed Chair Bernanke did not raise rates despite oil reaching $150/barrel and headline inflation at 6%, arguing the current Fed is making the opposite mistake by hiking into a similar supply shock.
