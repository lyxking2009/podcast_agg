# The Chopping Block: CLARITY Dies on the Senate Floor, Hunter Biden's LAPTOP Rug, & the AI Pause Pact — Unchained

## TL;DR

The Chopping Block crew discusses the Senate's failure to pass the CLARITY bill (47 votes, needing 60), which leaves crypto regulation to agency rulemaking; they also cover Hunter Biden's LAPTOP memecoin crashing 99.85% from its peak, SDNY charges against two Robinhood engineers for front-running Hyperliquid listings, Balancer winding down, and the AI 'pause the frontier' pact led by Anthropic's Dario Amodei, with debate over whether slowing AI development is feasible or just a cartel.

## Key points

- The CLARITY bill failed in the Senate with only 47 votes, short of the 60 needed; no Democrats voted for it, and not all Republicans supported it.
- The bill's death was largely driven by ethics provisions targeting Trump-affiliated tokens; Democrats wanted forced divestiture of tokens linked to the president or Congress, but that was not enough to secure votes.
- With CLARITY dead, crypto regulation will come from SEC and CFTC rulemaking rather than legislation, which can be reversed by future administrations; adoption is seen as the primary protection against unfavorable rules.
- Prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket had fluctuating odds on CLARITY passage, peaking at 84% in February and dropping to 8% by the vote, though participants debate their accuracy and influence on negotiations.
- Hunter Biden launched a memecoin called LAPTOP on Base; it crashed 99.85% from its all-time high, with market cap around $85 million after briefly spiking to a fully diluted valuation of $200 billion due to low initial liquidity ($50,000 pool by GSR). The token fell from over $200 to about $0.20.
- The LAPTOP token's collapse reinforces the lesson that celebrity coins almost never work; the hosts advise against buying them.
- SDNY charged two Robinhood engineers, Hefu Chai and Hasang Xiang, with commodities fraud and wire fraud for front-running token listings on Hyperliquid, making only $50,000 each but facing 10-20 years in prison.
- On-chain sleuths had flagged suspicious open interest on Hyperliquid before Robinhood listings months earlier, demonstrating blockchain transparency as a tool for market surveillance.
- Balancer, an early DeFi AMM, is winding down and returning about $9 million from its treasury to token holders; its multi-token pool innovation lost relevance and the protocol suffered hacks.
- The exchange sector is consolidating: Coinx, BitMEX, Ascendex, and BitMart have shut down, driven by licensing costs and competition.
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for slowing AI development, citing risks of recursive self-improvement; employee Jacob Coxin quit claiming AI could destroy humanity and labs are irresponsible.
- Major AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) agreed to third-party evaluators, coordinate limits possibly requiring antitrust exemptions, and US-China coordination on weapons/bioweapons.
- Trump dismissed the pause as a conspiracy, saying the only guardrail needed is a 'strong and smart high IQ president'; David Sacks and Lina Khan warned against suspending antitrust to form a cartel.
- The Chinese government and labs view the pause as a US ploy to lock them out of advanced chips, while hosts note open-weight models are auditable without third-party oversight.
- The hosts debate whether an AI slowdown would reduce capex; they argue it would shift spending to inference, data collection, and enterprise deployments rather than lower overall investment, but without China's cooperation the pause is futile.
- On AI doomsday scenarios, they discuss potential vectors like bioweapons, power grid attacks, and autonomous weapons; current systems are fragile and society is not prepared for a swarm of agents.
- Some hosts suggest that if AI doom is near, buying Bitcoin, a bunker, or a self-sufficient farm might be hedges, though they half-joke about AI buying Bitcoin.
