## Overview

In this 51-minute episode, Bankless hosts David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams sit down with Alex Thorn, Head of Research at Galaxy Digital, to assess the dramatically improved legislative odds for the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (CLARITY Act) following a surprise bipartisan Senate Banking Committee vote on May 14, 2026. Thorn raised his probability estimate for the bill being signed into law in 2026 from approximately 50% to 75%, driven by two unexpected Democratic votes that gave the committee a 15-9 majority. The episode maps the remaining seven-week legislative sprint from committee approval to a potential presidential signature in early August.

Thorn and the hosts dissect the key forces that could still derail the bill: an ethics provision that would restrict senior government officials' digital asset holdings has created friction with some White House offices seeking carve-outs; the banking lobby is engaged in quiet but determined opposition, arguing stablecoin competition could erode deposit bases; and the bill must still be merged with the Senate Agriculture Committee's CFTC framework before reaching the full Senate floor, where it needs 60 votes for cloture. The Tillis-Alsobrooks stablecoin yield compromise — which resolved the previously deadlocked question of whether stablecoins can pay yield — was identified as the structural breakthrough that enabled the committee vote.

The episode also examines which blockchain ecosystems stand to benefit most from CLARITY Act passage and what a presidential signature could mean for Bitcoin prices and broader market sentiment. White House crypto adviser Patrick Witt is quoted as saying passage would give the industry "roughly 90% of what it needs," while Senator Lummis cautioned that no one is "popping the champagne quite yet."

## Implications

For investors, a 75% probability of CLARITY Act passage by August represents one of the most significant regulatory re-ratings in crypto history. The bill's permanent classification of Bitcoin and Ethereum as non-securities would remove years of legal uncertainty, while the DeFi decentralization framework could unlock institutional capital currently sidelined by regulatory ambiguity. The compressed timeline — with a potential August presidential signature — creates a specific catalyst window that may be priced into markets before it arrives. For builders, the bill's passage would provide the clearest regulatory runway the industry has ever had in the U.S., particularly for DeFi protocols seeking to operate within a defined legal framework. The ethics provision standoff and bank lobby resistance are the primary tail risks; if either derails the bill past the November midterm window, the regulatory vacuum could persist for another congressional cycle.
