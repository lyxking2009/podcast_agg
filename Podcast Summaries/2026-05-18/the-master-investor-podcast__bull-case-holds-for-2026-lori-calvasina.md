## Key Points

- RBC Capital Markets raised its S&P 500 **12-month target to 7,900** (from 7,750 in early May 2026). **Bull case: ~8,100**; **bear case: 7,400**; stress-test scenario (5% EPS decline YoY): fair value ~6,300.
- The central framework is a **"two-speed economy"**: AI-related companies projected at **28% EPS growth in 2027**; non-AI components at only **~6% EPS growth**, also facing Middle East conflict headwinds.
- RBC applied a **5% haircut** to overall 2027 consensus EPS, held AI-related earnings at consensus, and cut non-AI estimates by 7.5%, arriving at ~$329/share S&P 500 EPS.
- **Strongest model tailwind: investor sentiment**. AAII net bulls are 1–2 standard deviations below the historical average — historically followed by ~15% S&P 500 gains over the next 12 months.
- **Current market P/E: 23.6x** — "not cheap" but contextually supportable. RBC's trailing P/E model based on current inflation expectations points to ~25.7x as fair value.
- **Critical risk threshold: 5% 10-year Treasury yield** (was ~4.54% at time of recording). This level "would challenge US stock bulls" and compress P/E multiples meaningfully.
- Five-factor target model: (1) investor sentiment, (2) cross-asset valuation, (3) earnings/P/E, (4) GDP backdrop (1.1–2% real GDP growth for 2026 — the main drag, historically associated with ~5.7% S&P returns), (5) Fed policy (assumes rates flat under new leadership).
- **Sector positioning**: Growth over value; US over international; AI/tech/semiconductors favored. **Health Care downgraded** to Market Weight from Overweight due to deteriorating earnings revisions and fund outflows. Industrials "a bit overvalued."
- **Small caps**: Cautiously favorable but data-dependent. Small-cap value investors are struggling — quality trades not working, heavy housing-cycle exposure, waiting on Fed cuts.
- Markets can "climb the wall of worry" as long as AI earnings growth remains the anchor of the bull case.

## Overview

Wilfred Frost interviews Lori Calvasina, Head of US Equity Strategy at RBC Capital Markets, in episode 52 of The Master Investor Podcast. Calvasina holds one of Wall Street's highest S&P 500 targets and walks through the full architecture of how it's constructed — the five-factor model, the "two-speed economy" framework separating AI from non-AI earnings, and scenario analysis. The conversation covers sector positioning, Fed and Treasury yield risks, small caps, and international equity comparisons. She also discusses GLP-1 drugs' supply chain implications and mid-term election effects on market sentiment. On the same day (May 18), Calvasina appeared on CNBC with the same message: "Markets will chase earnings growth over the course of next year."

## Implications

The "two-speed economy" thesis crystallizes the market bifurcation of 2026: AI-adjacent earnings growth is strong enough to sustain elevated multiples, while the rest of the index faces headwinds. If AI earnings disappoint (NVDA being the near-term test), the bull case collapses toward the bear case rapidly since the non-AI portion can't compensate. The 5% Treasury yield threshold is the key macro tripwire: a bond sell-off driven by persistent inflation or fiscal concerns would simultaneously compress P/E multiples and reduce the relative attractiveness of equities. Sentiment being 1–2 standard deviations below normal is the most concrete contrarian bullish signal — but it requires the AI earnings growth story to remain intact.
