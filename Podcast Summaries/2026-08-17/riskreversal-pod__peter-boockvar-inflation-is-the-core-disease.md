# Peter Boockvar: Inflation Is The Core Disease — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Dan Nathan and Guy Adami are joined by Peter Boockvar, CIO at OnePoint BFG Wealth Partners, to unpack the latest inflation data and why yields remain resilient, with the curve steepening as the two-year dips while the ten-year holds around 4.65%. Boockvar argues the Fed must weigh PPI alongside CPI, noting persistent producer pressures and limited pass-through that squeeze margins and hiring, contributing to weak consumer confidence and "running to stand still" wages. They discuss why the S&P 500 continues to levitate — attributing much of earnings and market leadership to massive AI capex spending and its spillovers into financials — preview key retail earnings, and debate US-China AI competition, pressure on the OpenAI/Anthropic business models, and Japan's yen intervention with rising odds of a BOJ rate hike.

## Key points
- Inflation remains "the core disease": producer-side pressures are persistent, and with limited pass-through to consumers, margins and hiring get squeezed even as headline CPI cools.
- Boockvar argues the Fed should weigh PPI alongside CPI — ignoring producer prices means misreading the inflation transmission pipeline.
- Wages are "running to stand still" against costs, feeding weak consumer confidence despite a strong-looking labor market.
- The S&P 500's levitation is driven heavily by massive AI capex and its spillovers into financials — a concentration of earnings leadership rather than broad-based strength.
- Retail earnings preview: Home Depot, Lowe's, Target, TJ Maxx, and Walmart will test consumer resilience.
- Energy stocks are strong amid high gasoline prices and inventory drawdown risks.
- US-China AI competition is intensifying, and OpenAI/Anthropic business models face margin pressure from the capex arms race.
- Japan: yen intervention is on the table, BOJ rate hike odds are rising, and potential repatriation flows could ripple into global markets.
