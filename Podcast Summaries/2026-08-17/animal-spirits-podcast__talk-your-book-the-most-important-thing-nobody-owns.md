# Talk Your Book: The Most Important Thing Nobody Owns — Animal Spirits Podcast

## TL;DR
On this Talk Your Book episode, Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson are joined by Steve Schoffstall of Sprott ETFs to discuss the case for critical materials and commodities as an under-owned asset class that has become an unexpected beneficiary of the AI and technology buildout. The conversation covers how Sprott defines critical materials and the nine metals it targets, why miners have been more disciplined this cycle than in past booms, how China's dominance in refining is reshaping the geopolitics of critical materials, and why uranium and small modular reactors could be the next leg of nuclear demand.

## Key points
- Commodities have become an unexpected AI and technology play: the electrification and compute buildout is a structural demand driver that investors are only starting to price.
- Sprott defines critical materials narrowly and targets nine specific metals — the bottleneck inputs for the energy transition and advanced technology.
- Miners have been more disciplined this cycle than in past booms: capital allocation is more restrained, which supports better supply/demand dynamics.
- China's dominance in refining is reshaping critical materials geopolitics — processing capacity concentration creates strategic vulnerabilities and pricing power.
- Uranium and small modular reactors (SMRs) are positioned as the next leg of nuclear demand, extending the critical-materials thesis beyond battery and electronics metals.
