# Uniswap Is Building The Liquidity Network For Everything | Hayden Adams — Empire

## TL;DR
Jason Yanowitz and Santiago Roel Santos bring on Uniswap founder Hayden Adams to explain why the company now frames itself as a "liquidity network" rather than a DEX, how the UNIfication shift pushed Uniswap Labs from building its own apps to empowering everyone building on top (hook developers, trading platforms, payments networks), and how v4 hooks — dismissed as hype a year ago — are finally having their moment. Adams also opens up on running the business: the company shrank from ~160 to ~120 people, got flatter, and he now manages 10-12 direct reports, having learned the irony that you can decentralize a protocol but you don't want consensus decision-making inside the company.

## Key points
- The "decentralized exchange" label has done Uniswap harm: exchanges hear "competitor," but when you say "liquidity/market-making system" they say "we integrate with market makers." Uniswap is better understood like Ethereum — a platform whose value comes from network effects and everything built on top.
- v4 hooks took ~4 years to pay off: the platform shipped before any hooks existed, drawing skepticism, but now custom market structures that would take years to build from scratch can be created in days within the entire Uniswap ecosystem.
- Post-UNIfication the company shifted from B2C apps to empowering others: supporting hook developers, exposing the same API its own frontend uses, a developer platform, docs, and a team of four deployed engineers helping teams integrate.
- "We want every asset": Adams resists prioritization with a YouTube analogy — a social platform hosts memes, protein supplements, and educational content, and finance has never worked like the internet; automated market making is what lets the model scale to millions of assets.
- Stablecoin dominance is the wedge: ~60-70% of EVM stablecoin swapping (and 40-50% across non-EVM) now flows through Uniswap protocol, positioning it with payments networks exploring multi-stablecoin acceptance — the "pay anything, receive anything" vision where the best decentralized money is any asset used as money.
- The Dual Pool hook (built with Spark) lets pool liquidity also earn lending yield, cutting the opportunity cost of market making — the kind of seamless plumbing tradFi can't do, since conversions and yield happen in separate systems there.
- Business evolution: peaked around 160 people, now ~120, flatter with pure-management layers removed and every leader in the weeds; Adams now has 10-12 reports vs 5-6 before and found more reports made things easier as long as people coordinate — the decentralized-systems founder's lesson that consensus decision-making is the failure mode.
- On DeFi as infrastructure (agreeing with Morpho's Paul Frambot): the underlying network gets more complex — like computing from punch cards to vibe coding — but abstraction layers mean end-user products get dramatically easier, not harder; v4's complexity is what enables simpler LPing experiences (stablecoin LPing hooks in the works).
- Tokenized stocks/RWAs abroad (24/7, fractional, cross-country flows) are a growing integration target, along with the Robinhood chain launch which drove a huge surge of usage.
