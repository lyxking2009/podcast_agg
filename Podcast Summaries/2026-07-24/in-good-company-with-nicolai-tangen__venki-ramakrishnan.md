## Key Points

- Despite huge investment in longevity research, the maximum human lifespan has hardly moved — the science is more hype than reality
- Cellular reprogramming shows promise but has significant limits — we're far from reversing aging
- The simple habits that matter most for longevity: sleep, moderation, and exercise — no fancy interventions needed
- The ethics of a longer-living society are underexplored — what happens to pensions, healthcare, and intergenerational equity?
- Longevity has become a massive investment trend, drawing billions in capital despite limited scientific breakthroughs
- Ramakrishnan, Nobel laureate in chemistry and former President of the Royal Society, brings rigorous scientific skepticism to the longevity hype

## Overview

Nicolai Tangen sits down with Sir Venki Ramakrishnan, Nobel laureate in chemistry and former President of the Royal Society, to separate longevity science from hype. They unpack what aging actually is, why the maximum human lifespan has hardly moved, the promise and limits of cellular reprogramming, and the ethics of a longer-living society. Ramakrishnan emphasizes that the simple habits — sleep, moderation, exercise — matter most, and that much of the longevity investment trend outruns the actual science.

## Implications

The longevity investment bubble may face a reckoning as science fails to deliver on promises. Simple lifestyle interventions remain the most evidence-based approach to extending healthspan. Society needs to grapple with the ethical implications of significantly extended lifespans before the technology arrives.
