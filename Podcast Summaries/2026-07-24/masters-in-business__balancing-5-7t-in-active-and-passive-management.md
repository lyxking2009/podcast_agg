## Key Points

- Lori Heinel oversees $5.7 trillion in assets at State Street Investment Management, spanning index funds, ETFs, active strategies, and alternatives
- The Black Monday crash of 1987 was her formative market experience — she was on the fixed income trading floor at First Boston, witnessing the birth of the 'Fed put'
- State Street's SPY (S&P 500 ETF) and GLD (Gold ETF) are the standard bearers for index investing — SPY was the first US ETF launched ~30 years ago
- The key lesson from her career: always listen to the client first — sometimes the problem isn't what you think it is, and the non-optimal answer may be the best one
- Active and passive management are not either/or — State Street's platform combines both through ETFs, separate accounts, and customized solutions
- The evolution from individual stock picking to index replication was a major innovation, but customization for institutional clients remains critical
- Her career path — from Princeton religious studies major to Credit Suisse analyst to global CIO — shows the value of liberal arts education in finance

## Overview

Barry Ritholtz interviews Lori Heinel, Executive Vice President and Global Chief Investment Officer at State Street Investment Management, who oversees $5.7 trillion in assets. The conversation traces her career from a religious studies major at Princeton through the 1987 Black Monday crash on the First Boston trading floor to her current role managing one of the world's largest asset platforms. Heinel shares formative lessons about client-centric investing, the evolution of index and ETF products, and how State Street balances active and passive management across its massive platform.

## Implications

The convergence of active and passive management is accelerating — the binary distinction is becoming less meaningful. Institutional clients increasingly want customized solutions that combine elements of both. The 'Fed put' established in 1987 continues to shape market behavior nearly 40 years later. Asset managers that can offer both scale (through ETFs/indexing) and customization (through separate accounts) will dominate.
