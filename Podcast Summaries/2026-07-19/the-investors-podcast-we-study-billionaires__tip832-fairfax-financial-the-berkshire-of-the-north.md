# TIP832: Fairfax Financial (FFO.TO): The Berkshire Of The North w/ Kyle Grieve & Shawn O'Malley

**Show:** The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)
**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Duration:** 66 min

## Key Points

- Fairfax Financial is an insurance conglomerate built by CEO Prem Watsa, often compared to Berkshire Hathaway. It began as a near-bankrupt Canadian trucking insurer with roughly $13 million in float, which has since grown to nearly $41 billion.
- The company has compounded book value at roughly 18% annually and its share price at roughly 18% annually for over 40 years, with EPS growth of about 15% since 1986 — a track record the hosts compare directly to Berkshire's ~20% historical book value compounding.
- Hosts Kyle Grieve and Shawn O'Malley break down Fairfax's insurance and non-insurance segments, its combined ratio and underwriting discipline, and how the float has been used to fund acquisitions and, more recently, share buybacks.
- Fairfax's history includes a notable short-selling period with setbacks, contrasted with a highly profitable bet against the US housing market ahead of the 2008 financial crisis that generated roughly $4.5 billion in gains.
- The discussion covers Fairfax's decentralized organizational structure, CEO compensation alignment with shareholders, and the company's competitive advantages relative to other insurers.
- Key risks flagged include catastrophe exposure inherent to the insurance business and succession risk given Prem Watsa's central role in the company's culture and capital allocation.
- The episode closes with the hosts' view on Fairfax's current valuation and whether it remains an attractive opportunity today, including its consideration for TIP's Intrinsic Value Portfolio.

## Overview

This episode is part of We Study Billionaires' ongoing series of single-company deep dives. Kyle Grieve and Shawn O'Malley trace Fairfax Financial's evolution from a distressed Canadian trucking insurer acquired by Prem Watsa in the 1980s into a global insurance and investment conglomerate frequently likened to Berkshire Hathaway. The conversation moves through the mechanics of how Fairfax uses insurance float to fund both acquisitions and public/private investments, its underwriting track record, a notable era of aggressive short-selling (including a highly profitable pre-2008 housing short), and the shift in recent years toward more disciplined capital allocation and buybacks. The hosts close by weighing Fairfax's competitive position, key risks, and current valuation.

## Implications

For investors, the episode frames Fairfax as a case study in long-duration compounding via insurance float — relevant for anyone evaluating insurance-linked or Berkshire-style holding-company investments. The emphasis on combined ratio discipline and decentralized management offers a template for assessing other insurer-conglomerates. The flagged succession risk is a reminder that founder-led compounders carry key-person risk that should be weighed against historical returns, and the discussion of Fairfax's valuation gives listeners a concrete reference point for whether the stock still offers a margin of safety at current prices.
