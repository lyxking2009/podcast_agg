# Netflix CPTO on AI and the future of product and tech roles | Elizabeth Stone

**Show:** Lenny's Podcast: Product | Career | Growth
**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Duration:** 72 min

## Key Points

- Elizabeth Stone, Netflix's Chief Product and Technology Officer, returns to Lenny's Podcast roughly two years after her first appearance — an episode that remained the show's second-most-popular for over a year. Since then, her remit has expanded from engineering to include product leadership as well.
- Stone identifies systems thinking as the most critical leadership skill in the current environment, arguing leaders need to reason about how organizational structure, incentives, and technology interact rather than optimizing any single piece in isolation.
- A major theme is managing the surge in AI-generated output (code, docs, designs) without losing quality or clarity — Stone discusses how Netflix is adapting review and quality processes as AI tools multiply the volume of work produced per person.
- Stone frames AI fluency as a universal expectation across Netflix now, not a skill confined to engineers or specific technical roles — product managers, designers, and other functions are expected to build working AI fluency.
- She discusses "excellence as an operating system" — a framework for embedding high standards into how Netflix teams work day to day, rather than treating quality as a separate review gate.
- The episode touches on tools like Claude Code and Claude Cowork in the context of how Netflix's product and engineering organization is adapting workflows to AI-assisted development.

## Overview

This is Elizabeth Stone's second appearance on Lenny's Podcast, recorded roughly two years after her first, which covered Netflix's culture of excellence and remained one of the show's most popular episodes. Since that conversation, Stone's role has grown from CTO to Chief Product and Technology Officer, giving her oversight of Engineering, Product, and Design. This episode focuses on how AI is reshaping product and technical leadership: the shift toward systems thinking as the core leadership skill, how to manage quality at the higher volume of output AI tools enable, and why Netflix now treats AI fluency as a baseline expectation across roles rather than a specialization. It extends her "excellence as an operating system" thesis from the earlier episode into the AI era.

## Implications

For product and engineering leaders, the episode suggests that AI-driven increases in output volume require rethinking quality processes rather than just scaling existing review structures — the bottleneck shifts from production to judgment and systems-level oversight. Treating AI fluency as a cross-functional, universal expectation (rather than an engineering-specific skill) signals a broader organizational shift that other companies may need to follow to keep pace. Leaders are encouraged to build "excellence" into workflows and operating systems rather than relying on end-stage review gates, which matters more as AI compresses the time between idea and shipped output.
