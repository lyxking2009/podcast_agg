# #211 - Jim Al-Khalili - Reality Is An Illusion: Quantum Entanglement, Time & Dark Energy — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR

Peter McCormack talks physics with Jim Al-Khalili — quantum entanglement and non-locality, why the flow of time exists in our heads but not in the equations, black holes and where physics breaks down, and dark energy as a placeholder name for ignorance. The episode ends with Al-Khalili's move into fiction with the novel Sunfall, and a shared appreciation for Project Hail Mary.

## Key points

- Al-Khalili's central distinction is between 'manifest time' — our psychological experience of time as something that flows — and time as it appears in physics, where there is no notion of flow. All times simply exist; nothing moves along a time axis.
- Clocks measure our invention, not time itself: humans invented seconds, minutes and hours, whereas the day and the year are facts about Earth's spin and orbit. Time existed long before humans did.
- Three separate questions are teased apart: does time flow (physics says no), is there an arrow of time (physics has real content, including irreversibility), and what does 'now' mean — a durationless dividing line that, if past and future do not exist, makes time look like an illusion.
- Einstein's block-universe position — all times equally real — is presented as the counterweight to the philosophical view that only the present exists; Al-Khalili frames this as a genuine unresolved debate between physics and philosophy.
- Quantum mechanics is fundamentally non-local: entangled particles exhibit correlations that cannot be explained by signals traveling at or below light speed, in conflict with Einstein's intuitions about locality. All you can ever say about the quantum world is the result of a measurement.
- Black holes are used as the example of where physics breaks down — the mass of a star accumulating in a point of zero size is where the equations stop making sense.
- Dark energy is discussed bluntly as a name for an observed effect: the expansion of space is accelerating, nobody knows why, so 'dark energy' is the placeholder term.
- Al-Khalili describes the switch from writing nonfiction to fiction as a steep learning curve, with Sunfall as the result, and the conversation closes on the pleasure of hard science fiction done well.
