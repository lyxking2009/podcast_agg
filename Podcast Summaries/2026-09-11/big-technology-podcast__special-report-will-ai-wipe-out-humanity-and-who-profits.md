# Special Report: Will AI Wipe Out Humanity? (And Who Profits) — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR

This special report examines the viral claim by former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon that AI has a 10% chance of wiping out humanity, supported by Anthropic's Evan Hubinger. Hosts Alex Kantrowitz and Ranjan Roy analyze the incentives behind the spread of this narrative, distinguishing between speculative 'p(doom)' percentages and concrete near-term risks like cybersecurity threats and data center moratoriums. They conclude the existential risk is overstated but real policy backlash could harm AI companies' business prospects.

## Key points

- Jacob Coxon, an Anthropic researcher focused on pre-training, resigned publicly, claiming neither OpenAI nor Anthropic is acting responsibly and that they are racing toward self-improving superintelligence. He stated a 10% chance AI could wipe out humanity, and his tweet went viral with over 165 million views.
- Evan Hubinger, a senior member of Anthropic's alignment division, echoed Coxon, stating he personally believes there is more than a 10% chance AI could kill all humans within the next decade and that Anthropic does not yet have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence.
- The hosts discuss incentives for amplifying the story: Elon Musk could benefit by entrenching existing AI leaders or encouraging regulation that favors incumbents, but they conclude the simplest explanation is that the story went viral because it was primed by recent events like the Hugging Face hack and the OpenAI Millennium Prize problem solution.
- The 'p(doom)' percentages (e.g., 10%, 30%, 70%) are criticized as speculation dressed in mathematical language; they are not based on calculations or evidence but are used to make science fiction guesses sound credible.
- There is a split between researchers who genuinely believe in existential risk and other parts of AI companies that may exploit the fear for marketing or self-importance; the episode argues the marketing benefit is questionable because it could trigger harmful regulation and data center moratoriums.
- Real near-term risks are highlighted: cybersecurity threats, agent swarms, data leakage, and the potential for powerful AI models (like OpenAI's solution to a Millennium Prize problem using 10,000 agents over 88 hours) to be repurposed for hacking banks or infrastructure.
- The Hugging Face incident was misreported as thousands of agents autonomously hacking; actually about 700 agents were prompted to probe vulnerabilities and exploited a misconfigured repository (Artifactory) that accidentally allowed access to other agent repos, not a spontaneous rogue action.
- Politicians reacted strongly: Illinois Governor JB Pritzker called for immediate AI safety regulations and hearings; Senator Josh Hawley launched an investigation into OpenAI; Senator Bernie Sanders announced legislation to ban superintelligence and pause AI development, including potential 20-year prison sentences for researchers.
- The episode discusses potential impact on Anthropic's upcoming IPO: the fear narrative could lead to regulatory backlash and local data center moratoriums, but it is uncertain whether this will delay the S-1 filing or IPO, as governments often move slowly.
- Kevin Roose's tweet is quoted: 'The problem with the view that we should ignore the scary sci-fi AI risks in favor of the immediate problems is that the scary sci-fi risks keep becoming immediate problems,' suggesting the line between speculative and real risks is blurring.
- The hosts conclude they are neutral on existential risk in the near term, with Ranjan Roy calling it a 'blip' and Alex Kantrowitz slightly more negative on business prospects due to possible backlash, but both emphasize focusing on science fact over science fiction and addressing concrete risks.
