# #211 - Jim Al-Khalili - Reality Is An Illusion: Quantum Entanglement, Time & Dark Energy — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR

In this episode, Peter McCormack interviews physicist Jim Al-Khalili about time, quantum mechanics, and cosmology. Al-Khalili explains that time in physics lacks a flow, is relative under Einstein's general relativity, and that quantum entanglement defies local realism, as confirmed by Nobel-winning Bell test experiments. He also details the evidence for dark matter and dark energy, including the 1998 discovery of accelerating expansion, and discusses black holes, wormholes, and how AI may aid future physics research.

## Key points

- Jim Al-Khalili distinguishes 'manifest time' (psychological experience of flowing time) from physical time where no flow exists; he notes Einstein's relativity shows time is relative and can be stretched by gravity, which corrects GPS satellite clocks.
- General relativity predicted black holes: Karl Schwarzschild solved Einstein's equations for a collapsing star, leading to event horizon and singularity; spinning black holes have ring singularities, mathematically suggesting white holes, but white holes are unstable.
- Wormholes are mathematically allowed by general relativity and could act as spacetime shortcuts or time machines; Kip Thorne originally developed the wormhole concept for Carl Sagan's novel Contact, and later used it in the film Interstellar.
- Quantum entanglement demonstrates non-locality: experiments by John Clauser, Alain Aspect, and others (Nobel Prize 2022) based on John Bell's inequality showed that entangled particles correlate instantaneously, violating Einstein's locality, though no signal can be sent faster than light.
- Interpretations of quantum mechanics include Copenhagen (nothing exists until measured), many-worlds, de Broglie-Bohm pilot wave, and spontaneous collapse; they all make identical experimental predictions but differ on underlying reality; Al-Khalili favors quantum realism.
- Dark matter is real, inferred from galaxy rotation curves and gravitational lensing; it clumped earlier than normal matter, forming cosmic structure, so modified gravity (MOND) must match all observations.
- Dark energy was discovered in 1998 when distant galaxies appeared to recede slower than expected, implying accelerating expansion; it is a placeholder for unknown energy, and its nature will determine universe's fate.
- The universe likely has no edge; it may be finite and closed like a higher-dimensional sphere or infinite; the visible universe's edge is due to light travel time and expansion, not a physical boundary.
- AI is now solving advanced mathematics, e.g., formalizing Fermat's Last Theorem and solving long-standing conjectures, and could assist physics by analyzing large astronomical surveys, though it hasn't yet cracked fundamental theory.
- Al-Khalili's recent book 'On Time' explores time's physics and philosophy; his sci-fi novel 'Sunfall' (set in 2040s) incorporates quantum computers, dark matter, and AI.
