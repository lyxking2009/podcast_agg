# Gene Munster: iPhone Duo Will Be A Category Killer & Meta Muse Will End The Algo — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR

Gene Munster returns to discuss his bullish reassessment of Apple's new foldable iPhone Duo, now expecting it to contribute 5% of fiscal 2027 revenue after being impressed by its thin form and nearly invisible crease. He also analyzes Meta's Muse agentic AI, calling it a shift from junior-varsity algorithms to professional-grade personalization that will deepen engagement, while expressing concern about teen mental health. The conversation covers Google's innovation struggles, Tesla's Cybercab impact on Uber via Travis Kalanick's Atoms, and the viral AI extinction warning from ex-Anthropic researcher Jacob Cox.

## Key points

- Gene Munster upgraded his Apple revenue contribution estimate for the new foldable iPhone Duo to 5% of fiscal 2027 revenue, double his prior 2-2.5% estimate, after seeing the device in person; he was impressed by its thinness (14% thicker than iPhone Pro Max when closed) and nearly invisible crease, making it feel like two phones rather than a foldable.
- Despite the positive product reaction, Wall Street analysts who updated estimates actually lowered their absolute revenue dollars by 1%, suggesting they don't expect version one to be material; Munster believes investors are more bullish than analysts, explaining the stock's 3% gain on a down market day.
- Munster highlighted that Apple's new Siri (Apple Intelligence) is still in beta in English and will beta in five other languages in October, but the footnote excludes EU and China; he sees this lack of prime-time availability as a negative and part of why the stock sold off during the event before recovering on Duo.
- On durability, Dan Nathan questioned how the device with three glass surfaces and a hinge could be protected with a case; Munster referenced Apple's learning from antennagate and screen scratching, asserting design considerations and Apple upgrade program insurance will mitigate breakage risk.
- Munster described Meta's Muse as a shift from algorithmic recommendations to agentic AI that thinks on the user's behalf, calling the existing algorithmic approach "junior varsity" and the new agentic approach "professional level," and said it will make Meta's 3.5 billion daily users harder to put down.
- Munster said the algorithm is essentially dead and will be meaningless in two years, as agentic AI moves beyond past behavior to predict wants; he expressed concern that teen mental health will worsen as these systems become more potent and engaging.
- On Google, Munster revealed Deepwater exited its long-time position partly due to brain drain and lack of exciting at-scale products, despite Gemini improvements; he noted search growth remains strong at mid-to-high teens, but the stock is punished for lack of innovation mojo.
- Dan Nathan said he would choose Google over Apple for personalized AI because Apple hasn't demonstrated capability and keeping the Siri name is a disaster; Munster noted Siri is invoked 2.5 billion times daily, so name retention has brand recognition despite user frustration.
- Munster sees Uber's key opportunity in Atoms, the private company founded by Travis Kalanick, which is working on autonomy, automated kitchens, and mining; he said Tesla's FSD is spectacular with near-zero interventions and the NHTSA filing shows confidence to deploy 1000 robotaxis.
- Munster discussed the viral warning from ex-Anthropic researcher Jacob Cox, who left before the IPO and claimed a 10% chance of human extinction from AI; Munster said Cox is credible but not famed, and his concerns about recursive learning leading to superintelligence are valid, though he doesn't believe the world will end.
