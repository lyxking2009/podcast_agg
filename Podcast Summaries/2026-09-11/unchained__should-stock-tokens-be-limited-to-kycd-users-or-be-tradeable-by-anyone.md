# Should Stock Tokens Be Limited to KYC'd Users? Or Be Tradeable by Anyone? — Unchained

## TL;DR

Unchained's Laura Shin hosts Brett Redford, president of Securitize, on the fight over Robinhood's tokenized AMC stock. Redford's position is careful: the offshore token structure is arguably legal, but the deeper question is whether issuers — the companies whose equity is being wrapped and distributed — should have a say in what happens to their stock onchain.

## Key points

- The trigger: AMC CEO Adam Aron called Robinhood's AMC tokens 'contemptible, outrageous, disgusting, detestable, inexcusable, vile,' Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev replied 'What's the concern?', and Robinhood CLO Dan Gallagher — a former SEC commissioner — responded that Robinhood knows a thing or two about US securities law and invited AMC to 'send your lawyers and we'll educate them.'
- Securitize has been tokenizing funds, private equity and Treasuries, and Redford sees tokenized stocks as the next major frontier for bringing public markets onchain.
- Robinhood's structure issues what amount to debt securities in offshore markets that can be traded there. Redford concedes Robinhood appears to be operating within the law, while arguing legality is not the whole question.
- The core governance question he raises: who are capital markets for? If tokenized wrappers can be created and distributed without issuer involvement, issuers lose any say over how their securities are represented and traded.
- The regulatory backdrop is a US offshore/onshore arbitrage: the SEC under Paul Atkins is trying to establish rules of the road, but most activity has migrated offshore where the regulatory environment is materially different.
- He credits Robinhood with defining the market and demonstrating one of the real use cases of tokenization, while noting they were not first — many synthetic or wrapper stock tokens already exist offshore.
- The KYC and permissionlessness thread runs through the episode, with Redford raising terrorist financing risk and the importance of not unlocking rails that make illicit finance easier — and tying the point to the anniversary of September 11 and his own experience at the World Trade Center.
- The framing question — should stock tokens be limited to KYC'd users or tradeable by anyone — remains unresolved, with Redford arguing that any answer has to accommodate both investor protection and issuer rights.
