# What's the Concern? — Money Stuff: The Podcast

## TL;DR

Money Stuff with Matt Levine and guest host Max Abelson opens on the Clippers and Kawhi Leonard — an NBA-commissioned Wachtell Lipton report confirming that Los Angeles funneled money to its star through a fraudulent 'environmental services' company to exceed the salary cap — then turns to digital asset treasury companies, the 2025 trade that turned into 2026's DAT-activism trade, complete with a judge quoting an activist's email to Levine.

## Key points

- The Clippers story: the team paid Aspiration Partners millions for vague environmental services and, per the report, Aspiration paid Kawhi Leonard essentially the same amount for endorsement work he barely performed — a structure that would let the club pay its star beyond the NBA maximum.
- The Wachtell Lipton investigation for the NBA substantiated the original reporting by Pablo Torre and found additional endorsement deals with the same pattern; the Clippers continue to deny it.
- Levine's framing is that salary caps create circumvention markets: whenever a league caps what a team can pay, both sides are motivated to route compensation through third parties, and the deniability is the point.
- The DAT (digital asset treasury) trade: in 2025 small public companies could announce a Bitcoin stash and see their stock trade at a premium — turning $1 of Bitcoin into $2 of stock. Emory Digital, an electric motorcycle company, pivoted to being a DAT.
- When the premium flipped to a discount, the trade reversed: buying a DAT at a discount and liquidating its Bitcoin produced more than the stock cost. Hence 'DAT activism' — buying stock and pressuring boards to sell the coins and wind down.
- Emory Digital then re-pivoted in 2026 from DAT to AI infrastructure, because that became the fashionable trade — a neat illustration of how small-cap narratives chase whatever public markets currently reward.
- A lawsuit over that activism produced a judicial opinion quoting an activist's email to Levine: 'I'd like to liquidate a Bitcoin treasury company. I'd like to speak publicly about it.' Levine treats the willingness of financial actors to narrate their own schemes as the raw material of the column.
- The host segment also notes co-host Katie Greifeld is on parental leave, with guest hosts filling in for several months.
- The running theme is the show's founding distinction: Levine writes about money, Abelson writes about money and power, and the most entertaining stories sit where the two overlap.
