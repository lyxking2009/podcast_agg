# Should Ethereum Really Burn Its Staking Yield to Zero? — Unchained

## TL;DR
The episode discusses Ethereum Improvement Proposal (EIP) 8361, which proposes reducing staking rewards to zero by burning newly issued ETH as the staking ratio rises, to curb excessive staking and preserve network security. Hosts Austin Campbell, Chris Perkins, and guest Seth debate the proposal's merits, process, and implications, with critics arguing it's an academic solution disconnected from builders and harmful to institutional adoption, while supporters see it as a necessary check on validator capture and a healthy debate on Ethereum's monetary policy.

## Key points
- Proposal EIP-8361 (referred to as BIP 8361 in transcript) aims to burn a rising share of validator rewards as staking ratio climbs, hitting 100% burn at 16.25 million ETH staked (~half of supply), reducing staking yield to zero; authors include Justin Drake and Jerome Taichi, phases in over 18 months and only burns newly issued ETH, not transaction fees.
- Currently 41.5 million ETH is staked (34% of supply) with 2.5 million more in queue; entry queue adds 1.75 million ETH per month at max churn, projecting over 55% of supply staked by Jan 1, 2028 if unchanged.
- Proponents argue that under current yield curve, yield never drops below 1.5% even with all ETH staked, so incentive to stake never switches off, leading to excessive staking; past a point more stake makes Ethereum less secure because social layer loses fork backstop against a captured validator set.
- The proposal has a 48-hour comment period, which critics like Mike Silagadze and Stani Kulechov say is far too short for such a fundamental monetary policy change; Stani called it one of the most resisted Ethereum proposals ever, criticizing EF's ivory tower academic approach as disconnected from builders.
- Seven of the top 10 protocols could face an exodus if the proposal passes, indicating potential dramatic loss of confidence and governance instability.
- Chainlink CEO Sergey Nazarov opposes the proposal, noting validators currently earn roughly 2.75% newly created ETH, with tips only 15% of yields; cutting issuance to zero would force validators to secure Ethereum for transaction tips alone, at a time of strong institutional momentum.
- Seth views the proposal as an academic push that was not front and center, surprising the community; he argues there should be broad lead-up discussion for fundamental changes, especially with large stakeholders like Bitmain (as mentioned) in a post-staking world.
- Chris Perkins believes the proposal is great because it shows the EF doesn't control Ethereum; the community and applications will resist and the proposal will fail, which is healthy for decentralization; he warns against recreating a Fed-like body controlling interest rates.
- Chris emphasizes that yield rates rule the world; Ethereum's staking yield is its risk-free rate, and any change should be open, transparent, gradual, and community-driven, otherwise it could become like LIBOR manipulation or create a yen carry trade dynamic if yield goes to zero.
- Austin Campbell argues that even if he supported the proposal, the 48-hour timeline is too short, and he would vote no; a narrow 51% pass could trigger big holders dumping and institutional flight, undermining the institutional adoption thesis.
- Austin highlights unexamined assumptions: if all real-world assets move to Ethereum, economic security would require ETH to be worth hundreds of trillions, which is unsustainable; TradFi systems often fall back to proof of authority because economic security is expensive, and cutting yields to zero makes ETH less attractive versus 3.5% money market funds.
- The proposal lumps together distinct concepts—capping staking ratio and cutting base staking rate—that could be disaggregated; Austin proposes a hypothetical alternative where staking rate is pegged to SOFR and degrades only if staking exceeds a threshold like 2/3, allowing dynamic adjustment.
- They debate real vs nominal yield: Chris argues real yield can be calculated from supply burn/inflation, but Austin counters ETH is not a unit of account for real goods, so only nominal yields matter for now.
- Dapplion, co-author of the proposal, criticized opponents by saying the network has been captured by a small group of interested parties, and that taxing ETH holders billions to benefit select parties is unacceptable; he noted real yield is zero if ETH yield equals inflation.
- The group agrees Ethereum needs increased activity and real-world use cases rather than constant tokenomic tweaks; driving transaction fees through more usage is the way to increase burn and value, with DTCC choosing Stellar as a warning sign.
