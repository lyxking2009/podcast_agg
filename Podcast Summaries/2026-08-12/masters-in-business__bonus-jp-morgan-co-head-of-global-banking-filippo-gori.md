# BONUS: JP Morgan Co-Head of Global Banking Filippo Gori — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Filippo Gori, co-head of Global Banking at JP Morgan, shares his journey from Bocconi University to London, Hong Kong, and New York, highlighting the firm's 26-year evolution into a global powerhouse with 330,000 employees. He analyzes regional opportunities: Asia Pacific will house 50% of global GDP by decade's end, the Middle East is a 'global South' winner, Africa is the next frontier, and Europe needs less bureaucracy and pan-European champions. Gori also discusses the resilience of dealmaking, the rise of private capital, and how AI is enabling JP Morgan to aim for 100,000 clients by 2030.

## Key points
- Gori joined JP Morgan in London in 1999 through an internal consulting graduate program, rotating in asset management and client relationship management before moving to FX sales in Milan; at the time the firm had ~15,000 employees versus 330,000 today.
- He moved to Hong Kong in 2013 after his wife, formerly at the Bank of England, enthusiastically supported the opportunity; he had never visited Asia and spent 12 years there, noting that leadership styles must adapt across cultures—Japan values indirect communication while Australia is direct.
- Gori sees the global economy as exceptionally resilient in 2025 despite geopolitical turmoil; in the U.S. optimism is palpable, Europe is stable but entering an electoral cycle with inflation concerns, and the Middle East is a winner of the global South narrative due to infrastructure and financial center ambitions.
- By the end of the decade, 50% of global GDP will be in Asia Pacific; China, India, and Japan are the second, third, and fourth largest economies; South Korea does not make the top four.
- Africa is the next major opportunity due to favorable demographics, urbanization, and critical minerals; Gori warns that Russia and China already have heavy influence there, and Europe must understand Africa as its southern border.
- Europe's GDP growth has been anemic at 0.5–0.7% for 25 years; while criticisms about bureaucracy and rigid labor markets are valid, Gori reminds that the EU was founded to prevent war and calls for implementing Mario Draghi's white paper recommendations and creating pan-European champions, including in financial services.
- JP Morgan's rise from a tier-two institution to a top global bank was driven by integrating acquisitions like Bank One (which brought Jamie Dimon), maintaining a fortress balance sheet, avoiding subprime excesses before 2008, and investing continuously through cycles; Gori notes 2013 firm-wide revenues were less than a single quarter's revenue in 2025.
- Private capital has grown significantly since the GFC; JP Morgan launched a direct lending initiative with $50 billion of its own capital allocated to offer clients an agnostic set of financing solutions alongside traditional syndicated loans.
- The IPO market returned in 2025 after a post-pandemic slowdown, and M&A volumes are near the record 2021 levels, driven by board confidence in stable costs of capital and the urgency of the AI revolution.
- AI is being used to scale the business efficiently; JP Morgan aims to cover 100,000 wholesale clients by 2030 without significantly increasing headcount, but Gori insists human judgment remains irreplaceable because clients deal with people.
- Gori's key leadership lessons: treat the career as a marathon, not a sprint; invest in human relationships; and avoid personal sacrifices that later cause regret.
