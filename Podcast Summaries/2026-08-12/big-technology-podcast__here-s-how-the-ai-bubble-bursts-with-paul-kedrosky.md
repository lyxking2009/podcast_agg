# Here's How The AI Bubble Bursts — With Paul Kedrosky — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Paul Kedrosky argues the current AI buildout is a massive bubble, with big tech capex reaching $700B this year and projected $1.5T next year, exceeding historical infrastructure buildouts as a share of GDP and non-residential fixed investment. He contends the economics are untenable because token prices are deflating 70-80% annually, requiring roughly 100 millionfold growth in demand to justify returns, while data centers demand continuous capital for GPU replacement. Investors keep funding due to groupthink and large check sizes, but triggers like rising rates, IPO pressure, or government intervention could stop the music, leading to a contagion similar to the 2008 financial crisis.

## Key points
- AI infrastructure spending is now larger than all historical US infrastructure buildouts except World War II rearmament when measured as a percentage of GDP, non-residential fixed investment, and contribution to GDP growth, and it is happening in a much shorter time frame.
- Big tech alone is spending approximately $700 billion on capex this year, up from $350-400 billion last year, with projections of $1.5 trillion in AI-related buildout next year.
- As of the second quarter of 2026, more than 50% of data center funding comes from external financing rather than cash flows, marking a shift from the earlier claim that tech companies were self-funding their AI investments.
- The analogy between data centers and commercial real estate is flawed because data centers require continuous capital for GPU replacement and upgrades, with some GPUs failing on an 18-month cycle depending on whether they were used for training or inference.
- Tokens, the output of AI models, are deflating 70-80% year-over-year on a constant performance basis, and to justify current investments, token demand would need to grow by roughly 100 millionfold over six years, which is mathematically unlikely.
- Frontier AI models are converging in capability, reducing variance and switching costs, which pushes competition toward price and marketing, benefiting Chinese models like DeepSeek and Qwen that dominate open router tables due to lower prices.
- OpenAI and Anthropic are moving upmarket into vertical applications to chase higher returns, as evidenced by Alex Karp's CNBC complaint, but Kedrosky argues this strategy is flawed because software buyers want someone to sue or shout at, not a vendor that competes with them.
- The current bubble sits at the intersection of four historical bubble drivers: technology, real estate, loose credit, and government policy, a combination unprecedented in US history.
- Investors are cynical about the AGI narrative and use it as marketing to LPs, but in partner meetings they compare data center returns to commercial real estate cap rates of 6-8%, not to undiscountable call options on AGI.
- The music could stop due to macro events raising hurdle rates, post-IPO pressure on frontier model companies to show returns, export controls balkanizing the market, or government stakes in companies like OpenAI changing investor multiples.
- AI-related debt is increasingly held by insurers and institutional investors, and because hyperscalers and AI names now constitute about 40% of the S&P 500, even index fund holders are heavily exposed, creating contagion risk similar to the 2008 financial crisis.
- Kedrosky avoids investing in AI as a venture capitalist because it is a crowded marathon with thousands of well-funded participants, and he has not committed new capital to broad passive indices for over two years; he works with hedge funds on short positions related to AI.
- China's state-driven overbuilding of data centers, with provincial governors racing to build, mirrors past overinvestment in batteries and solar, but China's economy is less reliant on consumer spending, so a collapse would likely be less systemically damaging than in the US.
- After the bubble, AI will become a utility, with models commoditizing and disappearing into the background like electricity; current LLMs are not a path to AGI because they cannot update model weights in real time.
