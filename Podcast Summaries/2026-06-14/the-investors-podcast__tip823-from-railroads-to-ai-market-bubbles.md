# TIP823: From Railroads to AI: The Timeless Patterns Behind Market Bubbles w/ Kyle Grieve

**Show:** The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)
**Date:** 2026-06-14

## Key Points

- Market bubbles are psychological phenomena, not technological ones — driven by greed, optimism, and social proof, regardless of the underlying technology (railroads, internet, AI).
- Bubbles never look obvious in real time; they feel exhilarating and justified by narratives like 'this time is different.'
- The episode draws on frameworks from Ron Insana (TrendWatching), Charles Kindleberger, and Howard Marks to analyze bubble dynamics.
- Kyle Grieve faces a personal conflict: wanting to capture upside while managing bubble risk with a concentrated portfolio.
- 'There comes a point where a rapidly rising share price feels exhilarating, but that excitement is often exactly when concern is most warranted.'
- The primary defense against bubbles is to focus on intrinsic value over narratives — a clear framework for analyzing bubbles is essential.
- The host sees a familiar pattern surfacing with the current massive shift of capital towards AI, marked by the belief that 'this time is different.'

## Overview

Kyle Grieve hosts this episode exploring the timeless psychological patterns behind market bubbles, drawing from historical examples spanning railroads to the current AI boom. The episode synthesizes frameworks from Ron Insana's 'TrendWatching,' Charles Kindleberger, and Howard Marks to argue that bubbles are fundamentally psychological phenomena driven by greed, optimism, and social proof — not by the technologies themselves. Grieve acknowledges his own tension between capturing upside in a concentrated portfolio and managing bubble risk, and advocates for focusing on intrinsic value over narratives as the primary defense.

## Implications

Investors should study historical bubble patterns to develop a protective lens for current market conditions. The AI capital rotation exhibits classic bubble markers — the 'this time is different' narrative, massive private valuations, and exhilarating price action. Focus on intrinsic value analysis rather than getting swept up in narrative-driven speculation.
