# 8 Ways To Improve Your Outbound Sales — Y Combinator Startup Podcast

## TL;DR

This episode provides eight actionable strategies for improving outbound sales, based on the speaker's experience closing millions at OneSchema and advising YC companies. Key advice includes doing at least 100 manual outreaches before automating, prioritizing precise targeting over copy tweaks, crafting short, credibility-focused emails with clear CTAs, optimizing LinkedIn profiles, diagnosing reply rates in a specific order, following up 2-4 times including breakup emails, scheduling dedicated outbound time, and mining customer language for messaging. The speaker emphasizes that founder-led outbound compounds in effectiveness as you learn intent signals and customer pain points.

## Key points

- Start with manual outreach before automation: send at least 100 handcrafted emails—visiting each prospect's LinkedIn and thinking about their specific problem—so that if you get zero replies you can still learn what to fix, rather than blasting automated campaigns that yield no insights.
- Prioritize targeting over perfect messaging because an email to the wrong job title will never get a reply; for example, a founder selling infrastructure to engineering leaders wasted hundreds of messages on DevRel, GTM, and customer success managers.
- Identify the right job titles by examining existing customers: find who pushed the last deal to close, who signed the contract, and who paid; if no customers exist, ask who has the most to gain from buying your product, such as a chief security officer for employee-monitoring software rather than an engineering manager.
- Use intent signals to find the right companies: job postings reveal priorities, and company size milestones matter—e.g., compensation planning software is needed when companies cross roughly 100 employees because below that they lack levels and above 500 they already have comp bands.
- Start outbound with your warm network—friends, family, investors, college roommates, cafe acquaintances—because conversion rates are much higher than cold outreach.
- Craft emails that explain crisply how you help the buyer; focus on the top three problems keeping them up at night, what gets them fired or promoted, and keep the product explanation to one sentence.
- Establish credibility in cold emails either by listing impressive customer companies or by citing a highly relevant personal background, as in the example from Aptton founder who referenced solving the same problem as a software engineer at Tesla.
- End emails with a specific call to action like 'Got 20 minutes to hop on a call this week?' rather than vague questions, and include a calendar link or time slots to make scheduling easy.
- Keep emails to 5–8 sentences and LinkedIn messages to 2–5 sentences; every filler word dilutes the message, and the first 20–30 characters of the email body matter because that's what shows in Gmail preview.
- Improve LinkedIn profiles by adding a profile picture, turning the banner into a clear ad for the company, rewriting headline and about sections with jargon-free descriptions, and elevating relevant experience while cutting irrelevant details.
- Build mutual connections on LinkedIn daily, as the number of shared contacts is the single biggest predictor of connection acceptance and response.
- Diagnose low reply rates in a specific order: first check you're contacting the right person and right company, then subject lines, then messaging, then materials like website and LinkedIn, and finally deliverability; if all are reasonable and still zero replies, consider a deeper product-market fit issue.
- Follow up 2–4 times spaced a couple days apart because prospects often simply miss the first email; use a low-pressure breakup message like 'Since I haven't heard back, it seems like sales tax automation isn't a priority for you right now, just let me know and I can spare your inbox,' which often gets a reply.
- Reply to prospect responses as fast as possible because 'time kills deals'—founders will painfully learn that delays lose deals.
- Block out dedicated time for outbound on the calendar, decide how many messages to send per day and how long each takes, and have a co-founder hold you accountable; the number one difference between successful and unsuccessful founders is following through on committed activity.
- Mine messaging from customers: on sales calls ask what got their attention about your message, and ask existing customers the single thing that got them to buy, then reuse their exact phrasing because customers write better copy than founders.
