# How Zcash and NEAR Are Driving This Crypto Bull Run — Unchained

## TL;DR
Illia Polosukhin of NEAR Protocol and Mert Mumtaz of Helius join Laura Shin to argue that Zcash’s and NEAR’s rallies are driven by confidential transactions rather than speculation — and that the two networks now function as one privacy-preserving liquidity layer for businesses and institutions.

## Key points
- The numbers: Zcash cracked the top 10 on CoinGecko, up 32% in a week and near an all-time high; NEAR is up 69% over the same stretch. Both rallies trace to the same catalyst — transactions nobody else can see.
- NEAR Intents has reached roughly $260 million in total value locked, providing cross-chain liquidity so users can transact privately across ecosystems without exposing financial data.
- The two networks solve confidentiality differently: NEAR uses multi-party computation and secure enclaves for confidential balances, while Zcash relies on a zero-knowledge shielded pool. Polosukhin and Mumtaz argue the architectures are complementary rather than competing.
- Zcash’s counterfeiting bug forced the Ironwood migration — discussed as a real test of the network’s ability to respond to a supply-integrity failure.
- NEAR’s Shield system halted transactions mid-hack during a Litecoin exploit, cited as evidence that the security tooling is already operating in production.
- Zcash’s next upgrade, Project Tachyon, aims to make the network quantum-proof and scale it 100x.
- Mumtaz’s contrarian point: privacy will not stay a competitive edge for long — the question is what protocols build on top of it once confidential transactions are table stakes.
- The wider thesis is the shift from speculative trading to functional business operations, which makes formal verification and AI-driven monitoring necessary rather than optional, and decouples privacy from the poor user experience that historically came with it.
