# At The Money: Who Is Wealthy in America, and How Did They Get Rich? — Masters in Business

## TL;DR

Economists Owen Zedar and Eric Zwick use new IRS-linked business ownership data to show that America's wealthy are more numerous and geographically/industrially diverse than commonly believed: roughly 3 million private business owners are worth at least $5 million, collectively holding over 10 times the wealth of the Forbes 400. Tax law changes since 1986, especially for pass-through businesses, have made owning businesses the dominant path to top incomes and wealth. While entrepreneurship involves risk, it offers substantial returns on average, and the authors recommend acquiring and scaling unglamorous local businesses as a realistic wealth-building strategy.

## Key points

- The authors built an IRS/Treasury database linking businesses to their owners and workers, enabling panel analysis of all new businesses since 2000 and revealing granular industry and geographic detail that surveys like the Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Finances lack.
- There are approximately 3 million private business owners with net worth of at least $5 million, collectively holding over 10 times the wealth of the Forbes 400 combined.
- Wealth is not confined to coasts, tech, or finance; it is spread across the country and through unglamorous industries such as auto dealerships, HVAC companies, medical and dental practices, restaurants, distributors, sanitation, coin laundromats, and car washes.
- The 1986 Tax Reform Act lowered individual income tax rates below corporate rates, making pass-through business status tax-advantaged for the first time and triggering massive growth; now more than half of business income and nearly half of employment are in pass-through businesses.
- Further tax advantages for business owners include the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act's 20% deduction for certain pass-through income and state-level pass-through entity tax (PTET) workarounds approved by the IRS for high-tax states.
- At the top 0.1% of the income distribution, most income comes from business ownership rather than salaries or labor income, demonstrating that owning something rather than earning is the main path to wealth.
- Despite survivorship bias concerns, data on 10 million founding events since 2000 shows founders earn on average 10% higher incomes than similar workers, even including failures, and about 20% of founders are serial founders.
- Private business owners are politically overrepresented: roughly a quarter of Congress members are business owners, and the share is even higher in state and local offices because these are often part-time, low-salary positions.
- A realistic path to becoming a multimillionaire in America is to gain domain expertise in an unglamorous business, work in it for several years, and then acquire it from a retiring owner, often using seller financing.
- Specific examples illustrate the pattern: Nancy Mueller built a fortune selling ready-to-bake frozen quiche, and Dick Portillo grew a single hot dog stand into a business sold for $1 billion, earning enough for a yacht called 'Top Dog'.
