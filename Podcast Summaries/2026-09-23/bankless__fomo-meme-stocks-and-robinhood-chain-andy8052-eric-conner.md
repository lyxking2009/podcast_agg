# FOMO, Meme Stocks and Robinhood Chain | Andy8052 & Eric Conner — Bankless

## TL;DR

Bankless host David Hoffman discusses the current meme coin meta with Eric Conner and Andy8052, focusing on stock-paired meme coins on Robinhood Chain like Artificial Inu (AI, paired with NVIDIA, at ~$350M market cap) and the FOMO app's social graph. They explore how meme coin infrastructure is maturing through perp listings, launchpad ticker locks, AI-based scam detection, and liquidity bootstrapping for tokenized stocks, while cautioning that most are extremely risky and not for everyone.

## Key points

- Meme coins have been a persistent meta across cycles despite most individually going to zero; the 2024 meme coin bull market did little for adoption and hurt crypto's brand, yet the sector continues to innovate and attract capital.
- Stock-paired meme coins emerged as a new meta: Artificial Inu (AI) on Robinhood Chain, paired with NVIDIA, reached ~$350 million market cap and acts as the bellwether for the sector. Wong.xyz launchpad pioneered stock-paired meme coins and implemented ticker-locking (24-hour lock for new tickers, permanent lock for large coins) to prevent vampire attacks and consolidate shelling points.
- The FOMO app introduced a social graph with most-held tabs, creator rewards, and public trade visibility, providing social accountability in an otherwise opaque market. Eric and Andy use FOMO for discovery but caution about timing asymmetry—buying after someone with large following often means you are late, and copy traders can distort charts.
- AI tooling like Jev (from an early OpenAI founder) could help analyze meme coin safety by detecting bundling, holder concentration, and scam patterns quickly and cheaply, making the space more accessible; however, scammers will also use the same tools, creating a cat-and-mouse dynamic.
- Stock-paired meme coins bootstrap liquidity for tokenized stocks: AI has $14 million on-chain liquidity; trading through meme coin pools drives volume and LP fees, benefiting Robinhood Chain's tokenized stock liquidity, especially on weekends. This creates a positive-sum feedback loop where meme coins provide liquidity that would otherwise be expensive to bootstrap.
- Meme coins can potentially influence traditional equities: Boner (a Hims meme coin) trading volume reached 10% of Hims stock volume on a day, and community engagement can serve as marketing for companies. Speculative scenarios include flipping market cap of smaller stocks or triggering short squeezes (Hims has 30% short float), though current tokenized stock pools are tiny relative to company market caps.
- Trading advice from the guests: look for originality and unique narratives, check holder distribution to avoid bundled/cabal-controlled supply, use launchpad reputation, and treat small-cap meme investments as lottery tickets. They recommend shifting to higher conviction, larger cap meme coins (over $10M) as capital spreads thin.
- Both guests emphasize not hastily selling losing positions ('dust') because of past fumbles: Andy sold over 1% of AI at a $300K market cap (now $350M), and Eric sold Flork before it pumped to $32M. They advise using the mental model 'if you wouldn't buy it now, sell it' rather than cutting based on loss alone.
- Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev engaged with the community asking about in-kind redemption, highlighting uncertainties around what happens if a meme coin flips the market cap of a stock. The guests suggest that eventually every major stock may have one winning meme stock and that some companies may embrace their meme communities.
