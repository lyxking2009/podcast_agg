# YouTube CEO Neal Mohan: Why We're Betting On AI And Not Afraid Of It — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR

YouTube CEO Neal Mohan argues AI is a tool to empower creators rather than replace human creativity, with features like dynamic thumbnails, A/B testing, and the Ask video Q&A. He explains YouTube's audience-driven model has no gatekeepers, that AI-generated content is judged by quality and audience resonance, and that DeepMind collaboration is central. Mohan also addresses NFL Sunday Ticket blackouts, Netflix's pursuit of podcasters, and view count changes.

## Key points

- YouTube is a $60 billion annual business with over 2 billion users and 13% annual growth; it accounts for 14% of all U.S. streaming, sees 1 billion daily hours watched on TV alone, and has paid out $100 billion to creators over the past four years.
- Neal Mohan describes YouTube's AI strategy as building the 'world's best stage' for creators: tools like dynamic thumbnails that A/B test different images for different audiences, creation tools powered by Google's Gemini and Omni models, and AI-driven recommendations are all in service of human creativity rather than replacing it.
- Mohan argues YouTube's approach has no gatekeepers and that viewers, not AI, decide what content is good; he says 'replace that word AI or algorithm with audience' because the audience gives instantaneous feedback through views, comments, and engagement, unlike traditional Hollywood where a few people make programming decisions.
- On AI-generated content, Mohan says YouTube does not penalize videos simply for being AI-made; the platform evaluates whether content connects with audiences and takes 'AI slop' seriously, explicitly stating he would never want YouTube Shorts to become dominated by low-quality AI videos.
- The new 'Ask' feature under videos, built with Google DeepMind's Gemini, lets viewers ask questions about a specific video, get timestamps, and dig deeper; Mohan uses it to explore music video lyrics with his daughters, and YouTube is expanding conversational AI to search, including on TV with voice activation.
- Mohan says AI answers are unlikely to take views away from creators because the Ask experience is video-forward and creator-forward, often surfacing videos or segments that might otherwise be missed; current data shows no impact on video consumption, and the feature is a small fraction of overall engagement.
- YouTube collaborates with Google DeepMind daily at both leadership and working-team levels; Mohan cites Ask as a classic example of blended teams, and says AI investment is pervasive across all Google products, with YouTube particularly aggressive in deploying it for creators, viewers, and brands.
- Responding to concerns about algorithm-driven homogenization, Mohan says YouTube's lack of gatekeepers produces millions of niche fandoms; he does not see homogeneity in data and notes that up-and-coming creators may mimic successful ones like MrBeast, but that is part of creator culture, not algorithmic sameness.
- On Netflix pursuing podcasters, Mohan says creators finding success off YouTube is positive as long as YouTube remains their primary home; he cites MrBeast's 'YouTube first' rule and says YouTube engages with creators bespoke to meet their specific business needs.
- YouTube changed public view counts to unify how Shorts and VODs are counted; Mohan calls it a clarification rather than grade inflation, and creators can still see engaged views in YouTube Studio, which is what the recommendation system uses.
- Regarding NFL Sunday Ticket blackouts, Mohan explains the package is out-of-market only, so local and national games are blacked out and available through broadcast partners; YouTube follows league rules and is in its fourth season of the partnership.
- On live content, Mohan says live programs itself and YouTube does not script events; examples include NFL games, Coachella livestreams, and new AI features like auto-dubbing for livestreams to expand global audiences.
