# Bits + Bips: Is Bitcoin Finally Being Priced on Fundamentals? — Unchained

## TL;DR

Bitcoin rallied above its 50-week moving average for the first time in 45 weeks, closing at $81,160, sparking debate over whether it was driven by short covering or fundamental buyers. Guests argue the move reflects a regime change toward fundamentals and regulatory clarity, with institutions underwriting Bitcoin as a store of value even if the Clarity Act fails. Focus now shifts to whether Congress passes the Clarity Act, though the current regulatory environment is seen as sufficient to support continued institutional adoption.

## Key points

- Bitcoin's weekly candle closed above its 50-week moving average for the first time in 45 weeks, ending at $81,160. Galaxy Digital's Alex Thorn notes that in four of five completed bear markets, once the 50-week moving average was first broken to the upside, the bear market bottom was definitively in; Benjamin Cowen says a rally to a higher high and weekly closes above the 50-week SMA would materially weaken the bear case, and Fidelity's Jurrien Timmer says a new four-year bull cycle may be underway.
- The rally sparked debate over whether it is driven by short covering or real spot buyers. Evidence for short covering includes Coinbase premium being negative for 166 of 168 hours, indicating no US retail bid, and listed companies adding only 5,900 BTC against 89,000 in July 2025. Counter-evidence includes Glassnode data showing corporate treasury cost basis around $80k and ETF cost basis around $85k, and the fact that a pure short squeeze usually gives back 50-70% of gains the next session, which did not happen.
- Ram Ahluwalia argues that while it is a short squeeze, that does not invalidate the rally; bottoms typically see shorts offside, then momentum and FOMO buyers. He attributes the surprise to the possibility that the Clarity Act may pass after consensus had been that it would not, creating dry timber and offside positioning; the path of Congress now determines next moves.
- Chris Perkins describes a regime change: crypto is moving from being priced as a frontier risk asset to being priced on fundamentals. He says regulators have removed the artificial barrier by providing clarity on securities and commodities, allowing institutions to underwrite based on fundamentals. Even with negative macro factors like a rate hike, geopolitics, and oil over $100, Bitcoin's store-of-value fundamentals are taking hold.
- Austin Campbell points out that when the Clarity Act initially failed, Bitcoin dipped to $75k but did not keep falling, invalidating short theses expecting a larger leg down. He notes that Avalanche rose nearly 50% over seven days on fundamental news, suggesting breadth beyond Bitcoin. If clarity is fully dead, the market is building anyway, and shorters face unlimited risk in a volatile asset.
- Chris Perkins emphasizes that even without the Clarity Act, regulatory clarity is sufficient: taxonomy has been resolved with roadmaps for both securities and commodities, there will be two to three years of precedent, and the post-Chevron deference environment means regulators like Chairman Celig are careful to craft durable rules. Institutions can move forward regardless of legislative outcome.
- Ram Ahluwalia estimates the current rally is not retail-driven: ETF flows were only $6 million, too small to explain the move. He attributes buying to cryptonative, quant, and macro trader components reacting to news flow, and says the event-driven repricing of Clarity Act probability reset the game. Austin notes retail is not yet involved, leaving room for another leg if they return.
