# What a Town Full of Vibecoders Says About the Chinese Economy — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Aphra Wong reports from China's 'vibe coding capital' Liangzhu and the AI education industry, finding an embrace of AI driven by state-led diffusion and limited alternatives. Polling shows 83% of Chinese believe AI will benefit them versus 38% in the U.S., but beneath optimism lies a low-trust, low-growth economy with youth unemployment near 19%. Local governments are flooding the market with AI-generated short dramas, yet only 0.16% become hits and 90% of AIGC companies lose money.

## Key points
- China's 'AI Plus' program, promoted by Xi Jinping, frames AI as the next internet and a 'high-quality productivity force,' aiming to diffuse AI across manufacturing, supply chains, education, and agriculture, unlike the U.S. focus on frontier models.
- DeepSeek R1's native Chinese-language training made it the first Chinese LLM that users felt kinship with, reversing an 'ego death' in China's tech scene after ChatGPT's emergence and producing a sense of 'guoyun' or national destiny.
- Liangzhu, a scenic town 20–30 minutes from Hangzhou, has become a hub for 'vibe coders'—laid-off big tech workers, tired of 996, and young graduates—who build quirky one-person apps such as FateTell, an AI fortune-telling app that is one of the few profitable ventures.
- The AI education industry is booming and largely unregulated; Shengcai Youshu, literally 'the art of getting rich,' has 80,000 paying members paying 3,000–4,000 yuan, uses a multi-level marketing structure with Dragon Ball points, and sells hope more than skills.
- Aphra Wong describes China as a low-trust, zero-sum society post-COVID, where civil servant exam competitors use 'dark forest' logic; despite meritocratic rhetoric, opportunities have shrunk and real incomes are stagnant.
- Local governments, needing to demonstrate AI investment to Beijing, have created an 'industrial slop complex' by subsidizing AI-generated content; cities like Qingdao, Wuhan, Zhengzhou, and Mentougou are producing massive volumes of AIGC dramas.
- The AIGC industry is mostly unprofitable: only 0.16% of AI-generated short dramas become hits, 90% of AIGC companies lose money, and a 'ghost city effect' means most content is never watched.
- High-speed rail symbolizes the disconnect: 80–85% of lines lose money, while surface-level modernity masks deflation, real estate losses, and a stock market that burned retail investors; youth unemployment is officially 18.9%.
- China's creative industry is constrained by censorship, described as walking in a dark room with 10 untouchable furniture pieces; this and strategic focus on manufacturing over soft power have limited cultural exports compared to Japan and Korea.
- AI is seen by many young Chinese as a rare path to upward mobility or 'revenge' against an older generation controlling resources, even as the broader economy has become 'Japanified' with little growth.
