# Your Coin Needs to Make Money, or Be Money | Tulip King — Bankless

## TL;DR
Bankless host and Tulip King discuss why 2021 crypto narratives failed because DeFi tokens became 'meme coins with extra steps' and only projects that are money or make money deserve attention. Tulip argues proof-of-work is superior for money because it forces distribution, while proof-of-stake concentrates supply, and he sees crypto as protest technology with ugly but compounding returns. The conversation shifts to Eli Lilly, which Tulip believes will become the greatest revenue generator through GLP-1 weight-loss drugs and AI-driven drug discovery, offering 5-6x upside with low downside.

## Key points
- Tulip King states that all DeFi tokens from 2021 were essentially meme coins with extra steps because protocols did not return value to token holders, leading market participants to skip straight to meme coins.
- He divides investable crypto into two buckets: money coins (Bitcoin, Zcash, Pearl) and revenue-generating coins (Hyperliquid, Derive, Venice), while smart contract L1 tokens like ETH, SOL, AVAX are awkward in-between assets.
- Tulip compares the current crypto market to the dot-com bottom: aggregate market cap may stay flat for years as worthless coins bleed, but quality assets like Hyperliquid, Derive, Venice can compound for decades similar to Amazon, Microsoft, and Apple after 2000.
- He outlines a simple framework for identifying 'obvious good coins': sort by revenue on DeFi Llama, assess whether the business can compound, and ask if the founder would treat token holders with respect, including returning revenue via buybacks.
- Tulip argues buybacks are mandatory in crypto because token holders have zero legal protections; unlike startups with shareholder rights, crypto projects must signal they will treat holders like shareholders, citing Helium as a negative example.
- He claims Hyperliquid is potentially a 100x asset if it wins on-chain derivatives, pointing to $250 trillion in asset market size and $4 quadrillion in annual derivatives volume, and notes its reflexive design where HYPE collateral grows as price rises.
- Tulip maintains proof-of-work is better for money than proof-of-stake because mining forces miners to sell ~99% of rewards to cover costs, creating healthy distribution and acting as an inverse Cantillon effect, while staking concentrates supply among liquid staking providers and MEV actors.
- He says the Ethereum network can be valuable while the ETH token disappoints, using a post office analogy: Ethereum charges per data weight, not per value transferred, so it captures little of the network's economic value, unlike Visa's percentage fees.
- Tulip argues crypto is protest technology with natural downward drift, but government fiscal constraints will cause episodic reallocations like forced 401k bond purchases, creating violent upside stair-steps in crypto.
- He notes the resurgence of old ideas: Zcash, Pearl, and Qantas are proof-of-work 21-million supply coins outperforming, attributing it to early innovators being correct and a return to sound money principles.
- On Eli Lilly, Tulip predicts GLP-1 weight-loss drugs will sell 10-100x Wall Street estimates, driven by a global obesity epidemic (1 billion obese, 3+ billion overweight) and drugs like Retta outperforming weight-loss surgery.
- He says Lilly will become the largest health research sponsor, surpassing the NIH, and its weight-loss franchise alone could generate more revenue than Apple's iPhone, Nvidia's GPUs, and Micron's memory combined, leading to a $5 trillion market cap.
- Tulip explains Lilly's AI advantage: drugs can be patented unlike software, so AI drug discovery benefits entrenched pharma; Lilly has the best clinical process, most proprietary data, largest Nvidia compute cluster, and will acquire AI-discovered drug startups.
- He describes a right-tail outcome: by curing obesity and heart disease, Lilly extends lifespans, converting patients into long-term customers for GLP-1s and later cancer medicines, creating a $10 trillion scenario.
- On media, Tulip says Counterparty focuses entirely on the ThreadGuy stream because social media is exponential and ThreadGuy is generational talent; they avoid spin-offs to keep a small team and maximize clip virality.
