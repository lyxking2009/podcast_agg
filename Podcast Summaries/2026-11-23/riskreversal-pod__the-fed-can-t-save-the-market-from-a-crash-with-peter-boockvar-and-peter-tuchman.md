# The Fed Can't Save The Market From A Crash with Peter Boockvar and Peter Tuchman — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Peter Boockvar argues that the AI capex cycle is breaking down, the Fed is unlikely to rescue equities, and rate cuts may not stop a market crash given fiscal deficits and sticky inflation. He sees a near-term bottom in gold and silver but warns Bitcoin is trading as a leveraged Nasdaq 100 rather than a store of value. Peter Tuchman recounts his 40-year NYSE floor career, attributing today's extreme volatility to a new generation of retail traders and emphasizing the need for financial education.

## Key points
- Peter Boockvar says the AI trade has suffered multiple blows: canceled data center IPOs (e.g., AirTrunk), Nex DC unplugging because the local community refused additional power, and AI supplier stocks peaking in June–July as markets priced in peak capex growth and margins.
- Nvidia is a 'picks and shovels' company dependent on data center construction, so it faces risk not only from Jensen Huang but from slowdowns in US and Asian data center buildout, especially if midterm politics restrict chip sales or leasing capacity to China.
- Anthropic's huge capital raise likely drained dollars from other parts of the market, adding to tech selling pressure.
- The Fed's hawkishness is colliding with a fragile AI capex cycle; Boockvar believes hawkish Fed members are ignoring falling commodity prices (copper, oil) and the surge in the cost of capital for data centers.
- He argues the market has reached peak hawkishness: the Fed may deliver one more hike in December, but members expecting hikes into 2027 will be 'mugged by reality' as AI trade reversal slows the economy.
- If the economy slows, the US budget deficit could balloon from about 6% of GDP to 12%, causing long-term Treasury yields to rise even if the Fed cuts short rates—a reversal of the typical recession playbook.
- The 2% inflation target is arbitrary, adopted from the Reserve Bank of New Zealand to create room to cut rates; Boockvar says moving the goalposts would push the 10-year yield from 5% to 6–7%, so Kevin Warsh would never do it.
- The federal government's 6% budget deficit is inherently inflationary, injecting roughly $2 trillion into the economy, leaving the Fed fighting inflation with one hand tied.
- Gold and silver are near capitulation: gold's 14-day RSI hit the lowest since October 2023 and the daily sentiment index is in single digits, historically a bottom; falling two-year yields support a near-term rebound.
- Hyperscaler capex is expected to grow about 100% in 2026, then decelerate to 50% growth expectations; absolute capex likely peaks in 2027 and declines in 2028, pressuring AI-related stocks.
- Fed rate cuts do not guarantee equity rescue: after the tech bubble, the Nasdaq fell 82% despite aggressive cuts, and in 2008 the S&P was cut in half despite easing; investors must do fundamental work on AI names.
- Bitcoin has traded as a leveraged Nasdaq 100, not a store of value; if it wants to be digital gold, it should trade like gold, but currently it is tethered to the AI/tech trade.
- Peter Tuchman started on the NYSE floor on March 28, 1985, the day Ronald Reagan rang the opening bell; he earned a seat in 3.5 years versus the typical 13–15 years.
- Tuchman attributes today's extreme single-stock volatility to a new generation of retail traders who came in around COVID with no education; he and David Green built an educational platform to teach financial literacy.
- Tuchman highlights market resilience: the Fed injected $3 trillion in 3–4 months after COVID versus $800 billion over 18 months in the GFC, and the market has had 68 record closes this year despite numerous shocks.
- Tuchman was offered a job by Ivan Boesky days before Boesky's indictment for insider trading involving Dennis Levine, but he never worked there.
