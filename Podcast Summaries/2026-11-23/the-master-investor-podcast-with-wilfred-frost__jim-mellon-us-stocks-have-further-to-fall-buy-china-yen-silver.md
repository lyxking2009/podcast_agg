# Jim Mellon: US Stocks Have Further To Fall; Buy China, Yen, & Silver — The Master Investor Podcast with Wilfred Frost

## TL;DR
Jim Mellon warns that US stocks have further to fall, predicting at least a one-third decline from recent highs due to record leverage, retail speculation, and foreign inflows. He advises selling any bounce and instead buying China, Japanese long bonds (4.5% yield on 2061s), and silver. Mellon also sees the AI bubble as having peaked with the Anthropic and SpaceX IPOs and cautions against catching falling knives.

## Key points
- Jim Mellon argues US stocks have not bottomed and expects at least a one-third peak-to-trough decline for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, driven by record margin debt, retail investors holding 40% of the market, and foreign investors accounting for $25 trillion or 80% of recent inflows.
- He recommends selling any meaningful bounce and holding dry powder or rotating into fundamentally cheaper alternatives such as China, Japanese yen, and silver.
- Mellon sees Nvidia as cheap at around three times forward earnings with indispensable chips and strong cash flow, but admits his short-term purchase was 'foolish'; he notes a possible return of Jensen Huang as CEO could trigger a 20% short squeeze.
- He is buying China because it trades at a fraction of US valuations (under 10 times earnings), with government stimulus expected, property market recovery, and companies moving away from destructive price competition; he sees potential for a 50% uplift over the next 18 months.
- Mellon reiterates a bullish stance on the Japanese yen and especially Japanese long bonds, highlighting the 4.5% yield on 2061 bonds as historically unprecedented and highly geared to interest rate declines.
- He recommends silver over gold, citing genuine industrial demand from electric vehicles and data centers, a supply shortage, and higher beta; he notes gold producers are locking in prices around $3,600 after gold's fall from $5,400 to $3,500-$3,600.
- Mellon believes the Anthropic and SpaceX IPOs marked the top of the AI bubble, pointing to massive lock-up expirations, Anthropic's $900 billion three-year capex requirement, and local voter opposition to data centers.
- He warns the US market is more leveraged than in 1929, with single-stock ETFs, retail gamification, and foreign speculation; he expects one or two major bankruptcies and notes OpenAI may struggle to go public.
- Mellon thinks oil could bounce above $100 if the US takes military action against Iran, but notes China's shift to EVs (70% of new car sales) is structurally reducing oil demand.
- He highlights UK investment trusts as attractive, noting 10% of the FTSE 250 was acquired in the past year at an average 70% premium, and recommends them for new portfolios due to low fees, active management, and discount control mechanisms.
- Mellon discusses his private investment in Quantinuum, where he was the first outside investor; he claims a return of 'at least 10,000 times my money in 14 years' from an initial stake of less than £100,000 and does not intend to sell.
- In clean food, Mellon is optimistic about precision fermentation company Clean Food Group, planning a UK IPO next year, and notes Meatly's lab-grown meat dog treats sold through Pets at Home; he personally invested another $30 million in the sector last year.
- Mellon criticizes potential Fed rate hikes as a mistake given wealth-effect risks, noting inflation at 3.6% and falling unemployment; he sees a 93% chance of a quarter-point rise but declining odds of a second hike.
