# The Shocking Return of Jensen Huang — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia CEO 11 days after resigning amid extramarital affairs and alleged Chinese spy links, following direct intervention by President Trump and a market crash. The reversal sparked a 32% Nvidia stock surge adding roughly $1 trillion in market cap, but investigations and governance concerns remain. The episode examines fallout for Nvidia, US AI, and China's AI industry, including tighter export controls and a likely board reshuffle.

## Key points
- Nvidia's board reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as CEO on Monday, 11 days after he resigned over revelations of at least two extramarital affairs. The board cited senior US government input, including President Trump, who joined the Sunday board meeting and said the return poses no national security risk.
- The reinstatement followed a market crumble: S&P 500 entered correction territory, down 3.7% on Friday; Nasdaq was down 17% from October highs; Nvidia stock had fallen 40% from its peak. After the announcement, Nvidia jumped 32% adding almost $1 trillion in market cap, S&P 500 up about 5%, Nasdaq up about 7%.
- Scandal details include Jensen's affair with Alice Wong, a Mega Speed executive whom some US officials suspect of being a spy. New York Times reporting says US intelligence tracked Wong's contact with a China Ministry of State Security officer from spring 2025 to fall of this year, overlapping the affair. Wong's business partner Kangwei also allegedly worked with the same Chinese spy agency.
- Ongoing investigations persist: SEC is probing Nvidia's accounting practices, FBI is looking into Jensen, DOJ is investigating Mega Speed, and Jensen will face congressional testimony. Nvidia formed an independent board committee and will hire a chief compliance officer and create a national security/export control committee.
- Corporate governance is heavily criticized: Nvidia had no succession plan and many major supplier/customer deals were handshake agreements with Jensen rather than written contracts. Board initially said Jensen would not return, then reversed under pressure, raising 'too big to fail' concerns and a precedent of government intervention in private business.
- China's AI industry faces negative fallout. The Southeast Asia rental loophole for Nvidia chips is closing, limiting Chinese labs' access. Switching to Huawei raises equipment supply constraints. Distillation from US models is becoming harder as OpenAI and Anthropic restrict access, forcing Chinese labs to fund more expensive original research.
- Political risk remains: Democrats swept Congress and may investigate after taking power. Trump could issue a pardon or assurances to shield Jensen from federal liability. The Nvidia board is expected to be reshuffled, though no succession plan was announced.
- US AI leaders like Sam Altman and Elon Musk welcomed Jensen's return. Stronger export controls on China could benefit US labs while Jensen's return stabilizes hardware supply. Host notes this may be 'best case scenario' for US AI: Jensen back, China competitors cut off.
- The episode closes with a quote from Ross Douet: 'One guy in a leather jacket is more important to the NASDAQ than mathematical demigod breakthroughs.' This was exemplified by Anthropic solving a millennium problem on Friday but stock falling, while Jensen's return immediately boosted markets.
