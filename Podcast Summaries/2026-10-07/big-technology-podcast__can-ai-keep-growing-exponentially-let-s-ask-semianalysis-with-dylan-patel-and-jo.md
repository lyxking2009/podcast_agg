# Can AI Keep Growing Exponentially? Let’s Ask SemiAnalysis — With Dylan Patel and Jordan Nanos — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
Dylan Patel and Jordan Nanos from SemiAnalysis discuss the unprecedented scale of AI infrastructure spending, arguing US capex will hit about $2 trillion next year—roughly 5-6% of GDP—far surpassing past buildouts like railroads. They expect hyperscaler capex growth to decelerate due to practical limits but project AI lab revenue will reach trillions, with OpenAI and Anthropic combined potentially reaching $600-700 billion by December 2026 and $1.5 trillion by end of 2027. The conversation also covers neocloud reliability and security, Nvidia's $588 billion backstop role, and Anthropic's strong economics ahead of its IPO.

## Key points
- US AI-related capex will reach approximately $2 trillion in 2026, around 5-6% of GDP, far exceeding the 3.6% median of past infrastructure buildouts because US data centers also serve global demand for inference and training.
- Hyperscaler capex growth hit 116% year-over-year in Q3 2026, but growth will inevitably decelerate due to larger bases, difficulty marshaling resources for additional gigawatts, and capital raising constraints, though demand still outstrips supply.
- Dylan Patel argues revenue forecasts from Goldman, Bain, and Columbia's Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh are too low; he projects OpenAI and Anthropic combined could reach $600-700 billion by December 2026 and $1.5 trillion by end of 2027.
- Meta is solvent and can halt new spending if needed; most hyperscaler contracts with neoclouds are 5-6 year take-or-pay, not monthly cancellable, reducing near-term cancellation risk for neocloud providers.
- Anthropic is already profitable on compute costs, with inference gross margins around 75%, similar or better than SaaS leaders like Salesforce, and near-zero customer acquisition cost, making it arguably a better business than traditional software.
- OpenAI is expected to reach compute profitability within 2-3 quarters, and Amazon's AI infrastructure investments are already profitable, boosting its gross margin from Bedrock and AI revenue.
- The source of AI revenue growth will come from accelerating R&D and COGS shifts, plus a falling labor share of GDP as capital (AI tokens) captures more value; Dylan believes the economy will grow enough that labor dollars won't shrink in the near term.
- Nvidia is backstopping $588 billion in off-balance sheet commitments, including revenue floors and leases, to help neoclouds secure investment-grade financing and diversify away from hyperscaler dependence; Nvidia also uses some GPUs for internal research but typically sells them to higher bidders.
- Neocloud security is poor: open-source Chinese models like GLM 5.3 can hack many providers, and SemiAnalysis accidentally accessed data from intelligence agencies on a shared cluster, prompting Ilya Sutskever to warn about agents taking over neoclouds.
- ClusterMax 3.0 ranks CoreWeave and Nebius as top-tier GPU clouds; Oracle and Google Cloud are in second tier for cluster quality, but Oracle faces execution risks like the New Mexico pipeline delay and fixed-margin contracts that limit upside from rising GPU prices.
- Many of the 323 tracked cloud providers fail basic reliability and security bars; in a seller's market, most prioritize building capacity over service quality, which is sad given how simple some security fixes are.
- Google's DeepMind is receiving a declining share of Google's compute, with less compute than OpenAI and Anthropic by end of 2025, forcing Google to find a niche for Gemini as it lags in coding compared to top models.
- AI labs are already keeping frontier models internal for months: Anthropic had Mythos ready in February but only released neutered versions; OpenAI delayed Astra and has not released Bell, showing deliberate pacing to prevent distillation.
- Dylan believes Anthropic could be the first $10 trillion company, but at $20 trillion valuation, AI would be so capable that wealth concentration could destabilize society; Jordan is more optimistic about applications like drug discovery.
