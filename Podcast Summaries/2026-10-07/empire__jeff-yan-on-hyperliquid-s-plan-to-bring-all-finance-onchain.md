# Jeff Yan On Hyperliquid’s Plan To Bring All Finance Onchain — Empire

## TL;DR
Jeff Yan, co-founder and CEO of Hyperliquid Labs, discusses how Hyperliquid aims to become neutral infrastructure for all global finance by building a few composable primitives like perpetuals, spot, lending, and options. He details HIP-4's portfolio margin and priority fee burn mechanics (nearly 180,000 HYPE burned), HIP-3 Star's permissioned marketplaces for entities like Kraken, and why transparent order books lead to better execution. Yan argues priority fees internalize capital-intensive latency races, options are the next obvious primitive, and Hyperliquid's success is measured by zero-to-one innovations rather than competitive victories.

## Key points
- Hyperliquid's overall strategy is to create a small number of surgical, broadly composable primitives that can eventually accommodate the entire financial system, rather than building many complex, disconnected tools. Portfolio margin is cited as an example of combining a lending primitive with a trading primitive, enabling elegant interactions between spot, perpetuals, and lending.
- HIP-4 released this year includes portfolio margin extended to native lending, priority fees, and HIP-3 Star on testnet. Portfolio margin allows users to use assets as collateral across multiple positions, making the lending primitive directly useful for trading.
- HIP-3 has been a major growth driver: open interest in real assets grew from $300 million in January to $3.9 billion today, a 13-fold increase. This has shifted Hyperliquid's comparisons from crypto-native protocols like Aevo and dYdX to traditional giants like CME and NASDAQ.
- HIP-3 Star adds permissioned features such as whitelisting traders. Kraken has announced plans, pending regulatory approval, to use HIP-3 Star to offer Hyperliquid to US citizens, leveraging Hyperliquid's transparency, self-custody, and system resilience.
- Priority fees consist of priority read fees (stream of unconfirmed orders from the mempool) and priority entry fees (higher rewards for queue priority). These mechanisms have already burned nearly 180,000 HYPE.
- Priority fees are an egalitarian mechanism to internalize the zero-sum latency arms race seen in traditional finance, where firms spend hundreds of millions on microwave towers and transatlantic fiber. Yan compares this to Ethereum's fee burn shift away from validator bribes, making markets more accessible.
- Transparent order books, including Hyperliquid's L4 application book with network addresses, are argued to increase liquidity and execution quality. An empirical study found public TWAP orders executed better than hidden TWAP orders because market makers could absorb non-toxic flow, similar to how leveraged ETFs rebalance transparently at market close.
- Jeff Yan believes options are the next obvious primitive. HIP-4 can support options and convex payoff curves, and traditional finance demand suggests options complement perpetuals. However, no option product with convex payout has reached escape velocity in crypto yet.
- Spot markets and native asset issuance are critical but slower to develop because asset storage and issuance are harder than tokenizing price feeds. Portfolio margin is only as useful as the assets it supports, so bringing real assets on-chain is necessary.
- Hyperliquid is compared to neutral public infrastructure like the internet or Linux. Yan says it is not trying to push out CME/Nasdaq but rather empower them and other financial entities to serve their users more efficiently.
- Success is measured qualitatively by zero-to-one innovations like HIP-3 and 24/7 markets, and by the quality of developers who choose to integrate. Metrics are tracked, but Yan finds more satisfaction in seeing new things created and used.
- Community feedback: stay humble, focus on the fundamental value of helping end users, and avoid complacency or feeling superior to other protocols. Yan emphasizes that Hyperliquid was just an idea three years ago and growth has been fortunate.
