# Animal Communicator

**Show:** Money Stuff: The Podcast  
**Date:** 2026-07-17  
**Duration:** 31 min

## Key Points

- The episode opens with Katie recounting a $75, 30-minute video session with an "animal communicator" who relayed to her cat and horse (Gus) that she is pregnant; the communicator reported the cat was relieved (having thought Katie was just getting sicker and less mobile) while the horse was reportedly "psyched" and possessive about the baby. Matt frames the whole exercise as classic cold reading.
- Matt and Katie discuss a new SEC filing for the Subversive All Seasons Sports ETF, which would hire a professional gambler to place ongoing sports bets on Kalshi and wrap the results in an ETF share structure, designed to keep running indefinitely rather than expire like earlier binary election-outcome ETFs.
- Matt notes his own Money Stuff column arguing the ETF removes the "fun" of sports betting was submitted into the SEC's actual public comment file on "novel" ETFs (leveraged, single-stock, and prediction-market products), making him an unwitting commenter in the rulemaking process.
- They debate whether outsourcing bets to a professional gambler defeats the purpose for retail sports bettors, since investors would just be buying "a diversified portfolio of bets placed by someone else"; they speculate a mature sports-ETF market might split into systematic strategies (e.g., betting heavy favorites in college football) versus leveraged single-game products that double or zero out daily.
- South Korean regulators moved to curb a leveraged single-stock ETF craze tied heavily to Samsung and SK Hynix, halting new fund listings, raising minimum deposit and training requirements for investors, and increasing minimum trading lot sizes. Matt explains why leveraged single-stock ETFs function as "distilled" margin debt, mechanically amplifying volatility more than traditional margin lending.
- Katie recaps her Bloomberg Television interview with Strategy's CEO about the bitcoin treasury business, and separately references a prior interview with former SEC Chair Gary Gensler, who said he personally opposed leveraged single-stock ETFs even while chairing the agency that allowed them, given the US disclosure-based regulatory approach.
- The hosts unpack Strategy's (formerly MicroStrategy's) unwinding "virtuous cycle" trade: the company used to issue stock at a premium to buy bitcoin, but with shares no longer trading at a premium, it must now sell bitcoin to buy back stock instead. They also discuss Stretch, Strategy's floating-rate preferred stock originally designed to trade at par, which now yields around 12% and trades near 85, with management aiming to rebuild credit rather than raise the rate further. The CEO said debt risk only becomes a real concern if bitcoin falls to roughly $8,000–$10,000.
- SpaceX shares have fallen below their $135 IPO price (to around $132) within weeks of debuting, having lost the tailwind of index-related buying demand. The hosts discuss upcoming lockup expirations after August earnings that could roughly double the tradable float, and whether early employees and insiders will choose to sell.

## Overview

The episode opens with a lighthearted personal anecdote from Katie about hiring an animal communicator to tell her pets she's pregnant, before turning to core finance topics. Matt and Katie discuss the newly filed sports-gambling ETF and the SEC's ongoing comment process on "novel" ETF structures, South Korea's regulatory crackdown on leveraged single-stock ETFs, Katie's interview with Strategy's CEO about the company's bitcoin treasury business and its unwinding premium-issuance trade, and SpaceX's stock slide below its IPO price ahead of major lockup expirations. Katie signs off the episode heading into an extended parental leave.

## Implications

The sports-gambling and leveraged/single-stock ETF filings underscore continued regulatory ambiguity in the US, where the SEC is soliciting comment on "novel" ETF wrappers without a clear framework yet, in contrast to South Korea's more interventionist crackdown on similar leveraged products — highlighting a global divergence in how regulators are responding to increasingly exotic retail-facing fund structures. Strategy's shift from issuing stock at a premium to buy bitcoin, to selling bitcoin to buy back stock, marks a reversal of its core business model; the $8,000–$10,000 bitcoin level flagged by management is now a key threshold to watch for the company's debt sustainability. SpaceX's post-IPO weakness ahead of a lockup-driven doubling of tradable float sets up a near-term supply/demand test for the stock heading into August earnings.
