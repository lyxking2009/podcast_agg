# ROLLUP: Bull Market? | Inflation Cools, War Heats | Robinhood Flips Base | ETH's Fee Problem

**Show:** Bankless  
**Date:** 2026-07-17  
**Duration:** 67 min

## Key Points

- **Macro backdrop is mixed: cooling inflation vs. escalating war risk.** US CPI came in at 3.5%, below the 3.8% consensus expectation, with core CPI at 2.6% — a disinflationary signal that would normally support risk assets. But oil prices jumped roughly 20% in July as the Iran conflict escalated, injecting fresh geopolitical uncertainty that complicates any clean "bull market" narrative.

- **Ryan and David debate whether the crypto cycle has bottomed.** David argues for a "never fade the cycle" thesis — that every attempt to call the top or bottom early has historically been wrong, and the four-year cycle pattern tends to reassert itself. Ryan is more hesitant to declare a bull market has started, noting there are "some changes" worth watching but wanting more confirmation before committing to the call.

- **Robinhood Chain has already overtaken Coinbase's Base in user activity, just weeks after launch.** The hosts flag this as a notable shift in L2 competitive dynamics. Morpho currently leads TVL on Robinhood Chain at $152M, and a meme-coin rivalry between Cash Cat and Ansem-linked tokens is emerging as a visible signal of ecosystem energy and speculative interest.

- **Base is publicly course-correcting.** Jesse Pollack acknowledged that Base's earlier focus on creator coins was a misstep, and the chain is now pivoting toward DeFi and trading infrastructure under new leadership — an implicit concession that the memecoin/social-token strategy did not deliver the durable activity Base needed to fend off newer entrants like Robinhood Chain.

- **Ethereum's fee-capture problem is under scrutiny.** Analysis cited in the episode (attributed to "Lorenzo") shows Robinhood Chain generated just $1,538 in value accrual to Ethereum despite the underlying activity producing $816K in revenue — a stark illustration of how little L2 activity currently flows back to ETH as an asset, reviving the long-running debate about L2s paying insufficient "rent" to the base layer.

- **Hosts disagree on whether ETH should be optimized for fee revenue.** Ryan argues that maximizing for fee capture would be a mistake for Ethereum long-term, since it risks distorting incentives and boxing ETH into a narrow "revenue asset" framing rather than a store-of-value/settlement-layer framing. David counters that in this early, bootstrapping phase, some degree of fee focus is necessary to prove out ETH's value-accrual story to skeptical markets.

- **A wave of Ethereum Foundation alumni are spinning out new institutional-facing organizations.** The episode references Ethlabs, "Ethereum Institutional," and a new privacy-focused group ("ETH Systems") as evidence that former EF talent is now building parallel, more commercially aggressive structures to push Ethereum's adoption and value capture forward.

- **Buy-and-burn token models continue to outperform.** David states plainly that "the buy and burn token model is undefeated," pointing to Hyperliquid and VVV as examples where directing protocol fee revenue into token buybacks/burns has proven an effective way to reward holders — implicitly contrasting this with Ethereum's more diffuse fee-burn dynamics under EIP-1559.

- **DeFi hack activity appears to be normalizing after an April 2026 peak.** The hosts note that exploit frequency and severity spiked in April but has trended smaller and less frequent since, suggesting the earlier "hackpocalypse" narrative may have been overstated rather than indicating an ongoing acceleration of DeFi security failures.

- **Michael Saylor's continued Bitcoin accumulation is framed within the broader cycle debate**, with David noting Saylor "turned on the money printer" (i.e., resumed aggressive buying/leverage), while the hosts maintain that individual actors like Saylor matter less than the overall cycle dynamics — "cycle is always bigger."

## Overview

In this weekly ROLLUP episode, Bankless co-founders David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams work through whether crypto has bottomed and a new bull leg is underway, weighing cooling US inflation data against escalating Iran-related war risk and oil price spikes. The bulk of the discussion centers on L2 competitive dynamics: Robinhood Chain has rapidly overtaken Coinbase's Base in activity just weeks post-launch, forcing Base to publicly pivot away from its creator-coin strategy toward DeFi infrastructure. This feeds into a broader and more contentious discussion about Ethereum's fee-capture problem — data showing Robinhood Chain returning only $1,538 in value to ETH against $816K in generated revenue crystallizes host disagreement over whether Ethereum should optimize aggressively for fee revenue (David's bootstrapping argument) or preserve its store-of-value/settlement-layer positioning (Ryan's concern about distorted incentives). The episode also surveys new Ethereum Foundation-alumni-led organizations (Ethlabs, Ethereum Institutional, ETH Systems) pushing more commercially assertive strategies, contrasts Ethereum's diffuse burn model with the "undefeated" buy-and-burn mechanics used by Hyperliquid and VVV, and closes by noting DeFi hack activity has cooled since an April 2026 peak.

## Implications

The Robinhood Chain vs. Base flip is an early but meaningful signal that L2 market share is far from settled and can shift quickly when a well-distributed consumer app (Robinhood) turns on-chain — a dynamic that pressures incumbent L2s to justify their differentiation beyond first-mover advantage. The $1,538-vs-$816K value-capture gap cited for Ethereum sharpens an increasingly urgent strategic question for the ecosystem: if L2 activity keeps scaling while returning negligible value to L1, ETH's investment thesis as a fee-capturing asset weakens, adding pressure toward either protocol-level changes (e.g., higher L2 settlement costs, blob pricing changes) or a full narrative pivot away from "ETH as cash-flow asset" toward "ETH as neutral settlement/collateral layer." The proliferation of EF-alumni spinouts (Ethlabs, Ethereum Institutional, ETH Systems) suggests growing recognition within the Ethereum core community that the Foundation's traditional structure is insufficient for competing commercially, which could reshape how Ethereum-aligned initiatives get funded and executed going forward. The buy-and-burn comparison to Hyperliquid/VVV implicitly raises the question of whether Ethereum's own tokenomics need updating to remain competitive with newer, more aggressively value-accretive token designs. Finally, the moderation in DeFi hack severity since April is a mildly bullish signal for institutional confidence in DeFi infrastructure, though the hosts stop short of declaring the security risk resolved.
