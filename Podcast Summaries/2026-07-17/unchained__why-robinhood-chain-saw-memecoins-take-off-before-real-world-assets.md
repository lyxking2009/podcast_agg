# Why Robinhood Chain Saw Memecoins Take Off Before Real World Assets

**Show:** Unchained  
**Date:** 2026-07-17  
**Duration:** 29 min

## Key Points

- **Memecoins, not RWAs, dominate Robinhood Chain's early activity.** Two weeks after launch, roughly 85% or more of daily DEX trading volume on Robinhood Chain is memecoins, while tokenized real-world assets (RWAs) account for only about 1%. This directly contradicts CEO Vlad Tenev's July 2 CNBC comments that RWAs, not memecoins, were the future of crypto on the chain — a stance he later softened by tweeting that the chain "also works great for memecoins" after the CashCat token (affiliated with Robinhood's old mascot) took off.

- **Johann Kerbrat (VP/GM of Robinhood Crypto) argues the memecoin surge is not a problem but a deliberate byproduct of strategy.** The chain was built permissionlessly and open to anyone, and memecoins are simply easier to build than complex RWA products. He frames memecoin trading (via CashCat and Pump.fun) as bringing early liquidity, users, and market-maker engagement that will eventually benefit RWA products too.

- **TVL and stablecoin activity are growing fast.** Robinhood Chain's TVL has passed $370 million, much of it stablecoin-driven, tied to Robinhood's "Earn" product that lets users get roughly 7% yield on stablecoins directly from the Robinhood app. Kerbrat calls this the "main aha moment" pushed at the London launch event alongside a dozen-plus other products.

- **Pump.fun listing Robinhood Chain tokens was not centrally orchestrated but fits the permissionless strategy.** Kerbrat says most third-party integrations after launch (including Pump.fun) weren't coordinated with Robinhood directly, but the chain was deliberately built on Arbitrum with heavy documentation and partnerships (Alchemy, Chainlink, etc.) specifically to make it easy for anyone to build on top of it.

- **USDG, not USDC or Tether, is the backbone for margin and lending — a deliberate choice tied to shared economics.** Kerbrat says USDG's model shares yield/economics among network participants rather than concentrating benefits with a single issuer, which Robinhood sees as more valuable for building products on top. Robinhood is a founding member of the Global Dollar Network but says that founder status was not the primary reason for the choice; multi-asset support (including USDC) is still planned to reduce single-point-of-failure risk.

- **Robinhood Chain's Lighter-powered perps run on a separate, unshared liquidity instance from Lighter's main app.** This was intentional — Robinhood wants a distinct, simplified UX (no manual entry tables, easy margin/contract management) inside the Robinhood Wallet, which required Lighter to build custom changes rather than plugging into shared liquidity.

- **Jurisdictional rollout of regulated products (stock tokens, RWAs) is handled at the wallet/contract level, not the chain level**, since the chain itself is permissionless. Tokenized stocks/ETFs are live in about 120 countries but not yet the US or UK; Kerbrat is optimistic about US access opening as the CLARITY Act, SEC sandbox initiatives, and the SEC's crypto task force progress.

- **Crypto transaction revenue fell ~63%** from about $358M at its Q4 2024 peak to about $134M in Q1 2026, but Kerbrat says Robinhood Chain wasn't built to smooth that volatility — it's one of 11+ Robinhood business lines generating nine-figure-plus revenue, and diversification (banking, credit cards, equities) is the actual hedge against crypto-cycle swings.

- **Looking ahead, Kerbrat frames the chain as "just the beginning"** two weeks post-launch, with continued focus on both memecoins and RWAs ("more of the same"), more product launches, and active engagement with the developer community and regulators to expand access.

## Overview

Laura Shin interviews Johann Kerbrat, VP and General Manager of Robinhood Crypto, about the surprising early trajectory of Robinhood Chain, the company's new permissionless Layer 2 blockchain (built on Arbitrum) intended to power tokenized real-world assets like stocks and ETFs. Despite CEO Vlad Tenev publicly framing RWAs as the chain's core future just before launch, the data two weeks in shows memecoins — led by the CashCat token and amplified by a Pump.fun listing — dominating roughly 85%+ of daily trading volume, while RWAs sit at about 1% (roughly $17 million market cap). Kerbrat defends this as an expected consequence of building an open, permissionless chain, arguing memecoin-driven liquidity and user activity lay groundwork that will eventually benefit RWA adoption. The conversation also covers Robinhood Chain's technical and business choices: using USDG instead of USDC/Tether for margin and lending, building a separate liquidity instance for its Lighter-powered perpetuals product, how jurisdictional compliance is enforced at the wallet/application layer rather than the chain layer, and how crypto revenue volatility fits into Robinhood's broader multi-business-line diversification strategy.

## Implications

The episode captures a recurring pattern in crypto infrastructure launches (echoing Coinbase's Base in 2023): permissionless chains attract speculative, low-friction use cases (memecoins) far faster than the complex, regulator-dependent products (tokenized RWAs) they were ostensibly built for. For Robinhood, this creates a messaging tension between its institutional pitch (Tenev's RWA framing) and on-chain reality, though Kerbrat's response suggests the company sees no need to reconcile that tension — it's treating memecoin liquidity as a bootstrapping mechanism rather than a distraction. The USDG choice signals a broader strategic bet on stablecoin issuer economics (shared yield vs. issuer-captured yield) that could influence how other platforms design their stablecoin plumbing. The separate-liquidity-instance approach with Lighter also suggests Robinhood is prioritizing proprietary UX control over shared liquidity depth, a trade-off other wallet/exchange partnerships in crypto may face. Finally, Kerbrat's comments on jurisdictional rollout suggest continued US regulatory uncertainty (CLARITY Act, SEC sandbox) remains the binding constraint on when tokenized stock trading can expand to Robinhood's largest home market.
