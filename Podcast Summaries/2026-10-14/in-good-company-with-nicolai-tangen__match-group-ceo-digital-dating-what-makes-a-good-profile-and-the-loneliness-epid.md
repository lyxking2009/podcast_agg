## Overview

Nicolai Tangen, CEO of Norges Bank Investment Management, interviews Spencer Rascoff, CEO of Match Group — the company behind Tinder, Hinge and a dozen other dating apps. The conversation covers how the matchmaking algorithm actually works, what separates a profile that generates matches from one that does not, what Gen Z has changed about dating, and why loneliness has become a central part of the story for the industry. Rascoff also discusses what he has learned about building companies and why Norway caught his attention. In Good Company publishes new episodes every Wednesday with a Friday wrap-up.

## Key Points

- The algorithm: how Match Group's apps actually determine which two strangers get shown to each other.
- What makes a good dating profile versus one that fails — concrete advice rather than generic tips.
- What Gen Z has changed about dating behaviour and expectations.
- Why loneliness has become a major part of the dating-app narrative, and what that implies for product design.
- Rascoff's lessons on building and running companies, drawing on a career spanning Zillow and Match Group.
- Why Norway specifically caught his attention as an operator and investor.
- Match Group's portfolio spans Tinder, Hinge and a dozen other apps, so the unit economics vary sharply by brand.
- The episode is part of a weekly cadence: full interviews on Wednesdays, wrap-ups on Fridays.

## Implications

For an owner like NBIM, the interesting question is whether dating apps are a mature cash-generating category or a structurally challenged one. The loneliness framing cuts both ways: it argues the underlying demand is durable and possibly growing, but also implies the product has not delivered on the outcome users actually want — which is where new entrants historically attack.
