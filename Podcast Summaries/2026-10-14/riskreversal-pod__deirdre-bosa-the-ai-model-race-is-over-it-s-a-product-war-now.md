# Deirdre Bosa: The AI Model Race Is Over. It’s a Product War Now

## Overview

Dan Nathan welcomes Deirdre Bosa to the Okay, Computer podcast, timed to her upcoming daily Yahoo Finance show "Deirdre Bosa Live" launching November 9 and airing weekdays 1-2 PM PT. The central claim is that the model race is over and a product war has begun: Bosa argues no one cares about the underlying model anymore, which she illustrates with Meta's product-driven resurgence. The discussion covers Microsoft shifting Copilot toward a model-agnostic approach that routes tasks to the most cost-effective model amid open-source commoditisation, and its Maia 300 custom AI chip as a way to manage supply and spending uncertainty. They also compare emerging personal AI assistants (Muse, Instinct, and OpenAI's "Dot"), and close on China's low-cost high-volume token usage pressuring global pricing.

## Key Points

- The model race is over and it is now a product race — Bosa says "no one cares about the underlying model," using Meta as the proof case.
- Microsoft has moved Copilot toward a model-agnostic architecture that routes tasks to the most cost-effective model, a direct response to open-source commoditisation.
- Microsoft is also launching the Maia 300 custom AI chip to manage supply and spending uncertainty.
- Meta's product-driven resurgence is the counterexample to the assumption that frontier model ownership determines outcomes.
- Enterprise adoption dynamics are changing as model choice becomes a cost lever rather than a capability statement.
- Investor concerns have shifted to AI infrastructure capex and the debt funding it.
- Personal AI assistants — Muse, Instinct, and OpenAI's "Dot" — are compared on utility, branding, and trust/security, with trust framed as a prerequisite for broader adoption.
- Apple and identity layers such as 1Password are seen as potential catalysts for mainstream assistant adoption.
- China's low-cost, high-volume token usage is pressuring global pricing and shaping open-source competition.
- Skepticism about claims that models have "reached the frontier" when a new release claims superiority every few days.

## Implications

If model capability is commoditising, then the defensible positions move to distribution, identity and trust — which is why the episode flags Apple and 1Password rather than labs as the potential catalysts. For investors, the capex-and-debt concern is the mirror image: infrastructure spending justified by a model advantage becomes harder to defend once models are interchangeable, and Chinese token-price pressure compounds that.
