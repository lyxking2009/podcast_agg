# At The Money: Is Technology Price Gouging Consumers?

**Show:** Masters in Business  
**Published:** 2026-10-14  
**Transcript source:** `rss_omny_srt`

## Overview

An At The Money segment of Masters in Business featuring Lindsay Owens, CEO of the Groundwork Collaborative and former economic policy advisor to Senator Elizabeth Warren, discussing her book Gouged: The End of a Fair Price and What That Means for Your Wallet. Owens's argument is that the impulse to overcharge is ancient but the technology is new: pricing has become a highly engineered science, with companies hiring armies of pricing consultants, physicists and acquiring AI companies specifically to extract more from each buyer. The conversation covers how consolidation creates the conditions for personalised pricing, how personalised prices short-circuit comparison shopping, and the data collection — including licence-plate reading — that makes all of it possible.

## Key Points

- Pricing used to be simple: work out your cost, add a margin, put a sticker on it, forget it. Today pricing is a highly engineered science inside large firms.
- Companies hire pricing consultants, physicists and acquire AI companies specifically to get better at "squeezing you and reaching further into your wallet."
- Consolidation is the precondition: 75% of industries are more concentrated now than a generation ago, which Owens calls a petri dish for more aggressive pricing technologies.
- Personalised pricing defeats the mechanism that polices prices. If a company quotes a price selected just for you, you cannot comparison shop, and comparison shopping is how competition keeps prices in check.
- Companies are building or planning capabilities to collect data far beyond what customers assume — including licence-plate reading to infer the income band of the ZIP code a car is registered in, as in a Walmart patent application.
- Owens says she was struck by how bullish companies were on their pricing power during inflation-era earnings calls, and that they expected not just to pass on rising costs but to go for more.
- Her aha moment came from listening to earnings calls rather than reading price data, which is a methodological point about where the evidence lives.
- The macro backdrop the host names alongside pricing technology: the war in Iran, tariffs and higher interest rates.

## Implications

The regulatory reading is that consumer-protection frameworks built around posted prices and comparison shopping are poorly matched to a world of individually quoted prices — existing antitrust and disclosure rules can be satisfied while the competitive mechanism is neutralised. For consumers and for anyone pricing products, the practical warning is that the observed price is increasingly a function of what the seller can infer about you, so the true cost of a purchase is partly the cost of your own data exposure.
