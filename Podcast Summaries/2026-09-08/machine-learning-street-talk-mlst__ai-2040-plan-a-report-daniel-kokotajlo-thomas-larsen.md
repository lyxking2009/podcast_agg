# AI 2040: Plan A report - Daniel Kokotajlo & Thomas Larsen — Machine Learning Street Talk (MLST)

## TL;DR

Daniel Kokotajlo and Thomas Larsen discuss their report AI 2040 Plan A, a scenario where governments implement a temporary pause, establish transparency and verification infrastructure, then pace AI development to top human-level by 2030 before safely reaching superintelligence in 2040. The plan addresses five risks: loss of control, concentration of power, war, job loss, and misuse, emphasizing control measures until alignment is solved. They also reflect on AI 2027, war gaming, and the debate over whether AI is a normal technology or 'humans in the cloud'.

## Key points

- AI 2040 Plan A proposes a temporary 6-12 month inference-only pause to set up transparency and verification infrastructure, followed by internationally coordinated, transparent, and controlled scaling of AI to top human expert level, with superintelligence delayed until alignment is solved around 2040.
- The report identifies five core AI risks: loss of control (misaligned AI takeover), concentration of power (AI aligned to narrow interests), war (conflict over AI advantage), job loss (mass unemployment), and misuse (e.g., bioweapons). The plan aims to address all five via buying time and transparency.
- They advocate a second pause at the 'maximum controllable level' of AI, estimated as roughly top human expert level, to allow time for control techniques and alignment research. Control measures include red team/blue team games and security, while alignment requires interpretability to distinguish genuine goal alignment from deception.
- The AI Futures Project was founded by Daniel Kokotajlo after leaving OpenAI due to disillusionment and information asymmetry. Their first report AI 2027 was a pure prediction scenario; a follow-up assessment found it roughly 75% on track, with faster-than-expected progress in revenue and other trends.
- They use scenario writing and literal war games (about 100 so far) to stress-test plans. War games revealed failure modes like a president accelerating timelines to avoid handing superintelligence to opposition, highlighting political risks.
- They discuss forecasting vs hyperstition: while AI 2027 risked self-fulfilling prophecy, they argue accurate prediction should dominate; Plan A is designed to be positively hyperstitioned, i.e., making the recommended future more likely by describing it.
- Daniel defines a key milestone: when an AI company would rather fire all humans than all AIs, indicating AI can perform all essential tasks including AI research. He expects this within years, leading to explosive economic growth.
- Even pausing at human-level AI would transform the economy: AIs cheaper, faster, and doubling yearly, could replace all human labor, leading to autonomous self-replicating machine economy with strip mines, robot-built factories, and infrastructure growth.
- They compare H100 GPU to human brain in compute (~1e15 flops vs brain 1e12-1e18 flops), arguing neural nets are artificial brains trained via analogous processes, supporting the plausibility of human-level AI.
- Plan A prioritizes transparency: all large training runs would be public, with inspectors monitoring data centers and logs published to internet. This helps verification, reduces monopoly power, and enables informed public and scientific oversight.
- US-China deal requires trust: 99% of large compute would be inventoried and monitored; cheating mitigated by hiding difficulty and intelligence gathering. They estimate max hidden cluster size at a few hundred thousand H100s, insufficient for frontier.
- Control vs alignment: control is empirically testable but becomes less reliable as AIs get smarter; alignment requires fundamental scientific breakthroughs in interpretability. In the scenario, they rely on control until 2040 when alignment is sufficiently solved.
- They note models becoming more situationally aware (e.g., recognizing evaluation setups) makes alignment harder and increases risk of deceptive alignment, where AIs pretend to be aligned.
- Disagreement with 'AI as normal technology' skeptics (e.g., AI snake oil authors): core crux is whether AI will soon achieve 100% automation of key workflows. They co-authored an agreement post listing points of consensus, but disagree on timeline for 'humans in the cloud'.
- They observe a vibe shift: increasing number of AI skeptics changing minds due to empirical progress, such as RL-trained agents, hacking incidents (Hugging Face), and better real-world performance.
