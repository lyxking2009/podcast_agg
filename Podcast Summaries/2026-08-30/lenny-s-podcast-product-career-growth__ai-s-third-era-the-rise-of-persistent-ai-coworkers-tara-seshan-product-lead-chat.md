# AI’s third era: the rise of persistent AI coworkers | Tara Seshan (Product Lead ChatGPT Work) — Lenny's Podcast: Product | Career | Growth

## TL;DR
Tara Seshan, product lead for Codex and ChatGPT Work at OpenAI, discusses the shift from 'rowing' to 'steering' as AI agents take on execution, and why human judgment and ambition become key differentiators. She explains OpenAI's approach of building for model capabilities two to three months out, the rise of persistent AI coworkers, and how the PM role evolves toward elevating ambitions and rapid empirical testing. Seshan also contrasts coding and knowledge work, shares lessons from Sutter Hill on product marketing fit, and emphasizes the importance of writing as thinking.

## Key points
- Tara Seshan leads product for Codex and ChatGPT Work at OpenAI, joining from Stripe where she was one of the first five PMs; she describes OpenAI as 'founders-led' with minimal top-down direction, where every team member acts like a founder and the distance to the market is very thin.
- She argues the PM role is shifting from theoretical long docs to fast empirical experimentation; the core remains forming the sharpest hypothesis and testing it quickly, using the 'eigen question' concept.
- OpenAI culture uses memes like 'Is this maximally accelerated?' and 'Are you mainlining it yet?' to enforce ambition and dogfooding.
- Ambition is the new bottleneck: AI tools expand individual capabilities, so the key PM job is elevating others' ambitions and pushing for higher ceilings and faster timelines.
- Building for AI models requires targeting capabilities two to three months out, not current or year-ahead models; both are equally wrong.
- The future of work shifts from rowing to steering: agents handle execution while humans provide direction, intuition, and accountability, and work becomes collaborative with multiple humans and agents.
- Persistent AI coworkers will work asynchronously in the cloud, with shared context and data access; making cloud agents useful requires infrastructure for third-party system access, not just model intelligence.
- Chat and Work modes in ChatGPT: Chat is conversation/search, Work is Codex under the hood with coding UI removed, aimed at knowledge work; the north star is to eliminate toggles and automatically pick the right harness.
- Codex's recent vibes shift on Twitter was due to the team's consistent user obsession and tight iteration loop, not an internal strategy change; the team practices 'chop wood, carry water' before and after success.
- Traditional role boundaries are blurring; at Stripe and OpenAI, everyone does whatever is needed, with a single DRI accountable for product success; craft is being abstracted by models, causing some mourning of old flow states.
- Tara uses AI to build 'sites' constantly for dashboards, games, trip planning; the /visualize command in Codex creates visualizations from chat data, making presentation easier.
- She distinguishes writing as thinking (never automated, done to clarify ideas) from writing as reporting (happily automated); at OpenAI she writes docs for herself but shares mocks, prototypes, and results instead.
- A key lesson from Sutter Hill: product marketing fit is underrated; test and refine the narrative and positioning before building the product, as Mike Speiser exemplifies.
- Coding tasks are output-verifiable via tests, but knowledge work requires seeing process, reasoning, inputs, and citations; ChatGPT Work is adapting to make the model a collaborator, showing proof of work.
- Tara recommends using ChatGPT Work on mobile for cloud tasks that finish during commutes; her book recommendations include Barbarian Days and Anna Karenina, and she lives by Toni Morrison's motto about work and identity.
