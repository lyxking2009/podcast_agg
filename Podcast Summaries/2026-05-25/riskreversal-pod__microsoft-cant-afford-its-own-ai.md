## Overview

The RiskReversal Pod discusses two main themes: the paradox of AI cost economics at major tech firms, and an interview with Darrell Crate of Easterly Government Properties on why government-focused REITs offer compelling risk-adjusted returns in volatile markets.

**AI Economics segment**: The central irony is that Microsoft — which builds and operates AI infrastructure for much of the world — canceled its own internal AI subscriptions because token-based pricing was too expensive. This reveals that even at the largest scale, AI inference costs are not yet economically sustainable for heavy internal use. GitHub's move to usage-based billing is the direct consequence. The hosts discuss whether this signals that AI cost curves haven't bent sufficiently, or whether it reflects internal accounting at a company that can cross-subsidize.

NVIDIA's momentum is acknowledged with a ceiling caveat: when the big-tech CapEx cycle cools, NVIDIA will feel it first. Meta's simultaneous mass layoffs and NBA-level AI hiring contracts represent a stark talent bifurcation — the industry is splitting between AI specialists (scarce, expensive) and everyone else (redundant, displaced).

**Easterly Government Properties with Darrell Crate**: Easterly specializes in mission-critical government facilities — specialized buildings (data centers, labs, VA hospitals, FBI field offices) that agencies cannot easily replicate or vacate. The DOGE narrative of government downsizing has punished the stock, but Crate argues that DOGE efficiency paradoxically increases demand for private leasing — an efficient government consolidates into purpose-built private facilities rather than maintaining sprawling government-owned real estate. 8% yield and treasury spread make this a compelling income play.

Crate's broader thesis: the post-2008 era of suppressed volatility is definitively over. Demographic trends, accelerating tech disruption, and geopolitical fracturing have created permanently elevated volatility. This argues for hedged equity strategies protecting against 10–30% drawdowns rather than unhedged long equity exposure.

## Implications

- **For AI companies**: Microsoft's inability to afford its own AI at internal scale suggests current pricing models are unsustainable for broad enterprise adoption. Inference costs must fall dramatically or usage patterns shift.
- **For REIT investors**: Government-adjacent REITs with specialized mission-critical facilities may be more resilient to DOGE/efficiency headlines than the market has priced — if the private-leasing-increases-with-efficiency thesis holds.
- **For portfolio construction**: Structural volatility thesis supports allocation to hedged strategies. Consumer bifurcation data supports barbell positioning over traditional mid-market exposure.
- **For AI infrastructure**: NVIDIA's dependence on big-tech CapEx is a single-point-of-failure risk. When hyperscaler spending moderates, NVIDIA's growth story resets.
