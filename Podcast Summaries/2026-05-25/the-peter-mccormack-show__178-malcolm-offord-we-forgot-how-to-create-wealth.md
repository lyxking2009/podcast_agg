## Key Points

- **UK has not created wealth in a generation**: State spending at 45% of GDP (Scotland at 55%), but schools, hospitals, roads, and public services are not measurably better. The core question: what has the extra spending bought?
- **QE protected the rich and locked out the young**: Post-2008 quantitative easing prevented necessary asset price corrections (an estimated 30% house price fall was suppressed). This locked a generation out of asset ownership while appearing to be a progressive policy.
- **Scotland's political crisis**: 2 million out of 4.3 million eligible voters abstained in the 2026 Holyrood election (52% turnout), vs. 84% in the 2014 independence referendum. The "scunnered factor" — Scots are deeply fed up.
- **Democracy fragmenting via digital platforms**: The analog-era filter system (you had to earn your platform) is gone. Everyone has a voice, producing shrillness, single-issue politics, and polarization rather than consensus.
- **Entry-level jobs are critical to social mobility**: A £15/hour minimum wage prices out starter jobs and creates "wage scarring" — young people who don't enter the workforce early have permanently lower lifetime earnings.
- **Arms-length bodies created a democratic deficit**: Scotland has 132 quangos costing £6.6 billion. When infrastructure has a problem, elected officials have no authority — it's all arms-length. This is why voting feels pointless.
- **Adam Smith's Wealth of Nations is 250 years old**: Written by a Scotsman, still outsells Friedman and Keynes combined 3-to-1. Yet Scotland won't talk about wealth creation.
- **Wealth economy vs. welfare economy**: Reform UK platform (Offord's): wealth economy via entrepreneurship, tax cuts, deregulation. Green Party: welfare economy via redistribution. Offord argues only one compounds long-term.
- **Planning system is catastrophically broken**: Examples: planning applications taking 54 weeks instead of 4; reconnecting water and electricity to an industrial site quoted at £500K; sports stadium compliance costs exceeding construction costs.
- **UK vs. US wealth gap is widening**: Americans are dramatically wealthier. Without structural reform, the UK will be massively left behind in 30 years — with consequences for services and defence.

## Overview

Peter McCormack interviews Malcolm Offord, a Scottish Conservative peer who stood as a Reform-aligned candidate in the 2026 Holyrood election and won 5,500 votes in Greenock, contributing to Reform Scotland's 17 MSPs. Offord is a former City of London professional who frames Scotland's — and Britain's — malaise in stark terms: a society that has forgotten how to create wealth.

**Democracy and fragmentation**: Both men observe simultaneous left and right populist surges across Western democracies. Offord argues that the digital age eliminated the filter system that historically moderated extreme views, producing party fragmentation, single-issue politics, and collapsed trust. In Scotland: 90% of votes went to two parties for 175 years; that is now shattering into six effective factions.

**Scotland as a 25-year experiment**: In 1999, Scottish state spending was 43% of GDP; today it's 55%. The policy debate has been dominated by centre-left parties whose only answer to any problem is more spending. 2 million Scots voted with their feet by not voting at all. The SNP (Whisper transcribed as "SMP") has declined from 1.6 million votes in 2014 to under 1 million in 2026.

**QE and wealth inequality**: QE was arguably the most consequential political decision of the past 20 years — made by unelected central bankers. A necessary 30% house price correction was suppressed, protecting existing asset holders (disproportionately older, wealthier) and locking the next generation out. The cruel irony: it was socialist chancellor Gordon Brown who implemented a policy that kept the rich rich.

**Entry-level jobs and minimum wage**: The £15/hour minimum wage prices out market-rate starter jobs (valued around £7.50). Young people miss the work habit, skills building, and structured pay progression. A "wage scar" shows people who don't enter the workforce between 17–22 have permanently lower lifetime earnings. The minimum wage also compresses the entire wage hierarchy, making SME employment unaffordable.

**Planning and arms-length bodies**: A gallery of dysfunctional government processes — 54-week planning timelines, £500K quotes for switching on water and electricity, 132 Scottish quangos costing £6.6B over which elected politicians have zero authority. This is why the Reform pitch resonates.

**Reform UK's path**: 17 MSPs, 383,000 votes, from nowhere. 2 million abstainers are the addressable market. Offord proposes a simple tax reform (three bands at 19/39/44%) as a £2B stimulus on a £72B budget — 3% — opposed fiercely by the IFS, which Offord finds absurd.

## Implications

- **For UK politics**: Reform's rise in Scotland demonstrates that former SNP and Labour voters are now available. The established parties are in structural decline.
- **For monetary policy history**: QE will likely be judged as the single most wealth-concentrating policy of the 21st century — a profound irony given it was implemented by governments claiming to protect ordinary people.
- **For labour policy**: The evidence from rent controls (Edinburgh rent controls raised rents 22%; Argentina lifted rent controls and rents fell) suggests economic counterintuition should be taken seriously before ideology is implemented.
- **For European defence**: The UK's fiscal trajectory directly undermines the capacity to fund defence independently of the US at a moment when that independence matters.
