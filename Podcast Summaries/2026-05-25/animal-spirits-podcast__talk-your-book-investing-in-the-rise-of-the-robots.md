## Key Points

- **Humanoid robotics is the next mega-theme**: Following internet → mobile → cloud → AI, the hosts and guest argue physical AI / humanoid robots represent the next 10–20 year investment cycle.
- **Physical AI unlocks multi-task robots**: Unlike traditional industrial robots that are single-purpose and hard-programmed, AI-enabled robots use Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to understand environments, plan, and perform multiple tasks — analogous to the GPT moment in digital AI.
- **KOID ETF focuses on the supply chain, not the brands**: KraneShares' ETF is equal-weighted and targets the picks-and-shovels — actuator manufacturers, harmonic drives, bearings, precision gearboxes, and sensors — not yet-unproven humanoid brands.
- **China leads hardware, US leads models**: China companies (Unitree, Agility, UBTECH) shipped ~20,000 units in 2025; US companies (Figure AI, Apptronik, 1X) focus on AI model capability. Open-source hardware + US intelligence may converge.
- **Aging demographics create structural demand**: WHO estimates 22% of the world population will be 60+ by 2050. Humanoid robots may arrive just in time to fill acute labor shortages in healthcare, logistics, and manufacturing.
- **Cost curves will follow EV/smartphone precedent**: Actuator costs (the largest component) are expected to fall sharply, potentially reaching $20,000 per unit from $50–100k today.
- **BofA projects 86% CAGR to 10 million units by 2035**: From a base of 20,000 in 2025, with a 300 million robot population possible by 2040.
- **The "GPT moment" for physical AI is still ahead**: When breakthroughs compound, adoption will accelerate rapidly, similar to the jump from GPT-2 to GPT-3.
- **Cultural adoption faster in Asia**: Asian consumers are more culturally comfortable with robots; US adoption likely starts in manufacturing and logistics.
- **ETF is not Mag-7 exposure**: Top 10 holdings ($168B combined market cap) are largely international industrials — Lynas Rare Earths, Rainbow Robotics, Doosan, Regal Rexnord, Teradyne — not household tech names.

## Overview

Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson interview Derek Yan, Senior Investment Strategist at KraneShares, to explore their new ETF **KOID** — the KraneShares Global Humanoid Robotics and Physical AI Index ETF. The conversation is framed around the thesis that after two decades of digital AI, the next decade will see "physical AI" — robots that can see, plan, and act in the physical world — achieve a transformative breakthrough.

Derek explains that traditional industrial robots are hard-coded, single-purpose machines. Physical AI robots use the same large-model training techniques that made ChatGPT possible, applied to 3D environments with action data (the VLA model). Companies like Tesla, Figure AI, Apptronik, and Chinese leaders Unitree and UBTECH are racing to build general-purpose humanoids.

The hosts probe whether this is another failed theme like 3D printing. Derek argues the scale of capital committed — Jensen Huang calling it a $43 trillion TAM, Elon Musk's repeated direction-setting — and structural demographic tailwinds make humanoids different. The KOID ETF captures the industrial "body" suppliers rather than bet on a single winning brand, since it's too early to know who the "iPhone of robots" will be.

## Implications

- **For investors**: The robotics supply chain in Korea, Japan, Europe, and the US may be significantly undervalued if humanoid adoption follows EV-style S-curves. This is an international, industrials-heavy exposure distinct from Nasdaq-100.
- **For the labor market**: Humanoid deployment in manufacturing and logistics may begin within 3–5 years, with home deployment in 5–10 years. Industries with repetitive or dangerous jobs face displacement first.
- **For geopolitics**: China-US bifurcation — hardware manufacturing vs. model intelligence — may produce an open-source convergence that commoditizes the hardware layer and concentrates value in AI software.
- **For demographics policy**: The intersection of declining fertility rates and humanoid deployment timelines suggests robots may become essential for elder care, not just a luxury productivity tool.
