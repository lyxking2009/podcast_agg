## Key Points
- Belski, founder/CEO/CIO of Humilis Investment Strategies, maintains a multi-year bullish view: the S&P 500 will ultimately reach the 8,000s, but a correction is likely first
- Year three of a bull market is historically the weakest of the first five years — 2026 is that year
- The market is shifting from multiple-expansion-driven momentum to an earnings-driven rally, which historically produces lower but still positive returns (high single-digit to low double-digit)
- He is overweight Communication Services, Financials, and Utilities; neutral on Technology; and underweight the Mag 7 as a group
- Financials remain his top conviction sector: deregulation, scale consolidation, and earnings revisions make large and small banks particularly attractive
- Small/mid-cap stocks are his "favorite child" given strong free cash flow fundamentals and expected M&A activity in a deregulating environment
- Belski sees increasing differentiation within Mag 7 as a healthy sign of market broadening — stocks like Amazon and Google diverging from laggards
- Long-term risks include private credit proliferation, off-balance-sheet AI infrastructure debt, and crypto treasury speculation, but he places a "big blowup" at least 10 years away

## Overview
Dan Nathan hosts Brian Belski, founder, CEO and CIO of Humilis Investment Strategies (formerly Chief Investment Strategist at BMO Capital Markets for over 35 years), on the RiskReversal Pod. Belski remains one of Wall Street's most prominent bulls, but acknowledges 2026 will be choppier than 2024–2025 as the market transitions to an earnings-driven regime. He expects a near-term correction before the S&P 500 ultimately runs into the 8,000s, and is rotating his conviction toward financials, small/mid-cap, value, and dividend growth strategies.

## Implications
A near-term market correction is Belski's base case before the next leg higher toward 8,000+, making active sector selection — rather than passive mega-cap concentration — the key to outperformance in 2026. His persistent overweight in financials and small/mid-cap, and underweight in the Mag 7, represents a significant deviation from consensus, with the payoff hinging on earnings breadth expanding beyond AI-linked names. Investors ignoring the long-term risks in private credit and AI infrastructure financing may find themselves in a difficult position when the eventual cycle turns, though Belski views that as a problem for the next decade.
