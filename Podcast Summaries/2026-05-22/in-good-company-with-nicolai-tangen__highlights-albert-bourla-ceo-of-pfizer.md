## Key Points
- Bourla led Pfizer through COVID-19 vaccine development in 8 months, achieving it through unprecedented alignment between government, industry, hospitals, and regulators — a "common enemy" driving extraordinary speed
- After COVID revenue fell from $56B (2022) to $6B, Bourla invested $80+ billion in acquisitions, with Seagen (ADC technology) and MYT Sciences (obesity/GLP-1) as the two largest bets
- ADC (antibody-drug conjugate) technology is described as "extremely precise missiles" — the antibody acts as a GPS to deliver a drug payload directly to cancer cells, enabling highly targeted oncology treatment
- Pfizer's clinical success rate has nearly doubled industry average: 18-19% vs ~10% industry norm; phase two success rates jumped from below-average to 50%+
- China's biotech sector is on track to surpass Western pharma in early-stage drug discovery within 1-2 years — operating at half the cost and three times the speed
- AI is transforming pharma R&D: Paxlovid was the "first machine learning molecule" with major real-world impact; Bourla advocates decentralized AI accountability (each unit owns its transformation) over a centralized AI team
- Bourla argues that culture is more impactful than strategy or leadership — "culture eats strategy for lunch" — and credits a 360-degree, peer-based leadership scoring system for continuous improvement
- His leadership philosophy is rooted in optimism from his mother, a Holocaust survivor: "There is nothing great that has happened in humanity without an optimist behind it"

## Overview
In this highlights episode of In Good Company, Norges Bank CEO Nicolai Tangen interviews Albert Bourla, Chairman and CEO of Pfizer, covering three decades at the company and seven years as CEO. Bourla reflects on the COVID vaccine sprint, the challenge of rebuilding momentum after revenues crashed post-pandemic, and Pfizer's strategic pivot toward oncology (via Seagen's ADC technology) and obesity (via MYT Sciences).

The conversation spans AI's transformative potential in pharma, the alarming rise of Chinese biotech, and Bourla's personal leadership philosophy — including a moving account of his mother's Holocaust survival and how it shaped his optimism.

## Implications
Bourla's framing of China's biotech ascent — "half the cost, three times the speed" — is a stark warning for the Western pharmaceutical industry. The comparison to China's electric vehicles, solar, and battery dominance suggests a structural competitive shift is underway, not a cyclical one. His prescription (AI-driven transformation within five years, plus regulatory and pricing reform in the US) implies that pharma companies that delay AI integration face existential risk. For investors, the pivot to ADCs and GLP-1 obesity drugs also signals where Pfizer is placing its highest-conviction long-term bets.
