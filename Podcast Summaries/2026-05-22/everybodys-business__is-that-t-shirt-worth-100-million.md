## Key Points
- Everlane, the millennial direct-to-consumer fashion brand built on "radical transparency," was acquired by Shein for approximately $100 million, creating a stark irony given Shein's opaque, fast-fashion supply chain
- Shein is valued at $100 billion and is actively buying Western brands to better understand Western consumer markets and strengthen its supply chain knowledge ahead of a potential IPO
- Everlane failed partly because venture-capital growth models don't apply well to product companies — building consumer trust and brand takes decades, not years
- The WNBA's new collective bargaining agreement delivered historic pay increases: the minimum salary is now higher than the average salary was under the previous contract, with a floor of $270,000
- The WNBA total team salary cap rose from $1.5 million to $7 million, and for the first time players secured a revenue share (20%, up from zero)
- Nobel Prize-winning economist Claudia Goldin consulted pro bono for the WNBA Players Association, and her data-driven research was credited as pivotal in securing the revenue-sharing agreement
- Clara Wusai, owner of the New York Liberty and co-owner with Joseph Tsai (Alibaba co-founder), is credited with transforming how the league treats players as professionals — from charter flights to expanded training staffs — setting standards others had to follow
- FIFA World Cup 2026 ticket prices sparked backlash, with some group-stage seats over $4,000; a developer named Luke built a viral website, Seat Sidekick, to help fans track and alert on price drops through FIFA's own dynamic-pricing marketplace

## Overview
This episode of Everybody's Business covers three intersecting stories about unexpected shifts in consumer culture and business. Fashion reporter Lauren Sherman breaks down Shein's acquisition of Everlane — a brand that built its identity on ethical sourcing and price transparency — and explains why Shein is now hunting Western brands to inform its supply chain and support a future IPO. The episode also examines the WNBA's landmark new labor deal, exploring how treating women's sports as a serious business drove unprecedented salary growth and a historic revenue-share arrangement. The show closes with a "Super Savers" segment on a developer who built a tool to decode FIFA's confusing World Cup ticket marketplace.

## Implications
Shein's acquisition of Everlane illustrates how fast-fashion conglomerates are using Western brand purchases not for their revenue, but for supply-chain intelligence and market legitimacy ahead of capital markets events. The WNBA pay deal demonstrates that professional investment in women's sports — training staff, travel, facilities — creates a positive feedback loop with fans that translates directly into negotiating power for players. FIFA's dynamic-pricing experiment and the fan backlash it generated suggest that sports leagues risk alienating core fans when pricing models are borrowed from entertainment rather than designed around accessibility.
