## Key Points
- Psychedelics are approaching mainstream psychiatry use, with several companies having completed or nearing readout from pivotal Phase 3 trials
- Depression and anxiety represent among the largest areas of unmet need in medicine — traditional antidepressants provide symptom relief but lack transformative impact; psychedelics offer rapid-onset, durable remission from single or short-course administrations
- The FDA has published draft guidance establishing a clear regulatory pathway for late-stage psychedelic drug development
- A Presidential executive order directs the FDA to issue Commissioner's National Priority Vouchers to breakthrough-designated programs, potentially compressing approval review timelines to two months post-filing
- The main clinical trial challenge is functional unblinding — participants typically recognize psychedelic effects versus placebo; companies are countering this through experienced trial sites and selection of patients without prior psychedelic exposure
- Scalability of delivery is the critical commercial concern: psychedelic treatments require hours-long in-person supervision
- RBC surveyed approximately 7,000 Spravato treatment centers and found existing capacity and enthusiasm among clinics to administer psychedelics
- An estimated 1,000 high-volume treatment centers are expected to serve as initial prescriber sites in year one of commercialization

## Overview
RBC Capital Markets analysts Brian Abrahams (Head of Global Healthcare Research) and Leonid Timashev (Biotechnology Analyst) discuss whether psychedelic therapies are finally reaching a breakthrough moment in mainstream psychiatry. The episode covers the favorable regulatory environment shaped by new FDA guidance and a Presidential executive order, the clinical trial design challenges unique to psychedelic drugs (functional unblinding, expectation bias), and why RBC's own survey of treatment centers suggests the healthcare system has more capacity to deliver psychedelic therapies than widely assumed.

## Implications
Regulatory clarity and compressible approval timelines — combined with existing clinical infrastructure — have substantially de-risked the psychedelic therapeutics sector compared to even 12 months ago. The key remaining variable is Phase 3 trial data: multiple readouts are expected in 2025–2026, and any positive results could rapidly shift investor and prescriber sentiment. The scalability bottleneck is less severe than feared, but the hours-long supervised session model will still require significant capacity planning, particularly for high-volume indications like treatment-resistant depression.
