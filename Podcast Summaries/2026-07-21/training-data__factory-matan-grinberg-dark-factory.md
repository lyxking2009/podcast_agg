# Factory's Matan Grinberg: The Coming 'Dark Factory' Where Software Builds Itself

**Show:** Training Data
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Guest:** Matan Grinberg, Co-founder and CEO of Factory
**Duration:** 51 min

## Key Points

- Factory started building fully autonomous coding agents in April 2023, two years before enterprises were ready — they spent two years 'in the desert' before market adoption caught up.
- Factory deploys 'Droids' — purpose-built autonomous agents for different tasks in the software development lifecycle, bucking the single-agent approach.
- The 'Dark Factory' concept: a future state where software essentially builds itself with minimal human intervention.
- 'Agent' is still synonymous with unreliable, stochastic, demoware, vaporware — Factory's mission is to build systems valuable to enterprise engineers today, not just cool demos.
- 'If you're unable to build a product that people are actually using with these incredible models, then chances are fine tuning and training will not save you.' — product-market fit matters more than model optimization.
- Factory's competitive advantage vs. Claude Code, Cognition, and others is the multi-agent 'Droid' architecture with purpose-built agents for different dev lifecycle stages.
- Customer obsession is an input metric — the output metric is whether customers themselves become obsessed with your product.
- The analogy: 'If you're a coach of a basketball team, you don't want to tell your players to make sure to sweat. Score points.'

## Overview

Sonya Huang and Lauren Reeder interview Matan Grinberg, co-founder and CEO of Factory, on Sequoia's Training Data podcast. Factory builds 'Droids' — autonomous agents purpose-built for different stages of the software development lifecycle. Grinberg shares the company's origin story (starting in April 2023 when enterprises weren't ready), the 'Dark Factory' vision of self-building software, and why product-market fit matters more than model fine-tuning. The conversation covers Factory's multi-agent architecture, competitive positioning against Claude Code and Cognition, and the philosophy of building systems that create obsessed customers rather than just obsessing over customers.

## Implications

The autonomous coding agent market is consolidating around different architectural approaches — Factory's multi-agent 'Droid' model vs. single-agent approaches. The 'Dark Factory' thesis suggests software development will be fundamentally transformed within a few years. Enterprise adoption of coding agents is accelerating after a two-year warmup period. The distinction between input metrics (customer obsession) and output metrics (customer obsession WITH your product) is a useful framework for evaluating AI startups.
