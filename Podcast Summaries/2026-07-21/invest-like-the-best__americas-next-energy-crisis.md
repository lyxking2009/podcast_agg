# America's Next Energy Crisis

**Show:** Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Guest:** Matthew Smith, Founder and CIO of Chronometer Partners
**Duration:** 55 min

## Key Points

- Starting in 2028, AI data centers and LNG exports will create a structural natural gas shortage in the US — demand will exceed what the country can produce and deliver.
- The US could exhaust its working natural gas storage by 2030, creating a true energy crisis with 'unbounded and convex' upside risk to prices.
- This crisis was set in motion long before AI — LNG export infrastructure buildout and declining shale productivity laid the groundwork.
- The US cannot simply turn off exports — long-term contracts, trade relationships, and geopolitical commitments make this impractical.
- The fundamental problem is timing, not supply — gas exists in the ground but infrastructure bottlenecks cap how fast new supply reaches market.
- Building more large-scale nuclear reactors is the only durable long-term solution, but SMRs (small modular reactors) remain unproven at scale.
- Utility-scale solar is positioned for a windfall as natural gas prices rise, though solar cannot provide baseload power alone.
- Hyperscalers (Google, Microsoft, Amazon) are exposed to gas price risk through their data center power purchase agreements.

## Overview

Patrick O'Shaughnessy interviews energy veteran Matthew Smith about his contrarian thesis that the US faces a natural gas crisis starting in 2028. Smith has modeled nearly every gas well, pipeline, and processing asset in the country and argues that combined demand from AI compute infrastructure and LNG exports will overwhelm supply capacity. He walks through the infrastructure bottlenecks that prevent rapid supply expansion, explains why the die was cast before AI arrived, and makes the case that only large-scale nuclear can provide a durable solution. The conversation covers winners among gas producers, risks to hyperscalers, the SMR question, and implications for global energy markets.

## Implications

Investors should consider overweighting natural gas producers, pipeline operators, and turbine manufacturers. Hyperscaler valuations may not fully price in energy cost risk. Nuclear energy plays become increasingly relevant. The thesis has a specific timeline (2028 onset) that creates actionable positioning.
