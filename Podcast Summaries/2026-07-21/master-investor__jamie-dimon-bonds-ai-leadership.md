# Jamie Dimon: Why I Won't Buy Bonds, AI's Future & Leadership Lessons

**Show:** The Master Investor Podcast with Wilfred Frost
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Guest:** Jamie Dimon, Chairman and CEO of JPMorgan Chase
**Duration:** 60 min

## Key Points

- Dimon would NOT be a buyer of long-dated government bonds — he draws parallels with the inflationary spiral of the 1970s and warns investors are underestimating risks from record deficits and rising rates.
- On AI: 'Will it pay off? Probably. Will it pay off the way you expect, and in the timetable you expect? Definitely not.' He would not buy the broad equity market at current levels.
- The world economy is more resilient to oil price increases than in the past — Dimon believes Trump should pursue Middle East goals regardless of economic effects.
- The 1950-2020 period was an 'unusually prosperous' era driven by favorable demographics, falling interest rates, and globalization — the next 30-40 years will be harder.
- Ageing populations, falling birth rates, rising dependency ratios, and public debt are creating a fiscal crisis governments don't want to admit.
- Central banks may struggle to keep inflation under control as political short-termism blocks necessary reforms.
- Democracy itself may block the long-term planning countries need — politicians cannot win elections by telling voters the truth.
- Dimon reflects on 20 years at JPMorgan (bank increased in value eightfold), the Bear Stearns acquisition, two life-threatening health crises, and the loneliness of leadership.

## Overview

Wilfred Frost sits down with Jamie Dimon for a wide-ranging conversation recorded in Washington DC. Dimon delivers his most bearish bond call in years, warning that record government deficits and rising rates make long-dated treasuries unattractive. He's cautious on AI timelines and broad equity valuations. The conversation shifts to macro themes: the end of the unusually prosperous 1950-2020 era, demographic headwinds, fiscal crises, and whether democracy can handle long-term planning. Dimon then opens up about leadership — the Bear Stearns acquisition during the financial crisis, two near-death health experiences, fighting bureaucracy, the importance of character over credentials, and why insecure CEOs make poor leaders.

## Implications

The bond market may be mispricing inflation risk from fiscal deficits. AI investment returns may take longer than consensus expects. The demographic/fiscal headwind thesis has implications for all asset classes. Dimon's leadership insights are directly applicable to portfolio company management.
