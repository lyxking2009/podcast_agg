# Running Out of Matter with Michelle Thaller

**Show:** StarTalk Radio
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Guest:** Michelle Thaller, Astrophysicist
**Duration:** 61 min

## Key Points

- The episode opens with an urgent conversation about NASA funding cuts and the exodus of top scientists and engineers leaving for overseas programs.
- Fan questions cover: what happens to a photon when absorbed and reemitted, the heat death of the universe, and why temperature increases moving away from the sun's surface.
- Michelle Thaller explains how LIGO detected gravitational waves from two black holes colliding 1.4 billion light years away.
- The discussion covers neutron stars, fast radio bursts, and the first generation of stars JWST is hunting for.
- The mysterious 'little red dots' observed by JWST may rewrite understanding of how the earliest stars formed.
- Thaller closes with an inspiring message for young girls considering science: 'you don't need a special kind of genius, you just need to be unable to get it out of your head.'

## Overview

Neil deGrasse Tyson and co-host Chuck Nice tackle fan questions about photons, heat death, gravitational waves, neutron stars and more with astrophysicist Michelle Thaller. The episode opens with a serious discussion about NASA funding cuts and scientists leaving for overseas programs. Fan questions drive the science conversation: photon absorption and reemission, the heat death of the universe, solar corona temperature, gravitational wave detection via LIGO, neutron stars, fast radio bursts, and JWST's hunt for first-generation stars. Thaller explains how the 'little red dots' JWST has observed may fundamentally change our understanding of early star formation.

## Implications

NASA funding cuts are creating a brain drain that threatens US leadership in space science. JWST continues to produce paradigm-shifting observations about early universe star formation. Public engagement with science (through shows like StarTalk) remains important for inspiring the next generation of scientists.
