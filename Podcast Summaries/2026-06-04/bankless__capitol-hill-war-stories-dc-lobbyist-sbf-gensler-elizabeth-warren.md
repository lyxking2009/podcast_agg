## Key Points

- **Ron Hammond's origin story**: Hammond began his Capitol Hill career at 24 working for Rep. Warren Davidson on the Token Taxonomy Act — the first bipartisan crypto regulatory bill — in an era when crypto had "no left versus right" policy alignment.
- **SBF's lobbying influence**: Sam Bankman-Fried's power in DC stemmed from funding events, not genuine industry support. When FTX pushed a CFTC exemption that would have harmed DeFi, Coinbase and other exchanges refused to support it — an early show of industry unity against bad-faith actors.
- **Elizabeth Warren's playbook**: Warren's anti-crypto campaign relied heavily on proxy groups and pre-written letters from outside parties. Mark Cohodes' letter against Silvergate is cited as an example: a short-seller whose letter was designed to profit from regulatory action.
- **Gensler's adversarial strategy**: The SEC chair's approach was deliberately political — timing the Coinbase lawsuit to drop during congressional testimony as a public signal rather than a purely legal move.
- **Crypto lobby growth**: From three lobbyists in 2016 to approximately 200 today. The industry has defeated banking interests multiple times and maintains bipartisan support in Congress.
- **Clarity Act odds**: Hammond estimates only 45% probability for the Clarity Act's passage — sober given the current political environment despite crypto's lobbying strength.
- **Next frontier**: Post-Clarity legislative priorities include mark-to-market tax treatment, de minimis exemptions, DeFi regulation, and self-custody protections.

## Overview

Ron Hammond, Head of Policy and Advocacy at Wintermute (formerly of the Blockchain Association and Rep. Warren Davidson's office), joins David Hoffman for a deep retrospective on a decade of crypto lobbying in Washington. Hammond traces the arc from crypto's early days as an untouched policy wilderness — when he was drafting the Token Taxonomy Act at 24 — through the FTX collapse and its political fallout, to today's professionalized lobbying apparatus of ~200 advocates. The episode is a frank insider account of how the sausage gets made: which politicians used crypto as a fundraising prop, how SBF bought influence without earning it, how Elizabeth Warren activated external proxies against crypto-friendly banks, and how Gary Gensler weaponized timing as a political tool. Hammond closes with a measured assessment of the Clarity Act's prospects and what the "new frontier" of DeFi, tax, and self-custody legislation will look like.

## Implications

This episode documents crypto's transformation from a punchline to a power center in Washington over roughly a decade. Hammond's inside account reveals that the industry's current strength is built on real legislative wins (defeating banking interests, passing stablecoin legislation) rather than just campaign donations. The 45% Clarity Act estimate is a useful calibration point — even a well-funded, professionally organized lobby faces serious structural barriers in a divided Congress. The focus on DeFi regulation and self-custody as "next frontier" issues signals that the next wave of policy battles will be more technically complex and harder to frame for mainstream legislators than earlier market structure debates.
