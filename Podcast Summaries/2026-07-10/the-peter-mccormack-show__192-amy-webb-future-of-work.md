# #192 - Amy Webb - The Future of Work, AI & Human Labor

**Show:** The Peter McCormack Show
**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Guest(s):** Amy Webb (futurist, founder of Future Today Strategy Group), Peter McCormack (host)

## Key Points

- AI is not just another technology shift — it's a civilisation-level reordering of work, power, wealth, biology and society
- The real AI question isn't which jobs disappear, but which people become vulnerable — the concept of 'thinkers vs think-nots'
- Human capital may stop being the main driver of productivity and wealth as AI capabilities expand
- GDP and unemployment could rise simultaneously — the economy may thrive while having less use for human labour
- China has a coherent AI strategy while Western governments are failing to plan for the transition
- The concept of 'contribution credit' — AI companies may owe people for the data and knowledge they absorbed during training
- UBI (Universal Basic Income) has limits as a solution — it doesn't address meaning, purpose, or social participation
- Synthetic biology and longevity technologies will intersect with AI to create unprecedented changes in human capability
- Current structures of regulation, politics and economics no longer make sense for the world we are entering
- The 'Gen T' generation is growing up in a world where AI is ubiquitous, fundamentally shaping their expectations and capabilities

## Overview

Amy Webb joins Peter McCormack to discuss why AI is a civilisation-level reordering, not just a technology shift. The conversation covers 'thinkers vs think-nots,' the vulnerability of human capital, the possibility that GDP and unemployment could rise simultaneously, China's coherent AI strategy vs Western governments' failure to plan, the concept of 'contribution credit' for AI training data, the limits of UBI, and how synthetic biology and longevity technologies will intersect with AI. Webb argues that current regulatory, political, and economic structures are inadequate for the world we're entering.

## Implications

The transition to an AI-driven economy requires new frameworks for regulation, compensation, and social participation. The concept of 'contribution credit' could become a major policy debate as AI companies monetize training data. Governments that fail to plan for this transition risk social instability.
