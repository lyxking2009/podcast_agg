# The Korean Levered ETFs Shaking Markets All Around the World

**Show:** Odd Lots
**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Guest(s):** Bloomberg hosts Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway

## Key Points

- Korean retail investors have become major players in leveraged ETF markets, creating outsized impacts on global financial markets
- The phenomenon of 'donghak ants' (Korean retail traders) using leveraged products has reached unprecedented scale
- These leveraged ETF flows are creating feedback loops that amplify market movements beyond what fundamentals would suggest
- The discussion covers how Korean retail trading culture differs from Western approaches, with higher risk tolerance
- Regulators in both Korea and the US are paying attention to the systemic risks these flows create
- The episode examines specific examples of how Korean leveraged ETF purchases have moved individual stocks and sectors
- The hosts explore whether this represents a new form of market microstructure that traditional models don't account for

## Overview

A deep dive into one of the most unusual market phenomena of 2026: Korean retail investors' massive participation in leveraged ETF markets and the ripple effects this creates across global financial markets. The hosts explain how 'donghak ants' — Korean retail traders — have become some of the most active participants in leveraged products, creating feedback loops that amplify market moves. The episode covers the cultural context, the mechanics of how these flows work, and the regulatory concerns they raise.

## Implications

The globalization of retail trading means that market microstructure is being reshaped by participants that traditional models don't account for. Korean leveraged ETF flows could become a persistent source of volatility that institutional investors need to monitor.
