# Adam Brown – A deep but accessible introduction to general relativity

**Show:** Dwarkesh Podcast
**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Guest(s):** Adam Brown (theoretical physicist, Stanford; lead of Blueshift at Google DeepMind)

## Key Points

- At the core of general relativity, Einstein was trying to understand why the mass that resists acceleration and the mass that gravity pulls on are exactly the same — this coincidence is the foundation of the theory
- Einstein's 'happiest thought' was the realization that gravity and acceleration are equivalent (the equivalence principle), which led to the geometric interpretation of gravity
- Black holes challenge special relativity — if they weren't truly black, you could create a perpetual motion machine using them
- The observations of an infalling observer and a distant bystander to a black hole would be radically different, illustrating the relativity of experience
- Black holes could be the ultimate power plants — the energy available from matter falling into a black hole far exceeds nuclear energy
- Adam Brown leads Blueshift at Google DeepMind, working on cracking science and reasoning with AI systems
- The conversation explores how close we are to AIs that could rediscover general relativity from scratch
- The episode bridges pure physics with AI capabilities, suggesting that conceptual breakthroughs in science may be the hardest test for AI systems

## Overview

Adam Brown returns to the Dwarkesh Podcast to deliver an accessible introduction to general relativity. He starts with Einstein's core insight — the equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass — and traces the path to the geometric theory of gravity. The conversation then moves to black holes, exploring why they're the most extreme objects in the universe and the ultimate energy sources. The episode concludes with a discussion of Brown's work leading Blueshift at Google DeepMind and the question of whether AI could rediscover these fundamental insights from scratch.

## Implications

The intersection of theoretical physics and AI research suggests that the next frontier for AI isn't just pattern recognition but conceptual reasoning at the level of Einstein-level breakthroughs. If AI systems can rediscover general relativity, it would represent a fundamental shift in what AI can do.
