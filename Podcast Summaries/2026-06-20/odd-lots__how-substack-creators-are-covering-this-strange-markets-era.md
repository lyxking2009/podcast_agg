# How Substack Creators Are Covering This Strange Markets Era

**Show:** Odd Lots  
**Guests:** James Van Zeilen (Satrini Research), Jasmine Sun (Substack author), Sam Ro (TKer newsletter), Joe Weisenthal & Tracy Alloway (hosts)  
**Date:** 2026-06-20  
**Transcript source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points

- James Van Zeilen (author of the viral AI jobs doom scenario) has received credible death threats but stands by his analysis of rapid AI-driven job displacement
- Jasmine Sun argues most AI media is 'by AI people for other AI people' — her Substack bridges the gap to normal people asking about AI and politics, parenting, education, affordability
- Sam Ro's TKer newsletter ('stocks usually go up') faces the challenge of AI being able to replicate his writing style — his sister built a 'Sam robot' that sounded like him
- Van Zeilen predicts investment research won't exist in its current form — the edge is being in the real world (sending analysts to the Strait of Hormuz), not producing desk research models can replicate
- China's AI culture is still in the 'academic era' — collaborative, pro-open source, unconcerned with safety/alignment debates. The government handles societal implications while companies focus on adoption
- One OpenAI researcher is allocated more compute than an entire Chinese lab due to chip controls
- Van Zeilen's 'spicy take': the next Chinese DeepSeek moment won't be about a model — it will be about hardware (memory/DRAM), as bottlenecks are 'made to be widened'
- Amazon will employ twice as many robots as humans this year; factory automation is experiencing a 'huge inflection'
- Jasmine visited Unitree's office in China and saw humanoid robots actively deployed in a 24/7 pharmacy, taking items off shelves for delivery drivers

## Overview

A live panel from Odd Lots' New York show featuring three Substack/newsletter writers discussing how they cover the AI era for non-technical audiences. The conversation spans the threat of AI replacing financial media, China's radically different AI culture, the robotics boom happening outside public attention, and what human journalists can still offer that AI cannot — secrets, gossip, and being physically present at critical moments.

## Implications

Financial media faces existential disruption from AI summarization. The China-US AI gap is narrowing on hardware despite chip controls. Factory automation is the quiet revolution that will reshape labor markets before consumer-facing AI does.
