# Friday Wrap-Up: Lessons from a Former Boss and the Decline of Shareholder Rights


## Key Points

- Weekly Friday Wrap-Up recaps Nicolai Tangen's conversation with John Armitage, co-founder and CIO of Egerton Capital and one of the most important people in Tangen's investing life.
- What Armitage taught Tangen about analysing companies - his former boss and five-year employer at Egerton.
- Why the best investors tend to be humble, and why Armitage calls permanent capital a trap.
- NBIM's recent view on the decline of shareholder rights, and why Tangen says it matters for long-term investors like the fund.
- Tangen's personal note: why we should all say thank you more often and the morning gratitude ritual he follows.

## Overview

Hosted by Marthe Skaar with Nicolai Tangen, this short Friday debrief distils the week's full interview (episode 290, with John Armitage) into its takeaways: company analysis discipline, investor humility, and the risks of permanent capital. It then turns to NBIM's stance on the erosion of shareholder rights as a governance concern for long-horizon owners, and closes on Tangen's gratitude practice.


## Implications

The governance point matters for anyone holding equities long term: weakened shareholder rights reduce the leverage owners have over management, exactly the constituency NBIM represents. The humility and 'permanent capital is a trap' themes are a useful counterweight to conviction-driven concentrated bets.
