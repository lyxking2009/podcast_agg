# The NFL Enters Its Flag Era


## Key Points

- Bloomberg Businessweek's cover package on the business of women's sports sits at the center of the episode: girls' high-school flag football participation rose 550% between 2021 and 2026.
- The NFL staged a huge U.S. Girls High School Flag Football tournament at the 400-acre Grand Park Sports Campus outside Indianapolis - 350 teams, age brackets from six-year-olds to high-school seniors, an international bracket (teams from as far as New Zealand), with marquee games on ABC and ESPN.
- Flag football is Olympic-bound and draws real money; the NFL sees it as a way to capture the high-personality, player-centric fandom that the WNBA tapped, at a moment when young Americans are less likely to self-identify as sports fans.
- Fandom is shifting from team-based to player-based; kids have more screens and less 'familial obligation' to a family team, so sports products must be marketed differently.
- Underrated story: Google's ~$4.3bn, 20-year power purchase with Constellation Energy (owner of the rebranded Three Mile Island) - evidence that energy, not chips, is AI's choke point.
- Data-center demand is pushing U.S. power prices up more than 16% year-over-year, fuelling data-center unpopularity; nuclear is cleaner but costlier, and heating oil is up over 50%.
- AI-powered dynamic and personalized pricing is edging toward groceries - technically 'price discrimination,' which is a third rail with consumers.

## Overview

Stacey Bannock-Smith, Max Chafkin and Brad Stone anchor a Bloomberg Businessweek news show. Amanda Mull joins to unpack her reporting from an Indianapolis girls' flag football championship, which becomes a window into how the NFL is trying to buy into the fastest-growing and most personality-driven corner of sports. The show also covers Google's 20-year nuclear power deal with Constellation as the AI energy bottleneck, and closes on the ethics and consumer backlash around AI-driven personalized pricing.


## Implications

For investors, the flag-football story is a template for how legacy leagues defend cultural relevance by seeding new, player-led formats. The Google-Constellation deal and rising power bills show the AI build-out's real constraint is electricity - and that its costs land on ordinary consumers, which is why data centers are politically unpopular. AI pricing in retail will keep colliding with consumer and regulatory resistance.
