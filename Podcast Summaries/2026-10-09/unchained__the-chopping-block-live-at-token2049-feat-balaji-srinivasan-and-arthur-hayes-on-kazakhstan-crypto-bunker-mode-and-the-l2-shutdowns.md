# The Chopping Block: Live at TOKEN2049 feat. Balaji Srinivasan & Arthur Hayes on Kazakhstan, Crypto Bunker Mode, and the L2 Shutdowns


## Key Points

- Live from TOKEN2049 in Singapore on the BingX Stage, Haseeb Qureshi, Tom Schmidt and Tarun Chitra are joined by Balaji Srinivasan and Arthur Hayes.
- Balaji explains why Network School moved its headquarters to Kazakhstan - which he calls 'Singapore with space to build' - and why most countries are small enough to deal with.
- OpenAI's release of ~700 AI-written math papers has Justin Drake calling for crypto 'bunker mode': a mass migration of funds to more secure wallets. The hosts urge calm rather than panic.
- Balaji argues there are hard mathematical bounds on what AI can do; Tarun discusses quantum money; Balaji describes blockchains that spend their own token budget trying to hack themselves; Arthur pitches the Flop Network as 'food for AI agents.'
- Blast and Abstract shut down, sharpening the question of whether crypto overbuilt its L2s - Tarun calls them 'Chinese ghost cities that can at least be demolished,' and Haseeb tallies the middlemen that make running one so expensive.
- Arthur notes TOKEN2049's Marquee venue, fully booked all week last year at half-a-million-Singapore-dollar minimum spends, had not had a single booking this year.

## Overview

A live TOKEN2049 panel from the Unchained/Chopping Block crew with Balaji Srinivasan and Arthur Hayes. Topics range from Network School's move to Kazakhstan and 'how to shop for a country,' to crypto's response to OpenAI's math-paper drop (bunker mode), the theoretical limits of AI and quantum money, and the wave of L2 shutdowns (Blast, Abstract) as evidence of overbuilding. The mood is skeptical about AI's crypto threat narrative and bearish on the L2 business model.


## Implications

For crypto builders, the L2 shutdowns are a warning that the rollup business model needs real differentiated demand, not just cheap blockspace. The 'bunker mode' discussion - prompted by AI's math advances and worries about cryptographic cracking - signals growing attention to post-quantum wallet migration, even if the hosts counsel against panic.
