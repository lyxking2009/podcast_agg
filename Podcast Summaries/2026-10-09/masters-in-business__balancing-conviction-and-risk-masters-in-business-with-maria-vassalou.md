# Balancing Conviction and Risk: Masters in Business with Maria Vassalou


## Key Points

- Career arc: PhD in financial economics (London Business School), professor of finance at Columbia 1995-2006, then head of quant at Soros, PM at SAC Capital, Citadel consultant, head of asset allocation at McKinsey, PM at Perella Weinberg, and co-CIO at Goldman Sachs Asset Management.
- Since 2024 she heads the PICTET Research Institute - an internal think tank of PhDs at the ~$1tn firm studying geopolitics, macro and portfolio implementation, deliberately avoiding short-term market calls.
- Her 2004 Journal of Finance paper 'Default Risk and Equity Returns' argues the small-cap and value premiums are largely a default-risk (Merton-model) effect that strengthens when economy-wide default risk rises and dilutes when it falls.
- On the academic-to-industry gap, her one-word answer is 'ego' - plus the discipline that public markets hand you a P&L scorecard every day, where losses hurt about twice as much as gains feel good (Kahneman-Tversky loss aversion).
- Risk management is integrated into portfolio construction: express a macro view through many different trades so no single trade dominates, protect the downside, and apply volatility targeting at the portfolio level.
- On China: decades of industrial policy with forced knowledge transfer and joint ventures, plus strategic accumulation of key resources and processing capacity, mean it controls many inputs needed to produce future technologies even if it trails the U.S. on innovation.

## Overview

Barry Ritholtz interviews Dr. Maria Vassalou about a career spanning academia, hedge funds and asset management, and about how she runs research at PICTET's new institute. The conversation moves from her default-risk explanation of factor premiums to the practicalities of conviction versus risk management - small, diversified expressions of a view plus volatility targeting - and ends on geopolitics, where she argues China's strategic control of critical resources is the defining long-term risk factor.


## Implications

For allocators, Vassalou's framework says conviction should be expressed as diversified trades with downside protection baked into construction, not as concentrated bets. Her default-risk read of the size/value premiums is a reminder that factor returns are regime-dependent. Her China analysis frames resource and processing control - not headline innovation metrics - as the real strategic lever.
