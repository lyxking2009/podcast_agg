# How AI Is Upending the World of Mathematics


## Key Points

- Guest Justin Solomon is Associate Dean of Engineering Education at MIT; the conversation opens on why AI is causing existential angst among mathematicians whose life's work a model can now tackle in a weekend.
- Math is the AI labs' 'holy grail' for proving reasoning ability and is tied to AGI claims - and the recent Navier-Stokes news cycle (a counterexample showing blow-up under a 'very crazy spoon') is a case study.
- AI is 'sort of doing it all and sort of doing nothing': current models are quite bad at proof-checking, so the real breakthrough is pairing an LLM with a separate verifier.
- Lean - a programming language encoding mathematical axioms - lets AI-generated proofs be machine-checked; some modern AI-generated proofs run to hundreds of thousands of lines of Lean code that no human could feasibly verify by hand.
- Proof should be understood as building from agreed simple logic principles to complex facts; math is 'a really social exercise,' not the lone-genius 'great man' image of the movies.
- Applied versus pure math is fuzzier than it sounds: Solomon worked at Pixar applying partial differential equations to cloth, smoke and rigid-body simulation, and notes pure-seeming areas (e.g. number theory) later produced modern cryptography.
- The pedagogy question: if a model can verify a proof, what are students actually learning in the process - and does knowing your times tables still matter (a nod to Jensen Huang's remark)?

## Overview

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway explore how AI is reshaping mathematics, from headline 'AI solved X' claims to the practical mechanics of proof. MIT's Justin Solomon explains the applied/pure divide, why current models fail at proof-checking and how verifiers like Lean changed things, and what a proof fundamentally is. The episode closes on teaching: what skills survive when a model can verify results a student no longer derives by hand.


## Implications

The AI-plus-verifier pattern - unreliable generator paired with a reliable checker - is a general template beyond math. For education, the episode raises the question of what math pedagogy is for if verification is automated; for research, it points to a future where humans set direction and machines grind proofs.
