# Jeremy Maletz


## Key Points

- Guest Jeremy Maletz heads prediction markets (a.k.a. 'goat markets') at Susquehanna International Group; the business grew out of SIG's macro and election trading rather than sports betting.
- Sports is more than half of the prediction-markets book, but non-sports is growing faster; sports has a head start because regulated sports betting already existed, so latent demand found a better product.
- Three ways SIG takes these risks: flow market-making (sports/elections, fully automated), parlays/combos, and bespoke block/OTC hedges for corporates and institutions - the block business Maletz says is the next phase.
- The famous goat-herder trade: a California goat-herding business paid $50,000 for $500,000 of coverage against the risk that California failed to change a minimum-wage law that would have forced 24/7 minimum-wage payment to herders.
- SIG priced the novel risk by canvassing - literally talking to goat herders, lobbyists and internal researchers - because no market-implied probability existed for a contract that did not yet exist.
- The trade was printed as a block on Kalshi (CFTC-regulated), giving exchange transparency whereas a swap offers better capital treatment and a regulated insurance product offers different trade-offs; SIG says it is 'execution platform agnostic.'
- The contract resolved: the law was changed in a way that relieved the herder's pain, so the hedge paid nothing and SIG kept the premium - but the herder could have exited his position early as it traded.

## Overview

Matt Levine interviews Jeremy Maletz about running prediction markets at Susquehanna. They cover the anatomy of the business (flow, parlays, block hedges), why sports dominates volumes while non-sports grows faster, and at length the goat-herder hedge - the first viral example of a bespoke corporate risk being turned into an exchange-listed contract on Kalshi. Maletz stresses SIG is bootstrapping liquidity and wants the market to scale so such risks become tradable in and out.


## Implications

Prediction markets are becoming a genuine corporate-risk transfer venue, competing with insurance and swaps. The mechanics matter: exchange-listed contracts offer transparency, swaps offer capital efficiency, insurance products offer different regulatory footing. For quant and prop firms, the block/OTC hedge business is the growth frontier.
