# How NEAR Intents Identified a Suspect and Got Its Stolen Funds Back


## Key Points

- A bug in NEAR Intents' Omni bridging infrastructure let an attacker get the protocol to credit USDT deposits on BNB Chain that never happened, netting $3.8 million; NEAR Intents halted services and covered the loss from its own pockets.
- Twelve hours after disclosing the hack, the team tweeted at the suspect: 'We have identified you, sir.'
- GM Alex Shevchenko explains how a Signal invite encrypted with the attacker's public key opened a private channel that ended with the funds coming back, partly in XMR.
- NEAR Intents' detection system, Shield, kept 99% of the more than $50 million tied to the Bitget hack from passing through.
- Shevchenko argues shielded Zcash could face Monero's fate, and that crypto which protects thieves can never become the economic backbone its builders want.

## Overview

Laura Shin interviews Alex Shevchenko, GM of NEAR Intents, on the $3.8m bridging exploit and its unusual resolution. Shevchenko details the bug (credit for deposits that never happened), the decision to make the protocol whole, the public call-out of the suspect, and the encrypted Signal channel that led to the funds' return in part via Monero. He also discusses Shield's role in blocking Bitget-hack funds and his view on privacy coins that shield bad actors.


## Implications

The episode is a case study in protocol-side incident response: covering losses, public attribution, and quiet negotiation to recover funds. It also sharpens the debate over privacy tech - Shevchenko argues anonymity tools that shield thieves undermine crypto's legitimacy, a position that will keep pressure on Zcash and Monero.
