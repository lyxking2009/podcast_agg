# Beam: The Great American Open Model with ReflectionAI Co-Founder and CEO Misha Laskin


## Key Points

- Reflection AI grew from ~30 people a year ago to ~300 and shipped Beam, a 500B-parameter open-weight model (23B active) pre-trained on 6,000 GB300s in a few weeks, with RL run on 10,000+ GB300s for four weeks - Laskin says he hasn't seen a documented open-source RL run at that scale.
- Frontier training costs compound roughly 4x per model generation: hundreds of millions of dollars 18 months ago, single-digit billions six months ago, and heading toward ~$10bn next year.
- Laskin argues open models will take the majority of token demand, likening the shift to Linux dominating servers while Microsoft and Apple remain hugely valuable.
- Gateway traffic has flipped: roughly six months ago gateways like OpenRouter and Vercel were ~70-30 closed-to-open; now he says it is ~70-30 open-to-closed and accelerating.
- Most enterprise token consumption will come from customized open-model systems (an open base plus a tailored agentic harness) rather than fine-tuned models; 90%+ of traffic at dedicated open-inference providers is already customized.
- His commercialization frame is rental versus ownership, and his formula is durable revenue = intelligence density x compute x trust; model margins compress while compute scarcity makes assembling compute a lasting advantage.
- On China, he calls its open-weight releases a gift that prevented closed providers from subsuming application builders, but frames them as geopolitical 'trojan horses for the infrastructure that they bring with them.'

## Overview

Elad Gil and Sarah Guo host Reflection AI CEO Misha Laskin, fresh off shipping Beam, a 500B open-weight model. The conversation is a working account of what it takes to build a frontier open lab in the U.S.: the 4x-per-generation compute cost ladder, why RL (not pre-training) dominated Beam's flops, and how openness changes commercialization. Laskin argues open models will absorb most token demand, that enterprises will buy customized open systems rather than fine-tunes, and that China's open releases simultaneously subsidize Western enterprise and advance Chinese infrastructure lock-in.


## Implications

For enterprises, the practical takeaway is that ownership-plus-customization of open models may beat renting closed tokens once AI spend is large enough. For investors, Laskin's margin-compression thesis argues against picking single model winners and toward the surrounding stack (inference software, harnesses, compute). The China framing is a strategic warning about infrastructure lock-in riding on 'free' open weights.
