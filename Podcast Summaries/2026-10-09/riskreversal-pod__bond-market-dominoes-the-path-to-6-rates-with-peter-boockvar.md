# Bond Market Dominoes: The Path to 6% Rates with Peter Boockvar


## Key Points

- Dan Nathan and Peter Boockvar argue longer-term yields are rising globally as the bond market increasingly sets the cost of capital and investors push back on excessive debts and deficits.
- France's fiscal problems and their European spillovers are the immediate trigger; the debate is whether Fed rate hikes can address inflation tied to AI-driven data-center construction and supply constraints.
- Hyperscalers may issue up to $500bn of debt to fund massive AI CapEx, with 'reverse crowding out' as a theme.
- Credit-market stress: triple-C spreads above 1,000bps and refinancing shocks versus cheap 2021-era borrowing.
- The conversation turns to AI agents - monetization and the disruption risk to search, e-commerce and banking (one linked FT piece argues AI agents could cost banks $500bn by winning savers better rates).
- Weak market breadth and elevated oil amid Middle East conflict dynamics round out the discussion ahead of bank earnings, where the focus is deposit costs, loan demand and consumer resilience.
- Boockvar's framing: a path to 6% rates as bond-market dominoes fall.

## Overview

Dan Nathan hosts Peter Boockvar (CIO, OnePoint BFG Wealth Partners) for the weekly RiskReversal market conversation. The core thesis is that the bond market, not the Fed, is now driving the cost of capital, with rising long yields, French fiscal stress and heavy hyperscaler issuance all in play. They work through credit spreads, the AI CapEx financing boom, agentic-AI risks to incumbent business models, weak breadth and oil, then preview bank earnings.


## Implications

If long yields keep climbing toward 6%, the cost of capital repricing hits both equity valuations and the AI build-out's financing math. Watch hyperscaler debt issuance and triple-C spreads as stress gauges, and bank earnings prints for deposit-cost and loan-demand signals.
