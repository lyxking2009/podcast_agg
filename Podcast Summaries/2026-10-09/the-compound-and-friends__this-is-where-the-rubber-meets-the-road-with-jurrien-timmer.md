# This Is Where the Rubber Meets the Road, with Jurrien Timmer


## Key Points

- Episode 263 features Jurrien Timmer, Director of Global Macro at Fidelity, on why year-end S&P 500 price targets miss the point.
- The $17 trillion rise in the national debt and the 'missing buyers of Treasuries' are central: who absorbs all the new supply?
- Hyperscaler borrowing and 'reverse crowding out' - AI CapEx debt competing with sovereign issuance.
- 5% bond yields and the 'stealth correction' underneath an apparently calm index; record market concentration.
- Is AI an earnings bubble? Kevin Warsh's Fed, and why Nvidia and Apple have become the new defensive trade.
- Gold and Bitcoin's role in a portfolio, why the predicted AI job losses haven't shown up, the 60/20/20 allocation, and whether today's AI bull follows the dot-com playbook.

## Overview

Downtown Josh Brown and Michael Batnick are joined by Fidelity's Jurrien Timmer for a wide-ranging macro discussion. The thread is that headline price targets obscure the real story: record concentration and a stealth correction beneath the surface, fiscal supply overwhelming Treasury demand, and AI CapEx financed with debt. They debate whether AI is an earnings bubble, what a Kevin Warsh Fed might mean, why Nvidia and Apple now behave like defensives, and how gold, Bitcoin and a 60/20/20 allocation fit together amid dot-com parallels.


## Implications

For allocators, Timmer's framing argues for looking past index levels to concentration and breadth, and for treating long-duration sovereign supply plus hyperscaler issuance as the key rates driver. The 60/20/20 and gold/Bitcoin discussion offers a template for diversifying away from a top-heavy index.
