# Grace Shao on What the World Should Know About Chinese AI

**Show:** Odd Lots
**Date:** 2026-06-22
**Transcript Source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points

- China's AI ecosystem feels more utilitarian than the US — large companies (Tencent, Alibaba) are using AI for existing business models rather than treating it as an existential new paradigm
- Chinese AI development is characterized by massive open-source model proliferation — seemingly every company, even toothpaste brands, has produced an LLM
- Capital constraints in China's AI ecosystem differ dramatically from the US, where investors are 'flinging money at anything with AI in it'
- China's hardware capabilities in robotics, EVs, and batteries could influence the direction of AI development — the melding of hardware and AI capabilities is a key differentiator
- The release of ChatGPT in late 2022 was a turning point for China's AI strategy — shifting from 'card tech' (hard tech) back to competing in consumer-facing AI
- Grace Shao's newsletter 'AI Prome' provides deep coverage of the Chinese AI landscape from Hong Kong
- China's tech crackdown of 2020 did not destroy entrepreneurial spirit — entrepreneurism is now 'basically everywhere'
- The capital stack for Chinese AI companies is much harder to raise enormous sums, which influences how models are developed and commercialized

## Overview

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway record from Bloomberg's Hong Kong office with Grace Shao, an independent researcher who writes the AI Prome newsletter. They explore the Chinese AI ecosystem through the lens of Hong Kong's unique position as a gateway between mainland China and global markets. The conversation covers why Chinese AI feels more utilitarian, the open-source model proliferation, capital constraints, and how China's hardware advantages in robotics and EVs might shape AI development differently than in the US.

## Implications

For investors and practitioners: China's AI approach — more utilitarian, hardware-integrated, and capital-constrained — may produce fundamentally different products than US AI. The open-source proliferation suggests commoditization risk for model providers. China's hardware advantages could create unique AI applications in robotics and manufacturing that the US cannot easily replicate.
