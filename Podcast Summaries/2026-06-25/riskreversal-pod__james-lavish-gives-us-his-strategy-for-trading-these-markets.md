# James Lavish Gives Us His "Strategy" for Trading These Markets

## Key Points
- **The U.S. is "trapped" by debt:** Approximately $14 trillion rolling over in the next year on top of ongoing $2 trillion annual deficits — significant consequences for rates, inflation, and the dollar
- **"Hot ball of money"** chasing AI and SpaceX IPO themes
- **K-shaped economy** driving retail investors toward speculative bets
- **Predicted rotation:** Lavish anticipates capital rotating out of high-flying AI names and back into Bitcoin
- **Strategy (MicroStrategy) debate:** Whether Saylor's leverage and perpetual preferred stock structure is a "trap" or position of strength — Lavish argues concerns about the balance sheet are overblown if one maintains long-term bullish conviction on Bitcoin
- **Bitcoin Opportunity Fund** invests in Bitcoin-adjacent opportunities in energy and AI
- **Kevin Warsh's first meeting as Fed chair:** More hawkish-but-mostly-bark tone
- **July rate hike odds** discussed alongside war and energy price impacts on inflation
- **Kalshi picks** of the week from Danny Moses

## Overview
Danny Moses welcomes James Lavish back to On the Tape for a wide-ranging macro conversation. Lavish, drawing on his background trading risk arbitrage on the NYSE floor and running the Bitcoin Opportunity Fund, breaks down why he believes the Fed and Treasury are trapped by a looming wall of debt. They discuss the "hot ball of money" chasing AI and SpaceX IPO themes, the K-shaped economy, and Lavish's prediction that capital will rotate from AI names back into Bitcoin. The conversation also covers Strategy (MicroStrategy) and whether Saylor's leveraged position is sustainable.

## Implications
- The $14T debt rollover creates a structural ceiling on how high rates can go
- Capital rotation from AI to Bitcoin could be a major theme in H2 2026
- Strategy/MicroStrategy's balance sheet risk is debated but may be manageable in a long-term Bitcoin bull case
- The K-shaped economy is intensifying retail speculation
