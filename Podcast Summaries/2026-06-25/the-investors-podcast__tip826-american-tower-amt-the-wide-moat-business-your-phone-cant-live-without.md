# TIP826: American Tower (AMT): The Wide Moat Business Your Phone Can't Live Without

## Key Points
- **Core business model:** AMT leases tower space to wireless carriers (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile) via recurring leases (5-10 years) with contractual rent escalators (1-3% annually)
- **Tower economics:** Adding a second tenant almost doubles EBITDA; the third tenant triples it — minimal incremental costs
- **High barriers to entry:** Zoning/regulatory hurdles make new tower construction nearly impossible; site acquisition costs exceed $1M/tower
- **Switching costs:** Carriers face service disruption risks; rewiring antennas is expensive
- **Global scale:** 180k+ towers across 40 countries reduces per-tower operating costs
- **Carrier consolidation risk:** T-Mobile/Sprint merger reduced potential tenants and pricing power
- **REIT structure pitfall:** Must distribute 90% of income as dividends → heavy debt reliance. "The balance sheet has just gotten a lot uglier."
- **CoreSite acquisition (2021):** Data center pivot to diversify, but high debt load questions capital efficiency. "It's hard to see the synergies with towers... jury's still out."
- **Historical performance:** Multi-bagger (280x for Chuck Akre) but ~40% drawdown since 2021
- **Akre Capital exit:** Reduced AMT to 0.14% of portfolio (vs. historic top holding). "A wonderful business isn't always a wonderful investment."

## Overview
Kyle Grieve and Shawn O'Malley provide a comprehensive investment analysis of American Tower (AMT), the global cell tower REIT. They examine AMT's powerful competitive moats — high barriers to entry, switching costs, and economies of scale — while highlighting key risks including carrier consolidation, REIT-related debt dependency, and the questionable CoreSite data center acquisition. The hosts note that despite being a 280x multi-bagger for Chuck Akre, AMT has drawn down ~40% since 2021, leading even Akre Capital to dramatically reduce its position.

## Implications
- AMT's tower infrastructure remains critical for 5G/tech evolution — the moat is enduring
- Rising interest rates + debt burden create valuation headwinds for REITs
- The CoreSite acquisition's synergies with towers remain unproven
- The drawdown may offer opportunity for long-term investors, but macro risks persist
- Carrier consolidation is a structural risk that could reduce tenant demand
