## Key Points

- Hudson River Trading believes it could theoretically build a DeepSeek-level LLM given its compute and training expertise, but considers reaching the true frontier too capital-intensive to pursue
- The proliferation of open models from both the US and China has shifted the landscape dramatically since the 'bolt from the blue' moment of DeepSeek's release
- HRT is applying a unified AI approach across equities, futures, crypto, and options markets globally — something Dunning says would not have been imaginable in 2024
- HRT uses LLMs primarily to accelerate internal research: coding, generating experiment ideas, and monitoring experiments
- A 'false start' occurred with Claude Opus 4.0 where the team felt AGI had arrived, only to be disappointed; Opus 4.5 then genuinely met those expectations six months later
- HRT has begun benchmarking AI agents against human quant researchers at tasks like signal ideation, currently rating them at roughly 'intern level'
- The competitive dynamics of AI in trading are self-compressing: as all major firms invest simultaneously, margins on prediction alpha may trend toward zero
- Dunning describes HRT's compute growth as looking exponential year-over-year, mirroring the broader lab scaling trend
- Modern quant trading may have moved beyond needing intuitive human narratives — pure backtesting without a causal story is increasingly the norm

## Overview

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway host Ian Dunning, Head of AI at Hudson River Trading, at their largest-ever live show in New York. The conversation covers HRT's AI capabilities, how they evaluate new model releases for quant research, and the exponential pace of AI progress in trading. Dunning reflects on going from skeptic to believer as compute and model capabilities have grown dramatically year over year.

## Implications

The rapid commoditization of AI capabilities in trading — with multiple firms scaling simultaneously — raises structural questions about whether AI-driven alpha can persist or whether it simply raises the competitive floor for all participants. The convergence of frontier model capabilities with domain-specific quant research tasks suggests the role of human researchers in trading firms may be meaningfully transformed within a short timeframe.
