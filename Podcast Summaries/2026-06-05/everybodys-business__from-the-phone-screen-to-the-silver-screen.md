## Key Points

- Javier Blass had predicted the US-Iran conflict would end within 60 days due to oil market pressure, but the conflict has now reached approximately 100 days with a ceasefire in place while the Strait of Hormuz remains closed.
- Oil prices have stayed below $100 per barrel despite the conflict, defying expectations of a catastrophic price spike; US gasoline prices are above $4 per gallon but well below feared levels.
- China has reduced its oil imports by roughly 4 million barrels per day, down about 40% from the prior year — less than it imported during COVID — which has been the single biggest factor stabilizing global oil markets.
- Rich countries including the US, Canada, Europe, and Japan deployed strategic petroleum reserves within two weeks of the conflict starting, far faster than the 6+ months it took during the First Gulf War in 1990.
- The modern oil industry of 2026 is significantly more flexible and sophisticated than 25 years ago, allowing refiners to quickly adjust output ratios — for example, increasing jet fuel yield per barrel to address shortages.
- There is speculation but no confirmed evidence of a deal between Beijing and the White House behind China's oil import reduction; it may also reflect China's own self-interest in avoiding domestic inflation and global economic slowdown.
- Had China maintained its prior oil import levels, analysts estimate gasoline prices in the US could have exceeded $5 per gallon, forcing Fed rate hikes and putting significant political pressure on the Trump administration.
- Some oil market participants view Beijing as effectively bailing out President Trump, raising questions about what China may be receiving in return.

## Overview

Hosts Max Schafkon and Stacey Annick Smith welcome back Bloomberg Opinion columnist Javier Blass roughly 100 days into a US-Iran conflict to assess the economic fallout. Blass reflects on his earlier prediction that the conflict would last no more than 60 days due to oil price pressures, explaining why that forecast proved wrong. The conversation centers on the surprising role China has played in stabilizing global oil markets by dramatically reducing its oil imports.

## Implications

The US-Iran conflict has revealed unexpected resilience in global energy markets, largely due to China's dramatic and still-unexplained reduction in oil imports and rapid deployment of strategic reserves by wealthy nations. The episode raises significant geopolitical questions about behind-the-scenes deals between Beijing and Washington, and highlights how dependent US economic and political stability has become on Chinese energy decisions.
