## Key Points

- Ellis wrote an early book on stock buybacks 50 years ago when the concept was novel; Goldman Sachs distributed it to 1,000 corporations as a legitimizer for the practice.
- The book covers 14 audacious American projects from the Louisiana Purchase through the Marshall Plan, each driven by determined individuals who overcame skepticism.
- Frances Perkins was the key champion who pushed Social Security into law, illustrating how transformative programs often depend on a single persistent advocate.
- Ellis argues that long-term investing in diversified index funds is the most reliable path to financial success for individual investors, citing the compounding power of time.
- A pivotal lunch with Sandy Gottesman in the early 1970s led Ellis to purchase Berkshire Hathaway at $700 per share, a position he has held long-term as a real-world example of conviction investing.
- Ellis speculates that AI may represent the next great American investment opportunity, continuing the historical pattern of transformative national-scale initiatives.
- American entrepreneurship and community-driven innovation remain ongoing forces that mirror the boldness of historical public investments.
- Behavioral economics plays a significant role in why most individual investors underperform — emotional decision-making undermines long-term compounding.

## Overview

Charley Ellis, founder of Greenwich Associates, joins Meb Faber to discuss his new book 'Great American Investments: A History of the Bold Initiatives that Shaped a Nation,' which examines 14 transformative public investments that shaped the United States. Ellis draws parallels between historical bold national investments and principles of long-term personal investing, touching on behavioral economics, index fund investing, and whether AI represents the next great American investment.

## Implications

Ellis's historical lens on public investment suggests that the boldest, most contested initiatives often yield the greatest long-term returns for society, and that the same patience and conviction required for transformative national projects applies directly to personal investing. The discussion positions AI as a potential next chapter in America's tradition of audacious, nation-shaping investments.
