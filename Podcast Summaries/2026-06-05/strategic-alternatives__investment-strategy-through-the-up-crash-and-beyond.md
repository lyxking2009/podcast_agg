## Key Points

- Markets are experiencing an 'up crash' — a term coined by Amy Wu Silverman — where valuations remain elevated even as volatility stays high, defying the typical inverse relationship between the two.
- Investors have been on a call-buying spree, suggesting continued bullish positioning despite macro uncertainty.
- The panel examines what factors or catalysts could finally bring about a correction after the sustained resilience in equities.
- U.S. equity strategy is navigating a complex backdrop where traditional risk signals are not translating into the expected market drawdowns.
- Derivatives markets are reflecting unusual dynamics, with options activity suggesting investors are hedging but also aggressively seeking upside exposure.
- Cross-asset hedge fund positioning is a key lens for understanding how sophisticated investors are managing risk in this environment.
- The durability of the 'up crash' depends on macro variables including interest rate trajectory, earnings, and geopolitical developments.
- The conference context — Global Energy, Power and Infrastructure — adds a sector-specific dimension to the broader market strategy discussion.

## Overview

This episode of Strategic Alternatives, recorded at RBC's Global Energy, Power and Infrastructure Conference, examines the paradox of markets sustaining high valuations despite elevated volatility — a phenomenon dubbed the 'up crash.' The discussion covers how long this unusual market dynamic can persist, what catalysts could trigger a correction, and how investors should position themselves across asset classes in this environment.

## Implications

The persistence of high valuations alongside high volatility challenges conventional risk frameworks and may force investors to rethink how they price uncertainty. If the 'up crash' dynamic unwinds abruptly, the concentrated call-buying and stretched valuations could amplify a correction significantly.
