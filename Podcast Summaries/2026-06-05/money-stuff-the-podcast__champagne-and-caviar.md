## Key Points

- The Jeffrey, a Bloomberg-affiliated bar in NYC owned by a hedge fund lawyer, offered free tabs (up to $100/guest) if the Knicks won their Wednesday game, then hedged the promotion by betting on Kalshi
- Kalshi framed the bar's sports bet as a legitimate business hedge against operational risk, issuing a press release celebrating the use of prediction markets for 'real world risk management'
- Matt Levine argues this is simply sports betting rebranded as hedging — the bar created the sports exposure itself by running the promotion, so calling Kalshi a hedge is misleading
- The Jeffrey also ran a prior promotion for Game 4 vs. the Cavs where each point in the Knicks' margin of victory reduced patron bills by 1%; the Knicks won by 37, cutting bills by 37%
- Google announced roughly $85 billion in equity issuance: ~$35 billion in common stock and mandatory convertibles, $10 billion privately placed with Berkshire Hathaway, and up to $40 billion in an at-the-market offering
- The Google offering signals a potential reversal of the decade-long trend of net equity buybacks by large US companies, as capital needs for AI infrastructure grow dramatically
- Major US tech firms once considered 'capital light' (e.g., Facebook as 'just a website') now require massive data center and chip investments, fundamentally changing their financing needs
- Google has been financing growth through debt, but is now shifting toward equity issuance, reflecting a preference for stronger equity capitalization given its fortress balance sheet

## Overview

Hosts Matt Levine and Katie Greifeld discuss a New York bar called The Jeffrey, which ran a promotion offering free tabs if the Knicks won their Wednesday game, and hedged that risk by betting on Kalshi prediction markets. The conversation then shifts to Google's massive ~$85 billion equity offering, exploring what it signals about the changing capital intensity of major US tech companies and a potential reversal of the long-running buyback trend.

## Implications

The Google mega-offering may mark a structural turning point where the largest US tech companies transition from returning capital via buybacks to issuing equity to fund AI infrastructure, reshaping equity markets and index fund dynamics. Meanwhile, the Jeffrey/Kalshi story highlights the blurring line between prediction markets and sports gambling, raising questions about how 'hedging' is defined in the new prediction market ecosystem.
