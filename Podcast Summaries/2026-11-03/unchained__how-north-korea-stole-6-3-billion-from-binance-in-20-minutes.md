# How North Korea Stole $6.3 Billion From Binance in 20 Minutes — Unchained

## TL;DR
North Korea stole $6.3 billion from Binance in about 20 minutes, the largest hack in history. Attackers used a compromised developer laptop to sign transactions from Binance's hot wallets, aided by an EIP-7702 upgrade. Approximately 43% of funds were frozen due to quick action by Circle, Tether, Arbitrum's Security Council, and Aave, and the episode explores laundering via THORChain and the TraderTraitor group's fake job interviews targeting developers.

## Key points
- North Korea's theft of $6.3 billion from Binance took only about 20 minutes, making it the largest hack in history (not just in crypto). The attack began with a compromised developer laptop that gave attackers the ability to sign transactions from Binance's hot wallets.
- The EIP-7702 upgrade allowed hackers to sweep hot wallets more quickly, exacerbating the theft. This delegation feature likely enabled transaction signing with less friction.
- Roughly 43% of the stolen funds were frozen by Circle, Tether, Arbitrum's Security Council, and Aave. In contrast, after the Bybit hack, less than 3% of funds were frozen, highlighting a more effective response this time.
- The hackers borrowed nearly 20,000 ETH on Aave as part of moving the stolen assets, and Binance's own liquidity may have inadvertently helped launder funds through THORChain.
- THORChain's decentralization was questioned after a three-vote halt, and the FBI publicly named THOR node operators, signaling increased scrutiny on decentralized services used for laundering.
- The laundering trail appears machine-driven, according to the discussion, indicating automated mixing or swapping of stolen funds.
- TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group, uses fake job interviews to compromise developers' laptops, which is one of the attack vectors discussed.
- Chainalysis reports that nearly 89% of this year's reported hack losses are tied to North Korea, underscoring the scale of the threat.
- The industry should implement stronger security measures and anti-laundering steps, such as reducing hot wallet exposure, improving developer device security, and coordinating rapid freeze responses.
