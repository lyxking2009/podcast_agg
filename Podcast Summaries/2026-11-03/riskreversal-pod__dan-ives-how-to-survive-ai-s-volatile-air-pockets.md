# Dan Ives: How to Survive AI’s Volatile "Air Pockets" — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Dan Ives joins Dan Nathan to discuss the sharp Nasdaq and semiconductor selloff, shifting AI leadership, and how investors can navigate volatile drawdowns. Ives outlines his move from Wedbush to the new Yorkville Ives “modern merchant bank,” the Ives ETF, and a $200 million IPO vehicle for private AI companies, framing AI as an 8–12 year buildout with a gap between massive capex and monetization, energy constraints, and leverage risks at Oracle and neo-clouds. The episode contrasts Microsoft’s enterprise AI upside with Alphabet’s ad/search cannibalization fears and covers stock-picking across chips, hyperscalers, software, cybersecurity, plus bear arguments on GPU depreciation and token economics.

## Key points
- Dan Ives has left Wedbush to join or co-found Yorkville Ives, described as a 'modern merchant bank,' while maintaining involvement with the Ives ETF.
- Ives has launched a $200 million IPO vehicle focused on investing in private AI companies, signaling continued exposure to AI infrastructure and application names.
- Ives frames AI as an 8–12 year buildout, not a dot-com-style bubble, but highlights a significant gap between massive capital expenditures and current monetization, with energy constraints adding risk.
- Leverage risks are prominent at Oracle and 'neo-cloud' companies, which may face financial stress if AI spending slows or financing conditions tighten.
- Microsoft is positioned as an enterprise-driven AI beneficiary, whereas Alphabet faces concerns about AI cannibalizing its core advertising and search revenue.
- Ives advocates stock-picking across the AI value chain including chips, hyperscalers, software, and cybersecurity rather than broad index exposure.
- Bear arguments include GPU depreciation, deteriorating token economics, and potential political restrictions on data-center construction after the midterms.
