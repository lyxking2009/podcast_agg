# Optimizing Life and Finances in Your Twenties with Jack Raines — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Barry Ritholtz interviews Jack Raines — venture capitalist, Young Money Substack author, and author of the new book "Young Money: A Field Guide to Wealth and Purpose in Your Twenties." Raines recounts his path from a remote-work purgatory job at UPS during COVID, to a fortuitous (and then humbling) run trading SPACs, to a year of hostel-hopping across dozens of countries, to Columbia Business School, Sherwood News at Robinhood, and finally writing the book. The core idea: the "stage specificity of life" — every experience has a window when you'll enjoy it most, and money in your twenties should be optimized for those windows, not just compounded for later.

## Key points
- The COVID experience: as a new grad at UPS he spent ~18 months in "work-from-home purgatory" — zoom calls and Excel fuel-cost models, bored, with business school (Columbia, deferred enrollment) three years out.
- The SPAC chapter: threw $10K into SPY puts at the pandemic bottom ("I thought I was the next Michael Burry"), round-tripped $10K → $30K → $10K, then a friend introduced him to SPACs (Desert Eagle SPAC → DraftKings); he made real money trading SPACs and warrants before the blow-up — later chronicled in his essay "How I Lost $150,000 in a Day Trading SPACs."
- The trip: quit his job in August 2021 and bought a one-way ticket to Barcelona — a year of hostels, food poisoning, and dozens of countries; his grandma (who took him on a safari at 11) and study-abroad in Spain seeded the travel bug.
- "Stage specificity of life": money compounds, but experiences have expiration dates — a 24-year-old in a 12-person hostel is having the time of his life; the 31-year-old in the same hostel is six or seven years too late. The right question isn't just "save more" but "when is the optimal time to spend on different things?"
- Writing: failed to get hired by Morning Brew and The Hustle, so he started his own Substack ("half investing and half travel blog") — "I write to figure out what I think" (Daniel Boorstin quote); a burner Reddit account (Barmelo's Anthony) honed his market commentary during the SPAC era.
- Career: interned at Redpoint Ventures (editorial/newsletter strategy), concluded he should either do media or investing — not the marketing arm of an investment group; joined Sherwood News (Robinhood's media subsidiary, "a new-age Bloomberg") where the pitch was autonomy plus fintech-company stability; got Robinhood stock; signed the book deal ~2 years before this conversation.
- The career panic: after structured media work, realized he actually likes free-range blogging more than nine-to-five financial media — "I probably should have gone investing track" — and describes the fall-2024 moment of total career panic before landing where he is now.

## Overview
The interview is part career memoir, part money philosophy for young people. Raines uses his own zig-zag path — SPAC speculation, a year abroad, media, venture — to argue that financial optimization in your twenties is really life-stage optimization: understanding which experiences are time-limited and spending accordingly, while still building the skills (writing, investing) that compound. He's candid about his mistakes: the SPAC blow-up, choosing media over the investing seat, and the pressure of the "daily beast that must be fed" in content jobs.

## Implications
- Young professionals: budget for stage-specific experiences while you can — hostel travel, living abroad, risk-taking — because those windows close; don't optimize purely for future compounding.
- Investors: the SPAC/warrants era was a brutal education in leverage and liquidity — his lesson was to know when to stop (he moved money into a Roth IRA and stopped trading).
- Writers: the path to a media career increasingly runs through your own Substack — build the audience before the job title.
- Career: don't split the middle between two tracks (media vs investing); pick the seat you actually want.
