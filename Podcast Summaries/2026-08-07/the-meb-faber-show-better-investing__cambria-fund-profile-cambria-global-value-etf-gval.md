# Cambria Fund Profile – Cambria Global Value ETF (GVAL) — The Meb Faber Show - Better Investing

## TL;DR
A Cambria Fund Profile episode in which Meb Faber walks through the investment case for the Cambria Global Value ETF (GVAL): why starting valuations matter for future returns, how the Shiller CAPE ratio shows US stocks are roughly double the global median valuation, why home-country bias should be avoided, and how GVAL's rules-based methodology selects the cheapest markets around the world to build a global value portfolio.

## Key points
- Valuations matter: research shows buying an expensive market raises the odds of big future drawdowns; high starting CAPE readings historically lead to below-average subsequent returns via mean reversion.
- The Shiller/CAPE ratio: the S&P 500 has traded above ~30 CAPE — higher than any time in the last 150 years except the Great Depression and the dot-com bubble; the median CAPE across 45 countries is 15 — the US is more than double the global median (country average ~17; US historical range from ~5 low to ~45 at the dot-com peak).
- Home-country bias: investors overweight their home market; going back to 1980, US and foreign stocks have traded at similar valuations — no specific market is any more special or deserving of a permanent valuation premium.
- GVAL's approach: applies a similar valuation methodology across 45 markets, selecting markets based on relative and absolute valuation (top quartile by cheapness), with a rules-based quantitative algorithm; seeks income and capital appreciation.
- Fund facts: actively managed (changed from passive on July 1, 2020), inception 3/12/2014, 0.66% total expense ratio, benchmark MSCI AC World TR USD; portfolio managers Mebane Faber and Jonathan Keetz.
- Positioning: GVAL is framed as an option for investors concerned that US stocks are simply too richly valued — a global-value diversifier rather than a US-concentrated growth bet.

## Overview
This is the fund-profile format: Meb educates shareholders on why valuation discipline matters, presents the historical CAPE evidence for global markets, and then explains how GVAL operationalizes the strategy — screening 45 markets for cheapness on relative and absolute valuations, weighting toward the most attractive countries, and holding a diversified portfolio of high-value global stocks. It's a compact primer on the global-value case with the fund as the worked example.

## Implications
- Investors: starting valuations are a meaningful predictor of long-run returns — current US CAPE levels argue for global diversification.
- Portfolio construction: GVAL-type strategies offer a systematic way to express the global-value view without market-cap concentration in expensive US markets.
- Behavioral finance: home-country bias is persistent and costly — the data shows no structural valuation premium for any single market.
