## Key points
- Venture mandates: crypto funds are drifting far outside their lanes into hardware, robotics, and nuclear; the panel argues you "earn the right to be a generalist" (Sequoia started niche in semis and only went generalist around its eighth fund) — "if you are everything for everybody, you are nothing to nobody."
- LP pushback is emerging: LPs are questioning whether to allocate to generalists instead of specialized funds; a strong GP brand is the real compounding asset (Mickey Malco on David Sacks's podcast: "your brand and your reputation is the real thing that compounds value").
- Andrew Kang / Robot Ventures as the case study: pivoting from crypto to robotics deals (Figure), picking a niche and owning it — but venture math makes many hardware deals money-losers for late-round investors (Anthropic $500K options → $20-25M example: 40x growth vs 1000x valuation).

- Cloudflare Wallets: a programmable wallet system for the "agentic internet" — a human-controlled account wallet spawns virtual agent wallets with guardrails (spend allowances, allowlists, max transaction sizes), uses human-readable handles, integrates the x42 protocol so micropayments attach directly to HTTP requests, and runs on stablecoins.
- Market color: "blood bath in memory and crypto," SpaceX unlocks ("low float, high FTV — made for this singular moment"), finance becoming more like crypto (Korea's retail bloodbath), and the Carta data showing 2022-vintage venture 90th-percentile TVPI of just 1.68x — "long gone are the days of 10x in two years."

## Implications
- VCs: mandate drift carries LP risk; earn generalist status through repeated success, and beware hardware deal math at multi-billion valuations.
- Crypto x AI: agent infrastructure (wallets, routers, payments) is where crypto rails meet AI spend — expect more agent-wallet standards like Cloudflare's and x42.
- Investors: 2022-vintage venture returns are poor even at the 90th percentile — capital efficiency, not beta, is the game now.
