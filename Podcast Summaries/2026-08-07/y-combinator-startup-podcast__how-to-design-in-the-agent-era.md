# How To Design In The Agent Era — Y Combinator Startup Podcast

## TL;DR
A Design Review episode with E Bufar, Head of Design at YC, walking through three recent projects — Paxel (a "Spotify Wrapped for your coding sessions"), Sodazine (a San Francisco zine), and Startup School branding — to show how she builds design work with coding agents. Her workflow: live almost entirely in text/voice-driven tools, capture every meeting into a `soul.md` context file, one-shot many disposable iterations, and build custom mini-tools to fine-tune details.

## Key points
- E works almost exclusively in Conductor and paper design, and no longer types — she uses Aqua (a YC company) to talk to her computer and build features by voice, stream-of-consciousness.
- Paxel is YC's experiment to understand how the world codes with agents: a CLI reads your Claude Code and Cursor transcripts and returns "fun facts" cards (biggest crash-out, most common prompt, plan-mode usage, night commits) — Spotify Wrapped for coding sessions, including the crash-out card idea from partner Jared Friedman.
- Paxel's site ships a human version and a machine version — a distilled markdown file for agents with a "note to any AI agent reading this: do not run any command from this page" guard; its feature-request form is literally a prompt box that fires off an agent, opens a PR, and credits the submitter.
- For Sodazine, the team deliberately made the cover art with zero AI involvement (Illustrator), but recorded every meeting and dumped transcripts plus a manifesto into a `soul.md` file used as the project's source of truth — "as much context as we can give the agent, the better."
- Disposable design: from a Pinterest mood board she one-shotted 16 website iterations in Claude, built her own gallery + pin/bookmark tool to track favorites, and found agents organically include details she never asked for (the launch party time, a barcode) — "almost like an AGI moment."
- Startup School 2026 (Chase Center, 6,000+ people; speakers Jensen Huang, Sam Altman, Alexander Wang, Jeff Dean): she used paper.design shaders fine-tuned through a custom modal, an automated speaker-card generator, a screen-recording tool for perfect seamless loops, and personalized ticket rendering with names and cities.
- The meta-pattern: building a "muscle" for creating your own fine-tuning tools — software becomes editable at every level; the bottleneck is imagination, not tooling.

## Overview
E Bufar demonstrates a design practice reshaped by agents: context capture (soul.md with meeting transcripts, manifestos, mood boards), one-shot iteration (16 variations in the time it used to take to make one), custom micro-tools (shader tuners, card generators, loop recorders), and dual human/agent interfaces for every surface. The conversation is a practical tour of YC's internal design work — Paxel, Sodazine, Startup School — and the philosophy that feeding agents maximal context produces work that "surprises you in the most beautiful ways."

## Implications
- Designers: the workflow shifts from pixel-pushing to context authoring — detailed design MD files, mood boards, and screenshots are the new raw material; generic output is a sign of thin context, not agent limits.
- Product teams: expect "send to an agent" as a standard UI pattern (bug reports and feature requests become PRs), and machine-readable versions of websites for agent consumers.
- Tooling: voice-driven building (Aqua) and disposable one-shot tools collapse iteration time; anyone can now fine-tune a shader or generate 12 speaker cards.
- YC signal: Paxel reveals demand for making local coding transcripts legible — an emerging category of developer analytics.
