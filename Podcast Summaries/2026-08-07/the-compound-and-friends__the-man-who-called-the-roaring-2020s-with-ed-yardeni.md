# The Man Who Called the Roaring 2020s with Ed Yardeni — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Josh Brown and Michael Batnick host Ed Yardeni (Yardeni Research) on episode 254 of The Compound and Friends. Yardeni defends his "Roaring 2020s" call — made in August 2020 — argues there will be no recession through the end of the decade, targets S&P 500 at 10,000 by 2029, frames AI as evolutionary (part of a digital revolution dating to the 1960s mainframe), and makes the case that today's earnings surge is real, not a bubble: 28.8% operating earnings growth, record profit margins, and breadth that bears no resemblance to 1999.

## Key points
- The Roaring 2020s call: made August 2020, right after the lockdowns, using the 1920s analog (Spanish flu, a 1920 depression — and then radio, assembly lines, autos); he kept betting on economic resilience through the "most widely anticipated recession that just ain't going to happen."
- No recession through 2029: the economy survived pandemic lockdowns, the 0-to-5.5% Fed tightening cycle, a weekend financial crisis in 2023, tariffs, and war — real GDP is at an all-time record; S&P 500 at 10,000 by the end of the decade.
- AI is evolutionary, not revolutionary: the revolution is the digital revolution starting with the IBM mainframe in the mid-1960s — processing information faster and cheaper; data is the fourth factor of production, and there will never be a shortage of data.
- Earnings are real: 28.8% operating earnings growth this quarter, profit margins at record ~15% (tech and communication services are 45% of the S&P and do creative destruction better), forward revenues at all-time highs — the "earnings bubble" claim fails because revenues × margins are both rising.
- Not 1999: then it was multiple expansion (250% in the run-up) with no earnings; now multiples are basically flat over three years (18% vs 250%) while the "impressive 493" have outperformed the Mag 7; the advance/decline line is at an all-time record — breadth everywhere, not just tech.
- On rates: bond vigilantes pushing yields toward 4-5% is "the range they should be at"; he doesn't believe in charting interest rates because at higher yields a ton of buyers come in — the opposite of a stock breakout.
- On capex: hyperscalers will spend an estimated ~$1 trillion next year; "shortages get cured by innovation"; the Fed was wrong to cut because inflation is not at 2% (a reason he wasn't on the shortlist for Fed chair).
- FEMMO: "fabulous earnings momentum" — forward earnings are the north star; analysts are good at predicting earnings except they never see recessions coming, "so that's my job."

## Overview
The episode is a retrospective-plus-outlook with the strategist who called the decade's bull run. Yardeni recounts how he made the call against a backdrop of 2020 gloom, explains why he thinks AI is an accelerator of a decades-old digital revolution rather than an anomaly, and systematically dismantles the 1999-echo arguments: valuation multiples are flat, earnings are real and broad-based, and the market has climbed a wall of worry all decade. The conversation also covers his market-calls scorecard (yardeni.com/market-calls), the bond vigilantes, and whether the hyperscaler capex cycle is pulling forward demand.

## Implications
- Equities: Yardeni's framework says stay constructive — earnings growth, not multiple expansion, is driving the market; breadth supports the bull case.
- Rates: expect 4-5% yields as the new normal range; treat rate spikes as buying opportunities rather than breakouts.
- AI investing: the debate should be about who makes the money in AI, not whether AI is a bubble — "we don't know who's actually going to make the money and who's going to go out of business."
- Recession calls: the 2020s have repeatedly punished recession forecasts; rolling recessions, not economy-wide ones, are the pattern.
