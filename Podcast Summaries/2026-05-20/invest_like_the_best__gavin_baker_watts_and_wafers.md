## Key Points

- Anthropic added $11B in ARR in one month—more than Palantir, Snowflake, and Databricks combined built in 10 years—representing an unprecedented moment in business history.
- Baker framed March/April 2026's market sell-off as a "pent-up alpha" opportunity: AI fundamentals were accelerating while valuations became historically attractive relative to the rest of the market.
- The Strait of Hormuz closure was net-positive for US AI competitiveness: US natural gas prices fell ~20% while Asian/European prices doubled or tripled, widening America's energy cost advantage.
- On watts: capitalism will solve the near-term power shortage with meaningful alleviation expected in 2027–2028; orbital compute (racks in sun-synchronous orbit connected by lasers through vacuum) is the long-term solution.
- Orbital compute should be reframed as racks in space (~the size of a Blackwell rack with 500-ft solar wings), connected via lasers already deployed on every Starlink satellite—not "pentagon-sized floating data centers."
- On wafers: TSMC's disciplined capacity expansion is the single most important variable preventing an AI bubble; if Jensen Huang had unlimited TSMC supply, Nvidia could sell $2–3 trillion of GPUs in 2026–2027, likely causing an overbuild.
- TSMC's restrained supply acts as a natural bubble-prevention mechanism; the risk is if Intel or Samsung gain 30%+ market share at the leading node and break that constraint.
- The disaggregation of prefill (memory-capacity-bound: "loading the cannon") and decode (memory-bandwidth-bound: "firing the cannon") extends GPU useful lives to 10–15 years, potentially saving the private credit industry that financed GPU fleets on 3–4 year assumptions.
- Frontier tokens continue to capture an overwhelming share of economic value at the model layer; the pareto frontier (intelligence vs. cost) has shifted from Google to Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI.
- The shift from all-you-can-eat to usage-based AI pricing is why OpenAI and Anthropic could exceed $200B in combined ARR; rate-limited plans deliver "lobotomized" AI.
- Three key open questions for AI investors: (1) Will the bitter lesson hold even for ASI-level models? (2) Will frontier tokens retain their value premium? (3) When will continual learning (dynamic weight updates) arrive?
- For new chip startups, Baker's rule of thumb: 1% market share = ~$100B in value; the winning formula is to do something different AND hard—anything merely "different but easy" will be replicated by Nvidia.
- Cerebras is highlighted as a genuine example of "different and hard"—wafer-scale computing took three chip generations to get right.
- Continual learning (models dynamically updating weights in real time) is "just around the corner" and would represent a third major paradigm shift, triggering an even faster AI capability takeoff.

## Overview

Gavin Baker, CIO of Atreides Management, joins Patrick O'Shaughnessy for their sixth conversation, centering on the two physical constraints shaping AI's next phase: watts (power) and wafers (semiconductor supply). Baker argues that the AI buildout is the most extraordinary moment in the history of capitalism—evidenced by Anthropic adding $11B in ARR in a single month—while examining how TSMC's disciplined capacity expansion and the eventual emergence of orbital compute will determine whether the AI infrastructure boom tips into bubble territory.

## Implications

TSMC's capacity discipline is the most important single variable to watch for AI investors—it is simultaneously the constraint preventing an overbuild bubble and the gating factor on Nvidia's revenue ceiling; investors should monitor TSMC expansion announcements and Intel/Samsung leading-node progress as early warning signals. The disaggregation of prefill and decode inference, combined with orbital compute's long-term power solution, materially extends the investment thesis for the AI infrastructure buildout well beyond the typical semiconductor cycle.
