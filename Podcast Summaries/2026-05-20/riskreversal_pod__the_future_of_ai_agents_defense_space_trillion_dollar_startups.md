## Key Points

- The post-ChatGPT era is defined by AI agents moving from novelty to enterprise-grade deployment across software, defense, and industrial applications.
- Defense and national security are increasingly intertwined with frontier AI, with companies like Anduril (a Lux Capital portfolio company) leading autonomous systems development.
- Space infrastructure is maturing as a commercial sector, with launch economics and orbital services unlocking new business models beyond government contracts.
- Semiconductor innovation remains the enabling constraint for AI progress, making compute and memory advances central to which AI applications become economically viable.
- Trillion-dollar startup valuations are being driven by hyperscaler capital allocation and the winner-take-most dynamics of foundational AI models and platforms.
- Ann Bordetsky's investment thesis centers on AI-first products that drive economic empowerment—backing companies like Perplexity, ElevenLabs, Suno, and Pika before market validation.
- Shahin Farshchi emphasizes deep-tech and hard-tech investing at Lux Capital, with a portfolio spanning AI (Hugging Face, Cognition), robotics (Physical Intelligence), and aerospace (Impulse Space).
- The key challenge for investors is distinguishing AI companies with durable moats from those riding a temporary wave of hype or cheap capital.
- Future-of-work and enterprise software are undergoing a structural shift as AI agents replace or augment knowledge-worker workflows at scale.

## Overview

Host Dan Nathan sits down with Lux Capital partner Shahin Farshchi and NEA partner Ann Bordetsky at the RBC Private Tech Conference to explore what comes after ChatGPT and where the next wave of AI is headed. The conversation spans AI agents, defense tech, space infrastructure, robotics, semiconductors, enterprise software, and the mechanics of trillion-dollar startup valuations, mapping out which technologies and companies are positioned to shape the next decade.

## Implications

The convergence of AI agents, defense autonomy, and space infrastructure is creating a new class of dual-use technology companies capable of reaching trillion-dollar scale—reshaping how venture capitalists, hyperscalers, and government customers allocate capital. Investors who can identify genuine AI moats early will capture outsized returns as winner-take-most dynamics compress the window for differentiation.
