## Key Points

- Sterling came under significant pressure in mid-May 2026, hitting five-week lows, driven by the dual shock of rising oil prices and a Labour Party leadership crisis following Health Secretary Wes Streeting's resignation.
- Oil prices surged toward $100/barrel as Strait of Hormuz disruption risks re-emerged from Middle East conflict, acting as a stagflationary shock for the UK—raising energy costs while dampening growth.
- The Labour government's approval rating had already collapsed to around 17%, and the leadership turmoil intensified speculation around a potential challenge, raising fears that fiscal discipline could be abandoned.
- The UK's persistent current account deficit and negative net investment position make sterling structurally vulnerable when global risk appetite deteriorates.
- The Bank of England faces a difficult policy dilemma: services inflation remains sticky (above 5%), wages elevated at 5.5–6%, yet slowing growth from 2025 tax increases argues for rate cuts.
- Potential Labour leadership change scenarios were discussed, including Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham entering Westminster and implications for fiscal policy and gilt markets.
- Despite the turbulence, UK Q1 2026 GDP showed a resilient 0.6% expansion, creating a mixed signal environment for markets and the Bank of England.

## Overview

Peter Schaffrik (Head of UK/European Rates & Economics) and Cathal Kennedy (Senior UK Economist) at RBC Capital Markets examine the simultaneous pressures hitting UK sterling markets: an oil price shock linked to Strait of Hormuz disruption and a deepening domestic political crisis surrounding the Labour government's leadership. The episode assesses the broader outlook for British markets and the UK's political landscape against a backdrop of sterling hitting five-week lows and oil approaching $100/barrel.

## Implications

The convergence of an oil shock and UK political instability creates a stagflationary risk that leaves the Bank of England with limited room to cut rates, while a potential Labour fiscal loosening could unsettle gilt markets and push sterling further lower—RBC sees GBP/EUR drifting toward 1.11 by end-2026 as the UK's weak fundamental backing makes it vulnerable in a risk-off environment.
