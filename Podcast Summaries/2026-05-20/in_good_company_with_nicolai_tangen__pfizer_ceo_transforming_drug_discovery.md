## Key Points

- Pfizer's COVID-19 vaccine success generated $56 billion in revenue in 2022 but dropped to roughly $12 billion in 2023, forcing a dramatic strategic pivot toward innovative medicine.
- Bourla declared Pfizer's new mission: "We saved the world from Covid, now we'll save the world from cancer," directing over 40% of annual R&D spending toward oncology.
- Pfizer embarked on a $23 billion acquisition spree in 2023, including winning a competitive bidding war against Novo Nordisk to acquire Metsera for GLP-1/obesity treatments.
- AI and machine learning are transforming drug discovery—Paxlovid's development was compressed from four years to four months using computational molecule design.
- China's rise in biotech is a pressing concern: China now files more patents than the US, leads in CRISPR research output, and one-third of pharma licensing deals in H1 2025 involved China-originated medicines.
- Bourla advocates strengthening US capabilities through faster drug development rather than restricting China, noting the issue has rare bipartisan consensus in Congress.
- Pfizer is targeting cancers it believes can be cured outright and expects remaining cancers to become manageable chronic diseases, similar to diabetes.
- During COVID vaccine development, Bourla employed "emotional blackmail"—making employees calculate the daily human cost of delays (~1,800 American deaths per day) to overcome resistance to seemingly impossible goals.
- Bourla reached a 10-day drug pricing agreement with the Trump administration to address affordability concerns while protecting biotech sector funding.
- His leadership philosophy centers on optimism: "Pessimists are usually right. But nothing great on this earth has been accomplished without an optimist behind it."

## Overview

Norges Bank Investment Management CEO Nicolai Tangen sits down with Pfizer Chairman and CEO Albert Bourla for a wide-ranging conversation on leadership, science, and the future of global healthcare. Bourla reflects on how leading Pfizer through the COVID-19 vaccine breakthrough transformed the company, and how it pivoted aggressively into oncology, obesity treatments, and AI-driven drug discovery. The discussion also explores the rapid rise of China in biotech, high-stakes acquisition decisions, and how Bourla's Holocaust-survivor mother underpins his philosophy that optimism is a strategic leadership advantage.

## Implications

Pfizer's aggressive post-COVID repositioning into oncology, obesity, and AI-driven drug discovery represents a high-stakes bet on the next pharmaceutical frontier, with China's accelerating biotech capabilities adding urgency to the need for both corporate and government innovation strategies. Bourla's leadership model—combining bold goal-setting, emotional motivation, and institutionalized optimism—offers a case study in navigating organizations through existential revenue crises toward new growth trajectories.
