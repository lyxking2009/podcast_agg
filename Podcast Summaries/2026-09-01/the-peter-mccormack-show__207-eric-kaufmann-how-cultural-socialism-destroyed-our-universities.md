# #207 - Eric Kaufmann - How 'Cultural Socialism' Destroyed Our Universities — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR
Eric Kaufmann argues that universities and wider culture have been captured by 'cultural socialism'—an ideology that prioritizes equal outcomes and emotional harm protection for historically marginalized groups over merit and standards. He uses the Jason Arday plagiarism scandal as an extreme symptom, citing data showing social science papers have become 50% political with a 90% left slant, and that 80% of young Brits have been exposed to critical race and gender terms in school. Kaufmann calls for political intervention and funding rebalancing to restore viewpoint diversity and reverse institutional capture.

## Key points
- The episode centers on Jason Arday, a Cambridge professor accused of extensive plagiarism; Kaufmann argues Arday is an extreme symptom of systemic 'cultural socialism' (EDI) that prioritizes equal outcomes and emotional harm protection for historically marginalized groups over merit and standards.
- Arday's hiring and career advancement through multiple UK universities reflect a system where race plus ideology confers social capital; ticking ideological boxes allows substandard scholarship to pass, while challenging it risks accusations of racism and ostracism.
- An AI-based meta-analysis of social science papers from 1960 to 2025 found political content rose from 5% to 50%, with a 90% left vs 2-6% right slant; top sociology journals are now dominated by inequality studies, ignoring other classic concerns.
- The left-right ratio in social sciences is about 14:1, and at Harvard political donations show a 96:3 Democrat to Republican ratio among faculty, creating an ideological bubble where radicalism thrives unchallenged.
- Surveys show 80% of 18-20 year olds have heard at least one of six critical race and gender terms from an adult at school, and 70% of the time without a counterargument, indicating indoctrination at scale in British schools.
- Kaufmann argues the Tory government was ineffective because legal definitions of political indoctrination were too narrow; only executive power and funding leverage, as used by Trump administration and red states, can reverse institutional capture.
- In Britain and Canada, unlike the US, EDI keywords in research funding continue to rise, while in the US they fell sharply after 2021 and especially after Trump; billions are being spent on ideologically skewed research.
- Trust in social science and humanities professors among 2019 Tory and Brexit party voters fell from two-thirds in 2021 to 57% after the Arday affair, but many parents still don't realize how ideologically skewed universities are.
- Kaufmann recommends a rebalancing rather than affirmative action for conservatives: funding neglected research topics, creating positions in red state universities, and building a new pipeline of doctoral students to study non-left perspectives.
- He distinguishes between ethnic nationalism and 'ethnotraditional nationalism,' noting many non-white and minority voters are also concerned about rapid ethnic change and support preserving traditional British composition, as shown by Trump's Hispanic support.
- The trans issue is the left's first major public opinion loss in 60 years; public opinion shifted 20 points against activist positions in three years, but progressives cannot abandon it because it is tied to their historical momentum.
- Kaufmann says the progressive left is the larger source of polarization because they live in bubbles and moralize immigration restrictions, while the right often uses respectable but false reasons (public services) to avoid discussing ethnicity and culture.
- He argues we must dismantle the taboo around race and immigration by distinguishing system-level collective composition from individual membership; e.g., a Muslim can be an equal British citizen, but if Britain becomes majority Muslim it will be less British.
- The episode ends with Kaufmann discussing his heterodox social science conferences, which aim to create infrastructure and funding for non-left scholarship, with the next planned in Austin.
