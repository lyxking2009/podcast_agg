# How Cory Klippsten Would Decide How to Secure Bitcoin Post-Coldcard — Unchained

## TL;DR
Cory Klippsten, CEO of Swan, discusses Bitcoin's 23% rally to $80K after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's debt-buyback comments, expresses skepticism of stock-to-flow and power-law price models, and analyzes the ColdCard hack. He advocates for self-custody, introduces Swan's RBX tool for converting GBTC to real Bitcoin, outlines a five-step custody spectrum, and dismisses quantum threats as overhyped.

## Key points
- Bitcoin rallied 23% in a week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced doubling of long-term debt buybacks from $2B to $4B per operation; Klippsten attributes the move to a short liquidation cascade and spot buying without leverage, with price settling around $80K.
- Despite $3B in ETF inflows over two weeks, on-chain data shows coins moving to exchanges; average ETF buyer purchase price was $87K, suggesting resistance in the 80-82K range; Klippsten is not in the camp expecting an all-time high this year.
- Klippsten criticizes stock-to-flow and power-law price models as unfalsifiable 'false gods' that overfit historical data (R^2 > 0.95) but lack predictive power; he advises buying Bitcoin based on fixed supply and long-term adoption instead of model-driven trading.
- The ColdCard hack resulted in over 1,000 bitcoins lost from self-custody; largest individual loss was 60 BTC; total centralized exchange losses exceed 1.5 million BTC, making this incident 1,000x smaller in magnitude.
- ColdCard is not used by institutional custodians like Swan Vault due to its small team (5-6 people) and lack of security certifications; the hack primarily affected retail self-custody users, and Klippsten still advocates self-custody as the long-term value proposition.
- Swan's RBX (Real Bitcoin Exchange) allows GBTC shareholders to convert their shares into actual on-chain Bitcoin in a tax-advantaged manner; the process has been streamlined from a 53-step manual process to a 24-step process taking 2-3 days; GBTC still holds $8.5B with fees 10x higher than competitors.
- Klippsten outlines a custody spectrum: paper Bitcoin (ETFs), supported self-custody (Swan Sovereign), collaborative multi-sig (Swan Vault, Casa, Unchained), delegated custody (Swan Safe, Coinbase), and multi-institutional multi-sig (Swan Trinity with BitGo and a UK partner, launching Q4).
- On quantum threats, Klippsten dismisses near-term risk as 'manufactured bullshit,' noting no serious quantum researcher sees an imminent threat; he argues the Bitcoin community could coordinate quickly if an existential threat emerged, and rejects freezing or tail-emitting Satoshi's coins, preferring to absorb any dump of the ~1M coins split across 22,000 addresses.
- Regarding Bitcoin's security budget, Klippsten argues the fee market will become robust as block space becomes scarce; he notes Bitcoin price has risen 20x in 8 years while miner revenue needed only 4x growth to stay stable, so no need to fix a problem that may not exist for 20-30 years.
