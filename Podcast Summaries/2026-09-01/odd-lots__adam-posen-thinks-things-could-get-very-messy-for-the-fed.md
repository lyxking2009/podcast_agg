# Adam Posen Thinks Things Could Get Very 'Messy' for the Fed — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Adam Posen, president of the Peterson Institute, discusses Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, grading it a B-minus but seeing it as a positive pivot after months of confusion. He expects the Fed to hike rates in September and December due to persistent inflation, and worries Warsh's desire for maximum discretion could personalize Fed credibility. Posen also weighs in on AI's economic impact, the communications task force, and political threats to Fed independence.

## Key points
- Posen grades Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole speech a B-minus, but says it could be upgraded to a B-plus if it proves to be a pivot point after Warsh's earlier missteps and July press conference confusion.
- Warsh's speech set up a likely rate hike by listing reasons inflation may persist, but he did not explicitly commit to one, leaving room for questions about political influence or being ahead of the committee.
- Warsh clarified core PCE 2% as the target, stated wage inflation is not a good predictor of overall inflation, and acknowledged financial conditions are important—positions Posen views as mainstream and credibility-enhancing.
- Posen is concerned that Warsh is trying to maximize last-minute discretion, similar to Alan Greenspan, which risks making the Fed's credibility overly dependent on one person and undermines committee accountability.
- The Fed's institutional norm is that the chair never loses a vote and dissent is limited, so a chair seeking maximum discretion could suppress healthy debate and lead to policy mistakes.
- Posen agrees with Warsh's claim that inflation has been above target for 64-65 months and that the Fed is at fault; he argues the Fed was late to hike in 2022 and premature in cutting rates last year.
- International comparisons show the Fed is an outlier on inflation after controlling for energy dependence, initial inflation levels, and fiscal policy; U.S. fiscal blowouts under Biden and Trump's tax cuts add inflationary pressure.
- Posen identifies political interference as ranging from normal scapegoating to unprecedented threats under Trump, including attacks on individual Fed governors, threats to replace regional Fed presidents, and risks of fiscal dominance.
- The communications task force led by Mervyn King and Peter Fisher is likely to produce radical recommendations; both have become skeptical of central bank forecasts and forward guidance, favoring less information and more market discipline.
- On AI, Posen sees a brain drain of economists to tech companies as concerning but not necessarily corrupt; he notes no visible job displacement yet, citing messy jobs theory and a J-curve for productivity gains.
- Posen acknowledges the 0.3% GDP undercount from NVIDIA chips matters for productivity measurement but is not a major distortion; the real productivity boost from AI will come when businesses transform their operations.
- Posen expects the Fed to hike rates in both September and December, leading to a 75-100 basis point increase over six months; he sees inflation staying in the 3.5-4.5% range until hikes take effect.
