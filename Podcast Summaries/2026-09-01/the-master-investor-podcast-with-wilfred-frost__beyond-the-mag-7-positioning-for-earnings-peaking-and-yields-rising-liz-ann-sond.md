## TL;DR
Liz Ann Sonders, Chief Investment Strategist at Charles Schwab, discusses why rising global bond yields may not derail equities if the move remains orderly, citing 5% on the US 10-year Treasury as a key level. She expects Kevin Warsh and the Federal Reserve to have more market impact than Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, believing Warsh remains hawkish and likely to hike rates soon, and warns that the AI-driven concentration of S&P 500 earnings among mega-caps may be peaking, creating risk but also opportunity as the market broadens. ## Key points
- Liz Ann Sonders is Chief Investment Strategist at Charles Schwab, which oversees $13.4 trillion in client assets.
- Global bond yields are surging, and while rising yields don't necessarily spell trouble for equities, the orderliness of the move matters; 5% on the US 10-year Treasury is an important level to watch.
- Sonders argues the Federal Reserve under Kevin Warsh will have more market impact than Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and the Treasury.
- She describes Warsh, whom she knows personally, as still a hawk at heart who wants to limit inflation and expects a rate hike soon.
- Historical analysis of Fed hiking cycles suggests the key question is whether the Fed takes an 'escalator or elevator' approach—gradual hikes tend to be more tolerable for equities than abrupt ones.
- The AI trade has driven a growing concentration of earnings growth among a small group of mega-cap companies, which still account for a disproportionately large share of overall S&P 500 earnings growth.
- Sonders warns of a coming inflection point in AI-driven earnings growth, which carries significant risk given that concentration.
- However, market broadening has already begun, and she sees significant opportunities in rotation away from mega-caps.
- She remains constructive on US equities long-term and advises against trying to time the market in the short term.
