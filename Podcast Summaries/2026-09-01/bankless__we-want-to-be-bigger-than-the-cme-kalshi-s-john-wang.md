# "We Want to Be Bigger Than the CME" | Kalshi's John Wang — Bankless

## TL;DR
Kalshi's John Wang discusses the regulated onshore strategy behind Kalshi's prediction markets and its new perpetual futures exchange, contrasting it with offshore rivals like Polymarket. He highlights Kalshi's 90% crypto prediction market share, deep partnerships with Coinbase and Robinhood Crypto, and plans to expand into commodities and institutional hedging to rival the CME. The conversation covers insider trading guardrails, international expansion via local brokers, and how prediction markets can climb the capital stack from retail to institutional block trades.

## Key points
- Kalshi is not an on-chain company but is deeply embedded in crypto: crypto is its second-largest business line behind sports, with 90% market share in crypto predictions; partners like Coinbase, Robinhood Crypto, and Phantom route crypto orders through Kalshi. Crypto rails are used for deposits and withdrawals, and are the only on-ramp in 140 countries internationally.
- Kalshi launched the first regulated perpetual futures exchange in the U.S., accessible via app store without VPN for retail and compliantly available to institutions. Fees are currently lower than Binance and Hyperliquid, liquidity supports six-figure orders with minimal slippage, and leverage limit is 6x (capturing 70–80% of traders who use 2–4x leverage). Non-crypto perps like gold and silver will have higher leverage due to lower volatility.
- Kalshi's international strategy is a long-term onshore approach: working with local regulators and partnering with the largest brokers in each country, such as Wealthsimple in Canada and XP in Brazil, to embed prediction markets inside existing products and unlock entire countries' tradable user bases.
- The Kalshi/Polymarket comparison to Circle/Tether is partly apt: Kalshi is the compliant onshore version like Circle, while Polymarket is the offshore version like Tether. John Wang says the analogy breaks down because stablecoins are a global dollar product with uniform demand, whereas prediction markets need localized events; however, the strategic approach is similar. Kalshi is lean and aggressive with ~180 employees, 20–40x smaller than Coinbase or Circle, and even sued the CFTC to legalize prediction markets.
- The steel man for Kalshi's onshore compliant strategy is that to reach the 99% TAM beyond crypto-native users, you need regulated infrastructure that allows paid ads, billboards, app store listing, partnerships with household names, and broker integrations. Offshore VPN platforms only attract crypto natives already used to such hoops.
- Kalshi draws a bright red line on insider trading using surveillance lists of politicians, campaign staff, sports team personnel, and corporate executives; politicians are prohibited from trading political markets. The rationale is market structure: allowing toxic informed flow causes liquidity providers to pull quotes, destroying liquidity and the price signal, which prevents scaling.
- In response to arguments that a little insider trading improves information aggregation (Robin Hanson's view), Wang argues such markets would remain small thought experiments and never achieve multi-billion dollar liquidity and scale. Regulation is necessary for market integrity and long-term growth.
- Kalshi distinguishes between shrewd public research and prohibited insider trading: the French trader who financed U.S. swing-state polls before the 2024 election was not insider trading, but a Google employee selling internal model release dates was selling company-owned IP. Kalshi supports regulatory clarity and has been pushing for codified rules.
- Kalshi's ambition is to be the largest exchange on the planet, similar to CME/NYSE. Institutional adoption is already occurring through block trades, broker relationships in all largest U.S. brokers, and integration into SCMs/trading terminals used by hedge funds. Examples include a Houston environmental hedge fund block trade hedging California carbon allowance auction prices, and an ice cream shop and bars hedging weather/World Cup promotions.
- Institutional clients can request bespoke event contracts from Kalshi, which are then opened to public lit markets for better pricing and liquidity. This aims to bring OTC structured products and bilaterally negotiated derivatives into transparent, liquid markets, increasing TAM by an order of magnitude. The top institutional request is margin on prediction markets, which Kalshi is working to provide.
- Kalshi is diversifying beyond sports: crypto prediction markets are 20–30% of total exchange volume, commodities markets grew 100x in the first days compared to crypto launch, and perps already do an order of magnitude more volume than prediction markets in the first month. Sports could fall to 20–30% as other categories grow.
- Kalshi has pulled ahead of Polymarket after a period of mirrored partnership announcements (XAI, NHL, pop-up grocery stores). Wang says Kalshi is now 4–5x larger overall and 10x larger in crypto volumes; partners usually choose Kalshi. Polymarket has not yet released regulated perps or matched Kalshi's institutional adoption and compute forward curve.
