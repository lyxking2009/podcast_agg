# David Byrne Explains How Forgotten Ideas Come Back to Life — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
David Byrne discusses his book 'Sleeping Beauties' about why good ideas go dormant and how they wake up, using examples like Hyman Minsky's financial theories, Moby Dick, and Roman concrete. He explores how crises, cross-disciplinary thinking, and technological shifts lead to rediscovery, and reflects on his own career's cycles of obscurity and revival.

## Key points
- A 'Sleeping Beauty' is an idea, artwork, or discovery that gained some attention when first introduced, then faded into obscurity before being revived because it becomes relevant again. Byrne's book examines not only why these ideas were forgotten—often due to institutional hubris, racism, sexism, or misplaced confidence in modern superiority—but, more importantly, how they are rediscovered.
- Hyman Minsky's work on financial instability, done in the 1960s, was largely ignored until the 2008 financial crisis. His behavioral economics insights argued that people do not always act rationally and tend to assume boom conditions will continue indefinitely, leading to excess risk-taking. The 2008 crash created a 'clearing in the woods' where old ideas like Minsky's suddenly became valuable for understanding the present.
- Moby Dick was a commercial and critical failure when first published, partly because the English edition omitted the epilogue in which Ishmael survives, leading reviewers to dismiss it as logically flawed. Herman Melville died in obscurity, but after World War I, readers turned to the book to understand the psychological and colonial forces behind mass violence, reviving its reputation.
- Roman concrete used a self-healing lime-clast mixture that has allowed structures like the Pantheon to last 2,000 years, whereas modern Portland cement concrete reinforced with rebar is rated for only 50–100 years. MIT researchers recently rediscovered the Roman formula and started a company to produce it, showing how ancient technology can solve modern problems like durability and CO2 emissions.
- Byrne highlights several mechanisms of rediscovery: crises and wars force societies to question assumptions; cross-disciplinary and pluralistic approaches allow ideas to migrate between fields; and digitization and AI can surface patterns that humans might miss. However, information overload can hide valuable signals, and vested interests may suppress superior ideas, as with early solar technology or Roman concrete.
- Byrne draws on his own career as an example of a 'sleeping beauty' in culture. His popularity waned in the 1990s before rising again, and the 2023 re-release of Stop Making Sense introduced Talking Heads to a new generation. He notes that streaming removes context but also allows listeners to discover music from any era purely on its own merits.
