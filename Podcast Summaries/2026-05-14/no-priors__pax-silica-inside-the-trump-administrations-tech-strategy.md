## Overview

Jacob Helberg discusses Pax Silica, an ambitious 14-country coalition aimed at securing AI supply chains for the United States and its allies. The strategy centers on creating forward-deployed industrial bases, starting with a 4,000-acre economic security zone in the Philippines that will operate under U.S. diplomatic custody before transitioning to a long-term commercial framework. This approach deliberately contrasts with China's Belt and Road Initiative by prioritizing private sector leadership over government-operated infrastructure, aiming for commercially viable platforms that can eventually operate independently.

The conversation reveals a comprehensive strategy that spans from critical minerals and rare earth processing to robotics manufacturing, with particular emphasis on reducing concentration risk in supply chains currently dominated by China. Helberg emphasizes the role of venture capital and private investors as crucial partners, both for assessing execution capabilities of potential partners and for driving innovation in areas like rare earth-free materials. The discussion also touches on broader themes of American competitiveness, with Helberg arguing that America's strength lies in its 'underdog mentality' and private sector innovation capabilities.

## Implications

For founders and entrepreneurs, Pax Silica represents significant partnership opportunities in supply chain security, particularly in robotics, critical minerals processing, and manufacturing infrastructure. The State Department is actively seeking private sector feedback on market access barriers and supply chain strategies, offering potential paths for expanded international market entry. Tech companies should expect increased government support for supply chain diversification away from China, with new economic security zones providing manufacturing alternatives. VCs and investors have an opportunity to play a strategic role in assessing and funding companies that can execute on supply chain security initiatives, potentially with government capital allocation influenced by private sector due diligence. The emphasis on commercially viable, private sector-led solutions suggests that successful approaches will need to be profitable and scalable beyond government contracts.
