# The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires) — TIP814: Formula One Group (FWONA): The Only Sports Franchise Worth Owning

## Key Points

- Formula One Group owns exclusive commercial rights to F1 racing for the next 86 years (through 2110), not the teams or athletes — a capital-light, highly durable business model.
- Only 24 events per year generate billions in revenue with free cash flow margins exceeding 24%, an enviable metric for any business.
- Revenue has compounded at approximately 70% annually since Liberty Media acquired the business in 2017.
- Global fan base estimated at ~800 million, with strong pricing power in race promotion fees, broadcast rights, and sponsorships.
- Netflix's Drive to Survive series was a major growth catalyst, dramatically increasing North American awareness of a 75-year-old sport.
- Key risks include limited event count (growth depends on raising fees per race or adding more races), complex capital structure under Liberty Media, and significant debt.
- The hosts debate whether current market prices exceed conservative intrinsic value estimates based on future cash flow projections.
- Recent growth also includes the MotoGP acquisition, which expands the portfolio beyond F1.

## Overview

Kyle Grieve and Shawn O'Malley analyze Formula One Group (ticker: FWONA), a Liberty Media subsidiary that holds the exclusive commercial rights to the FIA Formula One World Championship. The conversation opens by establishing the business's unique position: it owns no teams, no athletes, no tracks — just the right to broadcast, promote, and monetize every race. With only 24 events annually, F1 generates billions in revenue with over 24% free cash flow margins, a testament to its scarcity premium and pricing power.

The hosts trace the company's growth trajectory since Liberty Media's 2017 acquisition, noting the 70%+ annual revenue compound and the transformative impact of Netflix's Drive to Survive in expanding the North American fan base. The middle section examines the competitive moats: multi-year contracts providing revenue visibility, inflation-protected pricing, and high barriers to entry that effectively insulate the sport from competition. They then discuss the complex capital structure and significant debt load, weighing the risks against the durable revenue streams. The valuation discussion is contentious — while the business is clearly high-quality, current prices may exceed conservative intrinsic value estimates.

## Implications

For value investors: Formula One Group represents a rare combination of scarcity, durability, and growth — but at current prices, the margin of safety may be thin. The business is best evaluated as a long-duration compounder rather than a value play. The 86-year exclusive rights provide unusual revenue visibility, but the complex Liberty Media capital structure requires careful analysis. For sports/media analysts: F1's low-event-count, high-revenue-per-event model is fundamentally different from traditional sports leagues and may warrant different valuation multiples. The Drive to Survive catalyst is likely partially priced in — future growth depends on raising per-race economics rather than fan base expansion alone.
