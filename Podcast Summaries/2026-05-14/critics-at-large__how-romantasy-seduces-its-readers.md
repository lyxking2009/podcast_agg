# Critics at Large | The New Yorker — How Romantasy Seduces Its Readers

## Key Points

- Romantasy (romance + fantasy) is a massive publishing phenomenon with 5 of the top 10 adult bestsellers in 2023 coming from just two authors
- Rebecca Yaros' latest book sold 2.7 million copies in first week, making it the fastest-selling adult novel in 20 years
- Print romance sales more than doubled between 2020-2023, driven largely by COVID-era reading habits
- The genre is heavily trope-driven and amplified by BookTok, where readers use hashtags to find specific story elements
- Authors increasingly write to satisfy reader demands expressed on social media platforms
- Books feature 'chosen one' narratives with ordinary women discovering magical powers and romantic relationships with supernatural beings
- The genre provides escapism and empowerment fantasy, particularly appealing to millennial women facing career disappointments
- Community building around romantasy includes costume events, theorizing, and dedicated bookstores

## Overview

Critics at Large explores the explosive growth of romantasy, a genre blending romance and fantasy that has become a dominant force in publishing. The hosts, joined by New Yorker staff writer Katy Waldman, examine three major romantasy novels while discussing the genre's trope-driven nature and its symbiotic relationship with social media platforms like BookTok. The conversation reveals how romantasy serves as both escapist entertainment and community-building tool, particularly resonating with readers seeking empowerment fantasies in worlds where ordinary women discover extraordinary powers and find ideal romantic partners. The discussion contextualizes the genre's rise within broader cultural anxieties about modern life's limitations, comparing it to historical fantasy phenomena while questioning whether this represents genuine literary expression or algorithm-driven content creation.

## Implications

For product builders and founders, romantasy's success demonstrates the power of community-driven content discovery and the importance of understanding micro-audiences with specific preferences. The genre's trope-based structure and social media amplification provide a blueprint for how content can be systematically optimized for audience engagement. The phenomenon highlights opportunities in niche community platforms, content recommendation systems, and the creator economy where understanding granular user preferences can drive massive scale.
