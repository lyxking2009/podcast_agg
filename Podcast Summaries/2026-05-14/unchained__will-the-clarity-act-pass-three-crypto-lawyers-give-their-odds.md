# Unchained (DEX in the City) — Will the CLARITY Act Pass? Three Crypto Lawyers Give Their Odds

## Key Points

- A new 300+ page draft of the CLARITY Act has been released, with key provisions including the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act's specific intent standard for charging developers — prosecutors must prove the developer intended to commit a crime, rather than relying on strict liability.
- Circle's decision to issue a token as a public company signals a real shift in how the SEC thinks about the security/token distinction.
- Chair Atkins' remarks at the SEC's AI+ Expo suggest the SEC and Congress are now in lockstep on modernizing securities regulation for on-chain infrastructure.
- For the first time, on-chain vaults are explicitly included in the regulatory infrastructure being considered.
- The three lawyers debate the probability of the CLARITY Act passing and give individual odds during the episode.
- The specific developer protection provision in the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act is identified as the most consequential provision for DeFi builders.

## Overview

KK (Katherine Kirkpatrick Bos), Vy (TuongVy Le), and guest Josh Riezman of GSR dive into the newly released 300+ page draft of the CLARITY Act, the most comprehensive US crypto market structure bill to date. The conversation centers on the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act's specific intent standard — a provision that would require prosecutors to prove a developer intended to commit a crime, fundamentally changing the liability landscape for DeFi and blockchain builders.

The group then turns to a broader theme: Circle releasing a token as a public company represents a paradigm shift in how the SEC approaches the security/token distinction. Chair Atkins' speech at the SEC's AI+ Expo is analyzed as evidence that the SEC and Congress are now aligned on modernizing securities regulation, and that on-chain infrastructure — including vaults — is now explicitly part of the regulatory conversation. The episode also covers the politics of passage, with each lawyer giving their odds and explaining where the deal-making challenges remain.

## Implications

For DeFi developers and crypto founders: the specific intent standard in the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act is the most consequential provision to watch — it would fundamentally change your legal exposure. If passed, it would require prosecutors to prove malicious intent rather than relying on strict liability for code that could be used for illicit purposes. For investors: Circle's evolution as a public company issuing tokens signals SEC comfort with well-regulated entities in the token space, potentially opening the door for other compliant token issuances. For policy watchers: the alignment between the SEC and Congress described in the episode, if accurate, represents a genuine breakthrough after years of regulatory uncertainty in crypto.
