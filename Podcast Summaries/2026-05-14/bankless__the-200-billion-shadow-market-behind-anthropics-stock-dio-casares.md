# Bankless — The $200 Billion Shadow Market Behind Anthropic's Stock | Dio Casares

## Key Points

- Anthropic's secondary market is valued in the tens of billions of dollars, with SPVs stacked on top of SPVs charging 10% one-time fees plus carry — on a $10B round, that's $1B in fees up for grabs.
- The market operates in two tiers: company-approved deals that appear on the cap table (via large PE firms and employee tenders up to $30M), and an unauthorized 'dark market' using platforms like Forge and Hiive that bypass KYC and undercut round prices by ~20%.
- Fraud is rampant: 10-20% of executed deals involve fake share certificates, with Anthropic sending cease-and-desist letters to unauthorized platforms.
- Private market raises now exceed IPO raises — over $200B in recorded secondary and primary private transactions combined.
- The SPV nesting-doll structure creates liquidity delays (3 days to 2 weeks at IPO); third-layer SPV buyers face weeks or months before receiving liquid shares.
- Forward contracts and 'tokenized' private shares carry high risk: if an employee is fired or shares are rescinded (as seen at xAI, OpenAI), the buyer gets nothing but a legal claim.
- Middlemen are highly motivated — one broker made more brokering a single secondary deal than their entire net worth from working through their 20s.
- Anthropic has conducted official employee tenders allowing up to $30M in shares at round price to keep deals 'on-cap table' and reduce off-the-books selling pressure.

## Overview

Dio Casares of Patagon Markets joins Bankless to pull back the curtain on the massive, opaque secondary market for Anthropic stock — and private AI companies more broadly. The conversation traces how a company like Anthropic, staying private at multi-hundred-billion-dollar valuations, creates a Wild West of intermediaries, layered SPVs, and opportunistic brokers. Casares explains the two-tier market structure: the approved primary secondaries where companies like Anthropic work with large PE firms to syndicate access, and the unauthorized 'dark market' where platforms like Forge and Hiive sell blocks of equity with minimal KYC at discounts that undermine the company's own valuation.

The middle section dives into the specific mechanics of SPV layering — how a buyer in a third-layer SPV faces weeks of liquidity delays post-IPO, how forward contracts leave investors with nothing if the employee-seller is terminated, and how 10-20% of deals involve outright fraud. Casares also covers the regulatory landscape: companies that are aware of unauthorized secondary markets and do nothing risk scrutiny, and the coming 'mess of lawsuits' when Anthropic finally IPOs. The episode closes with a look at the professionalization trend — platforms moving toward proper KYC and legal structuring — and the warning that tokenized 'perp' structures and unregistered broker-dealers are dangerous for retail investors.

## Implications

For investors seeking exposure to private AI giants: prioritize company-approved secondary offerings through vetted platforms like Patagon, Forge, or Hiive — but remain vigilant that 10-20% of deals involve fraud. Avoid forward contracts and tokenized private shares that lack direct legal claim to underlying equity. Before committing to an SPV, verify the fee structure and ensure the manager must distribute shares immediately after IPO, not hold for more carry. For founders and operators: the secondary market for your equity is forming with or without your blessing — companies that proactively offer employee tenders retain more control over their cap table narrative. For regulators: the $200B+ private market is operating with minimal oversight and significant fraud; the coming wave of IPO lawsuits will test current legal frameworks.
