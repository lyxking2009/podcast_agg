# Why Bitwise's CIO Says the Blue Wave Won't Derail Crypto's Bull Market — Unchained

## TL;DR
Bitwise CIO Matt Hougan tells Laura Shin crypto bottomed in June, that the blue wave may not matter, and lays out the math behind a $1.6 million Bitcoin target — after a $6.3bn Binance hack and a Democratic sweep that barely moved the price.

## Key points
- The setup: crypto absorbed the largest hack in its history — $6.3bn stolen from Binance — plus a Democratic sweep that dims CLARITY Act odds, and Bitcoin barely flinched.
- Hougan’s read: the market bottomed in June, and bad news has stopped mattering — a regime change in how crypto prices information.
- He lays out three AI scenarios he says all end well for Bitcoin, without requiring a single bull case for AI itself.
- Institutions aren’t asking about hacks; the average Bitwise client allocates only after about eight meetings, which tells you where the demand curve is.
- Quantum risk to Satoshi’s coins gets a direct treatment rather than a deferral.
- He explains why a few dozen public companies opted out of tokenised trading, and makes the case for Hyperliquid as the Amazon of finance.
- The $1.6m target comes from store-of-value math — the same framework that makes him ambivalent about owning Zcash alongside Bitcoin.

## Overview
A thesis-driven interview with the CIO of a crypto asset manager, anchored in the paradox of the week: maximal bad news producing minimal price damage. Hougan uses that as evidence for a maturing market, then extends it into a long-horizon valuation argument.

## Implications
The investable claim is that crypto has decoupled from its own headlines, which changes the risk model: hack and regulatory shocks may matter less than flows and institutional allocation cycles. The counter-risk is that “bad news stopped mattering” is itself a late-cycle signal.
