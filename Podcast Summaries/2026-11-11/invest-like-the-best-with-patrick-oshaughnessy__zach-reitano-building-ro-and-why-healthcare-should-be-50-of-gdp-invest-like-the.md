# Zach Reitano - Building Ro and Why Healthcare Should Be 50% of GDP - [Invest Like the Best, EP.500] — Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy

## TL;DR
Ro CEO Zach Reitano argues the central flaw in American healthcare is that the patient isn’t the customer — and points to GLP-1 prices falling from $1,300 to $149 once drugmakers sold direct as proof that consumer-facing payment changes outcomes.

## Key points
- The diagnosis: the patient isn’t the customer, so no one in the chain is incentivised to lower cost or improve experience for the person receiving care.
- Evidence for the thesis: when drugmakers sold GLP-1s direct to consumers, prices fell from $1,300 to $149 — a price collapse driven by distribution, not science.
- Reitano argues healthcare should be a far larger share of GDP, and expects roughly half of Americans to take a GLP-1 eventually.
- Judging technology by outcomes rather than inputs — his frame spans driverless cars to medicine, where inputs are easy to measure and outcomes are what matter.
- The 2022 refounding of Ro: from a stalled company to a $3bn run rate with fewer people.
- Marketing into stigma is a deliberate strategy — the Serena Williams and Megan Fox campaigns are about normalising the demand rather than hiding it.
- Personal thread: his father, a doctor, shaped his own aggressive health regimen; he wants to recreate that physician relationship in software.

## Overview
Reitano’s first long-form podcast in nine years, structured as an argument rather than a company pitch: what’s broken (the customer), what fixing it looks like (direct payment), and what it takes to run a business on that premise. O’Shaughnessy pushes on GLP-1 economics and the turnaround.

## Implications
The generalisable idea: in any market where the payer and the user differ, the product degrades toward the payer’s priorities. Consumer-direct distribution is a wedge because it re-aligns incentives — and it can move price by an order of magnitude.
