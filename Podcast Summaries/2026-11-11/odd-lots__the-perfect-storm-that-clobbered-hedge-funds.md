# The Perfect Storm That Clobbered Hedge Funds — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Tracy Alloway and Joe Weisenthal ask whether multi-strategy hedge funds were ever actually diversified, after October produced historic losses across the pod model — with Nishant Kumar and Michael Ball on how bad it was and what it revealed.

## Key points
- The trigger: October performance numbers showed historic losses at multi-strats, the funds sold as diversified and uncorrelated by design.
- Joe’s central question — could you have made money over the last 12–24 months without implicitly being long AI, and leveraged long AI at that?
- Tracy’s framing: multi-strats turned out to be momentum dressed up in diversified sheep’s clothing, because correlation is the hardest thing on Wall Street to actually model.
- Nishant Kumar on the reporting: in 16 years covering hedge funds he had never seen a week like it — his story roundup drew 750,000 views.
- The risk-manager problem: if every pod was expressing the same underlying exposure in different clothing (chips, rates, neoclouds), the firm’s aggregate risk was never what the model said.
- Michael Ball situates the washout in the broader macro tape — vol, the AI trade unwinding, and what happens to risk appetite afterward.

## Overview
Recorded 10 November, immediately after the October performance disclosures. The first half is the hedge-fund autopsy with Kumar; the second half zooms out to markets with Ball. The recurring theme is that diversification claims are only as good as the correlation assumptions underneath them.

## Implications
For allocators, the pod model’s promise of uncorrelated returns needs stress-testing on common-factor exposure, not on stated strategy labels. For everyone else: the AI trade was the single factor under a great deal of apparent diversification.
