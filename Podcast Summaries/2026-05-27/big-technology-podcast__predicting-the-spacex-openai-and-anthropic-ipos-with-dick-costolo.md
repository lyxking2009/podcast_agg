# Predicting the SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic IPOs — With Dick Costolo

## Key Points
- Dick Costolo (former Twitter CEO) predicts SpaceX could be worth over $2 trillion on day one due to small float and massive demand, even if long-run math does not support the valuation
- The core IPO lesson from Twitter: the narrative set at the IPO becomes the quarterly measuring stick — Twitter was "dogged by MAU" for years because Costolo had framed a path to a billion users
- OpenAI faces the hardest public markets path: Sam Altman made concrete, named multi-hundred-billion dollar compute commitments that CFO Sarah Friar must reconcile with revenue growth on earnings calls
- Anthropic has the most coherent story of the three — consistent enterprise focus, Claude Code's perceived leadership in agentic coding, and no sprawling financial commitments to defend
- Going first to market matters: the first IPO accesses full liquidity; the third will face questions about available capital after the first two have absorbed their share
- A growing bipartisan "not in my backyard" backlash against data center construction is the most underappreciated risk to AI valuations — moratoriums are moving from local to national political platforms
- Platform economics question: Bill Gates defined a platform as one where participant revenues collectively exceed the platform's own; OpenAI's 89% share of AI startup revenue means it fails this test currently
- Meta is described as "Reels plus an AI project" — serial layoffs with shifting narratives are destroying employee trust more than any single large cut would
- Nikita Bier's product instincts credited with much of X/Twitter's resilience; text-based format has proven remarkably durable despite platform turbulence
- A San Francisco wealth bifurcation is forming: roughly 10,000 people at Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Nvidia, Meta have crossed $20M+ retirement wealth while everyone else feels permanently locked out

## Overview
Alex Kantrowitz hosts Dick Costolo for a deep dive on the upcoming AI IPO wave. Costolo draws on his 2013 Twitter IPO experience to argue that narrative discipline matters as much as financials — once you frame a metric in the S1, investors will hold you to it every quarter. The three companies get distinct assessments: Elon Musk's track record of sustaining long-horizon narratives gives SpaceX the most forgiving public market relationship. OpenAI's Sam Altman has made actual dollar commitments with counterparty names attached; the gap between those commitments and current revenue will be the subject of every analyst call. Anthropic's enterprise focus and consistent story, combined with Claude Code's agentic coding reputation, give it the most defensible positioning.

Capital sequencing is discussed as a real strategic variable: private market LPs who have been illiquid in VC funds for years will finally receive SpaceX and Anthropic distributions and recycle that capital into early-stage venture — creating a wave of new investment 6–12 months post-IPOs. The episode closes with Costolo on Meta's management dysfunction (layoffs with inconsistent rationales that "spin" rather than level with employees), the strange resilience of Twitter's text format, and a philosophical discussion on San Francisco's emerging winner-take-most wealth dynamic — where Costolo argues comparison-based misery is a losing game regardless of where one sits in the wealth stack.

## Implications
IPO ordering is a genuine strategic weapon. Anthropic's executives have discussed going before OpenAI so their story is not immediately benchmarked against OpenAI's capital commitments. If SpaceX absorbs market liquidity first at a $2 trillion valuation, subsequent offerings compete for a reduced pool. The data center backlash deserves serious attention as a regulatory risk — unlike abstract AI safety debates, "don't build this in my county" has concrete near-term legislative pathways and unites constituencies across the political spectrum.

OpenAI's agentic coding trajectory (Codex gaining share vs. Claude Code) could be the key financial swing factor: if Codex meaningfully closes the gap, OpenAI's revenue model improves substantially and the compute commitment math becomes more defensible. Anthropic's enterprise-first discipline may prove to be the right long-term positioning even if it means slower consumer growth.
