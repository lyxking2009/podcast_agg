# Prosus CEO: From Startup to Global Scale, Innovation and AI Transformation

## Key Points
- Fabricio Bloisi grew up in Bahia, Brazil, started programming at age eight or nine, studied computer science at Unicamp, and founded Movile upon graduation
- Movile grew into a group generating 20–25 billion dollars per year in sales, which became the foundation for Bloisi's ascent to running Prosus, a 150-billion-dollar company with 40,000 employees
- Prosus operates across food delivery (iFood, Swiggy, Just Eat), payments (PayU), and lifestyle services, serving roughly 1.5 billion people across Latin America, India, and Europe
- Bloisi describes a "jet ski" innovation model: small, fast-moving teams that pivot quickly rather than large slow structures, even inside a 40,000-person organisation
- Just Eat is being fundamentally rebuilt as an AI-first company, with AI embedded across operations, logistics, and customer experience
- Bloisi argues AI is still underhyped and most organisations are far behind where they need to be in adopting AI-first operating models
- His leadership philosophy centres on hiring people better than yourself, giving them radical empowerment, and never stopping to question the status quo
- India is a strategic priority for Prosus, targeting a 50-billion-dollar Indian portfolio within four years, backed by Swiggy and a new AI lab
- iFood commands roughly 100 million customers in Brazil with over 20 billion dollars in gross merchandise value
- Bloisi consistently credits culture—dreaming big and building high-performance teams—as the decisive advantage over capital or market selection

## Overview
Nicolai Tangen, CEO of Norges Bank Investment Management and host of In Good Company, sits down with Fabricio Bloisi, CEO of Prosus, to trace his journey from a child coding in Bahia to leading one of the world's largest technology holding companies. Bloisi grew up determined to build a major global company, and after studying at Unicamp and founding Movile, he spent years navigating near-bankruptcies and repeated failures before the group found its footing in mobile commerce and food delivery. Prosus—backed by Naspers—now owns or holds stakes in iFood (Brazil), Swiggy (India), Just Eat (Europe), PayU (emerging-market payments), and many others, collectively reaching over a billion users.

Bloisi shares the operational and cultural levers behind Prosus's scale. He articulates a belief that innovation at scale requires structural intentionality: his "jet ski" framework keeps small, autonomous teams in high-speed mode even within a large corporate structure, preventing the slowdown that typically accompanies growth. The episode dwells on his views about artificial intelligence, where Bloisi argues that AI adoption within enterprises is consistently underestimated and that companies treating AI as a feature rather than a foundational operating system will fall behind rapidly. Just Eat serves as his live case study—rather than treating it as a legacy asset, Prosus is rebuilding it from the inside as an AI-first business.

The conversation also covers specific market bets. India in particular is highlighted as a generational opportunity, connecting Prosus's venture strategy to long-term demographic and digital trends. Bloisi discusses working with the Indian government on AI policy and signals that the company is willing to concentrate bets rather than diversify for safety. Throughout, Bloisi returns to a consistent theme: the quality of people and culture is the only sustainable competitive advantage, more important than access to capital or the choice of market.

## Implications
For investors and observers of global technology, this episode illustrates how a holding company rooted in emerging markets can become a global-scale platform operator by combining patient capital with founder-led culture and aggressive AI adoption. Prosus's ambition to grow its Indian portfolio to 50 billion dollars in four years signals both confidence in India's digital trajectory and a willingness to make concentrated bets. The "jet ski" model is a meaningful organisational argument against the assumption that only very large teams can build at very large scale.

Bloisi's assertion that AI is still underhyped—at a moment when much of the investment world is debating AI bubbles—is a notable contrarian stance backed by a 150-billion-dollar operational reality. If companies of Prosus's scale are still in the early stages of embedding AI across their businesses, the implication is that AI-driven productivity gains and competitive reshuffling have significant runway ahead, and that the gap between AI-first and AI-adjacent companies will widen considerably.
