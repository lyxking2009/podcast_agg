## Key Points

- The AI industry is shifting from model-centric to agent-centric economics — the value is moving from raw intelligence to orchestration and tool use
- Agentic AI systems are creating new economic models where the cost of cognitive labor approaches zero, fundamentally changing software economics
- The post-agentic world requires rethinking pricing models — per-seat pricing breaks down when AI agents do the work
- Companies need to build moats around data, workflows, and integrations rather than model capabilities, which are commoditizing rapidly
- The shift creates both opportunity and disruption — startups can build agent-native products faster, but incumbents have distribution advantages

## Overview

This episode explores the economic transformation underway as AI agents become the dominant paradigm in software. The hosts discuss how the shift from model-centric to agent-centric AI is fundamentally changing software economics, with cognitive labor costs approaching zero. They examine new pricing models needed for an agent-driven world, the importance of building moats around data and workflows rather than model capabilities, and the competitive dynamics between startups and incumbents in this new landscape.

## Implications

Software companies that don't adapt their business models to the agentic AI paradigm risk being disrupted. The commoditization of AI models means the real value capture will be in orchestration, data, and workflow integration. Companies should focus on building defensible positions around unique data assets and deep workflow integrations.
