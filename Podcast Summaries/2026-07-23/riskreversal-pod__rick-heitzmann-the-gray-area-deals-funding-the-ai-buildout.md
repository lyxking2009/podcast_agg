## Key Points

- The AI infrastructure boom is being funded by circular financing arrangements — Nvidia backstopping Apollo's private credit for xAI, Meta's off-balance-sheet data center deals with KKR and Blue Owl
- Big Tech is hiding $1.65 trillion in debt through creative financing structures for AI infrastructure buildout
- The industry is shifting from 'tokenmaxxing' (maximizing compute) to an efficiency era driven by Chinese open-source models
- Memory stocks are showing frothiness as AI demand drives component shortages
- The IPO market after SpaceX could see a resurgence as AI infrastructure companies seek public capital
- SpaceXAI is exploring major data center expansion in Texas, signaling the convergence of space and AI infrastructure

## Overview

Dan Nathan sits down with Rick Heitzmann, co-founder and partner at FirstMark Capital, to kick off a new 'Okay, Computer' series on AI investing. They dig into the circular financing behind the AI infrastructure boom — from Nvidia backstopping Apollo's private credit for xAI to Meta's off-balance-sheet data center deals with KKR and Blue Owl. They discuss the shift from 'tokenmaxxing' to an efficiency era, the rise of Chinese open-source models, memory stock froth, and what's next for the IPO market after SpaceX.

## Implications

The circular financing arrangements in AI infrastructure could pose systemic risk if any major player falters. The shift to efficiency-era AI favors companies that can do more with less, potentially benefiting Chinese open-source model developers. Private credit exposure to AI buildouts is a growing concern for financial markets.
