# Economy of Scale: New Finance (Sponsored Content) — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
This sponsored episode of Economy of Scale features host Jacob Greaves in conversation with Sean McHugh of Dubai's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) and Rhys Merrick of Ripple. They discuss Dubai's emergence as a hub for virtual assets, noting VARA's growth from 14 to 57 licensed firms and $680 billion in regulated transaction volumes. The conversation covers tokenization's rapid growth from $1.5 billion in early 2023 to a projected $50 billion by 2026, the impact of institutional adoption after Bitcoin ETFs, and how regulatory clarity and stablecoins are reshaping cross-border payments.

## Key points
- Ripple, a digital asset infrastructure provider, uses XRP and the XRP Ledger to move funds cross-border in real time at low cost, and has expanded into a USD-backed stablecoin (RLUSD), custody infrastructure, and acquisitions over the past 12 months.
- VARA was established in 2022 as the world's first dedicated virtual asset regulator, with a mission to regulate and build Dubai's ecosystem in line with the D33 initiative; it has grown from 14 licensed firms to 57.
- Tokenization assets under management have surged from about $1.5 billion in early 2023 to an estimated $50 billion by 2026, according to Bloomberg Intelligence, driven by financial institutions seeking real utility rather than speculation.
- Major asset managers like Franklin Templeton and BlackRock are entering tokenization, adding credibility, while Dubai's Land Department is working with VARA, Ripple, and others to put real estate title deeds on chain as the first government entity to do so.
- The approval of Bitcoin ETFs changed the market irreversibly, encouraging institutional players such as digital-asset hedge funds and high-frequency traders to enter, and Ripple's conversations with large financial institutions have shifted from rejection to profit-and-loss ownership.
- Cross-border payment expectations now favor near-instant settlement; stablecoins are a major benefit because over 50% of global payments are settled in dollars, and many payments between non-US regions inefficiently route through the US.
- The Middle East-Africa trade corridor is growing 40–50% year on year, and Dubai's geographic position—4 hours from one-third of the world's population and 8 hours from two-thirds—attracts payment firms to obtain VARA licenses.
- VARA's regulatory approach combines principle-based rules with prescriptive licensing and a dedicated regulatory enablement team, aiming to provide clarity and adapt rules as technology evolves rather than acting as a "gotcha" regulator.
- VARA has recorded about $680 billion in regulated transaction volumes and by year-end expects every major retail platform in its ecosystem; it also wants to encourage startups and make Dubai the "Silicon Valley of blockchain technology."
- Rhys Merrick sees tokenized money market funds gaining traction and expects the use of tokenized assets as collateral and repo market growth to be a major next scalable area.
- Despite volatility in crypto markets, VARA emphasizes customer protection and requires firms to understand that being 100% invested in virtual assets means being 100% long volatility.
- Ripple chose Dubai because the UAE's non-oil trade is about $1 trillion and the regulatory environment enables scale; Ripple received a DFSA license as the first blockchain-enabled payment service provider in DIFC and has doubled its Dubai office headcount.
