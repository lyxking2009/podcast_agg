# #226 - Karl Friston - Top Neuroscientist: "AI Is Stunting the Human Brain" — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR
Karl Friston, a leading neuroscientist, argues that current AI lacks the active inference, agency, and curiosity that define natural intelligence, despite its brute-force scale. He warns that overexposure to generative AI and screens during development can stunt social-cue reading, curiosity, and neurodevelopment, undoing the coordination that drove human self-awareness. He advocates nurturing children's curiosity and agency rather than outsourcing learning to machines.

## Key points
- Friston contrasts natural intelligence with current AI by efficiency: the human brain runs on about 20 watts—the power of a small light bulb—whereas large language models require nuclear or gas turbine-scale energy, reflecting natural intelligence's pursuit of paths of least action and minimum redundancy.
- He defines intelligence operationally as 'inference to the best explanation,' which divides into sensemaking and decision-making; an intelligent agent must act on the world and sample data to resolve its uncertainty, a capacity current LLMs lack because they passively ingest all data without selecting what is informative.
- The brain is a constructive, statistical organ that predicts sensory inputs using a hierarchical generative model; this predictive-processing framework underlies perception, action, and even the sense of self, and it inspired deep-learning architectures such as attention heads in transformers.
- Friston argues there is no privileged access to objective reality; all experience is inferred, and perception can be described as controlled hallucination because the brain constructs its own version of the world based on sparse sensory data.
- The sense of self emerges to model other agents: because we must infer others' intentions to coordinate, we develop a model of ourselves; this shared 'we model' dissolves the infinite regress of modeling others modeling us and underpins theory of mind and morality.
- Social coordination and community were likely the selective pressures for human self-awareness; if young people lose face-to-face social-cue reading due to screens, it undoes this coordination, leaving a generation unable to read eye contact, micro-expressions, or emotional intentions.
- Friston warns that generative AI and social media can stunt neurodevelopment by removing the need to seek information actively; the LLM 'sees everything' while a child needs to learn where to look, and outsourcing curiosity to chatbots denies the acquisition of learning skills.
- He criticizes education systems that reward memorization: the important skill is curiosity and the journey of finding answers, not fact recall; schools should nurture curiosity rather than allow AI to shortcut the learning process.
- On AI alignment, Friston argues true alignment requires giving machines a minimal consciousness or selfhood so they can model users and have theory of mind; otherwise alignment is superficial because current AI lacks agency, consequences, and genuine curiosity.
- Friston dismisses hype around AI solving mathematics, noting current models solve puzzles and simulate known equations like Navier-Stokes rather than making new discoveries; machine learning 'learns the past' and cannot create the future without agency.
- He notes that generative AI's training data is becoming contaminated by AI-generated content, making pre-2021/2024 data more valuable, and leading to a potential stall in novel discovery if humans become dependent on frozen knowledge.
- Friston sees future AGI possibly emerging first as domestic companions—like smart pets or humanoid robots—because installing curiosity and alignment in artifacts would make them part of the family, not just tools.
