## Overview

Laura Shin interviews Carmen Li about her experience being courted by perpetual futures DEXs in the crypto market. Li, coming from traditional capital markets, was enthusiastic about the opportunity to license her index to crypto perp venues — until they asked her to also serve as market maker for her own index, a practice that would be a clear conflict of interest in traditional finance. She initially thought they were joking, then discovered that other index providers had already accepted similar arrangements. The conversation examines the cultural gap between TradFi standards (where index neutrality is fundamental) and crypto's perp market (where such conflicts are normalized), and what this means for retail traders and institutional adoption.

## Implications

This episode is a concrete case study of the institutional maturity gap in crypto derivatives. The fact that perp venues casually request that index providers market-make their own benchmarks — and that others have agreed — suggests the crypto derivatives market has structural integrity risks that aren't widely understood by retail participants. For institutional adoption to accelerate, these conflicts of interest need to be addressed through industry norms or regulation. Li's principled refusal is notable precisely because it's unusual in the current market structure.
