## Key Points

- Gavin Uberti and Rob Wachen, co-founders of Etched, built a working AI inference chip taped out on their first attempt — the first hardware company founded after ChatGPT to achieve this. They already have over $1 billion in customer demand and raised $800 million.
- Etched's architecture bets on low-voltage inference optimization, targeting transformer-specific workloads rather than general-purpose compute. This specialization allows them to achieve significantly better performance-per-watt than NVIDIA GPUs for inference tasks.
- The founders hired a mix of industry veterans and elite young engineers, relocating a dozen engineers to Bangalore for six months to accelerate development. Speed of execution was a core competitive advantage.
- Inference is projected to become one of the largest markets in the world as AI deployments scale. Etched designed their Gen 2 chip for gigawatt-scale production, anticipating trillion-dollar data center builds.
- The company's vertical integration approach covers the complete rack — chip, boards, power delivery, interconnects, and manufacturing — rather than just selling standalone chips.
- Patrick O'Shaughnessy noted that almost everyone he consulted before investing told him it could not be done — building a competitive AI chip against NVIDIA as a startup.

## Overview

Patrick O'Shaughnessy interviews Gavin Uberti and Rob Wachen, the 21-year-old co-founders of Etched, about their improbable journey building an AI inference chip that competes with NVIDIA. The episode covers the two key technical bets behind their architecture (low voltage and transformer specialization), how they recruited industry legends and paired them with elite young engineers, the culture of speed that overcame significant engineering and fundraising hurdles, and their vision for a world of billions of concurrent AI agents. The conversation explores TSMC relationships, the decision to move engineers to Bangalore for six months, raising the largest AI chip Series A ever, and designing Gen 2 for gigawatt-scale data center production.

## Implications

Etched represents the leading edge of the AI chip startup wave challenging NVIDIA's dominance in inference. Their success with a first-attempt tape-out and $1B+ demand signal that specialized inference hardware is a real market, not just a research curiosity. The low-voltage bet suggests that inference optimization is a fundamentally different design space from training, creating opportunities for focused startups. If Etched delivers on its roadmap, it could significantly reduce inference costs and accelerate the deployment of always-on AI agents at scale.
