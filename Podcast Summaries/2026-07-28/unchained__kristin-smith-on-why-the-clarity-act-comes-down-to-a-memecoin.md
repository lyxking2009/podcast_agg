# Kristin Smith on Why the Clarity Act Comes Down to a Memecoin

**Show:** Unchained
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Transcript source:** web_unchained

## Key Points
- The Clarity Act (crypto market structure legislation) needs 60 Senate votes to pass before an August recess deadline just weeks away, and Senate Majority Leader John Thune has signaled the votes likely won't be there in time.
- Polymarket prediction markets currently put the odds of Clarity Act passage this year at roughly 30 percent.
- A deal that once looked close has stalled over ethics language: Trump agreed to certain ethics provisions, but Senate Democrats do not trust the Department of Justice to actually enforce them.
- Smith walks through the vote math needed for passage: 53 Senate Republicans plus the Democrats who backed last year's Genius Act (stablecoin legislation) form the core coalition the bill needs.
- The bill would carry over Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act-style protections for software developers, shielding them from liability for code they don't control.
- The Clarity Act would create a new 'commodities pathway' letting token issuers launch and trade under commodities rules rather than being automatically treated as securities.
- The episode's framing ('comes down to a memecoin') points to how Trump's personal memecoin and other crypto ventures became the flashpoint in the ethics/conflict-of-interest standoff blocking the bill.
- Smith maps out where sidelined crypto capital and business activity could migrate — to markets like the Middle East and Japan — if the Clarity Act misses its window before the midterm elections.

## Overview
In this Unchained episode, host Laura Shin talks with Kristin Smith, President of the Solana Policy Institute (and former CEO of the Blockchain Association), about the stalled state of the Clarity Act, the crypto industry's marquee market structure bill. With an August recess deadline approaching, the bill needs 60 Senate votes, and Majority Leader John Thune has already signaled the chamber likely won't get there in time — a view reflected in Polymarket odds putting 2026 passage at only about 30 percent. Smith explains that the sticking point is no longer the substance of the crypto rules themselves but an ethics dispute: language Trump agreed to that would govern conflicts of interest tied to his own crypto business dealings, which Senate Democrats don't trust the DOJ to enforce impartially. She walks through the arithmetic Republicans need and describes substantive provisions still in play, including developer-liability protections and a new commodities pathway for token launches. The conversation closes on the stakes of inaction: if the bill's window closes before the midterms, capital and crypto business activity currently waiting on U.S. regulatory clarity will keep flowing to jurisdictions like the Middle East and Japan.

## Implications
The episode captures an unusual dynamic in U.S. crypto policy: a president's own financial entanglement in the industry has become the central obstacle to bipartisan market structure legislation the industry has lobbied for for years. That an ethics/conflicts-of-interest question, rather than a disagreement over SEC/CFTC jurisdiction, is now the binding constraint suggests the Clarity Act's fate is as much a story about presidential accountability politics as about crypto regulation itself. If the bill slips past the midterms, capital migration to the Middle East and Japan underscores real competitive costs of regulatory delay.
