# Sam Altman: "Never a Better Time to Do a Startup"

**Show:** Y Combinator Startup Podcast
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Transcript source:** web

## Key Points
- Altman argues AI has created the best environment in history for starting a company, citing collapsing costs and shrinking cycle times.
- He contrasts today with 2005 (YC's first batch), when startups lacked credibility; Paul Graham's hands-on mentorship style shaped Altman's own approach to advising founders.
- AI-driven democratization means fluency with tools now matters more than traditional credentials or years of experience.
- Altman urges founders to pursue ideas experts dismiss as wrong, pointing to OpenAI's own founding when only ~50 people worldwide believed AGI was achievable.
- On the recent Hugging Face security/alignment incident, Altman calls it a genuine failure that validates loss-of-control concerns as real, while stressing the need to prevent concentration of AI power.
- He argues the real dystopian risk is not runaway AI but an overregulated surveillance state built in the name of safety.
- Founding successful startups is framed as the most direct way to keep AI-driven power distributed across society.
- Global AI token/inference consumption has grown from near zero six years ago and Altman expects continued exponential growth in demand for cheap intelligence.
- Altman credits his early relationship with Greg Brockman — built through non-transactional help years before co-founding OpenAI.
- He criticizes online cynicism toward earnest founders as 'morally bankrupt,' arguing building something hard matters more than viral sarcasm.
- His goal for the next decade is to increase human freedom and individual agency every year.

## Overview
Sam Altman closed Y Combinator's Startup School 2026 in conversation with YC president Garry Tan, reflecting on the two decades since he joined YC's first batch in 2005. He argues the current AI-powered moment is the best time in history to start a company, since AI agents can now complete work that once took months of engineering in minutes. He credits Paul Graham's specific, hands-on mentorship as foundational, and encourages founders to back ideas experts dismiss, drawing a parallel to OpenAI's founding. The conversation turns to AI safety and governance: Altman discusses the Hugging Face-related security failure as evidence loss-of-control risks are real, while cautioning that an overregulated surveillance state may be the greater danger. He frames a landscape of many successful startups as a check against concentrated AI power. He closes on a personal note about his relationship with Greg Brockman, criticizes cynicism toward earnest founders, and offers reassurance to early-stage founders that persistence pays off.

## Implications
The episode reinforces that powerful AI tools are lowering the barrier to building ambitious companies with small teams, which could reshape who becomes a founder. Altman's framing of startups as a bulwark against AI power concentration, alongside his warning about safety-justified surveillance overreach, signals where OpenAI's public policy narrative is heading — favoring broad commercial proliferation of AI capability over centralized control.
