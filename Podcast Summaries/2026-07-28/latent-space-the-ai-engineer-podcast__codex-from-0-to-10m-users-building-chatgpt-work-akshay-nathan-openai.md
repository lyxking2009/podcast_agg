# Codex from 0 to 10M Users: Building ChatGPT Work — Akshay Nathan, OpenAI

**Show:** Latent Space: The AI Engineer Podcast
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Transcript source:** web_search_fallback

## Key Points
- OpenAI merged Codex and ChatGPT Work under a shared 'agent harness,' with UI differences on top of identical underlying capabilities.
- Codex unexpectedly gained traction among non-developers internally, prompting the pivot to a unified productivity platform.
- Non-developers now make up ~20% of Codex users and are growing 3x faster than developer users.
- Combined ChatGPT Work and Codex reached 10 million users within two weeks of the July 9 launch; Codex MAU grew more than 10x since January 2026.
- 'Sites' are positioned to replace traditional decks and spreadsheets, with agentic spreadsheet and Excel editing capabilities.
- Agent capabilities include sub-agents for parallel tasks, persistent computer environments with file storage, a plugin ecosystem, computer use, and MCP protocol support.
- Memory system is shared/inherited across ChatGPT and Work, plus a 'Chronicle' feature that learns from computer activity to reduce app fragmentation.
- Productivity measurement is shifting from proxy metrics (commits, story points) to outcome-based measurement.
- Roles are blurring between PM, engineering, and design as generalists with deep specialties become the norm, with ideas and taste becoming the primary bottleneck.

## Overview
This episode explores OpenAI's evolution from Codex, a developer-focused coding tool, into ChatGPT Work, a unified productivity platform for knowledge workers. Guest Akshay Nathan (Core Product Engineering Lead, OpenAI) explains how Codex unexpectedly gained traction among non-developers internally, prompting OpenAI to consolidate the experiences into a single platform sharing infrastructure while serving both technical and non-technical users, including agentic 'Sites,' spreadsheets, sub-agents, persistent environments, and a shared memory/Chronicle system.

## Implications
Signals a broader industry consolidation trend toward single unified agentic platforms replacing specialized tools, democratizing computational leverage for knowledge workers without requiring technical skill. It also points to a looming shift in productivity measurement away from commit/story-point metrics, a shift toward T-shaped generalist professionals, and rising importance of personal data/context integration and discoverable UX for flexible agent interfaces.
