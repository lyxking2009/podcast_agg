# Big Tech's Tipping Point Is Here: Jim Mellon On Biggest Short Of All Time

**Show:** The Master Investor Podcast with Wilfred Frost
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Transcript source:** youtube_autocaptions

## Key Points
- The 'Magnificent 7' mega-cap tech stocks are down an average of ~30% from their June 24 highs, which Mellon calls a broad market tipping point.
- Mellon calls SpaceX 'the biggest short of all time'; the stock is down ~50% from its high and has traded below its $135 IPO price, with a huge unlock of Musk's shares looming at the first quarterly results.
- Wall Street analyst price targets on SpaceX show the widest dispersion in stock market history ($80 to over $300), which Mellon attributes to banks writing bullish reports to protect investment-banking relationships.
- Semiconductor stocks are down ~20% from their peak; Apple has outperformed partly because its AI capex is roughly one-tenth of the other Mag 7 companies combined.
- On the Iran war, Mellon thinks the Strait of Hormuz is less critical than feared since workarounds exist; he speculates the pause in US strikes may reflect a Patriot missile shortage.
- Mellon is fading most of his oil & gas long positions but remains bullish on UK North Sea plays like Ithaca Energy.
- He sees rising bond yields as a longer-term debt problem, flags looming US social security insolvency, and prefers long-dated over short-dated bonds.
- He remains long-term bullish on the Japanese yen given cheap valuations and Japan's status as the world's largest international creditor.
- He is bullish on UK small-cap equities and sees potential further rallies in Japanese and Chinese equities.
- Top individual conviction ideas: ATAI Life Sciences/Beckley Psytech (subject to an Eli Lilly takeover bid) and Clean Food Group, a UK precision-fermentation company producing lab-made palm oil and cocoa butter.
- Overall market view for the rest of 2026 is cautious: markets are 'trading heavy' with retail speculation and margin debt at extremes.

## Overview
Jim Mellon returns to the Master Investor Podcast for his fourth appearance with host Wilfred Frost to argue that US markets, and mega-cap tech in particular, have reached a tipping point after years of concentrated gains. He points to the Magnificent 7's ~30% average decline since June highs and singles out SpaceX — down about 50% from its peak and trading below its IPO price, with a large Musk share unlock approaching at the first earnings report — as the key signal. The conversation moves through the reignited Iran conflict and its muted impact on oil, his active trading approach to oil, continued preference for UK North Sea energy names, rising bond yields as a slow-building debt problem, his long-standing bullish call on the yen, and preference for gold/silver mining equities over the metals themselves. On geography, Mellon favors UK small caps and sees room for upside in Japanese and Chinese equities. He highlights two personal investments — ATAI Life Sciences/Beckley Psytech and Clean Food Group — and closes with a caution against buy-and-hold complacency.

## Implications
Mellon's framing positions SpaceX's post-IPO collapse as a bellwether for a broader unwinding of speculative excess in US mega-cap technology, suggesting further rotation of capital away from concentrated US tech leadership toward cheaper markets. His comments on social security insolvency, elevated margin debt, and gamified retail trading point to structural fragilities that could compound any tech-led correction, while his yen and gold-miner theses reflect a bet on capital eventually flowing out of overextended US assets.
