# Paid Partnership: How Can DeFi Fix Its Liquidity Problem? 1inch's Aqua Offers a Solution

**Show:** Unchained
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Transcript source:** web_unchained

## Key Points
- This is a sponsored/paid-partnership episode featuring 1inch cofounder Sergej Kunz discussing the launch of Aqua, a self-custodial shared-liquidity protocol.
- Kunz frames DeFi's core problem as idle liquidity: as much as 83-95% of liquidity sitting in top pools across Uniswap and Curve is idle most of the year, with roughly $1.8 billion dormant in Uniswap v2 alone.
- The root cause is fragmentation — DeFi has more than 7 million pools scattered across protocols and chains, splitting liquidity into isolated buckets.
- Roughly half of liquidity providers lose money once impermanent loss is accounted for, and JIT (just-in-time) liquidity sniping by bots has cost LPs tens of millions of dollars.
- Aqua's fix is an intent-based, wallet-native model: a user approves a token balance from their own wallet, and that single balance can back multiple liquidity positions simultaneously — described as 'a virtual DEX engine running inside your wallet.'
- Kunz draws a historical parallel: in 2019, 1inch solved liquidity fragmentation for takers (traders); Aqua is meant to solve fragmentation for makers (liquidity providers).
- Because each Aqua position has a single owner, it is structurally protected from JIT fee-sniping the way pooled AMM liquidity is not.
- The episode covers sub-wallet infrastructure and compliance procedures aimed at institutional market makers.
- In 1inch's internal backtests, Aqua's model produced up to roughly 5x yield increases for LPs versus legacy AMM designs.
- Aqua rolled out publicly on July 28, 2026 across 13 EVM chains, including Base and Robinhood's chain.

## Overview
This sponsored Unchained episode features an interview with Sergej Kunz, cofounder of 1inch, about the public launch of Aqua, a shared-liquidity protocol meant to succeed Morpho-style pooled AMM designs. Kunz lays out DeFi's liquidity fragmentation problem — millions of scattered pools, mostly idle capital, and poor LP returns due to impermanent loss and bot-driven JIT sniping. Aqua's proposed solution is a wallet-native, intent-based 'registry' model where users approve a token balance directly from their wallet that can simultaneously back multiple positions without being locked into a single contract, framed as structurally resistant to JIT sniping since each position has a single owner. The episode also touches on sub-wallet infrastructure and compliance workflows for institutional market makers. Aqua went live publicly on July 28, 2026 across 13 EVM chains, with internal backtests cited showing roughly 5x yield improvements.

## Implications
As a sponsored episode, the statistics on idle liquidity and yield gains originate from company backtests rather than third-party audits, but the underlying liquidity-fragmentation problem is a widely acknowledged structural weakness in AMM-based DeFi. If Aqua's wallet-native model gains adoption, it could shift capital efficiency and design incentives across DeFi market-making broadly and lower the barrier for new DEXs to bootstrap liquidity, while the compliance tooling signals 1inch's push to court institutional liquidity providers.
