## Overview
In this episode, hosts Downtown Josh Brown and Michael Batnick preview the biggest earnings week of the summer, with Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Apple all reporting. They frame the debate around each company's AI story: whether Azure's growth rate justifies Microsoft's enormous AI infrastructure spending, whether Meta's AI-driven ad-targeting improvements can keep ad revenue growing, whether AWS is due to reaccelerate, and how Apple's more capital-light approach to AI compares as Tim Cook nears his last earnings call as CEO. The conversation broadens into market structure questions, including why stocks have kept shrugging off bad news. A significant chunk covers the sharp selloff in semiconductor names and Corning, connected to jitters over AI infrastructure spending and Nvidia's deal with OpenAI, plus Sam Altman's latest AI warnings and the Hugging Face controversy. The episode closes with Fed policy and rate-hike anxiety, and a forward look at SpaceX's first-ever earnings call.

## Implications
The episode captures a pivot point in the 2026 AI trade: after a long run where 'AI darling' mega-cap stocks were treated as nearly untouchable, cracks are forming as investors scrutinize whether hyperscaler capex is translating into commensurate revenue growth, while semiconductor and supply-chain names sell off on spending-sustainability fears. Apple's more conservative AI posture is presented as a potential hedge against this scrutiny, especially amid a leadership transition question around Tim Cook.
