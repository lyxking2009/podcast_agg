# Things You Thought You Knew – Your Zodiac is Wrong

**Show:** StarTalk Radio
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Transcript source:** web

## Key Points
- Special relativity shows that the faster you move relative to an observer, the slower your clock ticks for that observer; Einstein's original 1905 paper was titled 'On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies.'
- General relativity adds that stronger gravitational fields also slow time; GPS satellites experience faster-ticking clocks than Earth's surface, so their time signals must be pre-corrected.
- Particle physics confirms time dilation directly: unstable particles like muons measurably take longer to decay when accelerated to near-light speed.
- Photons experience no time at all — a photon emitted by a star tens of thousands of light-years away 'experiences' arrival in the same instant it was emitted.
- Gravity assists ('cosmic drafting') are not a symmetric slingshot: gravity that speeds a probe up as it falls toward a planet also slows it down by the same amount as it leaves; the net free speed boost comes from 'stealing' orbital energy by approaching from behind a planet's orbital path.
- Voyager 1 and 2 exploited multiple planetary gravity assists to gain enough cumulative speed to escape the solar system without a giant rocket.
- Polaris, the North Star, is not the brightest star in the sky — it's only the 49th brightest and is often confused with Venus; it's also not exactly above the celestial pole.
- Earth's axis wobbles (precession) on a ~26,000-year cycle, meaning the pole star changes over time — Polaris wasn't always the North Star and Vega will take over in about 14,000 years.
- Precession also shifts the sun's apparent position among the zodiac constellations: the zodiac was codified roughly 2,200–2,500 years ago, so most people's sun sign no longer matches where the sun actually is on their birthday.
- There is a 13th constellation the sun passes through, Ophiuchus, and the sun spends more time there than in Scorpius, meaning many 'Scorpios' are technically 'Ophiuchans.'

## Overview
In this 'Things You Thought You Knew' episode of StarTalk Radio, Neil deGrasse Tyson and comedian co-host Chuck Nice work through common misconceptions in astronomy and physics. They open with time dilation, explaining both the velocity-based slowing of time from special relativity and the gravity-based slowing from general relativity, using GPS satellite clock corrections and muon decay experiments as real-world proof. They then tackle gravity assists, debunking the popular 'slingshot' mental model by showing the speed gained is exactly canceled out unless the probe approaches from behind the planet's orbital motion — the mechanism behind Voyager's escape from the solar system. The back half is anchored by the episode's title theme: the North Star and the zodiac. Tyson dismantles folklore around Polaris (only the 49th-brightest star, not exactly at the celestial pole, a temporary pole star due to axial precession) and explains that the same precession is what makes 'your zodiac sign wrong' — astrology's constellation assignments were fixed millennia ago but the sun's actual position has since drifted an entire sign. They also note a 13th constellation, Ophiuchus, that the sun spends more time passing through than Scorpius.

## Implications
The episode is less about new discoveries than about correcting popular misconceptions using well-established physics, but the through-line (precession) elegantly links satellite timekeeping, deep-space navigation, celestial navigation history, and the cultural institution of astrology. By grounding the zodiac critique in the same axial-wobble mechanism used to explain the changing pole star, Tyson gives a physical, falsifiable reason astrology's sun signs are outdated rather than a purely dismissive argument.
