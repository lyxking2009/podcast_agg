# It's a bull market and nobody drinks anymore. — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Todd Sohn, ETF strategist at Strategas, joins The Compound and Friends to discuss the record pace of ETF launches—900 new funds year-to-date, with issuers like Corgi undercutting fees and Roundhill dominating thematic volume. The conversation also covers sports team valuations (Lakers' $12.4B deal lifting Knicks/MSG), Goldman's acquisitions of Innovator and Neos, the rise of buffer and option income ETFs, prediction market ETFs, and the persistent $3 trillion retail money market hoard. Key market signals include healthcare rotation, energy's negative beta, and resurgent demand for leverage.

## Key points
- ETF launches are at a record pace: 900 new funds year-to-date, expected to surpass last year's ~1,100, as lower launching costs have turned ETF creation into a 'Napster-like' mix-tape model.
- New issuer Corgi, backed by Y Combinator, is undercutting incumbents by launching thematic ETFs at 35 basis points and leveraged ETFs at 50 basis points, using a Hollywood box-office approach where a few hits subsidize many flops.
- Roundhill's brand strength is evident: its new photonics ETF attracted more trading volume in its first 3-4 days than the four existing photonic ETFs combined.
- Goldman Sachs acquired Innovator (buffered ETFs) and Neos (option income ETFs) for roughly $2.3B and $2B respectively; Neos had ~$30B AUM, and the deals target aging demographics seeking yield and downside protection.
- Sports team valuations are booming: the Lakers sold for $12.4B, implying the Knicks (MSGS) may be undervalued at ~$10B market cap and $11B enterprise value, with a planned October spin-off of the Rangers.
- The host attributes soaring sports franchise prices to stock market wealth among the top 1%, evidenced by Josh Kushner's $15B OpenAI position and his forced sale of minority stakes in the Grizzlies and Heat.
- A proposed rule would impose a 5-year holding period on NBA team ownership to discourage flipping and protect fan interests.
- Prediction market ETFs are coming: Subversive has filed for a fund that bets on heavy favorites (minus 850 odds and above), exploiting long-shot bias where favorites are systematically underpriced.
- Leveraged ETF assets reached $200B (notional $500B) before July's drawdown, but assets fell only 25% and demand is rebounding; Korea has required simulated trading for 2x funds.
- Retail money market funds sit at $3 trillion, up from $1.5 trillion pre-pandemic, and are likely to stay until Fed funds fall below 3% or a 35% stock market crash triggers buying.
- Healthcare is finally working after being in the bottom decile with heavy outflows; GLP-1 drugs caused healthcare's S&P 500 weight to drop from 16% to 8%.
- Energy's beta to the S&P 500 has collapsed to negative, making it a hedge; natural resources ETFs with active management can offer diversified exposure.
- Consumer staples weight in the S&P 500 has fallen to 4.5% and discretionary below 10%, reflecting tech dominance and the ability of option income funds to replace yield needs.
- Small-cap flows are tentative; the Russell 2000 inclusion of Bloom Energy at a $100B market cap is unprecedented and may signal upcoming index rule changes.
- Crypto ETF money is leaving as investors rotate into AI stocks; many niche crypto ETFs like 2x Dogecoin are effectively penny stocks with no demand.
- Compute-tracking ETFs are being filed by multiple issuers after the $500B AI infrastructure partnership announcement; even a Jensen Huang interview ETF has been filed.
- International ETFs are ignored except for Korea and Taiwan due to semiconductors; Japan and Europe have performed well but attract no retail interest.
- Commodity futures ETFs have structural problems (contango, K-1 tax forms) that limit adoption; only storable commodities like gold work as passive vehicles.
