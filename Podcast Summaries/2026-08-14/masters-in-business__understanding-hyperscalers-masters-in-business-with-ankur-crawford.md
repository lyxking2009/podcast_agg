# Understanding Hyperscalers: Masters in Business with Ankur Crawford — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Ankur Crawford, portfolio manager at Alger, discusses her path from Intel engineer to growth investor focused on technology and AI. She argues that AI is in early innings, with software writing software leading to exponential innovation and a shift in IT spending from software/services to hardware. Crawford sees hyperscaler capex as justified by insatiable compute demand, constrained by power, chips, and labor, and highlights AI's potential to transform healthcare and education.

## Key points
- Ankur Crawford has a BS in mechanical engineering and material science from UC Berkeley and a PhD in material science and engineering from Stanford, and worked as an engineer at Intel, earning multiple patents and the Intel PhD Fellowship before transitioning to investing at Alger.
- Crawford was hired on the spot by Alger CEO Dan Chung after a debate about Intel vs. AMD and NAND vs. hard drives, illustrating Alger's culture of encouraging dissenting perspectives and intellectual debate.
- She emphasizes that cultural differences affect management communication, citing Taiwan Semiconductor's Morris Chang philosophy of not raising prices despite being sole supplier of leading-edge chips, and a Russian CEO who always highlights negatives, which investors misinterpret.
- Alger's growth investing philosophy centers on 'change' rather than just growth, with two pillars: high unit volume growth and lifecycle change, where mature companies pivot via new management, M&A, or technology adoption, exemplified by Microsoft under Satya Nadella and hard drive companies in the AI era.
- Crawford argues that when software begins to write software, innovation becomes exponential and the cost to create software goes to zero, shifting value from IT services and software toward hardware and networking.
- She notes that hyperscalers are spending roughly $650 billion annually on capex, yet compute remains in shortage, with neo clouds reporting four times more demand than capacity, and supply constraints like power, chips, and skilled labor prevent overbuilding.
- Regarding DeepSeek and Chinese open-source models, Crawford explains they are distilled from US LLMs, similar to a bird on a giraffe, and suggests frontier models might restrict access to N-1 weights to prevent distillation, while inference (not training) drives most spending.
- Crawford sees AI's greatest overlooked impact in healthcare and education, arguing AI can bend the cost curve of care to enable universal healthcare and can address educational failures like college students who cannot read.
- She believes AI skeptics misunderstand the duration, scale, and economics of the investment cycle; unlike the dot-com era, we already have the necessary infrastructure and only needed the chips and data centers, so any overbuild will be absorbed.
- Her advice to college graduates is to do work they love and allow themselves to change careers, and as an investor she would not shortcut her painful learning experiences because they shaped her judgment.
