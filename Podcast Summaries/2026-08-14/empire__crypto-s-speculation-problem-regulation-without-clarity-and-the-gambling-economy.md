# Crypto's Speculation Problem, Regulation Without CLARITY & The Gambling Economy — Empire

## TL;DR
The Empire Roundup (Jason Yanowitz, Santi, and Rob) debates whether crypto has bottomed, what happens to regulation now that the CLARITY Act looks dead (20% Polymarket odds), the FOMO vs. Pump.fun social-trading war, and why even crypto-native VCs like Rob are increasingly deploying venture dollars into AI (SpaceX, Cerebras, accounting automation) instead of tokens.

## Key points
- Bottom signals: memory chips ripped ~15% in a day, S&P at all-time highs, Blockworks inbound leads up six weeks straight with record sales calls — mostly fintechs/brokerages (even Vanguard) pushing into crypto for APIs, listings data, and monitoring.
- Regulation without CLARITY: with the Act not passing, expect the SEC and CFTC to finally drive forward their own rulemaking — innovation exemptions, startup fundraising exemptions, and safe-harbor rules (Atkins' DAS keynote, Project Reg Crypto). Rob: rulemaking helps, but the largest institutions will still wait without legislation — "net worse" than passage.
- Stablecoin adoption ≠ token prices: adoption is real, but retail has scar tissue from low-float/high-FDV token vesting; "tokens are kind of dead" (Santi's headline) vs. Rob: "tokens are definitely not dead — networks need them to incentivize people."
- FOMO vs. Pump.fun: FOMO hit a new weekly revenue high ($1.88M, 50K daily active traders); Pump overtook Hyperliquid on key metrics and is doing ~$2M/day; Aug 1-7 FOMO routed $139M into Pump's pre-graduation bonding curves. Both will likely succeed; memecoin cycles historically short-lived.
- DeFi's rising share of volume is partly denominator math: CEX volume from normie users is collapsing; hyperliquid volume is roughly flat — the data under the hood isn't as bullish as headlines suggest.
- Venture reckoning: every company eventually hits a "reckoning" (Pump's past year; FOMO's is coming); Benchmark's Eric Vishria story — Cerebras' chips were literally melting in 2019-20 and the board thought the $500M was gone; "at the end of the day you're investing in the person."
- Rob's honest shift: less crypto, more AI infrastructure in his 2026 venture deployment — "it feels pretty damn great to be an investor in a company like SpaceX or Cerebras... I do think about that more and more."
