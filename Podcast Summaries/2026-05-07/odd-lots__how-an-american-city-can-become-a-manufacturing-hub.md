Allentown, Pennsylvania, once a center for early transistor manufacturing and now a key logistics hub within a day's drive of 40% of the US population, is pursuing reindustrialization under Mayor Matthew Tuerk. His strategy involves local industrial policy, rezoning, attracting companies, and leveraging 'weight-gaining industries' to rebuild the manufacturing base and de-risk supply chains. ## Key points
- Allentown was an early site of mass-produced transistors in the 1950s, making it a historical precursor to today's semiconductor industry.
- The city's location is a strategic logistics and e-commerce advantage, as it is within a day's drive of nearly 40% of the US population.
- Mayor Matthew Tuerk, in office since 2022, has made reindustrialization a central focus, aiming to sustain and rebuild the city's manufacturing base.
- Allentown specializes in 'weight-gaining industries'—products that become heavier or bulkier during manufacturing, making local production more cost-effective than long-distance shipping.
- Local industrial policy includes rezoning efforts that have evolved over the past decade to accommodate modern manufacturing and data center needs.
- Data centers present a political puzzle for the city, balancing economic benefits against potential community concerns.
- The city actively recruits companies to relocate or expand in Allentown as part of a strategy to de-risk the broader American supply chain.
