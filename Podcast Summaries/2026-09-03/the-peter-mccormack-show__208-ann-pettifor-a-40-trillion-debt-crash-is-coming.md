# #208 - Ann Pettifor - "A $40 Trillion Debt Crash Is Coming” — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR
Ann Pettifor recounts how she led Jubilee 2000 to cancel over $100 billion of developing-country debt through mass public mobilization, and warns that another global debt crash is imminent. She argues that rising U.S. and U.K. sovereign debt is a symptom of economic weakness and misallocation, not an inherent crisis, but that opaque private credit and shadow banking pose serious systemic risks. She links financial instability to the rise of authoritarian politics and calls for re-regulating finance.

## Key points
- Jubilee 2000 campaign: Pettifor was hired by NGOs to get debt cancelled; she decided public education and mobilization, not lobbyists in Washington; built one-page briefing notes; grew from small meetings to 70,000-100,000 protestors at the 1998 Birmingham G8 summit; forced Prime Minister Blair to fly back and meet, leading to cancellation of an estimated $100 billion of bilateral debt for poor countries.
- Export credit guarantees: UK's Export Credit Guarantee Department (ECGD) guaranteed loans to arms manufacturers selling weapons to Nigerian dictators; when dictators defaulted, British government went to the Paris Club to recover; Pettifor's campaign exposed this, forcing a name change; after debt relief, creditors quickly re-lent to countries like Uganda because they love empty balance sheets, showing lack of an international bankruptcy process.
- Credit creation: Ben Bernanke said the Fed transferred $11 billion to AIG by entering numbers into a computer, but required collateral and interest, and the Fed made a profit; Pettifor emphasizes that money is created through credit promises, not existing deposits, which enables investment but requires management and limits.
- US $40 trillion debt: mostly borrowed from American people and foreign holders like Japan, China, and UK; government can borrow because US credit is sound; debt is a function of a weak economy, not a cause; if the economy recovers with full employment, debt falls automatically, so the key worry should be fixing the American economy, not the debt itself.
- UK debt and investment: Britain has the lowest investment among OECD countries; government claims it has no money but could borrow; China invests heavily because the Communist Party fears social unrest; Western governments pretend helplessness and hide behind 'the market,' but they are serving wealthy creditors and speculators.
- Shadow banking and private credit: opaque private credit institutions have lent $1-3 trillion, purpose unknown; shadow banking operates beyond regulators; BlackRock uses pension savings to lend to unknown borrowers at unknown rates; this systemic opacity is more dangerous than public debt.
- Prediction of 2008: Pettifor wrote 'The Coming First World Debt Crisis' published September 2006, based on observing private debt levels in US and UK, not just poor countries; the crisis began August 9, 2007 and Lehman collapsed October 2008; she warned friends to move money from money markets in January 2008.
- Imminent crisis: she believes another crisis is imminent; stock market 'delusionism' has kept it going but Treasury Secretary Bessent's bond buyback error rattled markets; rising bond yields act like a dagger pointed at a bubble; Greenspan's 2004-2006 rate hikes preceded the 2008 bust and the same dynamic is in play.
- Political consequences: financial instability and stagnant wages in UK, US, France, India, Hungary, and Russia cause anger; people turn to strongmen like Trump and Le Pen; the 1930s debt crisis led to Hitler; this anger should be channeled into reining in Wall Street, not nationalism or authoritarianism.
- Bretton Woods and Nixon shock: 1945 Bretton Woods imposed capital controls, prevented crises, and created a 'golden age' from 1945 to 1971; Nixon unilaterally ended the system in 1971 without informing Kissinger, allowing market-driven imbalances, China's surplus, US deficits, and political tensions.
- Keynes misrepresented: Keynes did not advocate permanent deficits; he said government should fill the economic crater when the economy collapses and then stop to avoid inflation; his ideas were abused by those who printed endlessly and then blamed him, while the real architects are mainstream economists who design systems that funnel money upward.
