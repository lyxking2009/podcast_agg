# Less about Models; More about Architecture — Practical AI

## TL;DR
Chetan Gupta, Chief AI Officer at Rackspace, discusses his journey from industrial AI at Hitachi to leading enterprise AI architecture. He argues that the next frontier is not chasing models but building sovereign, governed AI architectures with layered stacks, harnesses, and internal evals. The conversation covers physical AI, data sovereignty, and the importance of thinking systematically about AI deployment.

## Key points
- Chetan Gupta, now Chief AI Officer at Rackspace, has a background spanning mathematics, HP Labs, and Hitachi, where he led global AI research and focused on industrial AI starting in 2016-2017, which he describes as a risky but forward-looking bet that paved his leadership path.
- Industrial AI initially centered on predictive maintenance, quality prediction, and defect detection using small machine learning and computer vision models; with deep learning and LLMs, it expanded to broader physical AI, robotics at scale, and metaverse-style virtual worlds for training and problem resolution.
- Cultural differences shape robotics adoption: Japan has been more open to robots, especially for elderly care and empathy-driven interactions, while North America lags; today, China is the center of gravity for industrial robotics, whereas North America leads in large language models.
- Generative AI democratized AI, shifting the enterprise challenge from building models with PhD teams to managing tokenomics, data sovereignty, and jagged capabilities where AI excels at coding but is mediocre at writing natural emails, necessitating workload-specific model selection and governance.
- Sovereignty has evolved from nation-state concerns to enterprise and even individual rights: enterprises must protect their IP and define their own governance and safety constitutions rather than relying on model providers' policies, especially as LLMs may expose proprietary context.
- Rackspace promotes an architecture-first approach with a layered stack: compute, data, model, inference, harness, orchestration, and consumption, all wrapped by governance and assurance planes; the harness is the key construct that ties a model to a specific outcome, and different harnesses on the same model yield different results.
- Enterprises should avoid marrying any model family; instead, they should build internal evaluation sets tailored to their own workloads, not rely solely on public benchmarks, enabling safe model swapping based on cost, safety, and performance for consistent customer experience.
- Rackspace is building a 'mirror org' where AI solutions are first developed and proven internally, then offered externally, and is focusing on underserved layers such as governance, assurance, and orchestration to provide sovereign, safe, and cost-effective AI to customers.
