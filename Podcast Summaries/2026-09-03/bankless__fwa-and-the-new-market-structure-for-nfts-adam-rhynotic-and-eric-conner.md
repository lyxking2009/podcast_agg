# FWA and the New Market Structure for NFTs | Adam (Rhynotic) and Eric Conner — Bankless

## TL;DR
Bankless hosts David, Adam (Rhynotic), and Eric Conner discuss FWA (Fake World Assets), an Ethereum protocol that creates a liquidity pool for NFTs where depositors back assets with ETH and users buy random NFTs. Adam explains the pricing incentives, token distribution, and upcoming Flair launch mechanism, while Eric highlights how FWA brings fun and liquidity back to NFTs and could become the new market structure for buying, selling, and launching NFTs. They also explore potential expansion to real-world assets and other EVM chains.

## Key points
- FWA (Fake World Assets) is an Ethereum NFT purchasing protocol where depositors create a pool of NFTs, each backed by ETH at a price set by the depositor. Buyers can purchase a random NFT from the pool and either keep it or sell it back at 90% of the backing price, with random selection handled by Chainlink.
- The protocol was inspired by Uniswap V2 liquidity pools and addresses the lack of on-chain NFT pricing by using depositor-set backing as the price signal; this creates a buyback guarantee and mitigates illiquid NFT markets.
- Depositors are incentivized to price NFTs fairly: underpricing increases the chance of being purchased, overpricing makes it unlikely to be pulled or results in the buyer taking the ETH rather than the NFT, while pricing within 10% of fair value means buyers typically take the NFT because of the 10% haircut.
- The token FWA launched with a two-week period where it could not be bought, only earned by participating (depositing or purchasing), to distribute to real users and avoid snipers. After emissions ended, protocol fees now buy FWA on market and distribute to active users (40% to purchasers, 30% to depositors, 30% burned).
- Eric Conner, an NFT enthusiast and FWA user, highlighted that many NFT collections lacked liquidity and bids, and FWA provides a permissionless way to get liquidity or sell NFTs by depositing them with ETH backing. He deposited MeBits and Doodles to farm fees.
- FWA is designed as a base protocol for others to build on: examples include Gasha Battles (five people's pulls compete, winner takes all five NFTs) and Mega Rip (group pulls many packs after a period and splits pool), demonstrating composability.
- A custom pool feature is planned, where a single entity (e.g., a punk whale or Yuga Labs) can create a dedicated pool with their own NFTs, potentially changing odds and buyback options; fees from user pools may flow back to the main pool and token.
- The Flair launch mechanism (spelled 'Flair' but pronounced 'fair') allows artists to launch new NFT collections through FWA without needing upfront ETH backing: backers provide ETH, when fully backed the collection is added to the pool, and artists earn fees over time as NFTs are purchased, not instantly.
- First Flair launches include a test collection of 111 NFTs backed at 0.25 ETH this weekend, followed by artist Sterling Crispin's 'Save Ethereum' collection. Jack Butcher previously launched 'Rappers' (80 NFTs) via the platform.
- Real-world assets (RWAs) are expected to become significant on FWA: Pokemon cards, luxury goods, possibly Rolex watches or house deeds, tokenized via custodians like Collector Crypt or Emblem Vault. Trust risk lies with depositor, and the buyer can take ETH backing if the asset is fake.
- The protocol has a single point of failure in Chainlink randomness, but if it fails, users can withdraw assets, making it a wind-down rather than a crash. There was an early exploit in the first version that was repaid and the code was audited.
- FWA can expand to other EVM chains with cheaper/faster UX by bridging the FWA token (e.g., via Layer Zero) and using Chainlink; potential to include tokenized stocks like NVIDIA 3x long tokens or Tesla shares as prizes in future custom pools.
- The general pool has about 6,000 assets and a current spin price of ~0.08 ETH, with the top NFT being a punk backed by 300 ETH (hoodie with smile). Pricing can be 'wrong' because there is no on-chain oracle, but that is a feature to allow rare NFTs.
