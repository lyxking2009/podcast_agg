# Why PMs are suddenly valuable again | Tulsee Doshi (Head of Product, Gemini) — Lenny's Podcast: Product | Career | Growth

## TL;DR
Tulsee Doshi, head of product for Google's frontier models - Gemini, the GenMedia models (Nano Banana, Lyria), Gemma and the audio stack - explains why model PM is a different job from normal PM. The short version: you cannot specify a model the way you specify software, so the job is as much about discovering what the model is unusually good at as it is about deciding what it should do.

## Key points
- The PM job on models in one image: 'It's kind of like Snape's potions class. You can put all the ingredients in, but you also have to expect that there will be a little bit of magic' - the model is not predictable in a traditional product sense.
- Implication for the job: set intentional goals and evals, but then study what comes out the other side, find where the model shines unexpectedly, and shape that into product. Specification is a starting point, not a contract.
- Hiring: she wants PMs who understand 'the vibes' of models, have an opinion, play with them daily, and stress-test them on things they personally care about - taste and hands-on usage over process pedigree.
- Why PMs are valuable again: as capability commoditises across labs, the differentiator shifts to product judgment - knowing which capability to expose, how to frame it, and how to make it feel reliable.
- The 60-20-20 framework: rather than only shipping an MVP that works today, deliberately build some features that are not yet working - a way of staging how far ahead of the capability curve to invest.
- On Google's position: she pushes back on 'Google isn't at the frontier', says the company does want to win the frontier conversation, and concedes there is more work to do on consumer-facing assistant experiences built on Google's own data.
- Scope of her portfolio: Gemini plus GenMedia (video/image generation and editing, Lyria music), Gemma open models, and the audio models - dialogue, audio understanding, TTS and translation - which she says are under-discussed relative to their usage.
- On evaluation: end-to-end setup matters, because serving and latency shape how a model feels. Naturalness of voice, inflection and tenor can be degraded by the serving stack even when the underlying model is good.
- Fun aside with a serious point: twelve years ago at Stanford she worked on swarms - agents colluding - long before it became a live product concern, which is why she is sceptical of treating multi-agent coordination as a new problem.
