## Key Points

- OpenAI's long-term product vision is to eliminate the interface entirely — replacing the current chat paradigm with a persistent agent that proactively works on your behalf, understanding your goals and taking action without you needing to click toggles or switch modes.
- Codex, originally framed as a coding tool, has become a general-purpose agentic tool used extensively by non-technical OpenAI employees for tasks like event planning, inbox management, and seating charts — usage now rivals Slack inside OpenAI.
- The merger of ChatGPT, Codex, and the browser into a unified product is already underway, and Brockman explicitly confirmed the "super app" framing is now appropriate: any task starts with a prompt and OpenAI's technology uses browser or computer control to complete it.
- Trust is a central design challenge for the agentic era — how much autonomy to delegate to AI is something Brockman says must be earned incrementally, with robust oversight, controls, and observability tools being built for enterprise customers.
- Scaling laws have not hit a wall after 70+ years of neural net research; Brockman argues every apparent plateau has turned out to be a fixable bug, and that more compute, data, and better architectures continue to yield improvement.
- Compute is the scarce resource in the AI economy, and OpenAI's strategy centers on acquiring as much as possible — including developing its own chip program — because demand from agentic AI will vastly exceed current global supply.
- On the voice interface frontier, Brockman described current voice models as hampered by unnatural turn-taking and called for truly bidirectional, interruptible, human-fluid voice interaction as the next major UX breakthrough.
- AI in healthcare is a deeply personal motivation for Brockman; he cited 230 million weekly ChatGPT health queries, his wife's use of the system to manage her conditions, and cases like AI-assisted cancer diagnosis as evidence this is not theoretical.
- Brockman pushed back on Satya Nadella's "models as commodity" framing, arguing intelligence, compute, and domain expertise are all non-commoditized layers where substantial value and margin will persist.
- OpenAI is working on custom hardware devices (publicly confirmed by Sam Altman), but Brockman frames these as interfaces to the AI — not the AI itself — analogous to how your phone is an interface to you, not a replacement.

## Overview

In this live conversation at the Big Technology AI Summit, Alex Kantrowitz sits down with OpenAI President and co-founder Greg Brockman for their fourth on-record discussion about OpenAI's product direction. The central theme is the ongoing unification of ChatGPT, Codex, and browser capabilities into a single agentic product — and what that ultimately means for how humans interact with AI. Brockman makes clear that the end state is not a super app in the traditional sense, but something closer to a persistent, context-aware intelligence that requires no interface at all: you provide goals, and the AI acts on your behalf.

Brockman spends significant time on the mechanics of the agentic transition, explaining that Codex — despite its coding-centric name — has already become a general-purpose agent used by non-technical OpenAI staff for administrative, operational, and communications work. The key primitive is tool use: hooking AI into Gmail, Slack, calendars, and file systems so it can not just suggest the next action but execute it. He frames trust as the critical product challenge, noting that OpenAI views agentic authority as something earned through demonstrated reliability, not simply granted.

The conversation also covers the competitive landscape in depth, including Brockman's response to Microsoft/Satya Nadella positioning models as commodities, OpenAI's compute investment thesis ("buy all of it"), the prospect of price cuts on frontier models, and how differentiation will persist across the stack — through raw intelligence, domain specialization, and the integration work needed to make AI genuinely useful inside specific industries. The episode closes with a heartfelt discussion of AI in medicine, where Brockman calls the technology's potential impact on personalized healthcare "one of the most astounding possibilities of AI."

## Implications

The convergence of chat and agents inside ChatGPT is not a future roadmap item — it is actively underway, with Brockman confirming that what Codex does today will be brought to all ChatGPT users. This means the operating model for AI software is shifting from "query and response" to "goal delegation and execution," which carries enormous implications for every software category that currently captures user intent (search, productivity suites, CRM, ERP). Enterprises that assume AI will stay in the assistant lane and not touch their workflows are likely underestimating the pace of integration.

Brockman's framing of compute as the defining scarce resource of the next era — and OpenAI's vertical integration strategy including custom chips — positions the company less like a software firm and more like an infrastructure monopolist in the making. His point that even older-generation H100 chips are appreciating in price due to demand inversion signals that the economics of AI are structurally different from prior technology waves. For investors, enterprises, and policymakers, the implication is that whoever controls compute at scale controls the pace and direction of the agentic economy.
