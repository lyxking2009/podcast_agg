# #217 - Lee Cronin - AI Will Never Be Conscious. This Is How We Create Real Life.

## Overview

Peter McCormack interviews Lee Cronin, chemist and professor at the University of Glasgow, who argues that the
current technology narrative is a “massive AI delusion” - that modern artificial intelligence is an advanced form of
plagiarism rather than a new life form. Where Silicon Valley predicts imminent sentient machines, Cronin holds that
models trained on human history are powerful tools but fundamentally incapable of the curiosity, consciousness and
biological intelligence that define life. His actual mission is more ambitious than the debate he is dismissing:
programming chemistry to solve the origin of life and to turn sand into living cells. The conversation covers why
biological consciousness is tied to an unbroken 3.8-billion-year evolutionary lineage, why DNA may be a cosmic fluke,
how chemistry can measure the complexity of life, and why he believes we will detect real alien life within two
decades.

## Key Points

- Cronin's central claim: AI models are trained on the record of human history, which makes them powerful tools but also makes them a form of advanced plagiarism rather than a new kind of life.
- Biological consciousness, in his framing, descends from an unbroken 3.8-billion-year evolutionary lineage, and silicon cannot replicate that lineage - so consciousness claims for AI are a category error.
- He argues consciousness is an illusion and that nobody can actually define life; the episode works through the definitions on offer, including the Adam Savage one.
- Molecules came before cells; DNA may be a cosmic fluke rather than an inevitable blueprint - chemistry, not information, is the substrate of life.
- Cronin's own research programme: use chemistry to solve the origin of life and ‘turn sand into living cells’, via digital chemistry and an AI-driven approach to chemical discovery.
- He describes six eureka moments in his career, and characterises some discoveries as ‘unfathomable’.
- He rejects the AI escape story as nonsense, and predicts we will detect actual alien life within the next two decades.
- Social media is characterised as ‘the new smoking’; curiosity is ‘the coolest thing we have’.

## Implications

The episode's value is that Cronin is not making a safety argument - he is making a metaphysics-of-chemistry argument,
and it is worth engaging on its own terms: if life is a chemical process rather than an informational one, then
silicon-based general intelligence is a different kind of claim than most AI discourse assumes. Practitioners should
note the distinction between ‘models as a learned compression of human output’ (which Cronin accepts as useful) and
‘models as a route to a new form of life’ (which he rejects). This record is built from the episode's full chapter
