## Key Points
- Bitcoin fell approximately 5.2% on the week to ~$73,000, while Ethereum dropped 6.2% to below $2,000 at $1,995, reflecting broad major-crypto weakness.
- BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF saw significant outflows including a $1.3 billion whale sale, and Polymarket data puts a 55% probability on Bitcoin hitting $55K by end of 2026.
- Select altcoins — Hyperliquid (HYPE), Venice (VVV), Zcash, and Near — reached all-time highs during the same period, raising questions about whether their momentum is sustainable or a 'false strength' signal.
- David Hoffman published 'Why I Sold My ETH,' arguing that favorable conditions for strong ETH use cases have not materialized since 2021 and distinguishing between Ethereum's technological success and ETH's underperformance as an asset.
- Vitalik Buterin clarified the Ethereum Foundation's focus areas as censorship resistance, open source, privacy, and security, while also emphasizing ETH as a financial asset and disclosing that nearly 90% of his net worth is held in ETH.
- Standard Chartered compared Ethereum's current position to Amazon after the 2000 dot-com collapse and projected ETH could reach $40,000 by 2030.
- SoFi launched the first U.S. bank-issued stablecoin embedded in a retail banking app, marking a milestone in mainstream financial adoption of stablecoins.
- A pseudonymous individual filed suit in New York Supreme Court claiming ownership of Satoshi Nakamoto's approximately 3.8 million dormant Bitcoin — valued at roughly $285 billion — under a 'finders keepers' property law theory.
- Geopolitical developments included leaked peace deal terms between the U.S. and Iran (Strait of Hormuz reopening, ceasefire extensions), with oil dropping to wartime lows (~$93-94/barrel) but elevated 10-year yields suggesting persistent stagflation concerns.
- Open Zeppelin's founder warned publicly about growing DeFi risks stemming from AI-powered vulnerability detection tools being used by bad actors.

## Overview
This weekly Bankless roundup examines a turbulent week for major cryptocurrencies, with Bitcoin falling to ~$73,000 and Ethereum dropping below $2,000, even as select altcoins like Hyperliquid, Venice, Zcash, and Near reached all-time highs. Hosts David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams debate whether the altcoin strength signals genuine momentum or a misleading divergence ahead of deeper market declines. The episode also covers an Ethereum sentiment crisis sparked by David Hoffman's own article arguing ETH's asset performance has decoupled from its technological success.

## Implications
The divergence between major crypto weakness and select altcoin all-time highs, combined with prominent figures like David Hoffman publicly selling ETH, points to a fragile and sentiment-driven market where conviction is fracturing — raising the question of whether current altcoin strength is a leading indicator of recovery or a final flush of speculative capital before broader declines. Institutional and regulatory developments (bank-issued stablecoins, ETF outflows, geopolitical macro shifts) suggest the crypto market is increasingly intertwined with traditional finance and global macro conditions.
