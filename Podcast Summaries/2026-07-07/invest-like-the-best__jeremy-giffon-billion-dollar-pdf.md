## Key Points

- The "posting class" — online commentators, essayists, and content creators — now wields disproportionate power over capital allocation, culture, and status in the late-stage information economy.
- Nearly every white-collar knowledge-work job is largely performative rather than genuinely productive; most value is created by a small fraction of actual doers.
- A neo-feudal system is forming around access to private companies, where a priestly class controls entry to deals and information flow, concentrating returns among insiders.
- "Billion Dollar PDFs" are rare documents that reshape entire fields: the Satoshi whitepaper, "Attention Is All You Need," and "Situational Awareness" are recent examples. These documents compress and transmit new mental models at scale.
- Jeremy Giffon founded Octave, a firm that has had hundreds of conversations with founders and the capital behind their companies, giving him a unique vantage on where real value creation concentrates in private markets.
- Private market dynamics have diverged sharply from public markets: information asymmetries, relationship-driven access, and non-standard deal structures create opportunities invisible to conventional investors.
- The episode title's phrase "posting priests" captures how those who synthesize and broadcast ideas can command influence far exceeding those who build things.

## Overview

Jeremy Giffon, founder of Octave, returns to Invest Like the Best for a wide-ranging conversation about the sociology of the modern information economy and its implications for private markets. His central thesis is that society has become subservient to those who can articulate, synthesize, and broadcast ideas — the "posting class" — at the expense of actual producers. He draws on his vantage point from hundreds of founder and investor conversations to argue that private market access is increasingly structured like feudalism, with a small gate-keeping priesthood controlling deal flow.

The "Billion Dollar PDF" concept — rare transformative documents that transmit paradigm shifts at low cost — serves as both a metaphor for the information economy's power dynamics and a framework for understanding how intellectual leverage compounds over time.

## Implications

Understanding the "posting class" dynamic is increasingly essential for investors navigating private markets, where relationships with influential commentators and synthesizers can gate access to the best deals. The neo-feudal access structure means returns will concentrate among those embedded in the right information networks, not necessarily the best analytical frameworks. The "Billion Dollar PDF" phenomenon suggests monitoring which new intellectual frameworks are gaining traction could be a leading indicator of where capital will flow next.
