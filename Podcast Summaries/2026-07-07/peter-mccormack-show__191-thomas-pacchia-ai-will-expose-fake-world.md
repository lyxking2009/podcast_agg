## Key Points

- Contemporary society increasingly feels artificial — political, media, and institutional systems generate outputs that feel performative rather than genuine, and trust has collapsed across the board.
- AI will initially worsen this authenticity crisis by making it cheaper and easier to manufacture convincing fake content, political messaging, and institutional theater.
- The longer arc is that AI may ultimately expose the fake world by making it impossible to sustain the illusions — a transparency forcing function operating at scale.
- Political polarization and media fragmentation have created a feedback loop of institutional dysfunction: government bureaucracy ("the blob"), censorship mechanisms, and algorithmic platform control all reinforce each other.
- "Show Us Receipts" is an AI fact-checking initiative Thomas is involved with, designed to automatically verify claims made by politicians and in government contracts.
- Decentralization, self-sovereignty, and privacy are framed as the structural solutions — building systems that cannot be captured or corrupted, rather than trying to reform captured systems.
- Community institutions like churches and pubs are underrated counterweights to systemic failure — places where trust is built at a human scale that institutions cannot replicate.

## Overview

Thomas Pacchia, co-founder of Pubkey (the Bitcoin bar in New York City), joins Peter McCormack for a 71-minute conversation about why the world feels increasingly fake and what role AI will play in either accelerating or resolving that crisis. The conversation ranges across political philosophy, institutional critique, and practical projects — including Pacchia's "Show Us Receipts" initiative to use AI for political accountability.

The episode is structured around 13 segments moving from "AI Psychosis" (opening, ~0:00) through specific critiques of British governance, American political divisions, and economic welfare traps, to "Churches and Pubs" (~1:07:52) as an optimistic coda about human-scale trust.

Pacchia's core thesis is that modernity's layers of mediation — platforms, institutions, governments, media — have become so abstracted from reality that they now primarily serve their own perpetuation rather than the underlying social functions they claim to serve.

## Implications

If AI becomes a transparency-forcing function rather than just a misinformation amplifier, the implications for institutions, media, and political systems are profound. "Show Us Receipts"-style projects could shift accountability from self-reporting to automated verification. The bet on decentralization and community institutions suggests a political philosophy that bypasses institutional reform in favor of parallel system building.
