## Key Points

- Tushar Jain (Multicoin Capital) frames Hyperliquid as "the everything exchange" — not just a perpetuals platform but universal infrastructure where anyone anywhere can get exposure to any asset, event contract, or derivative.
- Portfolio margining is Jain's primary bullish thesis: a unified collateral account across multiple asset classes automatically nets gains and losses, removing the need for additional collateral deposits during adverse price moves.
- HIP3 allows permissionless market creation — deployers stake 500,000 HYPE tokens and capture 50% of trading fees, transforming Hyperliquid from a first-party exchange into a platform infrastructure layer.
- Multicoin's base case valuation: $319/HYPE (5x from ~$63), assuming 35% annual derivatives volume growth, 32% DEX market share, and 30% Hyperliquid market share within that. Revenue from trading fees plus 90% yield on Coinbase USDC deposits (bought and burned).
- Hyperliquid's 14-person team has executed at a consistently high velocity, and the founders' continued engagement despite paper wealth suggests incentive alignment beyond short-term gain.
- The U.S. regulatory path is multi-step: perp legalization → Clarity Act passage with DeFi safe harbors → code open-sourcing → validator decentralization → regulated frontend integration.
- Hyperliquid compares favorably on liquidation metrics to competitors (Aster, Lighter) and has organic user activity with genuine directional risk-taking, not wash trading.

## Overview

Multicoin Capital partner Tushar Jain makes a detailed bull case for Hyperliquid as the backbone of next-generation finance infrastructure. The episode goes beyond the perpetual futures narrative to argue that Hyperliquid's combination of portfolio margining, permissionless market creation (HIP3), and revenue model positions it to become the settlement layer for the entire derivatives market.

The conversation covers Multicoin's valuation framework in unusual detail — including specific assumptions on volume growth, market share, and revenue — giving listeners a clear look at how professional crypto investors underwrite a position in a nascent protocol. Jain also addresses concerns about team size, founder incentives, and the regulatory pathway for decentralized perpetuals in the U.S.

## Implications

HIP3's permissionless market creation could be the feature that compounds Hyperliquid's value most quickly — if any developer can create a market and capture 50% of fees, the platform's surface area grows non-linearly. Portfolio margining, if it becomes the industry standard, would make Hyperliquid structurally superior for active traders. The regulatory pathway outlined by Jain is optimistic but provides a credible sequencing for how DeFi perps become legitimate in the U.S.
