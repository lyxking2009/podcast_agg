## Key Points

- Ari Redbord (TRM Labs Global Head of Policy, former senior U.S. Treasury official) brings a law enforcement and national security perspective to the DEX in the City panel on crypto regulation.
- The SEC is transitioning from an enforcement-first approach to one focused on providing regulatory clarity — a significant shift that the panel views as broadly positive for the industry.
- The CFTC is emerging as an increasingly important voice on digital assets, perpetual futures, and prediction markets, with potential to become the primary regulator for many DeFi activities.
- Regulators should focus on outcomes (investor protection, market integrity, national security) rather than regulating specific technologies — a tech-neutral framework that allows rules to adapt as markets evolve.
- National security implications of crypto are a core focus of Redbord's work at TRM Labs, which provides blockchain analytics to governments and financial institutions globally.
- Recent DeFi protocol exploits (Drift, Kelp) highlight that most hacks exploit operational security failures rather than smart contract bugs — basic practices like dedicated multi-sig devices remain critical.
- Privacy in crypto is essential for institutional adoption but must be compatible with compliance capabilities; programmable risk management can coexist with privacy protections.

## Overview

Ari Redbord sits down with the three hosts of DEX in the City — Tuongvy "Vy" Le (General Counsel, Veda; former SEC enforcement counsel), Katherine Kirkpatrick Bos (former King & Spalding partner, former GC of StarkWare), and Jessi Brooks (General Counsel, Ribbit Capital; former DOJ national security prosecutor) — for a conversation about the rapidly evolving crypto regulatory landscape.

The episode draws on Redbord's distinctive background: years as a senior Treasury official focused on national security and financial crime, now applied to blockchain analytics and crypto policy at TRM Labs. The DEX in the City hosts bring complementary perspectives from SEC enforcement, international crypto law, and national security prosecution.

Key discussion areas include the regulatory transition underway at the SEC and CFTC, DeFi security vulnerabilities, privacy technology in institutional contexts, and how agentic AI and cross-chain infrastructure will create the next wave of regulatory challenges.

## Implications

The convergence of three experienced women legal practitioners with a national security-focused blockchain analytics expert produces an unusually grounded view of where crypto regulation is heading. Their shared emphasis on outcome-based regulation — rather than technology-specific rules — suggests a more adaptive regulatory framework may be emerging. The focus on operational security failures in DeFi hacks is a reminder that most crypto security failures are human problems, not cryptographic ones.
