## Key Points

- APA Corporation's CFO believes investors will soon rediscover the value of conventional oil and gas assets, which have been underweighted amid the energy transition narrative.
- The company is positioned for a market shift that would favor established conventional producers over newer, higher-cost alternatives.
- APA operates across multiple geographies including the Permian Basin, North Sea, and Egypt, giving it diversified production leverage to any oil price recovery.
- The episode suggests that current market pricing does not fully reflect the long-term value of APA's conventional reserves.
- Capital discipline and balance sheet management are central to APA's positioning as a potential beneficiary of a sector rotation back into traditional energy.

## Overview

RBC Capital Markets' Strategic Alternatives podcast features a focused conversation with APA Corporation's CFO on why the company believes it is well-positioned for an impending shift in investor sentiment toward conventional oil and gas. The 11-minute episode is typical of the show's format: concise, analyst-driven, with emphasis on valuation catalysts and corporate strategy over macro speculation.

APA Corporation, formerly Apache Corporation, is an independent oil and gas exploration and production company. The CFO's view reflects a contrarian bet that the market has over-rotated away from conventional energy and that a re-rating is coming.

## Implications

Conventional oil and gas companies have faced a persistent valuation discount as ESG mandates and energy transition enthusiasm reduced institutional ownership. If APA's CFO thesis is correct, the sector is approaching an inflection point where supply constraints and durable demand could force a reappraisal. APA's diversified geographic footprint makes it a leveraged proxy for any such re-rating.
