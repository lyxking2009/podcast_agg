# What Jensen Huang's Stunning Return Means for the AI Trade — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
The episode analyzes the whipsaw in markets following Jensen Huang's firing and rehiring as Nvidia CEO, with the S&P 500 having its worst week since COVID and the Nasdaq entering a 20% drawdown before a sharp rebound. Guest Alexander Altman of Barclays explains that the selloff was driven by technical factors like levered ETF liquidations and negative dealer gamma, while the rebound reflects a shift in positioning and extreme volatility. He argues that fundamental concerns about AI financing, overcapacity, and ROI remain unresolved, and that the Federal Reserve's tightening path is a key risk that could determine whether the rally is sustainable.

## Key points
- The S&P 500 entered a correction on Friday, with the VIX spiking to 40 and the index suffering its worst week since COVID, down 7%. The Nasdaq had dropped 20% from peak, wiping out at least $7 trillion in wealth.
- Alexander Altman attributes the selloff to technical factors: high volatility regime, levered ETF selling (~$35 billion), negative dealer gamma around the 7100 S&P strike, and poor liquidity (Thursday was 58th worst day for S&P liquidity in 10 years).
- The rebound on Monday (Nasdaq 100 up 6.9%) is not necessarily a dead cat bounce; Altman frames it as a function of the high-vol environment where daily moves of 2-3% are expected, and positioning had become extremely bearish (vol control rock bottom, CTAs short, dealer gamma negative).
- Altman notes that Barclays' quantitative framework shifted from bearish in August to bullish tactically now, but fundamental questions about AI financing and revenue trajectory remain unanswered and could take months or years to resolve.
- The Federal Reserve's hawkish stance, including potential December hike and comments from Waller, Barr, and Hammack, is a major risk. Altman worries about a repeat of 2018's over-tightening, referencing the Druckenmiller-Warsh op-ed. He says if the Fed is out of tune with market internals, the bounce could be a dead cat bounce.
- The AI financing issues are still present: spreads on hyperscaler debt and neocloud high-yield bonds remain wide; CoreWeave bonds and SpaceX bond placements illustrate higher financing costs, which could dampen the AI build-out.
- Altman points out that price reset in semiconductors has been massive: semis dropped 30-40%, some names like SK Hynix trade at 2.2x forward EPS, but valuation alone is a poor timing tool. He notes the triple-levered SK Hynix ETF (7709HK) has fallen 90-95% on the year, showing leverage being flushed out.
- Despite near-term bounce, Altman maintains medium-term constructive view on AI, especially potential consumer application layer breakthrough, but cautions that even in dot-com bust the Nasdaq had multiple 15-20% rallies before ultimate low, so traders should navigate shorter timeframes.
- Political involvement: The Trump administration is seen as supporting the AI trade and Jensen Huang's reinstatement, but this creates new risks including potential Democratic investigations and the challenge of reacting to headline-driven policy. Altman analogizes to tariff relief rallies.
- The episode also notes that household equity exposure is at highest since late 1920s, making the stock market central to wealth and politics; 35% of US household net worth is in stocks and two-thirds of households own stocks.
