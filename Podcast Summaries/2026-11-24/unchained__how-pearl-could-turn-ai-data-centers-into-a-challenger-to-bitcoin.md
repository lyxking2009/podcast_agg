# How Pearl Could Turn AI Data Centers Into a Challenger to Bitcoin — Unchained

## TL;DR
Ryan Watkins of Syncrasy Capital argues Bitcoin faces a long-term security budget crisis because halvings require roughly 19% annual price appreciation, while no fee market has emerged. He presents Pearl, a GPU-mined proof-of-work cryptocurrency launched in April 2025, as a challenger that ties security to useful AI matrix multiplication and could see its compute/energy footprint surpass Bitcoin's before 2030 if about 12.5% of US data center capacity mines it. Pearl's near-zero overhead mining for AI clouds, the upcoming FP8 upgrade, and duplex miner economics are central to this venture bet.

## Key points
- Bitcoin's long-term security budget is a growing problem: with halvings every four years, Bitcoin's price must double every four years (~19% annually) to maintain miner incentives, which is unrealistic for a $1.5 trillion asset, and no transaction fee market has developed to replace block subsidies.
- Pearl is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency launched in April 2025 that replaces Bitcoin's SHA-256 hashing with GPU matrix multiplication, the foundational operation underpinning AI compute, allowing miners to potentially do useful work while securing the network.
- Currently about 80% of Pearl mining is synthetic (performing matrix multiplications without real AI inference); the FP8 upgrade expected in January or Q1 will expand supported GPUs and enable real inference workloads, lowering mining overhead to roughly 0–5%.
- Watkins's core thesis is that AI data centers can mine Pearl at near-zero incremental overhead while serving inference customers, effectively subsidizing compute costs and accumulating a store-of-value asset; early adopters could undercut competitors.
- He defines 'P-day' as the moment Pearl's compute and energy footprint surpasses Bitcoin's; his model suggests if about 12.5% of US data center capacity mines Pearl by 2030, P-day could arrive before the end of the decade, though it does not imply flipping Bitcoin's price.
- Duplex miners—entities that both earn revenue from real AI inference and mine Pearl—have better economics than synthetic-only miners and will likely outcompete them as the network matures, driving a shift toward useful work.
- Bitcoin miners already pivoting to AI data centers are natural early adopters of Pearl because they retain mining DNA and can add Pearl mining with low overhead while continuing to build out inference businesses.
- Pearl's tokenomics include a hard cap of 2.1 billion coins and a predictable issuance schedule; Watkins argues that a good store of value requires cheap verification, censorship resistance, potential privacy, and a credible long-term security budget, which Pearl aims to provide.
- The Pearl team and early investors reportedly hold about 5% of total supply, which Watkins compares to Satoshi Nakamoto's Bitcoin holdings and considers reasonable, noting that no true fair launch has existed since Bitcoin because every subsequent asset launches with profit expectations.
- The Pearl founders include an ex-Nvidia senior researcher and an academic; Watkins highlights GPU kernel engineering—the code that tells GPUs how to perform matrix multiplication efficiently—as an underappreciated competency that could drive mining overhead toward zero.
- Near-term milestones include the FP8 upgrade, expansion of supported GPU types (e.g., Blackwell, potentially TPUs), and deeper integrations with AI clouds like Together AI, which began supporting Pearl shortly after mainnet.
- On 51% attack risks, Watkins argues that while Pearl's GPUs have value beyond mining, at scale a meaningful fraction of GPU value would come from Pearl block rewards, creating deterrents; renting enough GPUs to attack is impractical and hyperscalers lack motivation.
- Watkins distinguishes Pearl from Venice: Pearl is a money/store of value and not a business, while Venice is a privacy wrapper around ChatGPT/cloud services; the two could be symbiotic, with Venice potentially routing requests to Pearl-mining data centers.
- Longer term, Pearl could expand proof-of-useful-work beyond inference to training or fine-tuning, enabling a verifiable historical record of AI compute and laying groundwork for compute marketplaces, verifiable inference, and agent traceability.
- The main risk is that Pearl's hash rate stalls or declines; Watkins says if that happens within one to two years, the thesis fails, and he frames Pearl as a multi-year, multi-cycle venture bet rather than a short-term trade.
