## Key Points
- Co-host Jason Yanowitz was absent, leaving Santiago "Santi" Santos and guest-turned-regular Rob Hadick (Dragonfly Capital) to run the roundup. They open on the CLARITY Act's fading odds: Polymarket's "clarity passes" contract has fallen from an all-time high of ~46% last week to roughly 27-30%. Rob frames two unresolved sticking points — (1) "ethics language" around whether the Trump family/administration must carve out ownership in crypto-related projects, and who (FBI vs. state AGs) could enforce complaints, and (2) developer-protection language (referred to as "BRCA") where Senator Cortez Masto reportedly floated compromise text that neither Republicans nor industry actually agreed to. Rob expects a possible partisan-line vote next week, but thinks the longer it drags into September the less likely passage becomes; he says he personally wouldn't buy the 30% "yes" on Polymarket but is still confident crypto's regulatory backdrop improves within 6-9 months regardless, since the SEC and CFTC are moving ahead with rulemaking even absent legislation.
- The hosts argue "everything is trading like crypto now": semiconductor and AI-related equities are seeing crypto-level volatility (e.g., large single-day moves), leverage in prime brokerage is at its highest since before the 2008 financial crisis per Goldman data, with roughly 20% of that leverage concentrated in a handful of AI/memory-chip names. They cite Korea's stock market having more circuit breakers this year than in its entire prior history, and discuss the rumor that "Leopold" (Leopold Aschenbrenner) sold a large, reportedly ~$14B notional, 4x-levered position after memory stocks fell ~50% in about a month.
- Rob argues institutional trading has become dominated by quantitative/automated strategies and short-term, momentum-driven behavior, squeezing out long-term fundamental "buy and hold" investors because LPs can't stomach intra-quarter volatility — a dynamic crypto funds have lived with for years. He cites anecdotes of LPs asking managers not to send quarterly statements to avoid discomfort at investment-committee reviews. Both hosts note that delta-neutral/market-neutral funds have been the best-performing liquid strategies in crypto over the past five years, and that many crypto-native prop/arb desks (citing a ~$4B market-neutral London fund as an example) are now running the same playbook against traditional markets.
- Rob reports hearing "AI fatigue" for the first time from large institutional LPs (endowments, funds-of-funds) during a week of West Coast meetings — a shift from insatiable AI-fund demand three to four months ago to concern that valuations got ahead of themselves (citing rumors of a $140-150M-revenue company valued near $10B). Both hosts discuss AI increasingly resembling early crypto in structure: overhyped and undervalued pockets coexist, quoting investor Gavin Baker on hard-to-justify private-market valuations, and noting rapid valuation step-ups (e.g., a round pricing at $3B, then $8B, then $16B within months) that mirror 2021 crypto price action.
- On AI unit economics, the hosts discuss a stealth portfolio company (an OpenRouter-style LLM routing/spend-optimization product) that reportedly cut one large client's inference costs by ~80%, and note that Coinbase built an internal model router and Ramp launched a similar cost-optimization product. They argue that as open-source models close the gap, "frontier model" pricing premiums will erode across most industries within 12-18 months, pressuring the business models of labs like Anthropic and OpenAI even as their revenue (Anthropic ARR reportedly nearing ~$150B mentioned in a tweet, called possibly "the fastest growing ARR in the history of mankind") keeps climbing.
- Robinhood Chain deep dive: launched a few weeks ago on Arbitrum, it reportedly became the largest network by tokenized-stockholder count within a month (per a tweet from "Frank"), surpassing Solana, BNB, Ethereum, and Base, though active accounts have trended down since the initial launch bump and user/wallet counting methodology is contested. Rob credits Robinhood's strong consumer product and distribution (bringing in Lighter, Morpho, Athena and Uniswap rather than building closed-loop protocols) and expects an initial-hype trough before durable growth data emerges, similar to Base's early trajectory.
- Robinhood's earnings: $1.3B in revenue, up ~32% YoY, beating consensus. Prediction markets generated $156M in revenue (10x YoY, boosted heavily by the World Cup) versus crypto trading revenue of ~$100M — prediction markets now bigger than crypto for Robinhood. Robinhood reportedly drove over half of Kalshi's volume historically, then acquired the old LedgerX business (NYX) and formed a JV with Susquehanna called "RothEra," its own prediction-market exchange, to which it is expected to migrate more of its own volume over time (owning "the full stack"). Separately, CME is suing the CFTC over perpetual futures while its earnings call struck a more positive tone on perps — which the hosts read as CME hedging/preparing to compete, and CME reportedly plans to launch sports-linked prediction market contracts.
- Ethereum L2 activity is described as collapsing: total L2 TVL is back to roughly $5B (last seen in 2023) per one chart cited (though L2BEAT's broader ecosystem TVL figure was closer to $33B, with some team-controlled tokens excluded from Arbitrum's count) — sources disagree on methodology but agree on the downward trend. The hosts attribute this to (1) low/underwhelming DeFi yields (basis trade rates compressed from ~10%+ to ~3-4%, RWA yields still above SOFR but with venues like Robinhood Chain subsidizing USDG yields up to ~7% and "X money" around 6%), (2) a brutal year for hacks — 212 exploits and over $1B in losses in H1 2026 alone (3.5x last year), with North Korea's Lazarus Group responsible for roughly half, and Kelp and Drift cited as the largest single incidents, mostly via social engineering rather than smart-contract exploits — and (3) capital rotating into AI/momentum trades instead. They flag active on-chain users (cited around 40-60M per an a16z-style estimate) as the more important long-term metric than TVL, since much of the TVL swings are just ETH/BTC price-denomination effects.
- Ondo Finance's newly announced private/institutional L1 is compared to Canton Network and older "Enterprise Ethereum"/Hyperledger efforts: verifiable like a blockchain but with private, non-public execution/order flow aimed at institutions that don't want order flow exposed, while still keeping settlement public per Rob's understanding of Ondo's positioning. The hosts frame this as one side of an ideological split against Robinhood Chain's fully public, permissionless retail approach — both models may coexist serving different customer bases (retail distribution monetization vs. institutional/TradFi migration).
- Visa announced a stablecoin platform enabling institutions to use stablecoins for settlement and card/network products, which the hosts frame as part of a broader "forward-deployed" adoption wave roughly a year after the GENIUS Act, similar to forward-deployed engineering trends in AI; they cite their portfolio company Velocity as active in this space. They close on a downbeat crypto VC data point — record-low new-deal count in crypto VC this quarter (though total dollars deployed held up due to later-stage mega-rounds), noting fundraising is broadly difficult outside AI/defense/robotics, fund sizes have come down across Paradigm, a16z, Haun, Framework and Variant, and LPs are increasingly scrutinizing whether existing investors are re-upping at ~80-85%+ participation rates as a signal of fund health.

## Overview
This Empire Weekly Roundup, recorded without regular co-host Jason Yanowitz, features Santiago Santos and Dragonfly Capital's Rob Hadick (a frequent guest now co-hosting) covering a week dominated by fading odds on the CLARITY Act, extreme cross-asset volatility, and early signs of "AI fatigue" among institutional allocators. They open with the CLARITY market-structure bill's Polymarket odds sliding from an all-time high near 46% to around 27-30%, driven by unresolved disputes over ethics/conflict-of-interest carve-outs for the Trump family and developer-protection ("BRCA") language, with a possible partisan vote expected next week but declining odds of ultimate passage the longer talks drag toward September.

The middle of the episode is a wide-ranging discussion of how traditional and crypto markets are converging structurally: record prime-brokerage leverage concentrated in AI/memory-chip names, a Leopold Aschenbrenner-related deleveraging rumor, Korea's stock market circuit-breaker record, and the hosts' shared observation that market-neutral/delta-neutral strategies have become the best performers in both crypto and increasingly TradFi. Rob relays a notable anecdote from a week of West Coast LP meetings: institutional allocators are voicing "AI fatigue" for the first time in months, worried that valuations (with anecdotes of funding rounds re-pricing from $3B to $16B in a few months) mirror the 2021 crypto cycle's excesses, even as frontier-lab revenue (particularly Anthropic's) continues to grow at unprecedented rates. This ties into a broader discussion of AI token-economics compression, where LLM routing/cost-optimization products are starting to erode frontier-model pricing power.

A substantial back half focuses on crypto-specific developments: Robinhood Chain's rapid but possibly front-run-driven early traction, Robinhood's Q2 earnings (prediction markets now outearning crypto trading for the company), the CME's defensive pivot on perpetual futures and prediction markets, a broad Ethereum L2 TVL and activity decline attributed to weak yields, a historically bad year for on-chain hacks, and AI capital rotation. They close comparing Ondo's new private/institutional L1 (Canton-like) against Robinhood Chain's public/permissionless retail model as two competing visions for how institutions come on-chain, note Visa's new stablecoin settlement platform, and flag a weak quarter for crypto VC deal counts alongside declining fund sizes industry-wide. The episode ends with lighter "content of the week" picks (the Apple TV+ show "Lucky," a Netflix Nadal documentary, and Sam Walton's autobiography) before signing off for a scheduled hiatus into August.

## Implications
For crypto/macro practitioners, the episode's throughline — "every market is starting to trade like crypto" — suggests risk-management lessons from crypto (avoiding leverage, expecting elevated intraday volatility, and favoring market-neutral or delta-neutral strategies) are becoming broadly applicable to equities and other traditional asset classes, not just digital assets. The CLARITY Act's declining odds argue for discounting near-term US market-structure legislative catalysts while still expecting incremental regulatory clarity via SEC/CFTC rulemaking regardless of the bill's fate. The reported "AI fatigue" among institutional LPs is an early signal worth monitoring for AI-fund allocators and founders raising at frontier-adjacent valuations — routing/cost-optimization products compressing model-inference margins could pressure both AI-native startups' unit economics and the pricing power of large labs within 12-18 months. For those tracking on-chain infrastructure, Robinhood Chain's rise and Ethereum L2 TVL/activity decline suggest capital and distribution advantage are increasingly flowing to consumer-fintech-led chains rather than crypto-native L2s, while the surge in on-chain hacks (particularly social-engineering-driven) argues for renewed scrutiny of operational security rather than assuming smart-contract risk alone. The Ondo-vs-Robinhood-Chain contrast (private/institutional vs. public/permissionless) is a useful framework for evaluating where different categories of tokenized-asset infrastructure will find product-market fit.
