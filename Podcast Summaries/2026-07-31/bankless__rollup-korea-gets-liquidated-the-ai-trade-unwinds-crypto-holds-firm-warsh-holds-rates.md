# ROLLUP: Korea Gets Liquidated | The AI Trade Unwinds | Crypto Holds Firm | Warsh Holds Rates

**Show:** Bankless
**Date:** 2026-07-31
**Transcript source:** web_search_fallback

## Key Points
- South Korea's KOSPI stock index fell roughly 40% in July 2026 after having risen 200-300% in prior months, wiping out an estimated $2 trillion in market value. Hosts David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams report that between 320,000-360,000 trading accounts were fully liquidated to zero — about 3.4% of South Korea's adult population — with over 500,000 accounts liquidated in total. Leveraged single-stock ETFs tied to memory-chip names like SK Hynix and Samsung amplified the losses as retail investors piled into concentrated, leveraged bets during the run-up.
- The AI trade is unwinding alongside the Korean crash. 24-year-old AI-focused investor Leopold Aschenbrenner was reportedly forced to unwind all of his public stock positions after running roughly 4x leverage; Citadel/Ken Griffin is said to have purchased his public book at favorable prices, prompting host speculation about whether Citadel timed the market opportunistically. The hosts compare the episode to the Three Arrows Capital collapse of 2022, though they characterize this unwind as potentially less systemically severe.
- Crypto showed relative resilience through the turmoil: Bitcoin was up about 11% and Ethereum up about 22% over the trailing 30 days, with both roughly flat-to-positive on the week despite the broader market chaos. The hosts frame this as possibly the first time since ChatGPT's 2023 launch that crypto has outperformed AI-linked equities, floating the idea that capital may be rotating out of AI hype and into harder monetary assets. Uniswap's UNI token was highlighted as up 36-37% on a three-month basis, and the ETH/BTC ratio is described as breaking a four-year downtrend.
- New Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh held his first FOMC meeting, with the committee voting 9-3 to hold rates steady at 3.5%-3.75%. Warsh emphasized a hard 2% inflation target even though current inflation is running around 3.6-4%, and the hosts read his approach as favoring quantitative tightening over further rate hikes. They note the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.7% (a multi-year high) and the 30-year yield at 5.2% (highest since 2008), which they connect to broader concerns about long-term debt sustainability.
- The CLARITY Act stalled again: a cloture vote failed in the Senate, and Polymarket odds of passage dropped from about 40% to 26% over the week. SEC Chair Paul Atkins responded by pledging that the agency is "ready, willing, and able to come out with rules" providing regulatory alternatives if Congress can't pass market-structure legislation, underscoring the ongoing uncertainty around digital-asset regulation in the US.
- The episode marks Ethereum's 11th anniversary (launched July 30, 2015, at $0.33; trading around $1,860 at time of recording). The Ethereum Foundation elected a new board member, Pascal Cavasacchio (a pseudonymous privacy/security maximalist and SEAL 911 member), the first new board addition alongside Vitalik Buterin and Aya Miyaguchi.
- New York City published a searchable public database listing nearly one million properties, including names and addresses of owners of units valued over $1 million, as required under state property-record disclosure law. Uniswap founder Hayden Adams called it "the worst mass doxing I've ever seen," and the hosts discuss the acute physical-security risk this creates for wealthy residents, including crypto entrepreneurs who are often public targets.
- Robinhood's Q2 results were discussed as a bellwether for crypto/tradfi convergence: total revenue of $1.3 billion (up 32% YoY), while crypto revenue fell 38% YoY to $100 million (now just 7.6% of total revenue). Event-contract (prediction market) revenue grew 10x YoY to $156 million, and the newly launched Robinhood Chain generated $3.1 million in fees in its first month. The hosts note memecoins are now trading against tokenized-stock pairs on decentralized exchanges, illustrating the blurring line between crypto-native and traditional assets.

## Overview
This ROLLUP episode of Bankless, hosted by David Hoffman and Ryan Sean Adams, surveys a turbulent week in global markets that saw South Korea's stock market suffer a historic collapse, an AI-focused hedge fund forced into a rapid unwind, and crypto emerging as a relative safe haven. The hosts open with the Korean crash — leveraged retail bets on memory-chip stocks like SK Hynix and Samsung drove the KOSPI up hundreds of percent before a 40% reversal wiped out roughly $2 trillion and liquidated hundreds of thousands of trading accounts, an event they contextualize as a cautionary tale about retail leverage in a euphoric market.

The conversation pivots to the AI trade broadly, using Leopold Aschenbrenner's forced liquidation of a highly leveraged public-equities book (reportedly bought up by Citadel) as a symbol of AI-stock froth deflating. Against this backdrop, the hosts highlight that Bitcoin and Ethereum have held up notably well — both posting double-digit monthly gains and outperforming AI-linked equities for what they argue may be the first time since ChatGPT's debut — and speculate this could mark either a durable bottom for crypto or a broader capital rotation away from AI speculation.

Macro and regulatory threads round out the episode: new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting held rates steady in a 9-3 vote while signaling a hawkish 2% inflation stance and a preference for quantitative tightening, against a backdrop of rising long-term Treasury yields. Meanwhile, the CLARITY Act's market-structure legislation stalled again in the Senate, prompting SEC Chair Paul Atkins to reiterate that the agency stands ready to issue its own rules if Congress fails to act. The hosts close with lighter but consequential items — Ethereum's 11th anniversary and a new Ethereum Foundation board member, NYC's controversial public property-ownership database, and Robinhood's Q2 earnings showing crypto revenue declining even as event contracts and its new Robinhood Chain scale rapidly.

## Implications
For crypto market participants, the episode's central takeaway is that Bitcoin and Ethereum's relative resilience during a sharp traditional-market selloff (Korean equities, AI-linked stocks) may signal a shift in capital allocation away from speculative AI plays and toward crypto as a harder, less-levered asset class — a dynamic worth monitoring as a potential bottom signal. The Korean liquidation event and the Aschenbrenner unwind are both framed as warnings about the dangers of high leverage in euphoric, narrow-theme trades (leveraged single-stock ETFs, concentrated AI bets), a lesson directly applicable to crypto traders who use similar leverage instruments. On the regulatory front, the CLARITY Act's continued stalling combined with the SEC's stated willingness to legislate via rulemaking suggests practitioners should prepare for regulatory clarity to arrive administratively rather than through Congress, at least in the near term. The NYC property database episode is a concrete reminder for high-net-worth crypto holders to actively manage personal and financial privacy, since public records can now trivially expose wealth and location. Finally, Robinhood's shifting revenue mix (declining crypto share, surging event contracts and tokenized-asset activity) points to prediction markets and tokenized equities as the faster-growing frontier of retail crypto-adjacent activity, which builders and investors may want to weight more heavily going forward.
