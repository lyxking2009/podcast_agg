# Talk Your Book: Why Momentum Investing Works — Animal Spirits Podcast

## TL;DR
On this Talk Your Book edition, Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson are joined by John Lewis of Nasdaq Dorsey Wright to explain the momentum factor: how it works, why it works, how to implement it correctly, and where its diversification benefits come from. The conversation covers relative-strength methodology, the behavioral and structural reasons momentum persists, volatility weighting, and how momentum differs from growth — plus a Nasdaq Dorsey Wright disclaimer on the rules-based, price-data-only nature of the strategy.

## Key points
- Momentum is a factor premised on relative strength: securities that have outperformed tend to keep outperforming over intermediate horizons, and the strategy systematically overweights them.
- John Lewis explains that relative strength ranking is rules-based and objective, computed from price data alone — no subjective inputs.
- The firm was founded by Tom Dorsey, a pioneer in technical analysis; Dorsey Wright applies that brain power to rules-based momentum strategies.
- Momentum works in part because of behavioral persistence (investor underreaction/overreaction cycles) and in part because of structural market features; it captures a persistent premium.
- Implementation matters: the podcast covers how to build and rebalance a momentum portfolio correctly, including volatility weighting to manage risk.
- Momentum and value have historically shown low correlation, which is where the diversification benefit lives when combined in a portfolio.
- Momentum is distinct from growth — momentum is about price trend/relative strength, not fundamentals or earnings growth.
- The episode includes a full Nasdaq Dorsey Wright disclaimer: relative strength is NOT predictive, is not a guarantee, and is based on historical price activity.

## Overview
In this Talk Your Book episode, hosts Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson interview John Lewis from Nasdaq Dorsey Wright about the momentum factor. Lewis walks through how momentum (measured via relative strength) works, why it has persisted as an anomaly across decades of market history, and the practical questions of implementation: how to rank securities, how frequently to rebalance, and how to size positions. The discussion highlights the diversification benefits of pairing momentum with value, since the two factors have historically exhibited low correlation. Lewis emphasizes that the Dorsey Wright methodology is entirely rules-based and objective — derived from price data alone — and the hosts note the distinction between momentum and growth, clarifying that momentum is about price trend rather than fundamentals. The episode closes with the standard relative strength disclaimers: the strategy is not predictive and past performance does not guarantee future results.

## Implications
- For investors: momentum can be a diversifying complement to value/quality allocations because of its historically low correlation with those factors.
- Implementation quality matters — how you rank, weight (e.g. volatility weighting), and rebalance determines whether you actually capture the premium.
- Momentum is not growth: investors should not confuse price-trend persistence with fundamental growth exposure.
- Rules-based relative strength removes emotional bias from buy/sell decisions, but comes with no guarantee of future outperformance.

## Notable quotes
