# Morpho Is Coming For The $200 Trillion Credit Market | Paul Frambot — Empire

## TL;DR
Jason Yanowitz interviews Paul Frambot, co-founder and CEO of Morpho, about the company's $175M raise, why Morpho believes DeFi lending is winner-take-most, the equity-vs-token design debate, and Morpho Midnight — the new fixed-rate, fixed-term protocol built to bring the $200 trillion traditional credit market onchain. Frambot explains why past fixed-rate lending attempts failed (low liquidity, high gas, retail-first design) and how Morpho's curator model pushes complexity to the edges.

## Key points
- Morpho raised ~$175M (Paradigm-led) in just two to three weeks; underwriting was easy because everything is open and the token is public.
- Frambot's thesis: crypto/DeFi networks are winner-take-most — protocols that unlock the most value for end users aggregate the most capital; only a few primitives (stablecoins, financing, trading, payments) are worth investing in.
- Decentralization's core value prop: neutral rails everyone can trust — the same way Morpho works with competing fintechs (Robinhood, Coinbase, Kraken) because the code is neutral, like HTTP.
- On tokens: a token should be a form of network equity; very few projects were structured that way until recently. Uniswap's unification of the token design is cited as a good example. He favors merging equity and token — one thing accrues value — and is skeptical of buyback-and-burn gimmicks.
- On the Gensler era: many projects created corporate structures to 'prove' decentralization — Frambot calls that a bad legal argument for decentralization; incentives define outcomes.
- Ribbit Capital / Micky Mala: not protocol-design or operating-machine VCs, but they understand incentives and the finance-tech intersection deeply (Ribbit = 'interest rate' in ancient Hebrew), and they build trusted relationships (Coinbase seed, Robinhood board) that helped Morpho.
- Morpho's napkin test: infrastructure connecting lenders (excess capital) to borrowers (need financing) — the pipe between the two; today crypto does only ~$50B of crypto-collateralized lending, and the goal is the entirety of the $200T credit market.
- Morpho Midnight: a fixed-rate, fixed-term protocol resembling traditional finance instruments — the point is not the fixed rate per se, it's giving users (especially institutions) CONTROL over the rate rather than an arbitrary formula.
- Why past fixed-rate lending failed: low liquidity, high gas costs, and retail-first complexity-abstraction design. Morpho's answer: modularize risk management, push complexity to the edges via 'curators' (smart participants like Steakhouse, Gauntlet, Block Analitica), and let institutional operators build abstraction layers for end users.
- Midnight is not yet in vaults — the switch is deliberately left off to build Lindy/trust — but billions in liquidity are already available to quote on it.

## Implications
- For crypto investors: Frambot's framework suggests most token value will accrue to a few winner-take-most primitives — stablecoins, financing, trading, payments.
- For founders: the equity-vs-token question should be resolved toward one clear value-accrual vehicle; clarity for investors matters more than decentralization theater.
- For institutions: Morpho Midnight is explicitly designed for rate control, not just fixed rates — the institutional demand signal is for control over risk parameters.
- For DeFi: the curator model (pushing complexity to the edges) is Morpho's answer to the retail-abstraction model that failed in earlier fixed-rate attempts.
