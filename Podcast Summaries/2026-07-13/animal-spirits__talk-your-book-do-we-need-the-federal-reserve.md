## Key Points

- Guest Alex Morris of F/m Investments argues the Federal Reserve, while imperfect, remains essential for managing monetary policy in a complex modern economy
- The bond market is sending signals about fiscal unsustainability — long-end rates staying elevated even as inflation moderates
- The "debasement trade" reflects investor concern that government debt monetization erodes purchasing power; hard assets and real assets benefit
- Former Fed Governor Kevin Warsh, seen as a potential future Fed Chair, has criticized the Fed's reaction function — F/m Labs is reportedly building an AI model trained on Warsh's written words to model his policy views
- TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) may offer better value than nominal Treasuries given inflation uncertainty and fiscal trajectory
- F/m's ZTOP high-yield bond strategy is positioned for spreads that remain compressed relative to historical norms

## Overview

This "Talk Your Book" episode of Animal Spirits is a sponsored segment with Alex Morris from F/m Investments. Hosts Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson discuss the Federal Reserve's necessity with Morris, who frames the question through the lens of bond markets, inflation, and the growing "debasement trade." The episode surfaces real tension: investors increasingly question the Fed's institutional independence and long-term credibility, even as the Fed's core mandate — managing inflation and employment — remains relevant. A notable innovation discussed is F/m Labs' effort to model future Fed policy under a potential Warsh chairmanship using AI trained on Warsh's own writings. The conversation covers treasury markets, TIPS vs. nominals, and high-yield positioning in a rate-uncertain environment.

## Implications

- Growing skepticism about Fed credibility reinforces demand for real assets, gold, and Bitcoin as debasement hedges
- A Kevin Warsh Fed could represent a more hawkish, rules-based approach — markets pricing this in could mean higher long rates
- TIPS outperforming nominal treasuries would be a sign that inflation expectations are de-anchoring
- Compressed high-yield spreads leave little margin for error if growth slows — ZTOP strategy depends on continued soft landing
