## Key Points

- Liz Thomas (SoFi) and Guy Adami present a constructive second-half 2026 outlook: stock gains are expected to continue despite elevated volatility
- Semiconductor stocks have been subject to sharp swings — the hosts examine whether the AI-driven rally has gotten ahead of earnings fundamentals
- Treasury yield pressures remain a key risk: the 10-year is range-bound but upward pressure persists due to fiscal deficits and debt issuance
- CPI expected at 3.8% and PPI at 6.2% — inflation remaining stickier than the Fed would prefer, constraining rate-cut flexibility
- Japan's weakening yen is flagged as an underappreciated macro risk — yen carry trade unwinds could trigger global volatility similar to August 2024
- Sector opportunities identified for H2: Mag Seven, financials, healthcare, commodities, and energy
- Earnings season is the near-term catalyst — companies that beat and raise guidance will be rewarded; those that disappoint face outsized downside

## Overview

Guy Adami and Liz Thomas of SoFi deliver a focused 31-minute second-half market playbook. The episode is structured as a strategic outlook rather than a news recap — the pair walk through macro risks (inflation, yields, yen), sector opportunities, and earnings-season dynamics. The central thesis is that markets can continue rising in H2 but with more volatility than the first half, as the easy-money macro tailwinds of early 2026 fade. Japan's yen weakness emerges as the sleeper risk: the yen carry trade has quietly re-emerged, and an unwind could be disruptive. The duo is selectively bullish on Mag Seven but warns that valuation compression is possible if AI monetization timelines slip.

## Implications

- Elevated CPI/PPI limits Fed rate-cut optionality through at least Q3, keeping the "higher for longer" trade alive
- Japan yen risk is under-discussed — any BOJ policy shift or yen reversal could cause rapid deleveraging in global equities
- Semiconductor sector could be volatile around earnings — company-specific guidance matters more than macro tailwinds
- Diversification into financials, healthcare, and energy provides defensible return streams if tech leadership rotates
- Second half will be more stock-specific and sector-specific than the broad momentum of H1
