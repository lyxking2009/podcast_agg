## Key Points

- Christopher Begg's investment framework centers on 'graph theory' — viewing exceptional companies as graphs with nodes and edges that strengthen over time, creating compounding network effects.
- His three-pillar framework for evaluating companies: (1) a moat with identifiable layers of competitive advantage that will persist 10+ years, (2) secular tailwinds driving long-term topline growth, and (3) great operators — the human element.
- Begg looks for companies with '8 layers of competitive advantage' — not every company needs all eight, but he wants multiple identifiable, long-lasting layers.
- Alphabet exemplifies his ideal investment: 'one of the most extraordinary businesses ever built' — a graph designed to organize the world's information with powerful moats and secular tailwinds.
- Tesla and SpaceX analysis: Begg initially avoided Elon Musk but changed his mind. He sees a likely Tesla-SpaceX merger within 12 months and has been underwriting SpaceX as a combined entity.
- SpaceX represents 'one of the most powerful emergent graphs built in the past two decades' — Starlink's satellite constellation is a graph with extraordinary network effects, reducing launch costs from $50K/kg to low Earth orbit to a fraction of that.
- The 'source built' concept: Begg discovered the word 'sourcebuilt' is an anagram of 'Heraclitus the Obscure' — his philosophical framework for investing aligned with first principles and natural order.
- Writing is central to Begg's investment process — compressing complexity into essence through quarterly and annual letters, treating the audience as a quality bar for articulating his philosophy.
- Nick Sleep's 'scale economics shared' pattern (as exemplified by Costco) resonates with Begg's framework — companies that share benefits with customers create flywheels aligned with natural laws.

## Overview

William Green interviews Christopher Begg, CEO and CIO of East Coast Asset Management and adjunct professor at Columbia Business School (teaching the Security Analysis course Ben Graham taught to Warren Buffett). The conversation explores Begg's unique investment philosophy built on graph theory, the concept of 'source built' (investing aligned with first principles and natural order), and detailed analyses of Alphabet, Tesla, SpaceX, and Constellation Software. Begg explains his three-pillar framework (moats, secular tailwinds, human element), his approach to 'compressing complexity into essence' through writing, and how studying Heraclitus, Da Vinci, and ancient cathedrals informs his view of enduring businesses. The interview also touches on the Tesla-SpaceX potential merger, EchoStar's spectrum deal with SpaceX, and the importance of proximity to learning through children and students.

## Implications

Begg's graph-theory framework offers a structured lens for evaluating compounders — companies whose competitive advantages form self-reinforcing networks. The Tesla-SpaceX merger thesis (if realized) would create an unprecedented combined entity spanning EVs, energy, satellite internet, and space launch. His emphasis on writing as a learning tool and 'compressing complexity into essence' suggests investors should develop systematic methods for articulating and testing their philosophy. The 'source built' concept — investing aligned with natural order — may appeal to investors seeking a more philosophical foundation for long-term compounding.
