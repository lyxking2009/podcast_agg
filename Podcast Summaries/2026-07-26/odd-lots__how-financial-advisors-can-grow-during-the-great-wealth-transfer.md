# How Financial Advisors Can Grow During the Great Wealth Transfer (Sponsored Content)

**Show:** Odd Lots  
**Date:** 2026-07-26  
**Duration:** 17:57  
**Transcript source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points

- Over $100 trillion is estimated to transfer between generations in the next 25 years, according to Prudential's Cerulli report — a massive opportunity and risk for financial advisors.
- Only about 19% of next-generation inheritors plan to stay with their parents' financial advisor, meaning advisors risk losing the vast majority of inherited assets.
- The primary reason inheritors leave: they feel unheard, unseen, and unable to relate to an advisor who doesn't understand their generation's goals, lifestyle, and planning needs.
- Women are particularly underserved — 62% of advisors think they discuss protection with clients, but only 27% of clients report having that conversation.
- Advisors need a team-based approach: no single advisor can connect with every family member, so building diverse teams that relate to different demographics is essential.
- For younger advisors with a younger client base, the strategy should be 'attack mode' — building infrastructure to engage the next generation as they gain wealth.
- For advisors with older, larger books of business, the focus should be on actively protecting assets by making connections with spouses and next-generation family members before wealth transfers.

## Overview

This sponsored episode of Odd Lots (produced by Prudential and Bloomberg Media Studios) explores the Great Wealth Transfer — the estimated $100+ trillion that will pass between generations over the next 25 years. Host Maggie Lake leads a panel discussion with financial advisor Chelsea Ransom Cooper (Zenith Wealth Partners), behavioral finance planner Britney Castro, and David Blanchette (Prudential's head of Retirement Research). The panel examines why only 19% of inheritors plan to keep their parents' advisor, the behavioral dynamics behind client attrition, the gender gap in advisory services, and practical strategies for advisors to retain and grow their client base through this generational shift.

## Implications

Financial advisors who fail to proactively engage the next generation — especially women and younger inheritors — will lose the majority of transferred assets. The advisory industry must evolve from individual-centric to team-based models that can relate to diverse client demographics. Technology and more holistic service models will be differentiators.
