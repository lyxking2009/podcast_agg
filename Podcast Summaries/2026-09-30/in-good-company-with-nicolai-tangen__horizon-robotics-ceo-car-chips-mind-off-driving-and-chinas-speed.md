# Horizon Robotics CEO: Car Chips, Mind-Off Driving and China's Speed

## Overview

Nicolai Tangen sits down with Dr. Kai Yu, founder and CEO of Horizon Robotics, whose chips and software sit
behind roughly one in three intelligent cars on Chinese roads. They discuss why Yu never wanted to build his own car -
preferring to supply the “brain” - the ferocity of competition in a market that launched 580 new models in six months,
and the roadmap to hands-off driving by 2028 and fully “mind-off” cars by 2035. Yu also explains his Zen-influenced
philosophy of focus and why he believes robots will ultimately hand freedom back to humans.

## Key Points

- Horizon Robotics supplies the compute and software for intelligent vehicles - the “brain inside a self-driving car” - and its technology appears in about one in three intelligent cars on Chinese roads.
- Kai Yu deliberately chose not to build a car company, positioning Horizon as the enabling layer rather than a vehicle brand.
- Chinese market intensity: 580 new car models launched in a six-month window, which sets the clock speed for chip and software roadmaps.
- Stated roadmap: hands-off driving by 2028 and fully mind-off cars by 2035.
- Yu frames his operating philosophy around Zen ideas of focus - doing fewer things, deeply.
- He argues robots will return freedom to humans rather than take it away.

## Implications

The episode is a good illustration of where the autonomous-driving value chain is concentrating outside the United
States: a pure-play chip and software supplier embedded across a third of an enormous domestic market. The 2028/2035
milestone framing is worth tracking against Western timelines, which are generally more conservative on full
mind-off autonomy. Note the limitation of this record: it is built from the episode's official show notes rather than
