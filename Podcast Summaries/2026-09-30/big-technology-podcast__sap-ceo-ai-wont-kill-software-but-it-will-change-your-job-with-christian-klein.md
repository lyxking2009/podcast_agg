# SAP CEO: AI Won't Kill Software, But It Will Change Your Job - With Christian Klein

## Overview

Alex Kantrowitz interviews Christian Klein, CEO of SAP, on the state of enterprise software after the
“SaaS-pocalypse” scare. Klein's framing is that SAP climbed the cloud mountain and is now climbing the AI mountain, with
obstacles to clear: accuracy, governance, and regulation. The discussion covers why mission-critical finance demands
100% accuracy rather than 90-93%, how customer mindsets shifted from experimentation to demanding measurable value,
why planning cycles have compressed from annual to continuous, how companies are introducing token limits to control
AI spend, how SAP is reskilling staff, and where he thinks European technology regulation has gone wrong.

## Key Points

- Klein describes the AI transition as a mountain climb with three obstacles: accuracy of models on business data, governance (who can access and share what data, and where it lives), and regulation.
- He argues AI must reach effectively 100% accuracy for mission-critical processes - financial closing agents at 90-93% are unusable because auditors cannot certify the numbers.
- He expects that level of trustworthiness in “months” rather than years, at which point customers can still choose to keep a human in the loop.
- Business mindset has shifted decisively: customers now ask what the value is rather than experimenting, and the fear of being disrupted has shortened planning cycles and accelerated execution.
- Token economics are becoming a board-level concern - companies are introducing token limits because the top 1% of users consume disproportionate capacity.
- Workforce change is handled through change management and reskilling programmes; Klein acknowledges increased cognitive demands and burnout risk but argues the shift can move people toward more fulfilling work.
- SAP's claimed moat as models improve is process context, data context and governance built over 50 years.
- He criticises European tech regulation for regulating the technology itself rather than focusing on societal outcomes.

## Implications

Klein's argument is essentially that the software industry's value does not disappear when models get better - it moves
to the layer that owns process context, data governance and auditability. For practitioners, the operational signals are
more interesting than the strategy talk: accuracy thresholds that differ by function, token budgets as a new line item,
and the compression of planning cycles. The token-limit observation is a concrete warning that agentic adoption will be
rationed by cost before it is rationed by capability.

## Notable Quotes

- Christian Klein: “We climbed the cloud mountain... and then now we have to climb the AI mountain and on this journey there will be obstacles we have to overcome.”
- Christian Klein: “Sometimes you need an accuracy of 100% ... 90% accuracy is not enough.”
- Christian Klein: “Our customers are now saying okay but what is in it, show me the value.”
