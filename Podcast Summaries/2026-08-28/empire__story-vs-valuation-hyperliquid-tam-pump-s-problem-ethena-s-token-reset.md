# Story vs Valuation, Hyperliquid TAM, Pump’s Problem & Ethena’s Token Reset — Empire

## TL;DR
The episode covers crypto market dynamics after Bitcoin's rally, with Santi arguing risk-reward remains poor. It compares Hyperliquid ($600M annualized revenue, $80B FDV) and Pump ($100M quarterly revenue, ~$1B market cap), attributing their valuation gap to TAM narratives. Ethena's token reset—buying out sellers, aligning equity, adding buybacks, and eliminating VC unlocks—sent ENA up 25% and is framed as a model for token projects.

## Key points
- Santi remains bearish on crypto risk-reward despite BTC rally from $60k to above $70k, arguing that investors are not being compensated for the risk relative to other opportunities, and crypto is highly beta to macro.
- The Bitcoin rally was catalyzed by a $2.7 billion short squeeze and financial media coverage reigniting the 'debasement trade' narrative after the White House increased liquidity, but Santi cautions that crypto is not a hedge and will sell off if equities decline.
- Hyperliquid generated $154 million in Q1 and $148 million in Q2 revenue (~$600 million annualized) with fewer than 20 employees, but quarterly revenue has declined from a peak of ~$300 million in Q3 2025; HYPE rose to $82 after Trump/CFTC comments hinted at US compliance expansion.
- Rob argues Hyperliquid is fully valued on fundamentals at an $80 billion fully diluted valuation, but the market is pricing in a larger TAM if it enters US institutional capital markets; 38% of Q3 notional volume on Hyperliquid is non-crypto assets, supporting a broader 'all assets on blockchain' narrative.
- Pump generates $90-100 million quarterly revenue (about 2x Ethena's current revenue) at a fraction of Hyperliquid's valuation (~$1 billion current market cap vs $18+ billion), but its TAM is perceived as limited to memecoins, and founders have failed to articulate a larger vision.
- The divergence in valuations between Hyperliquid and Pump is attributed to narrative and TAM expansion: Hyperliquid's potential to become a venue for stocks, bonds, and commodities is orders of magnitude larger than Pump's current memecoin focus.
- Ethena announced four major token changes: buyout of seed investors who sold ENA in the last nine months, alignment of token and equity (no residual cash flow to equity), a governance proposal to use net revenue for programmatic buybacks, and elimination of monthly VC unlocks (team tokens remain locked).
- The Ethena token reset was well-received, with ENA up 25%; Rob (Dragonfly) explained that buying out sellers and fully unlocking remaining investor tokens creates real price discovery, reduces overhang, and aligns long-term holders.
- Ethena's revenue has declined from $162 million in Q3 2025 to about $56 million currently, reflecting lower on-chain yields, but the project is diversifying into equity basis trading, secured lending (FalconX deal), and partnerships with Janus Henderson, BlackRock, and Robinhood.
- The conversation highlights a market shift toward story- and narrative-driven investing over DCF valuation, with examples like Instinct (2-person AI startup valued at $2.5B then $5B) and Palantir CEO's antics boosting stock, as well as venture investors pricing startups like options.
- FOMO app is identified as a breakout consumer crypto product due to its beautiful, non-crypto-native UX and data-driven customer acquisition on TikTok/Instagram, potentially a model for Pump to onboard normie users.
- Content recommendations: Invest Like the Best with Neil Moa (ex-Nvidia/Apple), Semi Analysis with Dylan Patel, Rick Rubin's The Creative Act reread, and Netflix show Tires.
