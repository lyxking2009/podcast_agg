# The Four Horsemen of the AI Apocalypse with Ed Zitron — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Ed Zitron presents the ultra-bear case on AI, arguing that hyperscaler revenue growth is dangerously concentrated in unprofitable OpenAI and Anthropic, which cannot realistically meet $440 billion in expected cloud spend. He details Nvidia’s customer concentration, debt-fueled data center buildout, and private credit exposure, predicting a severe financial reckoning when the spending cycle breaks.

## Key points
- Ed Zitron, CEO of EZPR and author of Where’s Your Ed At, became an AI skeptic after noticing hype without disclosed revenues following Sam Altman’s 2023 firing.
- Microsoft’s fiscal 2026 AI revenue was $34.33 billion, of which $24.1 billion came from OpenAI; non-OpenAI AI products like Copilot and GPU rentals remain single-digit billions, insufficient to justify $260+ billion capex.
- UBS, Barclays, and Wells Fargo models imply $440 billion of cloud revenue across Google, Amazon, and Microsoft must come solely from OpenAI and Anthropic—two unprofitable startups—by 2030.
- OpenAI’s own projections show $284 billion revenue by 2030, making it larger than Meta, while requiring over $200 billion annual compute spend, more than double Meta’s OPEX.
- OpenAI’s last $122 billion round included only $12 billion from traditional VCs; Nvidia, SoftBank, and Amazon contributed tens of billions, and such investments are unlikely to repeat.
- OpenAI has committed to spend $138 billion with Amazon over 8 years, $20 billion with Cerebras, $22.4 billion with CoreWeave, $300 billion+ with Oracle, and ~$12.5 billion/year with Google.
- Nvidia’s Q2 FY2027 revenue: 16% from one customer, 44% from three customers, 70% from five hyperscalers, creating extreme concentration risk as Nvidia guides to $670–690 billion FY2028 revenue.
- Nvidia’s equity investments in Anthropic, OpenAI, CoreWeave, Nebius, and others boost net income, resembling GE Capital’s vendor financing of customers, masking underlying demand weakness.
- The “run rate” revenue metrics used by OpenAI and Anthropic are misleading; token-based usage is not recurring and can drop sharply as customers shift to open-source models.
- Current compute demand is estimated at ~$22 billion, while planned data center capacity (190 GW) implies $1.5–3 trillion annual demand—an economic mismatch that cannot be filled by real enterprise demand.
- Stargate Abilene (1.2 GW, ~998k sqft) is behind schedule, and Blue Owl agreed to invest in 10 minutes, signaling poor due diligence in data center debt.
- CoreWeave, a neo-cloud, is unprofitable and must repeatedly raise debt at >9.2% rates; its customers are mostly OpenAI/Anthropic, and failure to roll debt could trigger crisis.
- Private credit funds, including Blackstone and insurance-linked vehicles, are heavily exposed to data center debt, with ratings agencies giving investment-grade ratings to risky neo-cloud debt.
- Oracle is a key warning: stock down 70%, revenue flat for 15 years adjusted for inflation, yet it signed massive OpenAI deals and is firing contract renewal staff.
- The “rot economy” thesis: tech firms prioritized growth over product quality, e.g., Google search degraded after Prabhakar Raghavan’s ads-first reorganization, and Meta sought 12% perpetual engagement growth.
- Ed Zitron admits he was wrong in 2024 for underestimating market irrationality and debt system support, but argues current dynamics make a railroad-style physical infrastructure bust likely.
- Possible triggers for the bust include a hyperscaler CAPEX cut, CoreWeave debt failure, bond sale underperformance, an AI startup insolvency (e.g., Perplexity), or an Oracle downgrade.
- Unlike dot-com, AI GPUs have limited alternative uses; AWS’s total 2003–2015 capex was $29.7 billion adjusted, while current AI buildout is trillions, leaving no second life for stranded assets.
- Ed Zitron holds no financial positions and does not give investment advice, but warns that regular retail investors and pension funds will suffer disproportionately.
