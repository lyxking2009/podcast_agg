# The Chopping Block: Crypto's Rebound, Reg Crypto, and AI Router Wars — Unchained

## TL;DR
The Chopping Block crew discusses crypto's market rebound—Bitcoin near $80k, $2B ETF inflows, and a macro debasement trade—plus Hyperliquid's all-time high after Trump hinted at CFTC compliance efforts. They analyze the SEC's proposed Reg Crypto fundraising rules, the Stripe-OpenRouter acquisition, and AI inference market parallels to DeFi, ending with sentiment from Bhutan where the king is bullish.

## Key points
- Tom and Tarun join from Bhutan where they met the king, who mined Bitcoin when it was $4 and is excited about AI, using tools like Cloud Code, Obsidian, and Kimmy.
- Bitcoin briefly surpassed $80,000 and now trades around $79,500, with over $2 billion in ETF inflows and significant short liquidations; Treasury Secretary Bessent's bond buyback of long-dated debt to issue short has stimulated risk assets via the 'debasement trade'.
- Hyperliquid's token hit an all-time high above $80 after Trump said at a White House crypto summit that CFTC Chairman Celig (likely Caroline Pham) is working to bring Hyperliquid into the US in a fully compliant manner; clarity legislation odds on Polymarket are about 15% this year and roughly 50/50 by 2028.
- Robert and others caution that US access to Hyperliquid will likely be a KYC-gated version similar to Polymarket US, with separate liquidity, market surveillance, and possibly restrictions on auto-deleveraging (ADL) which regulators may not approve.
- The SEC proposed 'Reg Crypto' rulemaking with a 60-day comment period, including a one-time $5 million fundraising exemption over four years and a $75 million over 12 months tier requiring PCAOB audits and ongoing reporting, plus a conditional safe harbor for tokens to exit securities laws.
- Robert argues the $5 million tier is exciting and could attract small projects, but the $75 million tier will likely be rarely used because of high compliance costs similar to Reg A+ tier 2; legal costs for the $5 million tier are estimated at $10,000-$20,000.
- Tarun and Tom note the rules seem designed for 2017-era ICOs where teams planned to decentralize, but modern tokens often have centralized companies and continuous managerial efforts; Tarun predicts someone will engineer multiple $5 million offerings via holding companies to bypass the $75 million threshold.
- Stripe acquired OpenRouter for $7 billion; OpenRouter was founded by Alex Atallah (co-founder of OpenSea) in 2023 as an aggregator of inference providers, analogous to DEX aggregators but for open-source LLMs.
- Tarun published a report titled 'Caching Cheaters' analyzing OpenRouter data, finding that inference providers manipulate cache token pricing by lowering sticker prices on expensive tokens while reporting no cache token usage, effectively cheating on the backend; some participants have crypto backgrounds.
- Tarun draws structural parallels between the AI inference stack and DeFi: front end/harness (wallet), router (DEX router), model (protocol), inference providers (liquidity providers/validators), predicting value will flow to the edges and a 'revenge of the SaaS company' where enterprises with user data become routers and monetize via payment-for-order-flow-like spreads.
- The discussion explores data monetization in AI: inference providers offer cheaper prices for zero-data-retention or rebates for training data, echoing 2017 crypto's 'get paid for your data' dream; AI labs like Anthropic are reportedly spending $10 billion on data payments this year.
- Market sentiment in Asia: Tom notes most teams are still in RWA/tokenization mode and caught off guard by the sudden crypto rebound; memecoin FOMO is largely a US phenomenon, but there is interest in localizing FOMO for Asia, with Binance Life (a Chinese-character memecoin) rallying.
- The group concludes crypto is becoming cool again with green shoots, but a full bull market is not yet confirmed; they note the king of Bhutan is bullish and the Asian market still loves tokens.
