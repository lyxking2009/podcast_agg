# Austan Goolsbee Is Worried the Economy Is Overheating — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee says he is nervous that inflation has not looked great over the last six months and wants evidence that recent price shocks will prove transitory before supporting further rate cuts. He discusses how AI investment and supply-chain disruptions complicate the Fed's read on whether policy is restrictive, and he advocates for revisiting communication tools like the dot plot while emphasizing reaction function over forward guidance. Goolsbee also dissented from the last rate cut because he felt front-loading cuts without data was premature.

## Key points
- Goolsbee says current policy restrictiveness depends on the assumed inflation rate; with 3% nominal rates and 2% inflation, the real rate is 1%, but if inflation is running above 3%, the real rate is much lower and policy may not be restrictive enough.
- He calls the neutral rate (r-star) a 'Sasquatch' because it is unobservable and only visible in hindsight, making it unhelpful for near-term policy decisions.
- AI could increase productivity and thus raise the long-run neutral rate, but if the AI hype leads to overheating, the Fed may need to hike rates, similar to the mid-to-late 1990s.
- Goolsbee decomposes recent inflation into temporary tariff and oil price shocks versus deeper services inflation, and he worries about repeating a 'transitory' narrative quarter after quarter.
- The Fed should think more about how to respond to frequent supply shocks, which cause unemployment and inflation to move together rather than the traditional trade-off.
- In the Chicago district, businesses report that AI data centers are buying up land, driving up construction costs, and creating shortages of electricians and HVAC workers, but this is not yet broad aggregate overheating.
- Short-run consumption-driven and investment-driven growth have similar implications for Fed policy, but long-run investment could boost potential output if productivity growth materializes; however, productivity growth has been weak for six months.
- The decline in inflation from 2022-23 was roughly two-thirds supply healing and one-third demand; the Fed's aggressive hikes anchored inflation expectations, as shown by TIPS breakevens holding near 2.3% CPI even when CPI approached 10%.
- Goolsbee attributes rising long-term bond yields to a combination of inflation expectations, Fed policy path, and increased issuance, not to default fears, and he cautions against overreacting to market moves.
- He supports the Fed's rethinking of communication tools, favoring less forward guidance but clearer reaction functions; he criticizes the dot plot because the median inflation forecast and median rate forecast may come from different people.
- Goolsbee's own reaction function is heavily focused on inflation: he dissented at the last meeting because he did not want to front-load rate cuts without seeing data confirming inflation is heading back to 2%.
- The FOMC under new Chair Kevin Warsh feels different, with more debate and a push to rethink formats; Goolsbee says former Chair Powell is still at the table.
- At Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, Goolsbee will be watching for clues about the chairman's reaction function and whether he sees inflation as transitory or persistent.
