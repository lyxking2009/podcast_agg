# Building the Foundation for the Agentic AI Era — Practical AI

## TL;DR
Angie Jones, VP of the Agentic AI Foundation, discusses how Block taught 12,000 employees to use AI agents, then used a 1% champion model to embed agent best practices into engineering repos. The foundation, formed by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Block under the Linux Foundation, hosts neutral open standards like MCP, AgentsMD, Goose, Agent Gateway, and A2A, enabling competitors to collaborate on critical infrastructure. Jones emphasizes the need for global perspectives—such as China's mobile-first agent use and EU AI Act compliance—and predicts that AI will become part of everyday life, but hopes humans and agents find a balanced division of labor.

## Key points
- Angie Jones previously led company-wide AI agent adoption at Block (12,000 employees), teaching finance, marketing, design, and HR to use agents by focusing on direct value like instant data access instead of waiting for report requests.
- To improve developer velocity, Block initially saw limited gains from general agent use; Jones implemented the 1-9-90 rule, recruiting 50 champion engineers representing major repos (frontend, backend, mobile iOS/Android) to test and embed agent best practices such as context engineering, agents.md files, and skills directly into repositories, so all team members benefit without individual training.
- The Agentic AI Foundation, formed in late 2025 by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Block under the Linux Foundation, provides a neutral home for open standards like MCP, AgentsMD, Goose, Agent Gateway, and A2A, preventing any single company from controlling critical agent infrastructure.
- MCP (Model Context Protocol), originally from Anthropic, standardizes how agents connect to applications and tools; moving it to the foundation gives companies confidence to build on it without fear of vendor lock-in or roadmap changes.
- AgentsMD, created by OpenAI, standardizes how codebases communicate operating instructions to agents, enabling any agent pointed at a repository to follow team-specific rules embedded in the code.
- Goose, one of the first open-source AI agents, provides an agentic runtime; Agent Gateway (from Solo) mediates MCP and A2A traffic, adding enterprise controls like observability and access management.
- A2A (Agent-to-Agent protocol), contributed by Google, enables agents to delegate work and collaborate with other agents; it is especially relevant in mobile-first markets like China where apps like WeChat need agent interoperation.
- The foundation hosts working groups for agentic commerce, security, identity, and EU AI Act compliance, where competitors like Visa, PayPal, and Stripe collaborate to define rules of the game before competing, ensuring standards reflect diverse global needs.
- Global perspectives highlight regional differences: China's mobile-first ecosystem drives demand for agent-to-agent protocols, while Europe's EU AI Act pushes for transparency standards like watermarking; the foundation's neutral working groups allow these voices to shape standards.
- Despite being competitors, companies collaborate effectively in foundation working groups because their own product roadmaps depend on common standards; Jones notes that MCP's Discord server shows 'the beauty of open source and people working together from competing companies.'
- Jones observes that AI adoption among the general public remains low compared to the tech bubble, and many people fear or resist AI; she advocates realistic education that acknowledges AI's strengths and weaknesses, helping people find tasks to delegate while retaining human advantages.
- Looking ahead, Jones hopes that as AI becomes ubiquitous, society will find a middle ground where agents handle repetitive tasks but humans are not relegated to serving the agents; the foundation's role will be to establish interoperable standards that support this balance.
