# Software’s Epic Comeback, Meta’s AI Layoffs Blunder, South Korea Stock Market Chaos — Big Technology Podcast

## TL;DR
This episode examines three AI-related overreactions: Salesforce and other software stocks rebounded after fears of a 'SaaS apocalypse' were deemed premature, with Salesforce up 41% in the past month but still down 1.94% YTD; Meta's 'Project OT' to replace engineers with AI failed, showing a 220% increase in code changes but only 36% increase in user-facing features and a 40% spike in incidents, leading Zuckerberg to cancel further layoffs; and South Korea's KOSPI index plunged 40% after retail investors piled into leveraged AI semiconductor ETFs, wiping out many 'ant' investors.

## Key points
- Salesforce stock initially dropped 22% YTD through Wednesday amid fears AI would undermine SaaS subscriptions, but then surged 12% after earnings, and by late August had risen 41% over the past month and 72% since June 22, though still down 1.94% for the year. Other software names also rebounded: Adobe +16.49% and Microsoft +29% over the past month, and the IGV software ETF +19.68%.
- Salesforce and Anthropic announced a partnership letting users query Salesforce data with Claude (referred to as 'Claw Force'), but Ranjan Roy argues the technology was already possible and the announcement was primarily PR to make Anthropic appear friendly pre-IPO; Salesforce's stock jumped 22% in a single day, its second-largest ever.
- Anthropic's $2 trillion valuation story requires disrupting enterprise software like Salesforce, so the partnership is strategically confusing; Salesforce risks ceding the user interface layer to AI assistants, potentially reducing its long-term pricing power.
- Meta's 'Project OT' (organizational transformation) planned to replace many employees with AI, exploring slashing team sizes by up to 60%, using small pods with AI tools and 4-week sprints, but internal data showed code changes up 220% YoY while user-facing features only rose 36%, and infrastructure incidents spiked 40% with firefighting time up 70%.
- Zuckerberg admitted AI agents aren't ready; Meta implemented a 10% cut but canceled additional companywide layoffs planned for November, instead promising stability; Zuckerberg framed tradeoff as rising compute costs vs. people expenses.
- Meta settled US state lawsuits over children's social media addiction for $18 billion (originally seeking $200 billion); settlement includes turning off like counts by default for minors, offering chronological feed option, 2-hour daily limit for under-18s, no usage midnight-6am, and no notifications during school hours.
- Ranjan Roy supports the settlement's demetrication and chronological feed ideas, having advocated since 2019; he believes these should apply to all users, not just teens, and suspects Meta's move is partly aimed at pressuring TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar limits.
- South Korea's KOSPI index tripled then fell 40% over six weeks in June-July, then rebounded 20% from lows; individual investors ('ants') account for 60-70% of daily trading volume, and many used leveraged ETFs on Samsung and SK Hynix, leading to massive losses.
- Examples from South Korea: a 44-year-old sound engineer lost $7,200 in a week after investing half his severance; a 25-year-old software developer who started with $14,000 saw her account up 58% by May, then fell to 5% below her starting amount after the crash; she said 'I got greedy.'
- The episode's overarching theme: AI capabilities are accelerating but organizational adoption and business disruption take years; markets repeatedly overreact to short-term narratives, creating volatility; retail investors using leverage risk being wiped out in corrections.
