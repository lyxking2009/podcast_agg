# The Science Behind The Markets: Masters in Business with David Booth — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
David Booth, founder and chairman of Dimensional Fund Advisors, recounts his journey from University of Chicago to creating one of the first index funds at Wells Fargo and later building DFA into a trillion-dollar asset manager. He argues markets are efficient, professional managers rarely beat them, and investors should stay calm, diversify across factors, and avoid reacting to short-term noise. Booth also discusses his book Stay Calm, the importance of financial advisors, ETF innovation, and major philanthropic gifts including $300 million to Chicago Booth and Kansas athletics.

## Key points
- David Booth began as a research assistant to Nobel laureate Gene Fama at the University of Chicago, where the CRSP database (starting in 1926) enabled empirical testing of market efficiency, leading to the efficient market hypothesis and evidence that professional managers cannot consistently beat the market.
- At Wells Fargo in the early 1970s, Mac McQuown created one of the first index funds, an S&P 500 portfolio for institutional clients that eventually became the cornerstone of BlackRock; Booth's team simultaneously developed a higher-beta strategy for Samsonite, which laid groundwork for Dimensional.
- Dimensional Fund Advisors was founded in 1981 in Brooklyn with no track record; its first fund focused on micro-cap stocks based on Rolf Banz's dissertation showing the smallest NYSE quintile outperformed, and later incorporated Fama-French multi-factor model including size, value, and other factors.
- Dimensional differentiates from pure indexing by avoiding forced trades around index changes; research shows S&P 500 additions experience about a 4% price run-up before inclusion, and DFA uses flexible trading and patience to provide liquidity and capture better execution prices.
- Booth emphasizes that uncertainty is the source of opportunity in both life and investing; if all investments were riskless, all would return the risk-free rate, so embracing uncertainty and staying invested through drawdowns is crucial.
- The book Stay Calm advocates planning rather than predicting, controlling what you can (savings, allocation, risk) and managing what you can't (market crashes, interest rates), and tuning out financial media noise which is mostly distracting.
- Dimensional initially served institutional clients and later partnered with fee-only financial advisors starting around 1989 through Dan Wheeler; advisors help educate clients and prevent panic selling, as one advisor said, 'I don't have clients with investment problems. I've got investments with client problems.'
- Dimensional avoided ETFs for decades because advisors didn't need them and mutual funds offered clean end-of-day NAV; it launched its first ETF in 2020, became the largest active ETF issuer, and recently received SEC approval to merge mutual fund and ETF share classes into a single pool.
- Booth made significant philanthropic gifts including $300 million to the University of Chicago Booth School of Business in 2008, $300 million to University of Kansas Athletics, and purchased James Naismith's original basketball rules for $4.5 million to donate to Kansas.
- He also endowed the conservation lab at MoMA and signed the Giving Pledge; his philosophy ties wealth to values and purpose, not just money, as his parents were 'wealthy' despite having little money.
- For young graduates, Booth advises aligning passion, skill, and personal values, working hard, and iterating toward meaningful work rather than compromising values for short-term jobs.
- Looking back, Booth says the hardest part was persuading people about evidence-based investing; after 55 years, adoption is still growing, but the data consistently shows that most active managers underperform, with only 27% beating the market in a recent 12-month period per WSJ.
