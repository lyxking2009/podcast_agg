# ROLLUP: The Debasement Trade is Back | Bessent Put | Tokenized Stocks | AI Capital Crunch — Bankless

## TL;DR
Hasib Qureshi joins Bankless to discuss the return of the debasement trade as Treasury Secretary Bessent floats yield curve control, the market's reaction, and the implications for crypto. They also cover Coinbase's tokenized stocks launch, Hyperliquid's new L2, Athena's token buyback and fee switch, and the AI capital crunch. The episode concludes with a deep dive into whether AI will absorb all global capital and what that means for crypto and macro.

## Key points
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent signaled a more aggressive approach to managing long-term yields, increasing long-end buybacks from $2B to $4B per week and suggesting the Treasury could tap the $950B Treasury General Account if needed. This 'Besant put' is being interpreted as a form of yield curve control, which markets initially reacted to but later questioned given the small size of the interventions relative to the market.
- Stan Druckenmiller publicly criticized Bessent in the Wall Street Journal, arguing that long-term Treasury yields are the most important price and the only fiscal disciplinarian the US has left. He warned that artificial yield suppression is a subsidy to procrastination and that if the 30-year must trade at 5.5% to clear, that is an invoice, not a crisis.
- Hasib Qureshi explained that high long-term yields are not just about US debt concerns but also about crowding out from hyperscaler AI capex debt. Companies like Amazon and Meta are issuing long-term debt at rates competitive with government bonds, causing investors to demand higher yields on Treasuries due to increased competition for capital.
- Qureshi attributed the recent crypto rally to seller exhaustion after months of bear market, combined with the reflexive nature of crypto. He noted that Bitcoin's largest weekly dollar gain in history occurred last week ($14K), and he believes Bitcoin is unlikely to trade below $60K this year, citing options market probabilities showing roughly even odds of hitting $100K vs $60-65K in 2026.
- Coinbase launched tokenized stocks on Base, but Qureshi is skeptical about near-term demand, noting that trading volume in tokenized stocks is dwarfed by perpetual derivatives. He argues that feature parity with TradFi is defensive, not offensive, and that meaningful adoption by savers holding stocks on-chain is likely 3-4 years away.
- Hyperliquid's L2 Elysium is an attempt to build a high-performance EVM co-located with HyperCore, using HYPE as gas and exposing order book data. Qureshi is skeptical, pointing out that HyperEVM underperformed and that most people use Hyperliquid for perps trading, not as a general-purpose ecosystem. He thinks the odds are difficult because most L2s do not make it.
- Athena (Ethena) executed a major token restructuring: buying out early investors who had been selling, eliminating monthly VC unlocks, and proposing a fee switch for governance. As a seed investor, Qureshi says the market loves it (ENA up 20%), and it may serve as a model for other tokens with 'baggage' from the previous regulatory regime.
- Coinbase launched Bitcoin-backed mortgages, which Qureshi sees as symbolic but positive, showing Bitcoin is being taken seriously as collateral. However, he notes that borrowing against Bitcoin and using cash for a down payment was already possible, so it's not a huge unlock.
- The Zcash ETF went live, and Qureshi argues that it's fine for cypherpunks because ETFs don't prevent shielded transactions and actually benefit by increasing Zcash's value. He says a truly libertarian technology would allow both suits and cypherpunks to use it.
- On the AI capital crunch, Qureshi is bullish, comparing AI capex to reinvesting in labor as human population growth declines. He believes money supply will expand to fund AI, and warns that Anthropic's upcoming IPO will bring extreme volatility. He sees parallels to crypto's boom-bust cycles but expects AI to drive long-term growth.
