# Imran Khan Isn't Worried About Nvidia's "Circular" Deals — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Imran Khan, CIO of Pro Am Asset Management, breaks down Nvidia's blowout quarter and argues the market already discounts concerns about circular deals, with the stock trading at ~15x forward EPS despite 70% growth guidance. He sees Nvidia as the AI ecosystem, not just a chipmaker, and views its investments in neo clouds and supply chain as strategic moat-building rather than reckless bubble behavior. Khan also discusses memory names (story fading from 10 to 8), Salesforce's improving fundamentals and Anthropic partnership, and acknowledges pockets of bubble in private valuations and capital issuance while defending large-cap AI valuations.

## Key points
- Nvidia reported an exceptional quarter, beating revenue estimates by more than $4 billion and guiding well above consensus, while management said demand is even higher than guidance—unusual visibility for a company of its size this early in the year.
- Nvidia guided to roughly 70% year-over-year revenue growth for next year, far above the ~45% Street consensus, which Imran Khan says reduces the key risk around durability of AI investment that plagued work-from-home stocks in 2021.
- Nvidia set a floor for gross margins despite rising memory costs, giving investors visibility into earnings; analysts flow through numbers around $15 in GAAP EPS for fiscal 2028 (calendar 2027).
- Pre-market after earnings, Nvidia traded at roughly 14.8–15x that $15 EPS estimate, well below its historical 25–30x multiple and below market peers, indicating the market is heavily discounting circular flow revenue.
- If one assumes 25% of Nvidia's revenue comes from investments in partners and removes it, EPS falls to about $12, still putting the stock around 20x earnings—below historical average—so Khan argues the circular flow discount is already priced in.
- Khan explains Nvidia's aggressive investing in neo clouds serves two purposes: diversifying customer concentration away from hyperscalers like Amazon and Google, and locking in the supply chain so competitors like OpenAI cannot source needed memory and components at scale.
- He describes Nvidia as not merely a GPU maker but the entire AI ecosystem, built through CUDA, supply partner relationships, neo-cloud investments, and open-weight models, making the statement 'If Nvidia doesn't work, I just don't see how AI works.'
- Khan believes compute is a new asset class with a longer lifetime value than most assume because the industry is supply-constrained; Nvidia's position as TSMC's largest customer and its backlog give it pricing power and the ability to later add high-margin revenue share agreements.
- On memory, he notes the sector's gross margins went from negative to around 85% in the past cycle, but the story has moved from '10 to 8'—margin expansion is largely over, and investors are now focused on buybacks and long-term agreements rather than fundamental improvements, making it a harder way to make money.
- Khan's rule of easy money: the best investments are when revenue, margins, and market share all improve simultaneously; buying a stock solely for multiple expansion or buyback speculation is a difficult way to compound capital, which is why he avoids memory names at this stage.
- Salesforce reported a quarter where growth rate accelerated by one point, margins and seat counts improved, and stock-based compensation abuse lessened; the stock jumped 18%, and the Anthropic partnership with Dario Amodei signals that software companies must pay AI tolls but can offset costs via customer pricing or cost cuts.
- Imran Khan acknowledges elements of bubble in private market valuations, M&A prices, and new capital issuance—noting 1999 saw new issues equal to 1% of market cap and we are approaching that level—but argues it is hard to call Nvidia a bubble at 15x earnings.
- The conversation highlights that off-balance-sheet AI financing via SPVs, private credit, and partnerships with firms like KKR, Apollo, and BlackRock spreads risk beyond hyperscalers; if AI capex fails, losses would be widespread and trigger a macro problem, so risk management is as important as stock picking.
