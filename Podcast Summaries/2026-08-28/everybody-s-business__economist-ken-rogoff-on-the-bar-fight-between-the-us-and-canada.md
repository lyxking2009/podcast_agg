# Economist Ken Rogoff on the “Bar Fight” between the US and Canada — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
Economist Ken Rogoff characterizes the US-Canada tariff fight as an ego-driven 'bar fight,' arguing the US holds most leverage but will suffer long-term reputational and strategic damage, especially by pushing Canada toward China. The episode also explores how lab-grown diamonds disrupt the natural diamond market, with production costs falling to $15 per carat and industry attempts to reposition natural stones as luxury goods. Additional segments cover Stanley Druckenmiller's AI-written Wall Street Journal op-ed and Dolly Parton's business legacy.

## Key points
- Ken Rogoff describes the US-Canada tariff conflict as an ego-driven 'bar fight,' noting that Canada's economy is less than one-tenth the size of the US, yet Canadians largely support Prime Minister Mark Carney's defiant retaliation.
- Mark Carney's January Davos speech declared 'we are in the midst of a rupture, not a transition,' accusing the international rules-based order of being asymmetrical; it made him a hero in Europe and deepened Trump's personal anger.
- Trump 1.0 signed the USMCA, which eliminated tariffs, and companies built cross-border supply chains on that agreement; current tariffs on steel, aluminum, seafood, plywood, wigs, dog muzzles, and buoys escalate far beyond the original deal.
- Carney strategically targets tariffs at border states—Maine, Michigan, and Ohio—which trade heavily with Canada, and Ontario Premier Doug Ford has also publicly told Trump to 'kiss my ass,' reflecting Canadian defiance.
- Rogoff argues the US holds most of the leverage because two-thirds of Canada's exports go to the US, but the trade war damages US soft power, elevates China, and pushes Canada toward China and Europe.
- Rogoff warns that these protectionist policies may persist even if Democrats win in 2029, citing Bernie Sanders' longstanding opposition to NAFTA as evidence that tariffs are not easily reversed.
- Lab-grown diamonds now cost about $15 per carat to produce using carbon, graphite, salt, and diamond seeds; they are molecularly identical to natural diamonds, requiring special machines to detect because they are too perfect.
- Pandemic-era technology and cheap production in India and China caused lab-grown diamond prices to collapse, driving average engagement ring sizes from half a carat to two carats and creating a K-shaped market where natural diamonds become luxury items.
- The natural diamond industry is trying to rebrand as 'natural diamonds' and may emulate Rolex, which retains high secondary market value despite functional obsolescence, but the industry faces existential questions about perceived value.
- Stanley Druckenmiller acknowledged using AI to write his Wall Street Journal op-ed on bond market manipulation, saying he was 'proud' of it; the Journal defended publishing AI-written prose if the argument is original.
- Reflecting on Dolly Parton's death at 80, hosts highlight her business savvy: she kept music rights, refused to let Elvis record 'I Will Always Love You' to preserve ownership, paid Dollywood employees' college tuition, and funded literacy programs.
