## TL;DR
David Reich and Ali Akbari's new study overturns the consensus that natural selection stalled after the agricultural revolution, showing it actually accelerated, especially during the Bronze Age. Over the past 10,000 years, genetic predictors of cognitive performance rose by a full standard deviation, mostly between 4,000–2,000 years ago. Reich also proposes a heretical model where Neanderthals are essentially modern humans genetically swamped by archaic admixture, challenging the separate-lineage narrative.

## Key points
- Reich and Akbari scaled ancient DNA sequencing and developed a new statistical method to find that natural selection has accelerated over the last 10,000 years, overturning the consensus that it was dormant since the agricultural revolution. Selection peaked during the Bronze Age (~3,000 years ago), affecting gene frequencies for immune function, body fat, and intelligence.
- The genetic predictor of cognitive performance increased by roughly one full standard deviation over the last 10,000 years, with most of the change concentrated between 4,000 and 2,000 years ago, suggesting strong recent selection on cognition.
- Reich proposed a new model: around 300,000 years ago, a small population near the Caucasus invented Middle Stone Age technology and expanded. Those who moved into Europe interbred with local archaics, becoming genetically swamped and evolving into Neanderthals. The expansion into Africa mixed with more diverged archaics, producing modern humans.
- Under this model, Neanderthals and modern humans share the same cultural ancestry and initial expansion, differing only in which archaic populations they later mixed with, making Neanderthals effectively genetically swamped modern humans rather than a separate lineage.
