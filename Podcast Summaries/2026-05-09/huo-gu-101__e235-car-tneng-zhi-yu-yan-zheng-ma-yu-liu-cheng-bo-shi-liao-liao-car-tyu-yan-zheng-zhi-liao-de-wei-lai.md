## TL;DR
CAR-T therapy represents a paradigm shift in cancer treatment by using a patient's own engineered immune cells to target cancer, achieving long-term remissions in some blood cancers. However, high costs (around $150,000 per dose) and limited efficacy in solid tumors remain hurdles. Recent large pharma investments in in vivo CAR-T—such as Lilly's $7 billion acquisition of Kelonia—signal a push toward cheaper, more scalable treatments, with guest Liu Cheng predicting that cancer could be largely curable within 20 years.

## Key points
- CAR-T therapy extracts a patient's T cells, engineers them with a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) to recognize cancer cells (e.g., via CD19 in B-cell cancers), and reinfuses them. This personalized approach yields 90% complete response rates in certain leukemias but costs about $150,000 per dose.
- In 2025–2026, AstraZeneca, AbbVie, Bristol Myers Squibb, and Eli Lilly made strategic acquisitions in in vivo CAR-T startups, with Lilly's purchase of Kelonia for up to $7 billion being the largest. In vivo CAR-T aims to engineer T cells inside the body using vectors, potentially cutting costs 10-fold.
- Allogeneic CAR-T using donor cells has struggled with efficacy and graft-versus-host disease; in vivo CAR-T combines the personalization of autologous therapy with the off-the-shelf convenience and lower cost of allogeneic approaches.
- CAR-T has shown limited success in solid tumors due to the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, lack of unique surface antigens, and poor T-cell infiltration. Overcoming these barriers requires better target selection and engineering cells to resist suppressive signals.
- Jennifer Doudna co-founded Azalea to apply CRISPR gene editing to improve the consistency and reliability of in vivo CAR-T by modifying T cells directly within the patient's body.
- China leads in CAR-T development owing to strong research foundations, a dual regulatory pathway (drug approval and medical technology), high patient volumes, and cost efficiencies (manufacturing costs are ~40% of US levels). The 818号令 regulation, effective May 2026, aims to govern cell and gene therapies.
- Guest Liu Cheng, a pioneer with extensive CAR-T patents, believes cancer will be 'curable' within 20 years, defining cure as not dying from cancer, through therapies like CAR-T that achieve durable remissions.
- In vitro CAR-T will coexist with in vivo methods: the former offers better quality control and proven safety; the latter addresses scalability and cost, and is better suited for broader adoption.
