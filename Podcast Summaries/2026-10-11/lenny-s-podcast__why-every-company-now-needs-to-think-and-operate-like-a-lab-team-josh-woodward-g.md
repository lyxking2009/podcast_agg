## Overview

Lenny Rachitsky interviews Josh Woodward, the head of Google Labs, the Gemini app, and AI Studio, who has spent over 17 years at Google running its longest-running and largest labs team (NotebookLM/Gemini Notebook, Project Genie, Flow). The conversation is about product culture under rapidly changing capability: where great ideas come from, how to run cheap experiments, how to recognise genuine product-market fit early, and how to know when to kill an idea. ## Key Points

- Core thesis: every company now needs to operate like a labs team, because what is possible changes every week and conventional product roadmaps cannot keep pace.
- The "almost possible" framework — Woodward's method for choosing bets that are just beyond current capability, which is where the leverage is.
- Where the best ideas actually come from inside a large company, and why Woodward treats idea generation as a designed culture rather than luck.
- How to recognise genuine product-market fit early, before the metrics are unambiguous.
- Knowing when to kill an idea is presented as the harder and more valuable skill than generating one.
- Skills rising in value in the AI era — and why roles are NOT actually blurring into one universal builder role.
- Practical guidance on setting up an internal labs team.

## Implications

For product leaders, the operational takeaway is to separate exploration from the core roadmap and to formalise quitting criteria up front. Woodward's point that roles are not collapsing into a single builder role argues against reorganising product, design, and engineering into one generalist function.
