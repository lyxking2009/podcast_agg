# Sell-side Indicator (BofA) is almost on Sell, Vix is still asleep, the Bear Case revisited, FICO massacred | WAYT? — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Josh Brown and Michael Batnick discuss Bank of America's sell-side indicator nearing a sell signal (30 bps away) while the VIX remains at 16, contrasting with rising FX and bond volatility. They revisit Michael Burry's bearish calls, noting that AI depreciation fears and bond-market skepticism have failed to materialize, as evidenced by strong demand for Paramount debt. The episode covers FICO's 60% collapse after regulators allowed VantageScore competition, ending its monopoly, plus the market's extreme 'un-broadening' where tech leads but 29% of Russell 3000 stocks are down 30%+.

## Key points
- Bank of America's Savita Subramanian reports the sell-side indicator increased in September and is now 30 basis points from a sell signal, tracking strategists' average recommended equity allocation. The indicator is contrarian; historically, extreme bullishness has marked near-term tops, but the AI bull-bear survey still shows bears, and next 12-month returns have been mixed.
- The VIX is at 16 and has flatlined since March, while the JPMorgan Global FX Volatility Index and BofA MOVE index show elevated currency and bond volatility. Josh Brown characterizes the market as 'no fear, tons of doubt'—record earnings and upside surprises coexist with shrinking multiples.
- Jeff deGraaf of Renaissance Macro highlights the market cycle clock in the upper-right quadrant, similar to October 1987 and 2018, when growth and inflation conditions led to sharp declines. He is not calling for a crash but warns that bad things historically happen in this zone with the Fed tightening. Josh expects a VIX spike in October due to the divergence.
- Michael Burry tweeted that the stock market is in the 'first stage of grief denial' per 2000 and 2008, lasting six to nine months. Josh and Michael note that Burry's earlier claim about overstated AI chip depreciation has been contradicted by a16z data showing GPU rental values stable and chips remaining in active use.
- Bond-market appetite for AI capex remains robust. Michael points to Paramount's debt offering attracting $150 billion in orders for a $52 billion deal, and the narrative shifted after Meta's stock surged on the Muse launch, easing concerns about hyperscaler bond auctions.
- FICO was the number one performing S&P 500 stock from 1990 through November 2024, compounding at ~20% annually, but is now down 60% year-to-date and the worst performer in the index. The FHFA allowed VantageScore (owned by TransUnion, Equifax, and Experian) as an alternative; Rocket Mortgage went from 0% to 41% usage and United Wholesale from 0% to 26% since May. FICO had raised prices from $0.60 to $10 per score, and VantageScore offers borrowers an average closing cost savings of $1,600 per loan.
- Despite the stock collapse, FICO's fundamentals remain strong: the CEO raised fiscal 2026 guidance, buybacks are heavy, and all key numbers are up and to the right. The street is re-rating the stock due to loss of monopoly pricing power, and securities fraud investigations are underway regarding management disclosures.
- Market breadth has un-broadened sharply. Tech is at all-time highs, and the correlation between tech and the rest of the market is the most negative since 2022. 29% of Russell 3000 stocks are in a 30%+ drawdown; software has 47% of stocks down 30%+, autos 40%. Only 1 in 3 S&P 500 stocks are outperforming the index year-to-date.
- Duality Research shows that when the rest of the market diverges downside relative to tech, it historically catches up rather than tech crashing. The warning sign is when tech weakens while the rest of the market strengthens (broadening), which occurred earlier in 2025, suggesting current divergence may be bullish for eventual broadening.
- Refiners Valero, Marathon, and Phillips 66 have broken out dramatically, with Valero up ~120% YTD (from $100 to $400+). They benefit from a de facto monopoly on refining capacity; Josh notes the FICO episode shows regulatory stroke-of-pen risk applies to any monopoly-like business, including refiners if government allows new capacity.
- Consumer discretionary stocks are signaling weakness: American Express, McDonald's, Home Depot, Pepsi, Walmart, and TJX are under pressure. Michael flags AMEX as a key signal, while many others are idiosyncratic or rate-sensitive, such as staples losing out to 5% Treasury yields.
