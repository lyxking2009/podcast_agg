# Cosmic Queries – Light Sails & Quantum Scales — StarTalk with Neil deGrasse Tyson

## TL;DR
Neil deGrasse Tyson and comic co-host Chuck Nice field a wide-ranging grab bag of fan questions: the bubble universe model of the multiverse, whether there is such a thing as a shared “now,” Hoag's Object and chance galaxy alignments, whether fractals are the key to understanding the universe or just entertaining, and whether Project Starshot's laser-propelled nanocraft could reach Alpha Centauri before a more advanced mission launched decades later.

## Key points
- Where does our universe end and another begin? They explore the bubble universe model, where separate universes expand without overlapping, each potentially operating under different laws of physics, and how we might test for another universe impinging on ours.
- Could Project Starshot's laser-propelled nanocraft reach Alpha Centauri before a more advanced mission launched decades later? Neil works through the math and explains why lightsails could be our most efficient way of accessing other star systems.
- Is there such a thing as a shared “now”? Simultaneity, relativity and the experience of time, including why a road trip seems to pass differently than it does.
- How the rubber-sheet visualization of gravity actually works in four dimensions.
- Hoag's Object and how calculating chance alignments between galaxies helped us discover binary star systems.
- Are fractals the key to understanding everything in the universe, or are they more entertaining than useful? (Romanesco broccoli puts in an appearance.)
- How an expanding universe affects time — did time pass differently in the past? — plus what the sky looked like when the cosmic microwave background was visible light.
- Neil's favourite and least favourite astronomical words, and why “planetary nebula” is a misnomer.
