## Overview

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil are joined by OpenAI President and co-founder Greg Brockman to discuss rapid progress across frontier models, cybersecurity and scientific discovery. Brockman says nearly 400 century-old math problems have been solved using OpenAI's AI agents, and argues the same capability can transform biology through drug discovery. He talks about proactive always-on agents like Dots and modern productivity, why he believes the best UI is no UI at all, and why cybersecurity is in a critical window where peak intelligence must be used to find and fix vulnerabilities before adversaries do. He closes on why OpenAI remains committed to keeping humans in the driver's seat even as models surpass us.

## Key Points

- Brockman claims nearly 400 century-old mathematics problems have been solved using OpenAI's AI agents.
- The same capability is framed as transferable to biology — specifically overcoming data bottlenecks and accelerating drug discovery.
- Cybersecurity is presented as a time-limited window: use peak intelligence now to find and fix vulnerabilities before attackers do.
- Proactive always-on agents (Dots) are positioned as the shift from request-response tools to persistent background workers.
- Brockman's product thesis: the best UI is no UI at all.
- The path runs through unifying work and consumer agents rather than keeping them separate.
- Regulatory change is named as a prerequisite for accelerating progress in biology.
- He addresses AI communication and oversight, plus why OpenAI stays committed to human control even as capabilities surpass human performance.
- Discussion of how UI will evolve as agents become the primary interface.

## Implications

The cybersecurity framing is the operationally urgent part: if agents can find vulnerabilities at superhuman scale, the asymmetry briefly favours defenders, and that window closes once the same tools are widely available. For biotech and pharma, the claim that AI overcomes biology's data bottleneck rather than its reasoning bottleneck points at where investment in data generation — not modelling — is the constraint.
