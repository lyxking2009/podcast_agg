## Overview

Kain Warwick, Taylor Monahan and Austin Griffith work through the Ledger supply-chain attack. Genuine Ledger devices sold through the reseller CryptoBilis shipped with an LTE chip and eSIM wired inside, intercepting seed phrases on their way to the screen. Losses are estimated at $70 million to $100 million over roughly four months. The panel traces how the implant passed Ledger's genuine check, why panicked users are now losing additional money to phishing and fake apps, and whether an iPhone or a DIY build makes a better hardware wallet. They also cover a recording device planted at OpenAI.

## Key Points

- Genuine Ledger devices sold through the reseller CryptoBilis shipped with a hidden LTE modem and eSIM wired inside.
- The implant intercepted seed phrases on their way to the screen — it read the recovery phrase before the user saw it.
- Estimated losses of $70 million to $100 million over roughly four months.
- The devices passed Ledger's genuine check, which is the detail that undermines the standard "buy from an authorised seller and verify authenticity" advice.
- Taylor Monahan lays out why panicking now costs users more money — the post-breach phishing and fake-app wave is the second loss event.
- Panelled discussion of whether an iPhone or a DIY build makes a better hardware wallet, given that vendor supply chains are now the attack surface.
- The discussion also covers a recording device planted in OpenAI.
- Unchained is running a listener survey through Sunday 18 October.

## Implications

The attack defeats device-authenticity verification, which means the trust model for hardware wallets has to move from "verify the device" to "verify the supply chain that delivered it" — a much harder problem that favours vendors who control their own distribution. The second-order lesson is that the immediate post-incident window is itself a risk period, since attackers monetise panic with fake recovery apps targeting users who still hold funds they have not moved.
