# Jeff Yan's Plan to Bring the Entire Financial Industry Onto Hyperliquid

**Show:** Odd Lots  
**Published:** 2026-10-15  
**Transcript source:** `rss_omny_srt`

## Overview

Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway talk to Jeff Yan of Hyperliquid about tokenising and extending every kind of financial exposure onto a single platform. Weisenthal frames the moment as one where instruments keep getting re-expressed as other instruments — stocks that are tokenised, crypto tokens that get ETF-ised, prediction markets that functionally predict a stock and then become one. Alloway offers a "inverse pyramid" media theory: traditional media had original reporting at the base with commentary stacked above, and finance has now inverted the same way, with the underlying asset the smallest part of the market and derivatives, futures, options and prediction markets layered on top. Yan argues the end goal is broadening access, and points at this year's IPOs as evidence that wealth creation is gatekept by the time a company lists.

## Key Points

- The framing question: everything can now be expressed as something else — tokenised stocks, ETF-ised tokens, prediction markets that trade like equities.
- Alloway's "inverse pyramid": in traditional media original reporting was the base; in finance today the underlying asset is the smallest layer and everything built on top is larger.
- Weisenthal calls this "financialization on steroids."
- Yan's core argument for permissionless markets is access: taking markets traditionally confined in various ways and extending them temporally (24/7) and geographically (equal access for retail worldwide).
- On IPOs: Yan argues that by the time trillion-dollar companies list, most wealth creation has already happened and has been gatekept — which he calls a limitation of the existing system rather than deliberate exclusion.
- The counterargument raised in the episode: buying a token that tracks Anthropic shares before IPO is not the same as owning a claim on future free cash flow or dividends, so the exposure is materially different from equity.
- Discussion of prediction markets as functional substitutes for stock positions.
- The episode was recorded 13 October, ahead of the hosts' trip to Chicago to interview trading-industry figures.

## Implications

The substantive debate is about what a tracking token actually gives you: Yan's access argument is strong on retail participation but weaker on the cash-flow rights that make equity valuable, and the hosts press exactly that gap. For anyone trading these venues, the practical issue is basis and counterparty risk — you hold exposure to a price feed, not to the issuer — which is a very different risk profile from the equity it tracks.
