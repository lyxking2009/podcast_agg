## Key Points

- Ledgers sold through CryptoBilis, an authorised reseller in Malaysia, carried a hardware implant with an eSIM that read the seed phrase off the screen and transmitted it over LTE. About $80 million was stolen.
- CryptoBilis' founders say they sold the company to a Chinese buyer months earlier and were under a confidentiality agreement until 20 October; Trezor has already cut off its authorised resellers.
- Robert's framing: someone has now prototyped buying a vendor in order to run a supply chain attack — Tarun explains it as North Korea buying the liquor distributor.
- Haseeb's practical guidance: buy hardware wallets directly from the manufacturer; Tom is sceptical of see-through cases and points to Ledger's honeypot problem.
- An OpenAI engineer was caught with a listening device.
- Papertrade launched 1000x perpetuals in which losing money mints you tokens — a structure the hosts treat as a red flag.
- The hosts argue over Nikita Bier's claim that technical moats are dead.
- Tarun says nothing in crypto feels like frontier tech anymore outside Zcash and quantum, and recalls a robotic shrimp farm that pivoted into an L2.

## Implications

The supply-chain point generalises well beyond Ledger: if attackers will buy an authorised reseller to insert hardware, then procurement through third-party channels is a systemic weakness for the entire hardware-wallet category, and direct-from-manufacturer purchase becomes a security requirement rather than a preference. The Papertrade discussion is the market-structure signal — a perp venue that pays you for losing changes the incentive of the trader rather than the venue's risk, which is the shape of past blow-ups.
