# E247｜对话盛贤：xAI，Infra的浪漫，SGLang，开源，平权与"缄默传" — 硅谷101 (Silicon Valley 101, host: Chen Qian)

## TL;DR
Silicon Valley 101 host Chen Qian interviews Sheng Ying, former Head of Reasoning at xAI and initiator of the open-source LLM inference engine SGLang, now co-founder/CEO of RadixArk, which closed a $100M seed round (from NVIDIA, AMD, Intel and others) at a $400M post-money valuation. Central themes: infrastructure as 'the product' rather than a support function, an ethos of 'closed source but not centralized,' and reflections on gender bias in attributing her achievements.

## Key points
- Sheng Ying grew up in resource-scarce Jiangxi province and taught herself programming entirely from code shared by strangers online, shaping her belief in open source as foundational infrastructure.
- Her academic path ran from Shanghai Jiao Tong University's ACM Honors Class to a Stanford PhD in formal verification under Clark Barrett, before pivoting toward AI/ML systems research.
- She became a core member of LMSYS and initiated SGLang, an inference engine whose key contribution, RadixAttention, indexes shared prefix structures so KV cache can be reused across requests.
- She joined xAI in October 2024 to lead the reasoning team working on Grok's inference efficiency, describing the culture as having zero organizational politics.
- She left xAI after about 10 months, deliberately before her equity cliff, because SGLang's volunteer community was burning out and needed her attention.
- RadixArk was founded in mid-2025, raised a $100M seed round from NVIDIA, AMD, and Intel among others, reached a $400M post-money valuation, and grew to 40+ people.
- Her core philosophy: 'infrastructure itself is the product,' not a supporting function, and infra work should have aesthetic elegance.
- She discusses experiencing gender-based attribution bias firsthand, with her accomplishments often minimized as 'hard work' rather than credited to underlying skill.
