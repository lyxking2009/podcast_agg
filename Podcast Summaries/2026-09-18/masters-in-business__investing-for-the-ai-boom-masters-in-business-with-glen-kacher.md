# Investing for the AI Boom: Masters in Business with Glen Kacher — Masters in Business

## TL;DR

Barry Ritholtz talks with Glenn Kacher, founder and CIO of Light Street Capital — one of the few hedge funds based in the middle of Silicon Valley — about a career spent on technology investing from Tiger through Integral to the AI era, and why AI adoption will take longer than the hype implies.

## Key points

- Kacher traces his path: a Peter Lynch book in college, a UVA commerce degree, then a job at Julian Robertson's Tiger Management from 1993-96, where he covered technology names including Dell, Microsoft, Compaq and Cisco.
- He spent 13 years at Roger McNamee's Integral Capital Partners, leading or co-leading venture investments in companies such as Agile, ArcSight, Blue Nile, Epiphany, Fortify, LogMeIn, OpenTable, Overture and GoTo.com.
- Light Street is a rare hedge fund located in Silicon Valley, which Kacher treats as an informational edge for technology and AI investing.
- His technology-adoption analogue is the smartphone: RIM's BlackBerry was the first real working smartphone and looked clunky by later standards, but the market only fully developed years after the first version shipped.
- He argues AI 'can do some incredible things, but it's going to do way more in a few years' — the obstacles are as much organisational as technical.
- The specific gating factors he names for enterprise AI are data security and the comfort of co-workers and superiors in granting AI agents access to data.
- He flags the ongoing battle between open-source models and closed frontier models as a key variable for where value accrues.
- His conclusion is that the industry is 'just scratching the surface' of what AI delivers, despite how fast the last few years have moved.
