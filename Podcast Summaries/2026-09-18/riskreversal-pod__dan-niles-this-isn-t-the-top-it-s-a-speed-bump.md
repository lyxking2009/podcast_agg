# Dan Niles: This Isn't the Top, It's a Speed Bump — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR

Dan Nathan hosts Dan Niles of Niles Investment Management on a rallying market, a 5% 10-year yield, rising global rates and negative September seasonality into the midterms — with Niles arguing the AI trade is not over but faces a real 'speed bump' and that the money should follow where AI dollars are actually spent.

## Key points

- Niles' central call is that this is not the top of the AI trade but that a speed bump is coming — he explicitly anchors on the late-1995 and late-1997 episodes where the index could still fall substantially before bottoming.
- His positioning conclusion is that capital should go toward semiconductors and memory, where AI dollars are actually being spent, rather than the hyperscalers doing the spending — which he notes are down year to date.
- He argues every great transformative technology has been over-invested and produced a bust on the other side, and memory will be at the epicentre: 'Micron, this isn't a pullback.'
- The demand-side risks he lists are AI budgets being exhausted quickly, a shift in strategy toward token minimisation, and the capex burden hyperscalers must carry.
- He points to comments from Altman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Musk about slowing AI as a sentiment shift, alongside falling model pricing and profitability concerns.
- He flags early signs of spend rotating toward cheaper open-source models, plus policy risks including Texas's data-centre moratorium and potential political pushback on the buildout.
- Macro risks: hiking cycles, deficits, a weak equity risk premium pressuring valuations, and the unwinding of carry trades against the backdrop of a 5% 10-year yield.
- He compares the buildout to the dot-com era, warning that backlogs and circular financing can reverse quickly — e.g. the CXMT IPO in China raising the question of whether expectations ran ahead.
- On the eventual winners he favours Anthropic in enterprise, expects Google to reassert leadership on data and cloud acceleration, sees Meta as a dark-horse beneficiary through consumer distribution, and credits Microsoft's OpenAI stake and security-focused Copilot.
