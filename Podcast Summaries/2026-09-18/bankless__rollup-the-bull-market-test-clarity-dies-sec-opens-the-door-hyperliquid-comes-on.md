# ROLLUP: The Bull Market Test | Clarity Dies | SEC Opens the Door | Hyperliquid Comes Onshore — Bankless

## TL;DR

Ryan Sean Adams and David Hoffman argue the week was the strongest confirmation yet of an early bull market: CLARITY died and the Fed hiked, yet crypto prices went up — and 21 hours after the bill failed, the SEC shipped an innovation exemption creating a legal lane for tokenized stocks.

## Key points

- The episode's thesis is that good news now moves prices up and bad news no longer matters. Both bearish catalysts — CLARITY failing to pass and a Fed rate hike — landed the same week and crypto prices were flat-to-higher.
- CLARITY died, but 21 hours later the SEC shipped an 'innovation exemption' — described as a legal lane for tokenized stocks, effective immediately. Hoffman's framing: 'crypto lost but we're winning.'
- Ryan flags that the Fed hiked for the first time since 2022, under Fed Chair Warsh, and asks how angry Trump was about it — while noting both pieces of bad news were arguably priced in.
- Eric Conner's read is that flushing both the no-CLARITY result and the rate hike in the same day was actually helpful, and that neither QQQ nor the S&P nosedived on the news.
- TDR's Michael Nadeau is watching a Bitcoin band of roughly 69.9K (200-day moving average) to 80.4K (50-week) for confirmation; holding the band without breaking down would suggest the low is in from June 30, while a decisive break above the 50-week sustained over weeks would confirm an early bull market.
- Hoffman's counter-risk: Bitcoin bounced off the 200-week, which it does repeatedly in bear markets. If it does so a second time, he sees a move into the high 60s (65-66K) that crypto Twitter is not positioned for.
- Zcash hit all-time highs above $1,500, up from $1,200 the previous week. Hoffman offers two explanations: pure momentum as privacy insurance on Bitcoin (a trade Naval has backed), or crypto expressing 'a Fed hike is bullish' through a faster horse than Bitcoin.
- The hosts note the abnormal sequencing — Bitcoin is supposed to lead and alts to follow — with Zcash and Hyperliquid outperforming, Hyperliquid at all-time highs and Near at $3 (from $2 two weeks earlier).
- They highlight a recovery in the 'middle of the market' — the startup layer that had dried out — with Near crossing $30bn in confidential intents and $70m in Near TVL.
- Tom Lee's clip: Q4 could be one of the biggest rallies, continuing next year into 'one of the biggest rallies of our lifetime,' with the S&P 'easily above 8,200 by the end of the year' as the MAG-7 and software names revive — contingent on the AI trade surviving.
- Macro counterweight: the US 10-year Treasury yield broke 5% (a level not seen in years) and the 30-year touched 5.4%, with Bessent's $6bn of long-end buying failing to quell yields — the 'Bessent is the house' debate.
- Kraken is bringing Hyperliquid to America through a compliant front end — essentially Hyperliquid with a CFTC licence in front of it — framed as the path for perps to come onshore.
- On Circle's Arc and tokenized markets, the hosts argue USDC is a back-end asset without front-end distribution, making Arc look like a fast-follower attempt at the Robinhood play.
- Closing item: S&P Global acquiring OpenZeppelin, one of crypto's best smart-contract audit teams — the hosts' reaction is that this is 'very much like crypto in 2026': we kind of made it, but in a way that kind of hurts.
