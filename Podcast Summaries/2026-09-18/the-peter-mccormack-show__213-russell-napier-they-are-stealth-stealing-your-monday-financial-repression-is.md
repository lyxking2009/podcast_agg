# #213 - Russell Napier - "They Are Stealth-Stealing Your Monday": Financial Repression is Here — The Peter McCormack Show

## TL;DR

Russell Napier — financial historian, author of The Solid Ground and co-founder of the Library of Mistakes — lays out how Western governments use quiet regulatory mandates to force pension funds into sovereign bonds, stealth-taxing savers to inflate away unpayable debts, and why the UK's private sector is coiled for a boom.

## Key points

- Napier's central mechanism is financial repression: rather than explicit taxation, governments use regulatory mandates to compel pension funds into negative-yielding sovereign bonds, transferring wealth from savers to the state.
- He quantifies the damage — risk-free assets have lost up to 78% of their real capital value, which is why savers do not feel better off even as nominal assets rise.
- The UK setup is his focus: the public sector is broke while the private sector is not, and he argues the private sector balance sheet is 'coiled like a spring' for an unprecedented boom.
- He argues the fix has never been easier, and that ideology — not capacity — is what holds Britain back; the economy needs roughly 4% growth.
- His explanation of how pension funds were made to buy the bonds is the episode's core institutional detail, alongside discussion of the state pension triple lock.
- On what a saver should actually do, he points to gold and bitcoin as the assets outside the repression mechanism.
- He warns the same dynamic is spreading: Europe wants your savings, and France's position is worse than America's; savers are being reframed politically as 'the enemy'.
- On AI he is bullish on the possibility of a productivity boom, but argues AI will ultimately trigger a debt crisis rather than solve one.
- He closes on concentration, observing that three firms now own everything.
