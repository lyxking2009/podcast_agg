# Are You in a Cognitive Deficit? — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
This episode examines two emerging risks: the NBA's unprecedented punishment of Steve Ballmer and the Clippers for salary-cap evasion, and the rise of AI-detection tools like Pangram Labs. Randall Williams breaks down the Clippers' penalties (five lost first-round picks, suspensions, $30 million fine) and argues money is changing but not ruining sports, while Wahini Vara explains how Pangram's detection model identifies AI-generated text and could become a spam filter for AI slop. The hosts also discuss the Fed's unanimous rate hike and the box-office success of 'Coyote vs. Acme'.

## Key points
- NBA sanctioned the Los Angeles Clippers and owner Steve Ballmer for circumventing the salary cap: the team loses five first-round picks, Ballmer is suspended one year, the team president is suspended one year, the general manager is suspended six months, and the team is fined $30 million.
- The violations involved side deals for star Kawhi Leonard, including payments to third parties and no-show endorsement deals that lacked legitimate commercial purpose, effectively paying Leonard outside the salary cap.
- Randall Williams notes that ESPN's earlier reporting suggested the Clippers might avoid punishment, but a rare on-the-record press release from NBA spokesperson Mike Bass signaled the league would not sweep the findings under the rug.
- Williams argues that salary caps work in the NFL and NBA, while MLB's lack of a cap is less a problem than owners who refuse to spend; he says private equity and new money in college sports are shifting power away from traditional family owners but haven't ruined fan experience.
- Sports bars are experiencing a boom as fans seek communal viewing to avoid high ticket prices, concession costs, and fragmented streaming subscriptions; bars benefit from selling reservations, minimum tabs, and premium sports packages.
- Pangram Labs' product Pangram is an AI detector built on a detection model, not a large language model; it was trained on human-written documents and AI paraphrases to learn stylistic differences and can output percentages of human vs AI vs AI-assisted content.
- Pangram gained public attention in the spring amid scandals involving novelists, journalists, and the Pope, where users ran suspected text through the tool; it can even identify which specific chatbot generated a piece.
- Evasion tactics like translating AI text into another language or generating random words fail because Pangram detects the consistency patterns of AI outputs rather than specific markers like the name 'Elara' or em dashes.
- CEO Max Spiro envisions Pangram as a browser or search-engine filter that flags or blocks AI slop, a potential mass-market solution as AI-generated content floods the internet.
- Wahini Vara argues AI-generated content threatens paid media: if words cost nothing to produce, subscriptions and book prices lose justification, and AI's narrow style and biased ideas could homogenize online discourse, even influencing baby names like Elara.
- The Federal Reserve raised interest rates with a unanimous 12-0 vote, including Governor Kevin Warsh, despite pressure from President Trump to lower rates; the unanimity was seen as a signal of central bank independence.
- Warner Bros. initially shelved the completed film 'Coyote vs. Acme' for a tax write-off, but Ketchup Entertainment later released it, and it became a top-five box office hit, mirroring its underdog-versus-corporation plot.
