# Crypto's Clarity Act Collapses. Two Days Later, the SEC Introduces Its Innovation Exemption — Unchained

## TL;DR

Laura Shin hosts Cody Carbone, CEO of the Digital Chamber, on why the CLARITY Act failed its Senate cloture vote — and what happens next now that the industry's plan A is dead and the SEC and CFTC are the only game in town.

## Key points

- CLARITY failed the cloture vote that would have allowed it to move to the floor for debate, prompting finger-pointing between Democrats and Republicans and across crypto Twitter.
- Carbone's verdict is blunt: 'politics beat policy.' Every Democrat voted no on the motion to invoke cloture, aligned as a bloc and unwilling to hand Republicans a win.
- The bill ran 630 pages, of which only four pages were ethics — but Carbone argues ethics carried the day, with substantive disputes around the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act and stablecoin yield also in play.
- The critical late change: in the Sunday-released text, the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act lost its criminal liability shield, removing all reference to the 1960 criminal statute.
- The consequence is that non-custodial developers now have civil liability protections but can still be held criminally liable if someone uses their technology for illicit activity — precisely what the industry was trying to prevent.
- Carbone expresses genuine surprise at how far the White House was willing to go on ethics, including state AGs gaining enforcement power and the president putting assets in a blind trust — which he describes as unprecedented.
- He estimates Democrats and Republicans agreed on roughly 80% of the Democrats' ethics demands, making their choice of the status quo over that compromise hard for him to square with their stated goals.
- The forward path is now plans B, C and D: with custody and market-structure questions pushed into SEC and CFTC rulemaking, Carbone expects agencies led by Paul Atkins and Mike Selig to push enabling rules as far as they can.
- His longer-run prediction is that Congress eventually codifies what the agencies have already done — much less partisan and much less newsworthy than drafting new concepts like the ancillary asset test, network token test and 'decentralization in name only' test from scratch.
- The episode was recorded as a live stream, with Laura Shin noting the disappointment across the industry after years spent trying to reach this point.
