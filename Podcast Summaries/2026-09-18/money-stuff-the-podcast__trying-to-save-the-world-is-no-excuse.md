# Trying to Save the World Is No Excuse — Money Stuff: The Podcast

## TL;DR

Matt Levine is joined by returning guest co-host Sylvia Killingsworth (editor of the OnBooks newsletter) for a books-and-law episode, leading with the Susquehanna options case over allegedly informed put buying in Chinese brokerage stocks and closing on the repackaging of private assets into retirement accounts.

## Key points

- Guest co-host Sylvia Killingsworth is sitting in while Matt Levine's usual co-host Katie Greifeld is on parental leave; the show will use fill-in co-hosts for several months.
- The opening books segment covers Levine reading Faulkner's 'Absalom, Absalom' after Dwight Garner's review in The Disappearers, and Colson Whitehead's 'Sag Harbor' as his phone book — a pick driven by a write-up in the East Hampton Star.
- Main legal story: China announced a crackdown on cross-border trading from its brokerages in May 2026, and several Chinese brokerage stocks fell sharply as firms were restricted from their most profitable trades.
- Just before that news broke, traders bought large volumes of short-dated puts on those brokerage stocks — and Susquehanna International Group, a major options market maker, had sold a lot of those puts.
- Susquehanna went to US court in June seeking to identify the buyers, suing roughly 100 'John Doe' defendants and asking for an injunction to freeze the accounts, arguing the buyers must have been regulators or brokerage employees with a duty of confidentiality.
- This month Susquehanna lost the preliminary injunction: the judge allowed the case to proceed but was not persuaded that all 100 accounts were insider trading, and declined to freeze them.
- Levine walks through why insider-trading law is awkward here — liability generally requires a fiduciary duty and a breach of it, which is why the plaintiffs had to characterise the traders as duty-bound insiders.
- The closing segment covers BlackRock and the shift of retirement money toward private assets: the structure buys assets and holds them until retirement, in effect focusing savers away from the daily dollar balance in their 401(k) that moves with AI-driven market swings.
