## Key Points

- OpenAI confidentially filed its S-1 IPO prospectus around May 22, 2026, targeting an $852B–$1T valuation on ~$25B annualized revenue — while losing $1.22 for every $1 earned.
- Kantrowitz argues OpenAI is racing to IPO before Anthropic, which is in talks to raise at a $900B valuation and reportedly surpassed $30B ARR.
- SpaceX simultaneously filed for IPO at a $1.75T valuation, aiming to raise $75B+ via Goldman Sachs on Nasdaq in June 2026.
- The "datacenters in space" narrative (pushed by Musk: 1 TW of compute launchable annually via Starship, Alphabet in talks with SpaceX) is framed as hype disconnected from near-term reality.
- AI has a severe public relations problem: Eric Schmidt was loudly booed at University of Arizona commencement; 7 in 10 Americans oppose AI datacenters near them (Gallup).
- Meta cut ~8,000 jobs (10% of workforce) + cancelled 6,000 open roles while doubling capex to $125–145B for AI infrastructure in 2026.
- Meta's "Model Capability Initiative" (MCI) software records keystrokes, mouse movements, and screenshots from employee computers to train AI to replicate how employees work; 1,000+ employees signed a petition against it before the layoffs.
- Marc Andreessen claimed on Joe Rogan that AI won't file HR complaints, framing it as a productivity argument — widely read as tone-deaf given mass layoffs hitting new graduates.

## Overview

Alex Kantrowitz and regular guest Ranjan Roy (Margins) work through the week's biggest tech news. The throughline is a tension between AI's trillion-dollar financial ambitions and its growing public backlash. OpenAI's IPO filing crystallizes this: the company is burning cash at scale, racing against Anthropic's fundraising momentum, and must tell a compelling growth story before its lead erodes. SpaceX's simultaneous IPO push adds to a sense that the biggest bets in tech are all trying to lock in their valuations now.

On infrastructure, the orbital datacenter idea gets skeptical treatment — Musk's claims about Starship-launched compute sound visionary but the economics and timelines don't hold up under scrutiny. Meanwhile the human cost of the AI buildout is landing in real time: Meta's mass layoff (cutting human workers while massively expanding AI capex) and its keystroke-tracking software represent the starkest version of the "AI replacing workers" story, and the commencement-speech booing shows that the message has reached the public.

## Implications

- The OpenAI vs. Anthropic IPO race is about narrative control as much as capital — the company that goes public first sets the benchmark valuation for the whole AI frontier lab category.
- AI's PR crisis is now quantifiable (Gallup, commencement reactions) and risks creating regulatory and political headwinds precisely when companies need public goodwill for datacenter siting and permitting.
- Meta's MCI program signals that "AI learning from workers before replacing them" is no longer a hypothetical — it is company policy at the largest social platform on Earth.
- Orbital compute remains a long-horizon moonshot; near-term AI infrastructure will continue to depend on terrestrial datacenter permitting, power, and water — all increasingly contested.
