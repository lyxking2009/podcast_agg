# Bonus: The Future of Ride-Hailing with Lyft CEO David Risher

**Show:** Masters in Business
**Date:** 2026-07-08
**Transcript source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points
- David Risher has been Lyft CEO for 3 years and board member for 5 years, bringing a deep tech background from Microsoft and Amazon
- Risher studied comparative literature at Princeton and Harvard Business School — he credits humanities training with developing curiosity, understanding, and empathy as leadership tools
- His mother bought an Apple II computer for her small business, which got him into technology as a kid; he wrote his thesis on a computer when that was unusual
- Lyft's business extends far beyond ride-hailing: bike share, autonomous vehicles, and fleet management are all part of the platform
- Risher's career path — consulting, Microsoft internship (1990), Amazon, then Lyft — illustrates how non-obvious career moves compound into CEO readiness

## Overview
Barry Ritholtz interviews Lyft CEO David Risher about his unconventional path from comparative literature to tech leadership. They discuss how a humanities background shaped his approach to business, the breadth of Lyft's transportation platform beyond ride-hailing, and the company's strategic direction. The conversation touches on Risher's early tech exposure through his mother's Apple II computer and his formative years at Microsoft and Amazon.

## Implications
Lyft is positioning itself as a comprehensive mobility platform, not just a ride-hailing app. The autonomous vehicle and fleet management bets signal a long-term play beyond human drivers. Risher's humanities-first leadership style may give Lyft an edge in understanding consumer behavior and building culture.
