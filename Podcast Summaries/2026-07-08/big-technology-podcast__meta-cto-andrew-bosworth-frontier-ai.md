# Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth: Our Path To Frontier AI, Renting Models, Consumer AI's Struggles

**Show:** Big Technology Podcast
**Date:** 2026-07-08
**Transcript source:** show_notes

## Key Points
- Andrew Bosworth (Boz) is Meta's CTO, leading efforts across AR, VR, AI, and consumer hardware (Quest, Ray-Ban Meta glasses)
- Meta fell behind in the frontier AI race but is now pursuing a path to catch up, with Meta Superintelligence Labs formed in early 2026
- The 'renting models' strategy: Meta is exploring licensing/partnering with frontier model providers rather than building everything in-house
- Consumer AI products have struggled to gain traction compared to enterprise AI — a challenge Meta is actively working to address
- Bosworth has been at Meta since 2006 (~10th engineer), building News Feed, Messenger, Groups, and running Ads ($4B to $40B revenue)
- Meta's AI code writing went from minimal to 99% AI-generated within a year, according to Bosworth at the Washington AI Network event

## Overview
Alex Kantrowitz interviews Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth about Meta's strategy for catching up in the frontier AI race, the decision to potentially rent rather than build all models in-house, and why consumer AI products have struggled. Bosworth brings 18+ years of Meta experience to the conversation, having built core products and scaled the advertising business 10x. The discussion covers Meta's unique position as both a consumer tech company and an AI infrastructure player.

## Implications
Meta's 'renting models' approach could reshape the AI competitive landscape — if Meta successfully partners with frontier labs while distributing AI through its 3B+ user base, it may not need to win the model race to win the AI race. The consumer AI struggle is industry-wide but particularly acute for Meta given its consumer-first identity. Bosworth's claim of 99% AI-written code at Meta signals how fast internal AI adoption is moving.
