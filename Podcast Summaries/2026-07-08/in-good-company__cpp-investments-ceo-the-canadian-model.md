# CPP Investments CEO: The Canadian Model, Public vs Private and Investing for 22 Million Canadians

**Show:** In Good Company with Nicolai Tangen
**Date:** 2026-07-08
**Transcript source:** show_notes

## Key Points
- John Graham runs CPP Investments, the largest Canadian pension fund managing $800 billion for 22 million Canadians
- The 'Canadian model' of institutional investing is a global blueprint, but Graham says imitators often get it wrong
- Graham calls diversification 'an act of humility' — a philosophical framing of portfolio construction
- The conversation covers how CPP weighs private markets, US exposure, and China allocations
- Graham is a scientist by training before becoming an investor — he reflects on leading through COVID
- AI's role in investment decisions remains uncertain according to Graham
- Graham describes investing as 'quantitative art' rather than pure science

## Overview
Nicolai Tangen interviews John Graham, CEO of CPP Investments, about managing $800 billion for 22 million Canadians. They explore the celebrated 'Canadian model' of institutional investing, why diversification is 'an act of humility,' and how CPP navigates private markets, US vs. international allocations, and the China question. Graham reflects on his transition from scientist to investor, leading through COVID, and his belief that investing is 'quantitative art.'

## Implications
The Canadian model's emphasis on long-term, diversified, internally-managed investing continues to outperform short-term, fee-heavy approaches. CPP's $800B scale gives it advantages in private markets that smaller funds can't replicate. Graham's framing of diversification as humility is a useful mental model for all investors.
