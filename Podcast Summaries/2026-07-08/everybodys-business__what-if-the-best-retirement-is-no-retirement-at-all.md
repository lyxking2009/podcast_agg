# What if the Best Retirement is No Retirement at All?

**Show:** Everybody's Business
**Date:** 2026-07-08
**Transcript source:** rss_omny_srt

## Key Points
- A growing segment of the global population is choosing to delay retirement or re-enter the workforce by choice, not necessity
- David Lloyd, a former commercial radio executive, founded Boom Radio during the pandemic — a station staffed by and for baby boomers who weren't ready to stop working
- In the US, entering your sixties traditionally marks senior citizenship with Medicare eligibility, but many are redefining what this life stage means
- The trend poses challenges for labor markets as older workers compete with younger talent, but also brings decades of experience back into the workforce
- Semi-retirement — doing bits of consulting and writing — is increasingly common as a transitional phase rather than a hard stop

## Overview
Stacey Vanoxsmith explores the rising trend of people delaying retirement or returning to work by choice. Through the story of David Lloyd and Boom Radio, the episode illustrates how baby boomers are reclaiming purpose and productivity. The piece examines the US labor market implications of this demographic shift and the personal fulfillment that comes from continued engagement.

## Implications
Financial advisors and employers need to account for longer career arcs. The traditional retirement-at-65 model is being replaced by more fluid transitions. Companies that accommodate older workers — with flexible arrangements and recognition of experience — will have a competitive advantage in tight labor markets.
