## Key Points

- Host David Hoffman interviews David C. Roy ("Bitcoin Dave") of Alpen Labs (transcript speaker label reads "David Seroy") about whether Bitcoin remains on a "hyper-Bitcoinization" path and how to unlock its next step-function increase in value.
- Roy argues Bitcoin's core monetary thesis is settled, but the open question is whether adding trustless computation/programmability to Bitcoin layer-twos can unlock further value beyond being a static non-sovereign store of value.
- He traces Bitcoin's technical roadmap from BitVM1 (verification could take months) to BitVM2 (verification down to ~2 weeks, but high on-chain costs) to BitVM3/garbled circuits (faster, moves most cost off-chain) — each iteration trading off verification time, on-chain cost, and trust assumptions, with a one-honest-operator security model rather than full trustlessness.
- Looking further ahead, Roy mentions "Pipes V2," a witness-encryption cryptographic scheme (from Misha's team) that could eventually bring covenants and native ZK verification to Bitcoin without a soft fork.
- Alpen Labs' architecture centers on an EVM execution layer plus a middleware/settlement layer called "Strata" (inspired by Celestia's ZK Accounts), designed to bridge Bitcoin to multiple execution environments (EVM, Solana, custom VMs), targeting mainnet in fall 2026.
- The core economic thesis: rebuild the ~$12T repo/shadow-banking credit market on-chain using Bitcoin as "pristine collateral," analogous to how mortgage-backed securities (pre-2008) and now US Treasuries serve as repo collateral in TradFi — with Bitcoin's low counterparty risk becoming the on-chain equivalent.
- Morpho Midnight (launched the same day as this episode) is highlighted as the key enabling primitive — it allows fixed-duration (not just variable-rate) Bitcoin-collateralized lending vaults, creating an instrument Roy compares to a "Bitcoin collateralized loan obligation (CLO)": a yield-bearing, dollar-denominated, Bitcoin-backed instrument that can be leveraged/looped (a carry trade) to compress borrowing rates and boost lender yields (potentially 6-9% vs. ~4% on Aave).
- Roy draws a direct comparison to Michael Saylor's Strategy "perpetual preferred" (STRC) instrument, arguing on-chain Bitcoin-CLOs are more resilient than STRC, which he says broke its peg (trading around $0.85) when investors tried to loop/unwind leveraged positions in it.
- Roy identifies Alpen Labs' real competition not as other Bitcoin L2s (many of which have failed to attract top-tier DeFi teams/liquidity) but as stablecoin-focused chains like Base, Ark, and Tempo, which are also building wrapped/tokenized Bitcoin products.
- He acknowledges Alpen is "behind" in network effects/liquidity versus Ethereum-based venues but argues a superior, potentially insured, form of Bitcoin collateral can still let them out-compete on economics rather than ideology.

## Overview

Bankless host David Hoffman catches up with Alpen Labs' David C. Roy ("Bitcoin Dave") on the state of Bitcoin's technical roadmap and a new economic thesis for the asset. The conversation opens with Bitcoin's monetary narrative and community dynamics, then moves into a detailed technical history of BitVM-family zero-knowledge verification schemes (BitVM1 through BitVM3/garbled circuits) that let Bitcoin support layer-two rollups without a soft fork. The bulk of the episode focuses on Roy's thesis that Bitcoin's next major value unlock is becoming the "pristine collateral" for an on-chain rebuild of the global repo/credit market — enabled by new primitives like Morpho Midnight's fixed-duration lending vaults, which allow Bitcoin-backed loan obligations to be created, leveraged, and looped in ways that compress borrowing costs and boost lender yields. Roy contrasts this on-chain approach with Michael Saylor's centralized perpetual-preferred strategy (STRC), arguing the decentralized version is more resilient. The episode closes with Alpen Labs' go-to-market plan (partnering with distribution-holding platforms à la Morpho/Coinbase), a fall 2026 mainnet target, and Roy's view that Alpen's real competitors are stablecoin chains like Base, Ark, and Tempo rather than other Bitcoin layer-twos.

## Implications

For crypto investors and DeFi participants, this episode lays out a concrete emerging narrative — "Bitcoin as digital credit" rather than just "digital gold" — that could open a new value-accrual pathway for BTC if Bitcoin-backed lending vaults scale the way Roy describes. The comparison to Saylor's STRC (and its reported peg break to ~$0.85) is a relevant data point for anyone evaluating leveraged Bitcoin-proxy instruments generally. The technical rundown of BitVM1→BitVM3 gives a useful reference for tracking maturity of Bitcoin ZK-rollup infrastructure, and the identification of stablecoin chains (Base, Ark, Tempo) as the real competitive battleground — rather than other Bitcoin L2s — reframes where capital and liquidity are likely to concentrate in this niche.
