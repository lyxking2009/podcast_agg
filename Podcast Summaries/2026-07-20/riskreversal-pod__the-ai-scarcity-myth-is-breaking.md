## Overview

In this RiskReversal Pod episode, Dan Nathan and Guy Adami cover a turbulent market week centered on the idea that the "AI scarcity" narrative — the assumption that compute and top-tier chips are the binding constraint on AI progress — is breaking down. Memory-chip stocks (Micron) plunged from record highs, Google suffered a sharp two-day selloff tied to Gemini news, and Chinese AI labs (DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot) showed they can close the capability gap with US frontier models despite chip export restrictions. The hosts also touch on Nvidia's margin dynamics relative to component suppliers, an unexpected Apple rally ahead of earnings, a bearish technical setup in Netflix after a cash-flow-driven selloff, and a segment on energy names benefiting from crude oil strength.

## Implications

For investors, the episode frames a potential inflection point in the AI trade: if Chinese labs can approach frontier capability without leading-edge Nvidia hardware, the compute-scarcity premium embedded in memory and AI-infrastructure valuations may be overstated, which helps explain the Micron and broader DRAM selloff. Google's selloff around Gemini underscores how execution/timing news (not just fundamentals) can rapidly reprice a hyperscaler despite deep AI investment. The Netflix and Apple discussions offer tactical technical/fundamental reference points for names entering earnings season, while the energy segment flags refiners and integrated majors (Exxon, ConocoPhillips) as a separate area of relative strength amid the tech volatility.
