# The playbook for building high talent density teams | Adam Ward, Head of Talent at Cursor — Lenny's Podcast: Product | Career | Growth

## TL;DR
Lenny Rachitsky talks with Adam Ward, Head of Talent at Cursor (previously founder of recruiting firm Growth by Design, and talent leader at Pinterest and Facebook), about building elite high-talent-density teams. Adam dismantles the traditional recruiting funnel — which he calls the 'funnel of doom' — and lays out his three-step playbook for treating every hire like an executive search: scoping (objectively defining what 'great' looks like), mapping (building target lists through trusted networks and triangulation), and relentless pursuit. The conversation also covers the 'tale of two cities' talent market, the rise of the forward deployed engineer, the return of the 'power IC', why recruiters are confidence engines rather than decision engines, and how the best companies keep hiring top of mind.

## Key points
- The 'funnel of doom': reaching out to 100 people and getting 20 replies gives you the 20 who happened to reply — by definition not the top 20% — and weeding out at each stage then hiring the remainder 'regresses you to the mean' over time.
- Instead, treat every hire like an executive search: identify the top ~1% of people in the world for the role and go after them directly, using assessment to validate the thesis rather than filter a pool.
- Three-step playbook: (1) Scoping — stack-rank the skills and experiences you objectively need before talking to any candidate; 'I'll know it when I see it' is a trap. (2) Mapping — ask trusted people targeted questions ('who's most collaborative with designers?' not 'who's the best engineer?'), triangulate the same names, and build company lists that share the characteristics you want. (3) Relentless pursuit — the highest-leverage, most time-intensive work is cultivating networks (e.g. weekly sessions digging into employees' networks).
- The hiring market is 'a tale of two cities' — NBA-level offers to new-grad PhDs alongside blue-chip layoffs — with time compression (the mobile-engineer moment lasted years; the AI moment is measured in weeks/days), and a 'resettlement' of talent and how work gets done.
- Most in-demand new role: the forward deployed engineer (FDE) — a deeply technical person who partners with sales and customers to deploy complex products; waning: narrow, over-specialized roles and new grads without experience (AI tools can do part of that work).
- The 'power IC' is rising: taste, decision-making, problem-solving, and curiosity are coming back to the surface, and AI is pushing work back toward craft.
- Recruiters are 'confidence engines, not decision-making engines' — hiring managers own the accountability for hires, and 'what gets measured gets done'; companies that hand hiring to a recruiting function lose the thread.
- Systems thinking, practically: anchor on 'what problem are we trying to solve?' and break it into small tactical pieces.

## Overview
Adam Ward, who spent 20 years building elite teams (Facebook 1,000→10,000 engineers, Pinterest 200→2,000, Growth by Design clients, now Cursor), argues that most companies hire via a 'funnel of doom' — broad outreach, weeding out, hiring the remainder — which mathematically guarantees average outcomes. His alternative: run every hire like an executive search. The episode walks through the three steps in detail: scoping defines great objectively (stack-ranked skills and experiences, role impact, success criteria) and sets up sourcing, pitch, and assessment; mapping uses trusted networks with precise questions plus company-level signals to triangulate a target list of ~50 people; relentless pursuit turns that list into engagement. Adam applies the framework to today's AI-driven market — the rise of the FDE, the squeezing together of engineering/product/design, the revival of craft — and closes with organizational advice: recruiters should build confidence for hiring managers, hiring must be measured like any other metric, and founders who say talent is their #1 priority must resource it accordingly.

## Implications
- For founders and hiring managers: replace funnel metrics with a scoping-mapping-pursuit playbook; define 'great' objectively before sourcing.
- For recruiters: position as a confidence engine that de-risks hiring-manager decisions rather than owning the goal without the decision rights.
- For ICs: the market rewards taste, judgment, systems thinking, and FDE-style customer-facing technical skills over narrow specialization.
- For teams: institute lightweight systems (e.g. a #hiring-ideas channel, network-mining sessions, measured hiring accountability) to keep talent density top of mind.
