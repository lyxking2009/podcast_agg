# The playbook for building high talent density teams | Adam Ward, Head of Talent at Cursor — Lenny's Podcast: Product | Career | Growth

## TL;DR
Adam Ward, Head of Talent at Cursor, shares his playbook for building high talent density teams by rejecting the traditional 'funnel of doom'—where companies hire from whoever responds—and instead treating every hire like an executive search. He details how to scope what 'great' looks like, map the top 50 candidates in the world, relentlessly pursue them through personalized, long‑term relationship building, and design a candidate experience that closes through genuine care, work samples, and daily team stand‑ups. The conversation also covers trends in the AI hiring market, the rise of forward deployed engineers, and why recruiters should behave like talent engineers.

## Key points
- The 'funnel of doom' is the flawed practice of reaching out to 100 people, getting 20 replies (who are not the top 20%, just those who responded), weeding candidates out at each stage, and hiring the remainder. This leads to average hires over time.
- Instead, treat every hire as an executive search: scope what the top 1% looks like by stack‑ranking required skills and experiences, then market‑map the ~50 people in the world who fit that profile.
- When market‑mapping, ask trusted contacts highly specific questions (e.g., 'Who stands out as the most collaborative product engineer with designers?') rather than generic ones like 'Who's the best product engineer?' This surfaces names through triangulation.
- Dig into current employees’ networks; nothing beats hand‑to‑hand cultivation of signal, meeting every other week for six weeks to review names and follow up on recommendations.
- Relentless pursuit means planting seeds over weeks, months, or even years. When a candidate says 'not good timing,' suggest a coffee, an introduction to a team member, or a talk—continuous touchpoints that move the relationship forward.
- The most critical moment is the hiring‑manager first conversation; put disproportionate energy there because it sets the tone for the entire process and often determines if the candidate continues.
- On‑site experiences should be curated and two‑way: use work samples (projects done side‑by‑side, the highest predictor of success), hand‑pick interviewers based on shared interests, and include meals to humanize the interaction.
- Recruiters are confidence engines, not decision‑makers. The hiring manager (who bears the joy and pain of a hire) must lead the process and hold the team accountable for input, while the recruiter orchestrates.
- Closing begins at first contact, not at the offer. Understand the candidate’s intrinsic motivations, remove objections throughout the process, and run daily 10‑minute stand‑ups with the extended team during the final stages.
- Personalization wins: a small, thoughtful gift (like a Turkish guitar for a classically trained violinist) can create a sense of belonging and leave a lasting impression, often outweighing pure compensation.
- Mitigate compensation as the deciding factor by focusing on the role, team, and impact. AI offers today are eye‑watering, but time is the most fixed asset—speed of execution matters more than out‑bidding.
- Currently trending up: Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) who combine deep technical skills with the ability to partner with customers and sales teams. Waning: overly specialized, narrow individual contributors and new grads without diverse experience.
- Systems thinking is increasingly valued: it means breaking a large problem into small, tactical pieces while staying anchored to the root question 'What problem are we trying to solve?'
- For early‑stage companies, do not collapse 'fill immediate roles' and 'build a scalable recruiting system' into one first recruiting hire; decouple them (e.g., hire for system, use a contingent recruiter for speed).
- Look for recruiters who thrive at 90‑110% capacity (the edge of overwhelm), are curious and business‑focused, and have high motor—they love the grind of early‑stage hiring.
- To build a loyal, world‑class recruiting team, hire excellent humans who are excellent at their craft, have a chip on their shoulder, and put team over self. Many former Heads of Talent join as ICs for the craft and learning.
- After offer acceptance, prevent reneges with a 'second motion': send a laptop, host dinners with other new hires, create community, and maintain regular touchpoints until day one.
