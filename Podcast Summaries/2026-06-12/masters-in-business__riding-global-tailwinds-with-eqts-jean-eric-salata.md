## Key Points

- Jean Eric Salata, chair of EQT Group, grew up in Chile and was drawn to investing from a young age, starting with paper delivery route earnings invested in the stock market
- EQT manages over $316 billion and is the largest alternative manager outside the US, having grown through strategic acquisitions including Baring Private Equity Asia
- Salata's career trajectory went from Wharton to Asia, where he built one of the region's premier private equity platforms before EQT acquired it
- The conversation covers global investing opportunities, the evolution of private equity in Asia, and how EQT has scaled into a truly global alternative asset manager
- Key themes include the importance of understanding local markets, building teams on the ground, and the growing sophistication of Asian institutional investors

## Overview

Barry Ritholtz interviews Jean Eric Salata about his remarkable career from Chile to Wharton to building Asia's premier private equity platform and eventually chairing EQT Group, now the largest non-US alternative manager at $316B+ AUM. The discussion covers Salata's early investing instincts, the evolution of Asian private equity, and EQT's global expansion strategy.

## Implications

The growth of non-US alternative asset managers like EQT signals the maturation of global private markets. Asia-focused PE continues to offer significant opportunities as institutional capital deepens across the region.
