# Why the AI Business Model Is Cracking and How Crypto Could Help Fix It

**Show:** Unchained  
**Host/Author:** Laura Shin  
**Date:** 2026-06-12  
**Duration:** 32:23

## Key Points

- Tom Shaughnessy (Delphi Ventures) identified a 40x subsidy gap between consumer AI subscriptions and enterprise API costs, pushing businesses toward open source inference at 1% of the price
- Citadel Securities published a near-identical thesis shortly after Shaughnessy's viral tweet
- The AI IPO wave faces a structural problem: cheap, capable open source alternatives are drawing enterprise users away before OpenAI/Anthropic go public
- Low floats and passive index demand should lift AI stocks initially, but public market disclosures will force revealing payback periods, margins, and subscriber numbers for the first time
- OpenAI's reported price cuts target Anthropic's growth metrics before the IPO, not user demand
- The China model wildcard and whether AI model restrictions amount to 'big brother fearmongering' were also discussed
- Crypto's tools for capital formation could potentially keep the AGI flywheel from stalling

## Overview

Laura Shin interviews Tom Shaughnessy about the structural cracks in the AI business model. The core thesis: a 40x gap between what consumers pay ($20/month) and what enterprise API access costs is unsustainable, pushing businesses toward open source alternatives at 1% of the price. This creates a race-to-the-bottom dynamic that threatens the AI IPO wave. The conversation explores whether crypto's capital formation mechanisms could offer an alternative path for AI development funding.

## Implications

The AI business model faces a fundamental challenge: open source models are becoming 'good enough' for most enterprise use cases at a fraction of the cost. This threatens the massive valuations underlying the AI IPO pipeline. Crypto-native funding mechanisms could emerge as an alternative to traditional VC/public market paths for AI development.
