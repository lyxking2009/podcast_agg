# Noah Shinn - Building Instinct: The Personal Agent - [Invest Like the Best, EP.493] — Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy

## TL;DR
Noah Shinn, founder of Instinct, discusses building a personal AI agent that interacts via messaging, email, and calls, functioning as a proactive executive assistant without a traditional app. He explains how Instinct automates tasks like travel booking, calendar coordination, subscription cancellation, and shopping, emphasizing simplicity and trust. Shinn also covers the platform's network effects through trusted connections, its free-to-user business model with transaction-based revenue, and the challenges of scaling compute with 10% daily growth.

## Key points
- Instinct is a personal AI agent founded about a year ago; it has no app, operates through phone, computer, and email, and can message or call users with a socially aware, proactive assistant persona.
- The product excels at end-to-end task execution: users can ask it to book travel (flights, hotel, seat preferences, credit card) by simply saying 'I need to be in New York tonight,' and it handles all details and booking.
- A popular use case involves fashion: users scan their wardrobe items, and Instinct generates daily outfit suggestions, plans weekly looks, and can even shop online and order items by scanning and asking to send to home.
- Instinct can connect to banking accounts, scan transactions and subscriptions, identify unused services, and cancel them, saving users money; it presents savings like '$2000 this month.'
- The platform has a trusted network effect: agents can coordinate schedules between connected users, with variable access levels (e.g., share calendar but not email); this requires mutual trust and creates new social dynamics.
- Trust is built gradually: after three weeks, approximately 40% of users share personal credit card information with Instinct, and the company emphasizes user control, data retrieval, and proactive security with separate verification systems to prevent hallucination errors before actions.
- Business model: Instinct is free for users, revenue comes from transaction commissions similar to Apple Pay; the goal is to become a distribution layer for merchants, with potential commission rates comparable to Shopify (2.5-3%) or Amazon (10%+) but likely lower.
- Growth has been 10% daily, invite-only with five invites per user, no marketing spend; invites have sold on eBay for around $300, indicating strong word-of-mouth demand.
- Scaling compute is a major challenge: compute needs double roughly every week, requiring pre-purchasing resources that may become unnecessary if growth slows; the company has raised significant capital to cover infrastructure costs.
- Long-term vision: user interfaces may collapse into a single simple agent; agents can generate dynamic interfaces on demand, eliminating the need for separate apps, and users can set high-level goals (e.g., weight loss, inventory management) that the agent works toward autonomously.
