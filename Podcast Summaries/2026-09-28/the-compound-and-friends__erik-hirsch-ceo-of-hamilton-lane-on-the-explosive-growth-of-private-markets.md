# Erik Hirsch, CEO of Hamilton Lane, on the Explosive Growth of Private Markets — The Compound and Friends

## TL;DR
Erik Hirsch, CEO of Hamilton Lane, discusses the explosive growth and structural evolution of private markets, including the firm's rise from $6B to $146B in AUM plus over $1T in advisory assets. He addresses misconceptions around correlation, dispersion of returns, private credit's boom and emerging risks, and the recent retail investor outflows from semi-liquid funds, arguing that select managers will thrive despite industry growing pains. Hirsch also defends secondary market accounting practices, highlights Hamilton Lane's selective deployment and strong inflows, and contends public markets undervalue alternative asset managers given the shift of value creation to private companies.

## Key points
- Hamilton Lane is a private markets solutions provider, not a traditional fund manager; it supplies capital to private market fund managers and serves institutions and individuals. AUM grew from $6 billion in 2005 to about $146 billion, with additional assets under advisory exceeding $1 trillion. CEO Erik Hirsch joined in the late 1990s and became CEO 2.5 years ago.
- Private markets are not truly uncorrelated with public equities; apparent low correlation arises from quarter-lag reporting. Hirsch states returns are totally correlated when the timing lag is removed.
- Dispersion of returns in private equity, private credit, and venture remains wide despite industry growth; top-to-bottom performance gap is as large as 20 years ago, driven by manager skill and operational decisions post-acquisition rather than purchase price.
- The private market manager universe is vast with thousands of firms globally, most unknown and managing $1-2 billion, while only about 20 firms have viable franchises for individual investors; most depend on institutional capital.
- Closed-end private markets fundraising is down across real assets, credit, and equity; distributions are down and holding periods up, but evergreen and secondary market activity are not captured in that data, making the decline less complete than it appears.
- Private credit expanded because regional banks retreated from lending to private companies; it filled the financing gap, but rapid growth led to many new managers who have never experienced a down credit cycle.
- Current private credit fundamentals remain relatively healthy: default rates around 2%, bankruptcies not rising materially, and much of the portfolio still cash-pay; however, some portfolios show questionable underwriting, and wide dispersion is expected over the next five years.
- Retail investors are pulling money from private credit funds despite stable marks due to fear from headlines and asymmetric advisor incentives; for example, Apollo Debt Solutions BDC capped withdrawals at 5% after 14.7% sought redemptions.
- Secondary market transactions often involve buying LP positions at discounts (average around 13%), but accounting rules require using the GP's marks, which can create apparent day-one markups; these marks are complicated by valuation date mismatches and transaction timing.
- Hamilton Lane's semi-liquid funds generated approximately $640 million of net inflows in the quarter ended June, ended with over $19 billion in AUM, did not enact gates, and saw positive net inflows in 10 of 12 funds.
- Hamilton Lane invests in less than 1% of secondary deal flow it evaluates, emphasizing rigorous selection and portfolio construction as critical components often overlooked in private markets discussions.
- Public equity markets have sharply de-rated alternative asset managers despite strong fundamentals; Hamilton Lane stock was cut nearly in half even though incentive fees rose to $175 million for fiscal year 2026 so far from $54 million in 2022, and the company is buying back stock and insiders are buying.
- Long-term structural trends favor private markets: the number of public companies is not growing, companies can stay private indefinitely with ample capital, and significant value creation (e.g., SpaceX, Anthropic) occurs before public listing, so investors need private exposure for diversification.
