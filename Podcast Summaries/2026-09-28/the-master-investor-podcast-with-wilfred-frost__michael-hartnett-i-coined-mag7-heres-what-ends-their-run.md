# Michael Hartnett: I coined “Mag7” - Here’s What Ends Their Run — The Master Investor Podcast with Wilfred Frost

## TL;DR
Wilfred Frost's rare extended interview with Michael Hartnett - Bank of America's chief investment strategist and the man who coined "Magnificent Seven" - on fiscal blowout, the rotation away from sovereign debt, and why he thinks the AI trade faces a twin threat from bond market discipline and voter backlash at the midterms.

## Key points
- Hartnett's structural frame for the decade: ballooning fiscal spending and nominal growth have made government bonds unattractive as a store of value, and capital is rotating away from sovereign debt. He says the Mag7 trade was born exactly out of that - it existed because nobody wanted to hold government bonds, so investors put money with companies that have lots of cash and do not spend it.
- The AI trade now faces a twin threat: bond market discipline on Wall Street and political pushback from voters on Main Street. He singles out the midterms as the political event that could derail the AI trade, because bubbles historically pop via bonds and social upheaval rather than via valuation alone.
- The Mag7 have changed character: they have shifted from cash-flow machines to capital spenders, with roughly $1.2 trillion of capex planned. He dates the peak of the Mag7 as a group to 29 October of last year, immediately after the Fed cut rates - a classic "you're cutting rates and the market does not believe you" moment.
- On Fed credibility: central banks never run out of ammunition but they can run out of credibility. He argues the Fed has been forced to act to restore credibility, that it is panicking, and that it will win.
- Historical analogies he leans on are the 1880s railroad boom, the 1990s dot-com bubble and the Nifty Fifty - his preferred comparable among bubbles because of the concentration dynamics.
- His personal mantra is "Buy Humiliation, Sell Hubris." The contrarian exposures flowing from it are certain bonds, China, small caps and real estate - the assets investors are most embarrassed to own.
