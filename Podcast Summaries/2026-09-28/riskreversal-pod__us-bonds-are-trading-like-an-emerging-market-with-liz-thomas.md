# U.S. Bonds Are Trading Like an Emerging Market with Liz Thomas — RiskReversal Pod

## TL;DR
Liz Thomas joins RiskReversal to discuss historic moves in U.S. Treasuries, including 10-year yields above 5% and yields repricing like an emerging market. She argues high yields will persist for decades and outlines a barbell portfolio of AI/tech and Treasuries/gold/commodities, while warning about rate-hike cycle risks, bank margin pressure, and midterm election volatility.

## Key points
- The episode focuses on historic U.S. Treasury bond market moves: 10-year yields crossed 5% for the first time in years, 5-year yields exceeded 5%, the average Treasury curve yield surpassed 5%, and yields jumped 20 basis points in two days, likened to emerging-market behavior despite the U.S. being a $30 trillion economy.
- Guest Liz Thomas argues yields are likely to stay higher for decades, driven by persistent inflation that won't fall without a recession, requiring investors to rethink asset allocation; she does not see this as a collapse but a new era.
- Host Guy Adami says he anticipated rising yields for months and bought 10-year Treasuries around 5% into his personal account, contrary to many who disagreed.
- Thomas describes a barbell portfolio: fully invested in software, semiconductors, cybersecurity, and AI on one side; and 10-year Treasuries, gold, and commodities on the other, avoiding mid-cap assets.
- She outlines a decision-tree: if war continues and oil stays above $90, the Fed will keep hiking, which historically leads quickly to recession; if war abates and oil falls, the Fed may slow hikes, which could be near-term positive for stocks.
- Thomas warns investors not to be complacent about rate-hike cycles: stocks often rally 12 months after the first hike and markets can become more irrational before correcting, but initial reactions are usually bearish and volatility is a risk.
- On gold, Thomas sees current weakness as technical, with price at 50- and 100-day moving averages, and maintains upside view; she bought gold weeks ago. She says central bank buying was a big driver early in the year but now global anxiety, not central bank accumulation, will support gold.
- Banks have pulled back about 3% over five days, landing in the bottom ten industries; Thomas attributes pressure to a flattening yield curve squeezing net interest margins, with Bank of America hurt by retail lending exposure and Goldman Sachs by delayed deal flow, such as OpenAI's postponed IPO to 2027.
- Brian Moynihan's surprisingly downbeat comments at the Barclays Global Finance Conference around September 14 added to bank sector concerns; Thomas notes financials are her benchmark for market health, and the pullback alongside industrials is a warning.
- For midterm elections, Thomas expects pre-election volatility and a possible dip, with healthcare and consumer staples performing well; she also notes a potential truckers' strike in October over diesel prices and controversy around a diesel export ban that colleague Mario argues would raise inflation.
