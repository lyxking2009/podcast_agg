# Ben Cowen Says You Have Permission to be Bullish — Bankless

## TL;DR
Ben Cowen returns to Bankless after publicly admitting his Q4-flush call was wrong, and walks through what actually broke his thesis - Bitcoin taking out the May high - why this bear market has been unusually short and painless, and why he cannot be deterministically bearish while price holds above roughly $83K.

## Key points
- Cowen explains that his "I was wrong" post was not about what happens in Q4 - nobody knows that yet - but specifically about his refusal to give weight to Bitcoin taking out the May high. In every prior bear market, once price crossed the 50-week moving average and printed a higher high, the bear market was over.
- Where the thesis broke: he treated the ~$57K July 1 low as the summer bottom (the 2018 analogue bottomed around $5,700 at a similar point in the calendar) and expected a July rally. The rally did not come in July - price sat near $60K - and when it arrived in August it was the move he had been forecasting a month early.
- The tell that unsettled him: even in bear markets Bitcoin can rally 30 percent, but this cycle did it twice, from ~$63K to ~$77K and then again to the mid-$80Ks. Two such moves that close together does not fit a bear-market template.
- Bear-market length is the uncomfortable part of the bull case. This drawdown has run roughly nine months, comparable to 2019, but 2019 also featured genuine price-based capitulation - a large drop accompanied by a large volume spike - and this cycle has had no such volume capitulation. His own counterargument: if the market topped on apathy rather than on a volume climax, it may not need a volume climax to bottom.
- His honest position is that the chart does not make sense to him. With the long end of the curve rising, energy prices heading higher and the dollar strengthening, a typical Q4 drop would fit - but the market does not have to conform to his expectations, and is not.
- The $83K line is the practical boundary: as long as Bitcoin holds above roughly $83K, deterministically bearish positioning is hard to justify.
- The guest and host compare the two bear markets directly and find the difference explanatory: last cycle spent around 270 days below the 200-week moving average and got as much as 30 percent below it, while this one dipped only briefly and by single digits for roughly 100 days.
- The "sins" comparison is the frame they settle on. The last bear market had to absorb Terra/Luna, Three Arrows Capital, FTX and the Gensler-era regulatory campaign. This one has had almost nothing comparable - in their telling, essentially just one large holder selling Bitcoin below its average buy price - alongside the most favorable regulatory conditions the industry has ever had. Fewer sins to pay for means a shorter, shallower bear market.
- On positioning, Cowen describes a mechanical approach rather than a view: buy in the second half of the midterm year when Bitcoin is below his 0.3 risk level. He did it in 2018 and 2022 and got wrecked in Q4 both times, and this cycle his regret was not following his own rule in July when the signal was below the 0.3 risk level.
