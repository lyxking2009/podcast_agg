# At The Money: Hungry? Should You Invest in Wheat?

**Show:** Masters in Business

**Duration:** 19 min

## Key Points

- Wheat futures are a fundamentally different animal than stocks or bonds - they require margin accounts, are highly volatile, and carry significantly more downside risk than options
- The wheat ETF (WEAT) doubled after Russia invaded Ukraine and has since returned to pre-war levels, illustrating the boom-bust cycle of commodity investing
- The WAT ETF was designed to solve the problem of investors wanting wheat exposure without the complexity and risk of holding futures directly
- Commodity futures have a negative expected return over long periods due to contango (the cost of rolling contracts), making them poor long-term holdings
- Wheat prices are influenced by weather, geopolitics, and global supply chains - making them inherently unpredictable for retail investors
- The 2022 Ukraine war spike was a once-in-a-generation event; wheat has historically been one of the most volatile agricultural commodities
- For most investors, commodity exposure through ETFs is better used as a tactical hedge rather than a strategic portfolio allocation
- Sal Gilbertie, founder of Teucrium Trading, has been trading commodities since 1982 and emphasizes that futures trading is not for the uninitiated

## Overview

Barry Ritholtz explores whether wheat belongs in a retail investor's portfolio with Sal Gilbertie, founder of Teucrium Trading and creator of agricultural commodity ETFs. The conversation covers the mechanics of wheat futures, the risks of commodity investing, the impact of the Ukraine war on grain prices, and why most investors should approach commodities with caution. Gilbertie explains that futures carry more downside than options and that the negative roll yield from contango makes them poor long-term holdings.

## Implications

Commodity ETFs can provide tactical exposure to inflation hedges or geopolitical risks, but they are not suitable as core portfolio holdings due to structural negative expected returns from contango. Investors seeking agricultural exposure should understand the mechanics of futures rolling and the difference between spot price movements and ETF returns.
