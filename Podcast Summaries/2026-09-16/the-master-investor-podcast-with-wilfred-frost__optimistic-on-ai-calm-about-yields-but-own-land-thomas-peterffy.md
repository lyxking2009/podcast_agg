# Optimistic On AI, Calm About Yields – But Own Land – Thomas Peterffy — The Master Investor Podcast with Wilfred Frost

## TL;DR

Thomas Peterffy, founder and chairman of Interactive Brokers, explains that the firm's growth comes from serving professional traders with sophisticated tools, and he is optimistic about AI's impact on productivity and earnings. He believes the Fed will likely raise rates despite his disagreement, and he sees prediction markets eventually becoming the majority of IBKR's earnings. Peterffy also discusses buying land as a hedge against a 20% chance of US political upheaval, drawing on his family's experience in communist Hungary.

## Key points

- Interactive Brokers was founded in 1978 and now has a market cap of $160 billion; Peterffy owns 75% and is worth over $100 billion, with earnings growing 40% per year since 2022 driven by its focus on professional traders.
- IBKR initially targeted floor traders moving to electronic trading (2000-2010), then expanded to hedge funds, professional investors, and retail clients; its platform offers advanced features like arbitrage, pair trading, shorting, best execution, and low financing rates.
- Peterffy says interest rate levels don't affect IBKR's net interest income significantly because the firm lends at half over fed funds and pays half under fed funds; he is not concerned about rising yields as long as increases are gradual.
- He expects the Fed to raise rates despite his opposition, to demonstrate independence; he argues oil prices, driven by the war and Iran's threat to the Strait of Hormuz, are the real inflation driver, and stifling Iran is more important than rate hikes.
- Peterffy is optimistic on AI, citing Nvidia at 27 times earnings with 70% expected earnings growth as a relatively low valuation; he believes AI adoption by all companies will drastically increase productivity and earnings.
- Hyperscalers are in a race to be one of two winners, so they buy all compute capacity, which will eventually lower compute prices and benefit others, but they may have to write down much of that compute.
- He does not believe in a 'Hindenburg moment' for AI; hacks have existed for a long time and haven't caused substantial problems, but IBKR continuously works to limit damage if hacked.
- Prediction markets are a major focus: IBKR launched forecast trader in 2024 and expanded in 2025, originally built a phantom prediction market 10 years ago that Kalshi and Polymarket copied; he tried to buy Kalshi but was refused, then got his own CFTC license.
- Peterffy says prediction markets should interdigitate with equity and commodities trading because the economy is the major determinant of stock prices; these markets will become more liquid as economic interests hedge, and in the fullness of time prediction markets will be the majority of IBKR's earnings.
- Political prediction markets are deep and more accurate than polls; currently they point to the House flipping Democratic and the Senate remaining Republican.
- The single biggest risk he worries about is leverage in the entire financial system, especially over-the-counter products where counterparty credit creates 'spaghetti'; IBKR itself only offers exchange-cleared products and auto-liquidates margin violations.
- He is not worried about AI threatening IBKR because it offers AI models to customers; current AI models have frozen weights and cannot update with new information, so they are poor at forecasting earnings, making prediction markets more important for forward projections.
- IBKR is pursuing a bank trust charter to custody mutual funds and ETFs, which aids securities lending; its online short inventory and lending rates are more transparent than big custodians like Northern Trust, State Street, and JPMorgan.
- Peterffy bought large amounts of land based on his family's experience in communist Hungary, where land was the only asset returned after 45 years; he puts a 20% chance on the US experiencing a similar socialist takeover and says land is the logical asset for heirs, unlike gold which can be confiscated.
- His advice: have a plan, be open to change, and learn as much as possible about AI to use it to your benefit.
