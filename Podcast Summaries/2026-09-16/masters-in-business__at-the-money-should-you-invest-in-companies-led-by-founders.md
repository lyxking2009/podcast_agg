# At The Money: Should You Invest in Companies Led by Founders? — Masters in Business

## TL;DR

Barry Ritholtz interviews Michael Monahan, partner and portfolio manager of the Founders 100 ETF (FFF), about investing in founder-led companies. Monahan says data from 11,000 stocks over 30 years shows founder-led firms outperform by about 3x and grow 4% faster than the S&P, driven by vision, execution, charisma, grit, and long-term thinking. He details FFF's selection process—screening ~800 founder-led stocks, taking the 200 largest by market cap, then applying a valuation model—along with its modified market-cap weighting, 7.5% position cap, and sell discipline triggered by founder departure or fundamental flags.

## Key points

- Founder-led companies outperformed by about 3x in a 30-year study of 11,000 stocks, and on average grew 4% more than the S&P 500, according to Michael Monahan.
- The outperformance is not due to technology concentration or survivorship bias; founder-led firms exist across the economy and founders bring unique vision, execution, charisma, grit, and a decades-long perspective, unlike hired CEOs who focus on quarterly results and board PowerPoints.
- Monahan argues founders actually de-risk their businesses more than non-founders, citing Marc Andreessen's stage-based approach where each stage (zero, one, two) focuses on de-risking.
- Monahan's own experience founding Beartooth Radio reframed his view—building a company from zero to one is fundamentally different from fitting into a corporate system, and exposure to tech founders revealed a different level of passion and vision.
- The ETF defines 'founder-led' strictly as the original founder still serving as an executive—typically CEO, but sometimes CTO or chief medical/scientific officer; board members or chairmen do not count because only daily operational leadership shows efficacy.
- Edge cases are evaluated case-by-case: Elon Musk was legally confirmed as one of five original Tesla founders in a 2012 or 2014 court case; SpaceX is clearly founder-led; Berkshire Hathaway and Monster Beverage are considered founder-led despite being built on acquisitions.
- FFF's selection universe starts with roughly 800 founder-led stocks at any time; it takes the 200 largest by market capitalization, then applies a valuation model built by Monahan's co-founder, a Columbia Business School-trained value investor, to select the final 100.
- The portfolio has about 80% active share versus the NASDAQ 100, with 100% founder-led exposure compared to about 20% for the NASDAQ 100; semiconductor weighting is only 10% versus 30% for NASDAQ 100 and 20% for S&P 500, and includes industrials, energy, and financials.
- FFF uses a modified market-cap weighting with a hard cap of 7.5% per position and quarterly rebalancing; this concentrated approach can cause top holdings to decline while the rest of the portfolio drives gains.
- The primary sell trigger is a founder announcing resignation—the fund sells within 90 days of announcement; a fundamental overlay flags 'burnt pizza crust' situations where a company's fundamentals deteriorate, prompting replacement with the next best candidate.
