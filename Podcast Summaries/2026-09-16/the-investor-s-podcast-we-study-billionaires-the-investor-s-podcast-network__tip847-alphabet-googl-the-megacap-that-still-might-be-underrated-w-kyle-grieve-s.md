# TIP847: Alphabet (GOOGL): The Megacap That Still Might Be Underrated w/ Kyle Grieve & Shawn O’Malley — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)  - The Investor’s Podcast Network

## TL;DR

This episode revisits Alphabet after a 90% gain, with hosts determining the megacap may no longer be undervalued but remains an exceptional business. Google's search business has withstood AI disruption, cloud is scaling rapidly with a $500B backlog, and regulatory risks have eased. However, massive AI data center spending, negative free cash flow, and capital intensity introduce new uncertainties, making the stock fairly valued rather than a bargain.

## Key points

- Alphabet is the second-largest position in the Intrinsic Value portfolio at ~14%, up over 90% since being added 18 months ago; hosts emphasize it's not a sell, but they wouldn't add at current valuation.
- The original thesis centered on Alphabet's collection of world-class assets—Google Search, YouTube, Google Cloud, and Android—with YouTube generating ~$36B annual revenue from a $1.6B purchase and Google Ventures managing >$10B across ~400 portfolio companies.
- Contrary to early fears that ChatGPT would kill Google Search, search revenue has grown at a 14% CAGR over the last two years; AI Overviews and AI Mode are monetized with ads, and only about 20-25% of searches historically have ads, leaving room for additional monetization.
- Google's distribution advantage is strong: AI features are being rolled out to over a billion monthly active users, and CEO Sundar Pichai reported that Google sends billions of clicks to websites weekly from AI-embedded search results.
- Regulatory overhang has diminished: the DOJ sought to force divestiture of Chrome but the court instead imposed restrictions on Google's distribution agreements; Google can still pay Apple ~$20B annually to remain default search, but deals are now limited to one-year terms.
- Apple is deepening its dependence on Google, paying ~$1B per year for access to Gemini to power Siri, and increasingly relying on Google Cloud, adding another layer of intertwined business.
- Google Cloud is the standout growth engine: revenue reached $58B in 2025 with a 24% operating margin, then grew 63% and 82% YoY in Q1 and Q2 2026, with Q2 annualized run rate near $100B and margins expanding to ~36%.
- Cloud backlog skyrocketed from $106B in Q2 2024 to over $500B in Q2 2025, but carries concentration risk: the five-year $200B deal with Anthropic may represent ~40% of backlog.
- Google's custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) give a cost advantage, priced ~40% below Nvidia equivalents, and are increasingly sold externally, reducing reliance on Nvidia while monetizing internal infrastructure.
- Alphabet's subscription segment, including Google One and YouTube Premium, generated over $25B in the first half of 2026; YouTube is the largest streaming service and its asset-light model yields outsized profitability versus peers.
- Waymo has appreciated from ~$50B to ~$130B valuation, driven by 127M autonomous miles, 90% fewer serious injury crashes than human drivers, 15M rides in 2025, and 500K weekly rides; Alphabet's 5% SpaceX stake is worth ~$95B.
- Capital intensity has surged: Alphabet plans ~$200B in data center capex in 2026, required equity raise of $85B, long-term debt up to $98B, and over $800B in off-balance sheet liabilities; free cash flow turned negative in Q2 2026 and buybacks were paused.
- Michael Burry warns that hyperscalers may be understating depreciation by as much as $175B over the next few years, and Alphabet carries $122B in assets not yet in service, potentially inflating near-term margins.
- Q2 2026 net income of $112B included $99B in non-cash gains from marking up private stakes like SpaceX and Anthropic, making operating income or free cash flow more reliable measures.
- Hosts estimate that each $200B of data center spending must generate $70–100B of new annual revenue to justify the investment; current demand is strong with neoclouds raising prices and capacity sold out, but long-term pricing durability is uncertain.
