# Arc Mainnet, AI Agents, and Tokenized Markets | Nikhil Chandhok, CTO of Circle — Bankless

## TL;DR

Circle launches Arc mainnet, a new Layer 1 blockchain designed as an 'economic OS' with payment finality in half a second, gas payable in USDC, and permissionless deployment on a permissioned validator set. The episode covers Arc's privacy via trusted execution environments, immutability stance, and focus on AI agents as economic actors, including agent credit markets and agentic commerce. Nikhil Chandhok also discusses tokenized stocks, on-ramps, and Circle's role as infrastructure rather than a competitor to exchanges.

## Key points

- Arc mainnet launches today after approximately 18 months of work, positioning Circle beyond stablecoin issuance to become a full-stack Layer 1 blockchain.
- Arc offers payment finality with settlement time of half a second, reducing counterparty risk and enabling rapid transactions.
- Gas on Arc is paid in stablecoins starting with USDC, eliminating the need for users to hold a native network token; 16 stablecoins launch on Arc today.
- The validator set is permissioned to attract institutional traffic, but contract deployment is permissionless; anyone can deploy contracts without Circle approval.
- Privacy on Arc uses Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs), so validators and Circle cannot view private transactions; users can toggle between public and private transactions in the same wallet.
- Arc plans to introduce post-quantum signatures in beta, allowing wallets to enforce post-quantum security.
- The chain is immutable; there are no rollbacks or special powers to reverse transactions, even in the event of large exploits, to preserve trust in public infrastructure.
- Arc positions itself as an 'economic OS' with full-stack developer tools including Arc Studio for AI contract deployment and kits for building custom applications.
- A key venture bet is on AI agents becoming independent economic actors; Arc is building primitives such as an agent marketplace, reputation system, Nano payments for microtransactions, and agent credit markets.
- Agentic commerce is emerging: personal agents can now make purchases using credit cards (e.g., buying pet medication from Chewy), while services commerce where agents perform piecemeal work is still nascent.
- Circle sees tokenized stocks not as a competitor to exchanges but as infrastructure for settlement and complex financial transactions, expecting more real-world asset issuers on Arc.
- Circle has off-ramped over $900 billion in USDC over its lifespan and is expanding retail-focused on-ramps, stable FX for local currency swaps, and integration with Circle Mint and liquidity pools.
- The GENIUS Act, effective in January, will provide US stablecoin regulatory clarity, and other countries are waiting for US rules before finalizing their own; Circle plans to partner with local stablecoin issuers rather than issue in most jurisdictions.
- Short-term priorities include a war room to ensure all promised functionality works, adding more validators, rolling out new privacy and agent features, and eventually launching proof of stake.
