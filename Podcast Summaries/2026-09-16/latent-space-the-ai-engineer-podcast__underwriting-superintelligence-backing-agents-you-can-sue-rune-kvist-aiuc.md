# Underwriting Superintelligence: Backing Agents you can Sue — Rune Kvist, AIUC — Latent Space: The AI Engineer Podcast

## TL;DR

Rune Kvist, co-founder of AIUC, discusses the company's $40M Series A led by Ribbit Capital and First Harmonic and its mission to build confidence infrastructure for AI through standards and insurance. AIUC's AIUC-1 certification provides a quarterly-updated framework for agent security, safety, and reliability, with customers including Cursor, Harvey, Lovable, and ElevenLabs. Kvist explains the historical role of standards and insurance in enabling technology adoption, the current risk bottlenecks for AI agents and models (citing Mythos and Fable), and AIUC's plans to expand into model certification and robotics.

## Key points

- AIUC raised a $40M Series A led by Ribbit Capital and First Harmonic, and its customers include Cursor, Harvey, Lovable, and ElevenLabs.
- Rune Kvist's path to AI started with reading the Kaplan Scaling Laws paper in late 2021, which convinced him that capital would fund model scaling, leading him to join early Anthropic around 2022.
- At Anthropic, Kvist observed early vision documents predicting compute needs, CapEx, and societal impacts, and later saw Waymo as an example where liability, not capability, was the adoption bottleneck.
- AIUC builds 'confidence infrastructure' through standards and insurance; historically, every major technology wave (electricity, cars, nuclear) needed market-driven standards and insurance to enable adoption.
- The AIUC-1 standard for agent security, safety, and reliability is updated quarterly with input from a consortium of risk leaders from Fortune 1000 companies to address evolving risks.
- Certification involves three types of requirements: technical controls (e.g., groundedness filters), third-party testing (e.g., jailbreak/hallucination evals), and policy controls (e.g., accountability plans).
- Auditors like KPMG or Schellman verify that technical controls exist, while AIUC itself performs the third-party technical testing, including thousands of simulations for jailbreaks, hallucinations, and data leaks.
- The certification process typically takes 3-10 weeks, depending on the company's existing security posture, and includes quarterly re-testing and updates.
- ElevenLabs purchased the first AI agent insurance policy through AIUC and Lloyd's of London, covering core customer concerns and providing trust to enterprise buyers.
- AIUC plans to expand from agent certification to model-level certification, addressing the trust gap between frontier AI labs and the US government, exemplified by the Fable and Mythos incidents.
- Model risks differ from agent risks because models have an infinite risk surface; AIUC proposes a neutral third-party audit function similar to Moody's for bonds, combining technical evals with legible reports.
- Key emerging risks for models include cyberattacks, child safety, and biological weapons, with the challenge of coordinating subject matter experts under one framework.
- AIUC sees robotics as the next frontier after models, noting that physical AI will have even higher liability stakes (e.g., Waymo hitting a dog, robot harming a toddler).
- Copyright infringement is currently uninsurable due to adverse selection: companies seeking coverage often know they have used copyrighted data, and insurers lack independent information.
- AIUC is hiring full-stack technical staff and plans to have representation from 50% of Fortune 1000 companies in its consortium by end of year.
