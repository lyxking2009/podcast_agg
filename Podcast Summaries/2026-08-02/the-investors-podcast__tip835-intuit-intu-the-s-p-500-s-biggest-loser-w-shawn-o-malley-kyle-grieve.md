# TIP835: Intuit (INTU): The S&P 500's Biggest Loser w/ Shawn O'Malley & Kyle Grieve — The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)  - The Investor's Podcast Network

## TL;DR
Shawn O'Malley and Kyle Grieve dig into Intuit (INTU), the S&P 500's worst performer of the year after a 60%+ selloff, and debate whether AI disruption fears have created a bargain or a genuine value trap. Despite the drawdown, Intuit still posted 15% revenue growth and 30% operating profit growth, and the hosts land on a fair value near $400/share against a ~$320 entry target, adding a modest 2% tracker position to the portfolio.

## Key points
- Intuit's core franchise spans QuickBooks (80-90% share of small business accounting), TurboTax (40M+ annual DIY tax filers), Credit Karma (personal finance and acquisition funnel), and the weaker-performing MailChimp email marketing unit.
- Despite the stock's collapse, fundamentals remain strong: ~15% revenue growth, ~30% operating profit growth, ~80% gross margins, and ~30% operating margins, with cash generation funding dividends, buybacks, and reinvestment.
- The bear case centers on AI tools potentially automating tax prep and bookkeeping, obsoleting software like TurboTax and QuickBooks entirely.
- The bull case rests on switching costs from integrated financial data, proprietary data for lending/underwriting, brand trust for high-stakes financial decisions, and network effects among accountants and businesses — the hosts argue tax filing carries too much audit risk for users to trust untested AI alternatives.
- Valuation has compressed sharply: the stock trades at 15-16x trailing earnings versus a historical ~60x, with a forward P/E near 10x. The hosts estimate fair value around $400/share with a $320 entry target and project ~18% annualized returns over five years, likely front-loaded.
- Risk factors flagged include stock-based compensation doubling as a share of revenue since 2020, a 17% workforce reduction that raises questions about management priorities, an underperforming MailChimp acquisition, and relatively modest CEO stock ownership (~$5M).
- The hosts add a 2% tracker position in Intuit, with room to expand toward 5% if conviction increases, framing the investment as a bet on whether Intuit's moat widens or shrinks amid AI-driven disruption.
