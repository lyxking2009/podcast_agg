# 103. Seven Questions to Ask About Any Democracy — Wiser World

*(Host: Alli Roper. Source: RSS show notes — the episode's own structured summary, including the seven principles.)*

## TL;DR

A framework episode rather than a history episode. Alli Roper opens with the question that structures the whole hour: if a country holds elections, does that make it a democracy? Not necessarily. The episode steps past the ballot box to lay out seven principles that make a democratic government actually democratic — popular sovereignty, real elections, limits on power, the rule of law, legitimate political opposition, access to independent information, and the peaceful transfer of power — and then confronts democracy's longest-running unresolved question: who counts as "the people"? Roper frames the result explicitly as a practical diagnostic rather than a complete history of democracy: a checklist for recognising what democracy requires, and what to watch for when those requirements start to disappear.

## Key points

- **The framing question:** if a country holds elections, does that make it a democracy? The episode's answer is no — elections are necessary but far from sufficient.
- **Principle 1 — popular sovereignty.** The idea that legitimate authority ultimately rests with the people, not with a ruler, party or institution that merely permits voting.
- **Principle 2 — real elections.** Not just the existence of a ballot, but elections that are genuinely contested and meaningful.
- **Principle 3 — limits on power.** Democratic government is defined partly by what it structurally cannot do.
- **Principle 4 — the rule of law.** Law applying to the powerful as well as the powerless.
- **Principle 5 — legitimate political opposition.** A recognised, functioning opposition rather than a nominal one that exists to be defeated.
- **Principle 6 — access to independent information.** A citizenry that can find out what is actually happening, independent of the state.
- **Principle 7 — the peaceful transfer of power.** The test that most cleanly separates democratic government from authoritarian forms that use democratic aesthetics.
- **The unresolved core question: who counts as 'the people'?** Roper calls this one of democracy's longest-running questions and treats it as a live one rather than settled history.
- **Explicit scope caveat:** this is not a complete history of democracy. It is a practical framework — the episode is designed to be used as a diagnostic lens on current events.
- **The diagnostic purpose:** recognising what democracy requires, and knowing what to watch for when those principles begin to disappear.
- **Host context:** Wiser World is built on the premise that history explains headlines — host Alli Roper is a world history teacher who turns complex global history into compact episodes for busy adults.
