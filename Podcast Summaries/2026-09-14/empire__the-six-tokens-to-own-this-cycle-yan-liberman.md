# The Six Tokens To Own This Cycle | Yan Liberman — Empire

*(Guest: Yan Liberman, co-founder of Delphi Digital and managing partner of its venture book. YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIv7MNKqnEQ — duration matched the RSS feed at 3,568s.)*

## TL;DR

Yan Liberman of Delphi Digital sits down with the Empire hosts to talk positioning rather than narrative. He is long and has not taken profits, arguing the move still has legs because the market was caught offsides by expectations of a QE-like intervention at the long end of the bond curve, which lit the fuse for a short squeeze out of a bottom. But he is explicit that he cannot see new all-time highs without material new QE, because the buyer who historically took the market there — Saylor — is now absent and has become instead a steady seller of preferreds. The practical conclusion is that the alt market is shallower than it used to be, the same 10-15 tokens absorb all fund allocations, and the real edge is in plausibly-revenue-generating names that have been forgotten rather than the crowded ones. He also reveals his venture book is now mostly AI, and walks through the one crypto deal it closed in the last year — a dollar-hedged Turkish lira carry trade being brought on chain.

## Key points

- **Liberman is long and hasn't de-risked.** "I'm pretty long. I haven't really been taking profit on any of the positions yet. Just holding steady. It feels like there's still legs."
- **What lit the fuse:** the market got caught offsides on the news of what amounts to a form of QE via long-end bond-curve control. That catalyst arrived at a moment when everyone agreed the market was bottomy and Bitcoin was "just chilling," producing a large short squeeze. The open question is whether it has legs.
- **Saylor's absence is the key structural point.** Liberman reads Saylor's not buying as historically a bearish signal; he has been "cashmaxing" and paying off preferreds, and STRC is approaching 100. Liberman thinks that rep is hard to sustain given the derivatives market on it — infinite selling is possible while downside is capped at 100 — so Saylor is unlikely to return to buying soon. Net, he reads this as favourable.
- **Leverage is not the problem.** He notes leverage hasn't built up much and the tape looks "pretty measured." He wants an ordering where the market grinds higher, pauses, and then alts run — not everything at once.
- **No new all-time highs without new QE.** "I personally have a hard time seeing new all-time highs anytime soon without some material new form of QE." Saylor was what took the market to the prior highs and that took a great deal of capital; there is no obvious buyer now, and there will be skeptics along the way.
- **The bullish counter:** a lot of old holders sold and the supply went into stronger hands (Saylor's balance sheet), which is constructive for a future high — but it takes time and a lot of money.
- **The alt market is shallower, and that cuts both ways.** Less depth means easier for capital to coalesce around a handful of assets, so you don't need a whole new buyer cohort. But every liquid crypto fund is already allocated to the same 10-15 names.
- **Valuation dispersion is the opportunity.** Valuations aren't cheap but aren't overly expensive; multiple expansion requires growth. Hyperliquid trades at a higher multiple because the USDC bid creates a stable buyback source; Pump-style names trade cheaply because everyone doubts the durability of those earnings.
- **The stated edge:** "look for tokens that look and smell like those but aren't necessarily as crowded of a trade." There is appetite for compelling alts that haven't been bid up.
- **Specific views:** Lighter could still go up quite a bit. Hyperliquid is easy to be bullish over a longer time horizon, but going to 200 within four months would be surprising — for shorter-term outperformance you have to look beyond the obvious names.
- **Crypto vs AI in the venture book.** When crypto heats up the moves are bigger than anything else, so it's worth attention in those windows and worth weighting down as it cools. His most recent venture fund has five investments and only one is in crypto — and they've done roughly one venture crypto deal in the past year. AI is absorbing talent, attention and capital. The counter-argument he makes is that on the crypto venture side it's now much clearer what can accrue value and what is defensible, which lets you screen out most of the market quickly.
- **The one crypto deal: Toku/Tori-style tokenized carry.** A tokenized, dollar-hedged Turkish lira carry trade yielding about 10.5% organically with no incentives. It requires private-wealth and ultra-high-net-worth relationships to reach the right counterparties — all first-tier with minimal credit risk, and one counterparty is a bank backed by the central bank of Turkey that never had problems even during the unwind. The pitch is durable, crypto-exogenous yield brought on chain with massive capacity, and you can layer a USDC borrow against the tokenized position to lever the carry.
- **On net-interest-margin businesses:** he is less interested in NIM-type structures except from a very early venture position — Athena will do well, but that is not a venture return. Higher yield means a higher take rate, so each dollar of TVL is more accretive to the top line and the protocol doesn't need to get as large to generate the same return.
- **Liquid over private right now.** Both generally and at present there are more opportunities on the liquid side — a numbers game with much shorter return profiles. On an IRR basis, liquid situations are interesting because there are legacy tokens that have "fallen by the wayside" and been completely forgotten that are actually building in the background, offering what he calls incredible value.
- **Host positioning:** Santi admits he has ramped up and been buying tokens on FOMO, describing it as his YOLO account.
