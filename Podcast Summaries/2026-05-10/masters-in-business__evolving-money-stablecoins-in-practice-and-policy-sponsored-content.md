# Evolving Money: Stablecoins in Practice and Policy (Sponsored Content) — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Stablecoins settled over $30 trillion in transactions in 2025 — more than Visa and Mastercard combined. Checkout.com is rolling out stablecoin settlement for enterprise merchants starting in the US, offering 24/7 immediate cross-border payments. Coinbase Chief Policy Officer Faryar Shirzad explains the Genius Act (passed July 2025) created a federal framework for stablecoin issuers, the CFTC now allows stablecoins for derivatives settlement, and over 250 financial institutions have announced crypto integration projects. Incumbent banks are resisting the speed of change, especially around eliminating settlement lag (T+0), while the Bank of England is proposing restrictive caps on pound stablecoins that Coinbase argues will backfire.

## Key points
- Checkout.com is relaunching stablecoin settlement after a failed 2021 attempt — the regulatory framework now exists. Merchants can settle 24/7 instantly into their wallets rather than depending on bank hours and rails. CPO Moron Kilbeggi says the US market rollout starts first, with cross-border payments and developing economies as the strongest use cases where stablecoins provide dollar access and avoid FX fees.
- Stablecoins transacted $30+ trillion in 2025, exceeding Visa and Mastercard combined. While most was crypto trading, use cases in payroll, international purchasing, and retail shopping are growing rapidly. Checkout.com sees it as an experiment to gauge consumer preference — merchants with crypto-wallet-holding customers are interested in enabling stablecoin retail payments.
- The Genius Act, passed by Congress and signed into law in July 2025, provides a federal framework for stablecoin issuers. The CFTC is now allowing stablecoins for derivatives trade settlement even before full implementation — creating a regulatory sandbox that lets market participants use the technology with regulator blessing.
- Coinbase's Shirzad warns that incumbent financial players (banks, payment processors, credit card companies) are launching 'furious attacks' to slow regulatory changes that threaten their economic rents from settlement intermediation. The SEC chairman wants on-chain T+0 capital markets settlement, eliminating the lag that intermediaries profit from.
- Internationally, the US moving first on stablecoin regulation creates pressure on other currencies to tokenize or risk irrelevance. The Bank of England is proposing tight caps on how much pound sterling stablecoin any individual can hold — which Shirzad calls 'a big mistake' that will cede ground to dollar stablecoins. US-UK treasury dialogue on crypto regulation harmonization is ongoing.
- Kilbeggi notes that the technological build is the easiest part — regulatory complexity across 50 US states (e.g., New York's separate license, different state regimes) and making all stakeholders (regulators, banks, partners) comfortable with stablecoin operations is the real challenge. The vision is an ecosystem where vendor-to-vendor payments, cross-border payments, and eventually treasury management all run on stablecoin rails.
