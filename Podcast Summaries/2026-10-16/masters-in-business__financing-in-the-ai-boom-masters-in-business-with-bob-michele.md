# Financing In The AI Boom: Masters  in Business with Bob Michele — Masters in Business

## TL;DR
Bob Michele, CIO and head of global fixed income at J.P. Morgan Asset Management, discusses the fixed income landscape amid the AI boom. He sees a normalization of interest rates, not a return to zero, with healthy yields and active management advantages. Michele highlights opportunities in munis, emerging market local debt, and private credit, while cautioning about political gridlock and the end of fiscal stimulus.

## Key points
- Bob Michele's unconventional path from classics and pre-med to fixed income taught him to do his own research and treat client money as a fiduciary responsibility. The 2008 financial crisis reinforced that every bond is different and requires deep analysis of collateral, cash flows, and covenants.
- The current fixed income environment resembles the early 2000s, with real demand for capital and a cost of capital that is no longer zero. Michele expects the Fed's neutral rate around 4.25-4.5%, and investment-grade yields above 6% provide a meaningful cushion, favoring active management over passive indexes.
- AI is driving a seismic financing need among hyperscalers, who must borrow to build capacity quickly or risk becoming the next BlackBerry. J.P. Morgan sees AI as transformative across the bank and is funding multiple internal AI proposals. Private credit and public debt markets are converging, offering borrowers more options.
- Emerging market local debt is a hidden gem, with countries like Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Czech Republic, Poland, and South Africa offering real yields near 5% and nominal yields near 9%. These markets are now largely domestically financed, reducing dependence on cross-border flows.
- Municipal bonds are attractive, especially for high-tax state residents, with taxable-equivalent yields exceeding 8%. Strong rainy day funds and tax-loss harvesting have created buying opportunities. Michele also views TIPS as showing well-anchored inflation expectations but elevated real yields due to policy and geopolitical uncertainty.
- The U.S. economy has an 85% probability of expansion, driven by broad-based capital expenditures in technology, defense, energy, and manufacturing. However, the last 18 months of the Trump presidency could bring gridlock and the absence of fiscal stimulus, raising the risk of a hard landing after seven years without a normal recession.
- Michele advises young professionals to master AI tools much like earlier generations mastered spreadsheets, and to take risks in the bond market. He also argues that Bitcoin and crypto are legitimate stores of value, comparable to fine art, and that a digital dollar or stablecoin is almost inevitable.
