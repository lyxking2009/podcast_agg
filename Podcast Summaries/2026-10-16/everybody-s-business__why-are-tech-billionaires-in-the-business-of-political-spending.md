# Why are Tech Billionaires in the Business of Political Spending? — Everybody's Business

## TL;DR
This episode explores tech billionaires' political spending, focusing on Marc Andreessen's shift to right-wing politics and his firm Andreessen Horowitz's massive donations to influence crypto and AI policy. Bloomberg reporter Austin Carr details how Andreessen has become one of the largest political donors, surpassing traditional heavyweights. The episode also features economist Isabella Weber discussing her book Anti-Fascist Economics, arguing for strategic price controls and democratic economic policies to counter fascism, drawing on historical lessons. A lighter segment covers hypothetical mergers.

## Key points
- Marc Andreessen, co-founder of Netscape and co-founder of venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz, has become one of the largest political donors in the 2024-2026 cycle, contributing at least $116 million through super PACs Fair Shake and Leading the Future, surpassing contributions from George Soros, the Koch family, and the Adelson family. His political spending focuses on crypto and AI policy.
- Andreessen's political shift from backing Hillary Clinton to supporting Trump-aligned Republicans stems from grievances over regulation, crypto and AI policy, and a perceived decline of American meritocracy and pride. He has placed allies in key government positions, including Scott Kupor as head of the Office of Personnel Management and David Sacks as AI and crypto czar, enabling policy influence without legislation.
- Despite gridlock in Congress being beneficial for deregulation, Andreessen continues heavy spending to elect aligned candidates. For example, a super PAC associated with him spent $30 million against Senator Sherrod Brown of Ohio, a crypto critic, even though Brown was not directly responsible for blocking crypto legislation. This illustrates strategic retribution and ideological alignment.
- Economist Isabella Weber argues that using interest rate hikes to fight inflation by increasing unemployment is anti-democratic and inappropriate for supply-shock-driven inflation, which characterized recent price increases. She advocates for strategic price controls as one tool in a democratic government's arsenal to protect affordability of essentials.
- Weber's 2021 Guardian op-ed proposing strategic price controls caused controversy within economics. She notes that Kamala Harris's price gouging ban proposal was highly popular among swing voters but was attacked by economists and ultimately withdrawn. Weber connects economic policy to democracy, citing FDR's economic bill of rights and warning that ignoring economic grievances can fuel fascism.
