# Release Me From This Demon — Money Stuff: The Podcast

## TL;DR
Matt Levine and Mary Childs discuss the legal fight over whether Kalshi's sports betting contracts are federally regulated swaps, and the CFTC's recent emergency rule trying to exclude state sportsbooks from that definition. They also cover Goldman Sachs' equities division where prime brokerage financing revenue has surpassed trading intermediation, reflecting banks shifting to financing hedge funds and prop firms. Finally, they talk about Martin Shkreli's contempt case for violating his lifetime ban from the pharmaceutical industry by trading and tweeting about pharma stocks.

## Key points
- Kalshi, a CFTC-regulated prediction market, has expanded into sports betting, arguing its contracts are federally regulated swaps. Many states are challenging this, claiming Kalshi is operating an illegal sportsbook. The CFTC initially supported Kalshi's position but recently issued an emergency rule declaring that state-licensed sportsbooks are not swaps, while Kalshi's sports contracts remain under federal jurisdiction. The issue may reach the Supreme Court due to a circuit split, and the outcome is uncertain.
- Goldman Sachs' equities division now earns more from prime brokerage and financing than from trading intermediation. This reflects a broader shift where banks reduce proprietary risk-taking and instead lend to hedge funds and market-making firms like Jane Street and Citadel Securities. While this moves risk to entities with longer-term funding, banks still retain exposure through leverage and margin lending, as seen in past blowups like Archegos.
- Martin Shkreli, banned from the pharmaceutical industry for life after antitrust violations, is facing a contempt order from the FTC and several states for continuing to trade and tweet about pharma stocks. He claims shorting stocks does not constitute 'holding' them, but regulators argue his activities violate the ban. The case highlights the unusual punishment of restricting his speech and trading, and his history of obnoxious behavior that led to such broad restrictions.
