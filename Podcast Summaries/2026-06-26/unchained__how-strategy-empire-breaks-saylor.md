# How the Strategy Empire Breaks, and Whether Saylor Can Stop It

## Key Points
- Vinny Lingham warned 18 months ago that Michael Saylor would do more damage to Bitcoin than FTX — Strategy (MSTR) has fallen over 80% from its November 2024 high
- The strategy becomes unstable when MSTR's market cap falls below the value of its Bitcoin holdings (adjusted for debt)
- Key actions that 'blew Saylor's runway': the 32-Bitcoin sale and the $1.5 billion buyback of 2029 convertible notes
- $6.7 billion in convertible notes raises significant default risk by 2028
- Lingham's fix: stop buying Bitcoin, stop diluting shareholders, and wait it out — the one thing Saylor won't do
- The central question: 'Who removes the biggest buyer of Bitcoin — him or the market?'

## Overview
Laura Shin interviews Vinny Lingham about the collapse of MicroStrategy's Bitcoin accumulation strategy. Lingham maps how the empire breaks — through mNAV discount triggers, convertible debt risks, and the fundamental instability of leveraged Bitcoin buying — and proposes the one fix Saylor won't implement.

## Implications
MicroStrategy's collapse is a cautionary tale about leveraged crypto exposure. The convertible debt timeline (2028 default risk) creates a structural overhang on Bitcoin markets. If Saylor is forced to stop buying, it removes the single largest consistent Bitcoin buyer from the market.
