# Ben Carlson: The Numbers That Break Your Brain About Long Term Investing | #637

## Key Points
- Ben Carlson unpacks the counterintuitive math behind long-term investing
- Picking the wrong asset every year still produces acceptable returns over long time horizons — the math of compounding works regardless
- The secret to investing is staying invested through inevitable downturns rather than timing the market
- Tax management represents a new form of alpha that most investors overlook
- Inflation's impact on different asset classes varies significantly — understanding this is crucial for portfolio construction
- Japan's 'lost decade' provides cautionary lessons about concentration and diversification
- Hyperactive traders consistently underperform patient, disciplined investors

## Overview
Meb Faber interviews Ben Carlson about the counterintuitive math of long-term investing. They cover why even poor annual picks compound into acceptable returns, the power of tax management, inflation's differential impact, Japan's lessons, and why hyperactive trading destroys wealth.

## Implications
The biggest edge in investing isn't stock selection or timing — it's behavioral discipline and tax efficiency. Investors who understand the math of compounding over decades will make better decisions than those focused on short-term performance.
