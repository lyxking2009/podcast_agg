# Rory Johnston on Why His $200 Oil Prediction Didn't Turn Out Right

## Key Points
- Rory Johnston made a high-profile $200/barrel oil prediction that didn't materialize, and he explains why
- The oil market structurally changed due to US shale production growth and OPEC+ discipline
- Energy transition dynamics are creating complex cross-currents in oil supply and demand forecasts
- The conversation explores how commodity analysts should think about tail risks vs. base cases
- Johnston discusses the challenges of making public market predictions and the accountability that comes with them

## Overview
Tracy Alloway and Joe Weisenthal interview Rory Johnston about his famous $200 oil prediction and what went wrong. The conversation covers structural changes in oil markets, the role of US shale, OPEC+ dynamics, and lessons about commodity forecasting.

## Implications
The oil market's structural transformation — driven by US shale and OPEC+ coordination — has permanently changed how analysts should model supply-side dynamics. Public predictions create accountability but also reveal the difficulty of commodity forecasting in a rapidly changing energy landscape.
