# The Chopping Block: Crypto's Brutal Week With FalconX's Josh Lim, CZ vs. Star, the Anthropic IPO, and Should Jensen Huang Come Back? — Unchained

## TL;DR
The Chopping Block panel dissects a brutal crypto week with BTC dropping to $62k, over $2 billion in liquidations, and a $20 billion stablecoin supply contraction. Guest Josh Lim attributes the selloff to AI hack fears, de-risking, positioning for the Anthropic IPO, and hidden leverage, with weekend selling concentrated in Bitcoin on Coinbase. The group also covers the CZ vs Star feud over Binance's role, Anthropic IPO scenarios, and debates whether Jensen Huang should return as Nvidia CEO amid scandal and export control pressure, with consensus against his return.

## Key points
- Crypto markets suffered a severe drawdown: BTC fell to $62k intraday, ETH to $1,800, SOL below $80, fear and greed index at 10, over $2 billion in leveraged long liquidations, six consecutive red weeks with a 23% drop from the November 4 local high, and $2 billion in outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs in the latest session.
- Stablecoin supply contracted by roughly $20 billion, breaking the narrative of stablecoin growth as a fundamental strength, driven by de-risking from onchain strategies, AI-assisted hack fears, and removal of assets from centralized exchanges.
- Josh Lim of FalconX identified hidden leverage buildup as a key driver: Bitcoin open interest reached range highs before the crash, and significant hedging via iBit puts saw up to $40 million in premium spent on end-of-year puts struck at $55k-$60k equivalent.
- Weekend selling was concentrated in Bitcoin and on Coinbase, with volumes 2-3x normal for BTC versus 1-1.5x for alts, suggesting specific institutional participants, possibly levered DAT vehicles facing debt payments, were forced to sell into illiquid markets.
- The upcoming Anthropic IPO is absorbing risk capital: total IPO size is in the $60 billion range and oversubscribed 6x at an implied valuation of $1.3-$1.7 trillion, prompting crypto holders to sell Bitcoin and stablecoins as the most liquid risk assets in their portfolios.
- On the CZ vs Star feud: CZ attributed the crash to ETF flows, hot inflation, midterms, AI fears, and the Anthropic IPO; Star accused CZ of lying and blamed Binance for the crash, citing the hack and alleged selling. CZ denied Binance sold any crypto, stating Binance was buying with stablecoins, while Binance CEO Hui showed a personal loss of $90 million.
- Josh Lim does not find the Binance-selling theory plausible: Binance needed to buy back missing assets after the hack (only a couple hundred million), but for altcoins the net impact is negative because buybacks from foundations increase circulating supply, and North Korea's swapping of stolen assets back to ETH creates sell pressure.
- The Hyperliquid (HYPE) token faced a critical overhang when a large $50-$60 million leveraged position was near liquidation at the $58 level; because the largest traders are also the largest holders, HYPE is reflexive and was sold down to fund accounts, though funds are now buying back as HYPE outperforms on the rebound.
- Josh Lim expects continued near-term pain: with hot CPI pushing Fed hike odds to 92%, political uncertainty, and rate hikes likely into Q1, the market may see a one-month malaise, possibly a Santa Claus rally in late December, but a real recovery likely only in January-February after Anthropic IPO digestion.
- The panel debated Anthropic IPO scenarios: if it does well, long-term it could benefit crypto as wealth transfers to AI employees who are more likely to buy digital assets after lockup expiry around April/May; if it does poorly, portfolio weights between AI and crypto may stay similar, reducing rotation pressure.
- Tarun Chitra noted the apathy in the current bear market resembles past bottoms, with many traders down 92% and voices leaving the room, but the key difference is unclear next catalyst because stablecoin volumes are rising while market caps fall, and adoption has decoupled from asset prices.
- On Nvidia's CEO scandal: Jensen Huang resigned after revelations of affairs with alleged spy Alice Wong and subordinate Kimberly Powell, plus a third affair; the CFO was installed as interim CEO, and there are calls from tech leaders and Trump to consider his return, but the panel overwhelmingly believes he should not come back.
- Robert Leshner argued Jensen crossed a major line either intentionally or through foolishness, and if he was that foolish he deserves to go; he also said Nvidia might actually run faster without him after the scandal, despite Jensen being one of the greatest founders.
- Tom Schmidt, while acknowledging Jensen's pivotal role in Nvidia's survival through many near-death moments, said he hopes for a return but doesn't think it will happen; he noted that all suggested replacements seem 'mid' and unable to take the same risks.
- Haseeb Qureshi emphasized that this is not like Sam Altman: Jensen potentially circumventing national security by selling chips to China and lying to the board about affairs is disqualifying, and tech people calling for his return are miscalibrated, especially with Democrats taking Congress and public opinion against AI.
