# #505 – GTA 6, Crime, Jewel Heists, Prison, Corrupt Cops, Mafia, Miami & Money — Lex Fridman Podcast

## TL;DR
Larry Lawton, a former prolific jewelry thief associated with the Gambino crime family, discusses the realism of Grand Theft Auto 6, his extensive experience casing and robbing over 25 jewelry stores, and his eventual arrest by the FBI. He reflects on his decade in federal prison, including 11 months in solitary confinement, and his transformation into a youth mentor whose Reality Check program boasts a 90% success rate in keeping at-risk kids out of jail. The conversation also covers Miami's drug-fueled history, corrupt cops, and his proposals for prison reform.

## Key points
- Larry Lawton robbed at least 25 jewelry stores across the East Coast after his first robbery was a setup for insurance fraud; he was associated with the Gambino crime family and avoided New York because stores there were mob-protected.
- Casing was the most critical part of a heist: Lawton spent two to six weeks studying a store, including delivery schedules, employee habits, sun glare on windows, and neighboring businesses; he says people are habitual so technology like cameras can be circumvented.
- He targeted wholesale jewelers and prioritized loose diamonds, which are easy to carry and fence; he received 25-30% of value from fences, with standard Rolex watches paying a flat $2,000 each.
- Lawton was caught by FBI agent Matt Mullen after a six-year investigation; he was offered two years in prison and allowed to keep $6.1 million if he cooperated, but refused because his father taught him that your word is all you have.
- He served over 12 years in federal prison, including 11 months in the Special Housing Unit (SHU), where he was stripped, beaten, and urinated on; he sued the Bureau of Prisons over a masturbation charge and succeeded in changing policy.
- In prison, he observed that the Aryan Brotherhood, Mexican Mafia, and other gangs operate with strict codes and often cooperate; he became a legal helper for shot callers and ran a bookmaking operation using books of postage stamps as currency.
- Lawton credits mental resilience, reading a book a day, and the compassion of one guard with saving him from suicide and insanity during solitary confinement.
- After release at age 46, he was unexpectedly asked by Judge Rymann to present to judges; two people were sentenced to his nonexistent program, forcing him to create Reality Check, which now reports a 90% success rate in keeping kids out of jail and a 43% improvement in educational outcomes and 31% increase in attendance.
- He advocates a zero-cost prison reform: an empowered inspection team of ex-inmate, former guard, warden, and medical staff with authority to enter any prison unannounced, firing anyone who blocks them; he says this would curb violence and abuse quickly.
- Lawton argues that most traffic stops and minor laws are a 'money grab' that taxes the poor and creates an 'us against them' mentality; he trains police to first ask if a driver is okay before demanding license and registration.
- He describes Miami as built on drug money, citing Willy Falcon and Sal Magluta, who ran a massive cocaine trafficking organization; Lawton himself kept $50,000 in his trunk to pay off corrupt cops during that era.
- Regarding GTA 6, Lawton is eager to see if Rockstar accurately simulates casing; he says the trailer's opening scene is unrealistic because no criminal operation would allow unknown people to walk in, and a dog that doesn't bark implies inside connections.
- He recounts his Coast Guard service during the Mariel boatlift, seeing dead bodies and later a Cuban gunboat confrontation, which shaped his view that soldiers need mental health support.
