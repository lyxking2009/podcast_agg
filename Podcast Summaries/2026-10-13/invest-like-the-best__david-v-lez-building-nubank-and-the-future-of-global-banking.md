## Overview

Patrick O'Shaughnessy interviews David Vélez, founder and CEO of Nubank, five years after their previous conversation when Nubank had 35 million customers; it now has 140 million. Vélez argues the fintech transformation is in its early innings, not its late ones: global consumer financial services is roughly a $10 trillion industry, about 90% still flows through bank branches owned by incumbents, and all neobanks and fintechs combined hold less than 10%. He covers the roughly $600 billion US consumers pay for bank branches they do not need, how 30 million people shut out by traditional lenders became creditworthy, data as a moat, and why AI strengthens the case for returning to the office.

## Key Points

- Nubank has scaled from 35 million customers five years ago to 140 million today, run with about 10,000 employees.
- Vélez rejects the "late innings of fintech" framing: financial services is a ~$10T industry and 90% still goes through incumbent bank branches, with all fintechs holding under 10%.
- He describes roughly $600 billion in annual US consumer cost as effectively a tax paid for branch infrastructure customers do not need.
- Nubank's credit model made 30 million people previously shut out by traditional lenders creditworthy, by using data rather than traditional credit histories.
- Data is framed as the durable moat, not the app or the brand.
- When Nubank took AI seriously, and how AI changed internal operations — including Vélez's argument that AI strengthens the case for in-office work.
- Nubank's planned US expansion, and what he would expect a startup attacking Nubank to do.
- He discusses stablecoins and the end of cash, agents as a customer segment, and the bear case for Nubank.
- Founder versus CEO: what he learned from Disney and Amazon, and what motivates him now.

## Implications

If Vélez is right that 90% of a $10T industry still sits with incumbents, then the competitive question for fintech investors is not "who has the best app" but "who can underwrite credit that incumbents cannot." The stablecoin and agent-as-customer threads are the forward-looking risk to incumbent deposit franchises — agents transacting on behalf of users will optimise away the frictions that currently protect bank economics.
