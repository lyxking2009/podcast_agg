## Overview

Sonya Huang (Sequoia Capital) interviews David Silver and Lasse Espeholt, co-founders of Ineffable Intelligence. Silver built AlphaGo on human data, then removed human data entirely for AlphaGo Zero, which surpassed AlphaGo in under two days; he left DeepMind to run that same bet toward superintelligence. Their plan is a "superlearner" — an agent that starts knowing nothing and discovers science, mathematics and language for itself through direct experience rather than a pretraining run. Silver argues human-language reasoning is a ceiling rather than a foundation, that alignment via chain-of-thought monitoring is already hitting its limits, and that the science of how superintelligences coexist should be worked out in simulation before anything touches the real world. Espeholt describes the GPU cluster and the lineage from IMPALA and SEED RL that makes the experiment possible. Silver's forecast: strong evidence within a year, superintelligence in three.

## Key Points

- The AlphaGo to AlphaGo Zero lesson: removing human data produced a stronger system in under two days — the bet Ineffable is built on.
- A "superlearner" is defined as an agent that starts knowing nothing and acquires science, maths and language through direct experience, rather than through a pretraining pass over human text.
- Silver's key claim: human-language reasoning is a ceiling, not a foundation.
- He argues alignment via chain-of-thought monitoring is already reaching its limits — relevant because CoT monitoring is a mainstay of current safety practice.
- Core design principle: "Don't breadcrumb the objective" — simple rewards with emergent complexity, rather than hand-shaped intermediate targets.
- Crucibles: the environments where the superlearner is meant to discover structure for itself.
- The Go observation that one agent is boring and two is infinite — suggesting multi-agent dynamics are a source of open-ended progress.
- The open question of whether agents will rediscover language.
- Espeholt covers the GPU cluster and the technical lineage from IMPALA and SEED RL.
- Silver's timeline: strong evidence within a year, superintelligence in three.

## Implications

If the superlearner approach works, it undermines the assumption that scale on human text is the path to general capability — which would reprice the value of pretraining data and the labs' current moats. The safety implication is sharper: the episode argues the field's main current tool for detecting misalignment is close to its limits at the same moment as a deliberate attempt to build a system that does not reason in human language.
