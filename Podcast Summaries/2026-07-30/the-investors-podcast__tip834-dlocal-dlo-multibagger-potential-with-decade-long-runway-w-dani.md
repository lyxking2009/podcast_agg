# TIP834: DLocal (DLO): Multibagger Potential with Decade-Long Runway w/ Daniel Mahncke & Shawn O'Malley

**Show:** The Investor's Podcast (We Study Billionaires)
**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Transcript source:** podscripts.co (full transcript)

## Key Points
- **What DLocal does:** DLocal (NASDAQ: DLO) is a B2B emerging-markets payments company, spun off from AstroPay (founded in Uruguay in 2016) after its founders realized the business-to-business opportunity was larger than the consumer-facing one. It became the first Uruguayan unicorn. DLocal sells merchants a single API that replaces the need to integrate separately with dozens of local acquirers, licenses, tax regimes, and banking systems across 60+ emerging markets. Customers include Amazon, Meta, Netflix, Uber, Spotify, and Alibaba/Pinduoduo.
- **Why it's different from Visa/Mastercard:** Visa and Mastercard are largely messaging/settlement networks built on Western credit-card rails, which are far less dominant in emerging markets. Local systems like Pix (Brazil), UPI (India), and Verve (Nigeria) are bank-to-bank rails created by local central banks, often with legal restrictions on cross-border currency conversion. DLocal exists to navigate this fragmentation.
- **Two megatrends:** The investment thesis rests on (1) growth and digitalization of emerging markets/Latin America, and (2) global tech giants expanding market share into those regions and increasingly relying on DLocal to process payments there. CEO Pedro Arnt (formerly Mercado Libre's CFO for over a decade) describes DLocal as "the single most precise proxy" for how the Magnificent Seven and other global digital leaders are performing across Latam, Africa, the Middle East, and Asia.
- **Customer/geographic concentration risk:** Latin America is ~80% of revenue, with Brazil, Argentina, and Mexico making up about half of total revenue (80% of LatAm revenue). The top 10 of ~760 enterprise customers account for 62% of revenue. Net revenue retention was 150% in 2023, dipped to 130% in 2024, and recovered to ~145% in 2025, staying above 140% for four straight quarters into 2026.
- **The take-rate debate:** DLocal's take rate (gross profit / total payment volume) fell from 2.9% at its 2020 peak to 0.9% today. Hosts debate whether this reflects a structural "race to the bottom" in payments (Shawn's worry) or a deliberate strategy (Daniel's view, echoing CEO Pedro Arnt) of offering volume discounts to lock in large merchants, betting that scale economies shared (à la Nick Sleep) and future consolidation/pricing power will stabilize or lift take rates. Take rate and margin are explicitly distinguished — operating leverage means net profit can still grow faster than gross profit even as the take rate declines.
- **Moat discussion:** Neither host believes individual DLocal products (Smart Pigs/Smart APMs for recurring billing, smart payment routing, BNPL aggregation) are truly uncopyable — Brazil's government-run "Pix Automático" already threatens to replicate recurring-payment functionality. The more durable advantages cited are scale (60+ markets, 600+ local payment integrations, 38 regulatory licenses plus more in process), regulatory head-start (licenses can take years to obtain), and data/network effects from routing 3.5B+ pay-in transactions annually.
- **Stablecoins:** DLocal launched a "Stablecoin Hub" in April 2026. Two-thirds of global stablecoin holdings sit in emerging markets as a hedge against local currency depreciation (Argentina alone did ~$34B in stablecoin transactions in a year). Daniel argues stablecoins mainly make DLocal's settlement cheaper rather than disintermediating it, since consumers still need pesos/local currency and full compliance handling, not a USDC wallet balance — referencing a "last mile" argument from the earlier Remitly episode.
- **Muddy Waters short report (2022):** DLocal's stock fell 50% in a day after a Muddy Waters short report alleging overstated TPV, unrealistically high take rates, commingled merchant/business funds, and $1B of insider selling after IPO lockup expiration. An independent review verified merchant and corporate cash were properly segregated; no merchants left the company after the report. A separate federal lawsuit concerned inadequate disclosure of Argentina control risks — after which the company's CFO, Pedro Arnt, became CEO, a change Daniel views very positively.
- **Management and incentives:** Insider ownership (founders plus management) is about 33% of the company. Pedro Arnt owns roughly 0.8%. Because DLocal is incorporated in the Cayman Islands (despite being headquartered in Uruguay), it's exempt from typical U.S. pay-disclosure requirements; blended management compensation was about $20M in 2024, up from ~$5M two years earlier. Stock-based comp is small relative to revenue (0.2%) but more material against gross profit (~5%); the mix leans heavily toward time-vested restricted stock rather than performance-based awards, which Daniel flags as a negative.
- **Argentina dynamics:** Argentina is ~20% of LatAm revenue. Historically a highly profitable market due to currency-control complexity (which let DLocal charge high fees to move money out of the country), Argentina's margin has become more volatile and, following Javier Milei's removal of currency controls, structurally lower — though also lower-risk and higher quality of earnings.
- **Capital allocation:** DLocal pays a dividend of about 30% of free cash flow (yield ~3-4%) and authorized a new $300M buyback in March 2026 (on top of ~$100M buybacks each in 2023 and 2024), representing roughly 7-8% of the company over two-to-three years. Little to no M&A history — DLocal only acquired a single technology asset (~$23M) from Kenya-based Asaak/AZA Finance after a broader acquisition talks stalled.
- **Financial trajectory and valuation:** TPV grew to about $40B last year (+60% YoY; ~80% CAGR since 2019), with management guiding 60% TPV growth for 2026. Daniel models TPV growth at ~38% through 2028, decelerating to 20%, with the take rate declining toward ~0.7% over five years, implying gross profit CAGR of 19-20% and continued operating-leverage-driven margin expansion (the "V-shaped" TPV → gross profit → net profit dynamic). Combined with buybacks, Daniel's base-case model — using a conservative low-teens exit multiple and ~20% margin of safety — implies roughly 22% expected annual returns from current levels (stock trading ~$14-15 at recording, versus Daniel's own average cost basis near $10). Comparable/reference companies discussed include Visa, Amazon, Sea Limited, Mercado Libre, Shopify, Stripe, and Adyen (DLO trades at ~15x earnings vs. Adyen's ~25x).
- **Portfolio decision:** Daniel and Shawn add DLocal to the Intrinsic Value Portfolio as a 2% position (matching Wise's weight, for ~4% combined payments exposure), citing DLocal's own volatility as a reason to start small and add on weakness rather than take a larger initial stake, given the portfolio already holds its target of 15 companies.

## Overview
Daniel Mahncke pitches Shawn O'Malley on DLocal (NASDAQ: DLO), a B2B payments company built to solve the fragmentation of emerging-market payment rails — enabling global merchants like Amazon, Netflix, Spotify, and Uber to accept and disburse payments across Latin America, Africa, and parts of Asia through a single API rather than building out dozens of local integrations, licenses, and banking relationships themselves. The conversation traces DLocal's origins as a 2016 spin-off of consumer-facing AstroPay, its rapid rise to become the first Uruguayan unicorn, and CEO Pedro Arnt's background as Mercado Libre's longtime CFO, which Daniel treats as a strong signal of quality.

Much of the episode wrestles with the central bear case for any payments business: a declining take rate (down from 2.9% to 0.9% since 2020). Daniel argues this reflects deliberate strategy rather than pure competitive pressure — DLocal offers volume discounts to lock in large merchants and grow total payment volume, betting that scale, regulatory head starts (38+ licenses across markets that can take years to obtain), and eventual industry consolidation will let it act as a "price influencer" over time. The hosts also address customer and geographic concentration (top 10 customers are 62% of revenue; LatAm is ~80% of revenue), the 2022 Muddy Waters short report and subsequent independent verification of DLocal's finances, Argentina-specific currency-control dynamics, and the potential impact of stablecoins on DLocal's settlement business.

On financials and capital return, DLocal is framed as a "cash-printing machine" that pairs continued high TPV growth (60%+ guided for 2026) with operating leverage, a ~30%-of-free-cash-flow dividend, and an active buyback program ($300M authorized in March 2026). Daniel's valuation model, using a conservative growth deceleration and exit multiple, implies roughly 22% expected annual returns. The episode ends with Daniel and Shawn agreeing to add DLocal to their Intrinsic Value Portfolio as a 2% position, citing the stock's history of volatility as a reason to size in gradually rather than take a full position immediately.

## Implications
For investors, DLocal represents a leveraged bet on two intersecting trends — emerging-market digitalization and Western tech giants' expansion into those markets — with a payments toll-booth business model that benefits from both. The take-rate decline is the key risk to monitor: if DLocal cannot eventually stabilize pricing power through scale/consolidation, margins could compress further even as volume grows, and the hosts explicitly warn that a "bear case" scenario (margins declining instead of expanding) could roughly halve the stock from current levels. The customer and geographic concentration (especially reliance on a handful of large merchants and on Brazil/Argentina/Mexico) means idiosyncratic client or country-level shocks (e.g., a major merchant bringing payments in-house, or renewed Argentine currency instability) could create material short-term volatility, even if the long-term secular thesis remains intact. Investors also need to weigh the Cayman Islands incorporation (which limits visibility into executive compensation) and a stock-based compensation structure weighted toward time-vested rather than performance-based awards as governance considerations. Overall, the episode frames DLocal as a cheap (~15x earnings), high-growth, cash-generative compounder candidate suitable for a smaller, incrementally-built position given its historical volatility.
