# Trucking Is Booming Again, And Drivers Aren't Happy About It — Odd Lots

## TL;DR
Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway revisit the trucking market with Red Lustilow, chief marketing officer of Truck Parking Club, to explain why freight is booming again after a three-plus-year lull — and why drivers are stressed about it. The surge is driven less by roaring demand than by structural supply destruction: the DOT's crackdown on non-domiciled CDLs and English-language proficiency under Secretary Sean Duffy, a Supreme Court ruling (the Montgomery case) that threatens freight brokers with carrier-liability exposure, and a post-COVID shakeout. Rates have climbed from $1.94 to over $3 per mile on the Internet Truck Stop Van Rate Index, flatbed hauling is booming off data-center construction, and Knight-Swift has outperformed META year-to-date. The catch: a structurally constrained capacity base means the next demand spike may not be met the way it was during COVID, and the shortage of truck parking has become a market in itself.

## Key points
- Freight stocks have run hard: Knight-Swift has outperformed META year-to-date and JB Hunt nearly doubled; "forget about AI, the money apparently is in trucking" — but the hosts stress trucking is one of the most cyclical industries in the economy, with mini-cycles inside every boom and bust.
- The Internet Truck Stop Van Rate Index went from ~$1.94/mile in December to above $3/mile, before easing slightly — a clear sign of tightening capacity and rising costs.
- Why rates are up: after a mid-2022-to-2025 lull, talking heads blamed demand, but the real driver has been supply destruction — DOT under Sean Duffy (from ~June 2025) cracked down on non-domiciled CDLs and English-language proficiency, chopping capacity out of the market.
- Non-domiciled CDLs: issued to foreign nationals legally allowed to work but not domiciled in the US; scrutiny grew after the "freight bros" community found licenses literally reading "No Name Given" as a first name and hundreds of trucking companies registered to single PO boxes; California, Minnesota and New York had 10x spikes in CDL issuance around COVID.
- The Supreme Court's Montgomery ruling puts freight-broker liability for carrier negligence in question — brokers move roughly 30% of freight volumes, and now safety ratings and carrier selection face new scrutiny, pushing marginal capacity out and making safe capacity more expensive.
- Spot vs contract rate spreads and anecdotes from owner-operators confirm the squeeze; driver turnover at some fleets exceeds 100% a year, and with supply shrinking, pay should rise for drivers who fit the new compliance profile.
- Flatbed has been booming because data centers under construction need physical goods moved — the AI buildout is a freight story too.
- Truck parking is a structural bottleneck: parking capacity is expensive and politically hard to build, so Truck Parking Club built an Airbnb-style marketplace that unlocks millions of private parking spaces for drivers.
- The deeper concern: barriers to entry are now much higher, so if a COVID-level demand event hits again, the market likely cannot flood in new capacity the way it did before — structurally higher rates ahead.
