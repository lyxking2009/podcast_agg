# What Chess.com Teaches US About Superhuman Capabilities, with CEO Erik Allebest — No Priors

## TL;DR
Sarah Guo talks with Chess.com CEO Erik Allebest about the central paradox of superhuman AI: Deep Blue, Stockfish and neural-net engines were supposed to kill chess, yet AI made the game bigger than ever. Allebest walks through bootstrapping Chess.com from a $56,000 bankruptcy-auction domain purchase in 2005 to ~250 million registered members and $200M+ in projected 2026 revenue with zero venture capital, how AI tools (game review, personalized puzzles, an AI coach) accelerate human skill development, the statistical anti-cheating systems that protect ratings, and why he is exporting the chess playbook to poker via Gambit. He closes with a deliberately unfashionable take: AGI is less a technology problem than a cultural problem of concentration and guardrails.

## Key points
- Allebest and a friend bought the chess.com domain (the remains of a failed chess-software business) at a 2005 bankruptcy auction for $56,000; Sand Hill VCs called it uninvestable and a Stanford classmate declined to join because he wanted to do something more serious.
- The company bootstrapped: zero paid customer acquisition, remote from day one, funded by profits from prior businesses plus a small loan from a friend's mother; profitable within ~18 months of the 2007 launch and never sought venture capital.
- Scale as of the August 2026 conversation: ~10M daily active users, 40-50M monthly actives, 250M+ registered members, ~650 fully remote employees, and projected 2026 revenue just over $200M; mission is to get a billion people to play chess.
- Superhuman AI did not kill chess: Deep Blue's 1997 win triggered predictions of irrelevance that never materialized; Stockfish-era engines made endgames "perfect" and boring, but the neural-net generation (Leela Chess Zero) played aggressive, unconventional chess that pushed humans forward.
- AI products at Chess.com: game review, personalized puzzle generation, an in-development chat-interactive AI coach, plus LLMs in support automation, data analysis and agentic development (internal GNS auth/knowledge layer).
- Learning still requires reps: "if you do five chess puzzles a day, multiply that by a thousand, you've done 5,000 chess puzzles"; AI collapses access to information, not the need for practice; seven-year-olds now compete near Grandmaster level.
- Anti-cheating is existential infrastructure: proprietary statistical/ML models know what human play and computer play look like across full data history; detection is split between platform-wide and professional prize-event workstreams; methodology is kept secret to avoid giving cheaters a roadmap.
- Chess.com's moat is product, community and content — chess is unpatentable and there are tens of thousands of apps; Lichess coexists as "the Linux of chess."
- The playbook is being exported to poker via Gambit (gambit.com), where a rating system is the central design choice; Allebest argues ratings will rival money in importance.
- On AGI, Allebest argues the binding constraint is cultural — concentration and guardrails — rather than pure capability; he also discusses the risk that AI users disengage their own brains (LLMs inflating egos, telling users what they want to hear).
