# DEX in the City: A Founder's Death Sparked a Fight for Ondo's Boardroom — Unchained (DEX in the City)

## TL;DR
The DEX in the City crew (Jessi Brooks, Katherine Kirkpatrick Bos) breaks down the bitter corporate-control fight erupting at Ondo Finance, the tokenized real-world-asset issuer, after the death of founder Nathan Allman in late May 2026. Allman died as CEO, sole director and majority shareholder of a charter that allowed two board seats — one vacant — leaving "no directors left" and management mechanisms paralyzed. His mother, Caitlin Allman, was appointed administrator of the estate through Hawaii probate on June 26, and now alleges that then-president Ian De Bode exploited the interim window to declare himself CEO, appoint himself sole director, and act unilaterally. The Delaware lawsuit seeks to void De Bode's appointment and all subsequent decisions; the board was expanded to four seats and voted on July 24 to remove him, leaving two centers of control, each claiming legitimacy, while the tokenized RWA sector (roughly $31-34B as of May 2026) watches. ## Key points
- Nathan Allman, Ondo's founder, died unexpectedly in late May 2026 at age 32; at his death he held the roles of CEO, sole director and majority shareholder, and the company's charter allowed two board seats with one vacant — "When Nate died, there were no directors left."
- The death paralyzed governance: no one could appoint a new CEO or convene a meeting to form a new board, and the voting shares sat in the estate with no one authorized to manage them.
- The impasse lasted about a month until June 26, when a Hawaii court appointed Caitlin Allman administrator of the estate, granting her voting rights over the shares.
- The lawsuit alleges De Bode used the interim window to cite the charter for an automatic transition to CEO, then used a shareholder agreement to appoint himself sole director and act independently — hiring consultants, approving bonuses, adding a board member — and allegedly used corporate resources to pressure Allman into signing documents cementing his control.
- Allman first sought a peaceful resolution: she joined the board, approved a temporary operating procedure, and confirmed De Bode as president (not CEO); after rejection, she expanded the board from two to four seats — Gordon Liao declined, but Tania Towill (Nathan's sister) accepted, giving Allman a decisive second vote.
- On July 24 the two voted to remove De Bode from all positions; Allman assumed the roles of board chair, CEO, secretary and treasurer the same day — leaving two competing centers of control.
- The plaintiff asks the Delaware court to determine lawful control and prohibit extraordinary corporate actions until a verdict; an expedited trial is requested because the validity of contracts, expense orders and share issuances is in limbo.
- De Bode's position: "Caitlin Allman's decision to go to court is regrettable. This path clearly contradicts the interests of the company, its shareholders, the team, and the entire Ondo community" — adding that leading investors and the Ondo Foundation support the company.
- Market context: RWA tokenization reached roughly $31-34B by May 2026 (US Treasuries and Ethereum-based products leading); the control fight adds legal overhang to Ondo's institutional-facing, regulated-products business.
