# ROLLUP: Bitcoin's Fork Died in 2 Blocks | Saylor Sells Again | Robinhood Chain | Fidelity Staking — Bankless

## TL;DR
Ryan Sean Adams and David Hoffman recap a week in which Bitcoin's anti-Ordinals soft fork died on delivery (two blocks, ~2.5% of hash power), Michael Saylor sold Bitcoin for the third straight week (1,691 BTC into the 200-week moving average), Robinhood Chain cemented its status as the #1 Ethereum L2 by revenue in its first month ($3.6M July), and Fidelity filed to stake up to 100% of its ETH ETF and pay holders quarterly cash dividends. They also cover gold's PBOC-driven rebound, the FOMO-vs-Pump app war, the Clarity Act's fading odds (~18% on Polymarket), and the ETH issuance-cap EIP that looks effectively dead. Recorded and released a day early due to scheduling.

## Key points
- Bitcoin's purist fork failed: on Saturday Aug 8 at block 961,632, nodes enforcing BIP-110 (the anti-Ordinals, anti-arbitrary-data soft fork championed by the Luke Dash Jr camp) forked off; they drew only 2.53% of hash power versus a 55% goal and, having forgotten to adjust difficulty, mined ~2 blocks before the chain effectively died. No exchanges listed it. Nick Carter called it the death of Bitcoin maximalism; Jameson Lopp said he would not unblock BIP-110 supporters.
- Saylor sells again: 1,691 BTC sold over the week, adding ~$650M to the USD reserve; STRC preferred shares now trade above 95 (near 100) after the raise, but it is the third week in a row of sales, from an average cost basis around $75K — "he's selling for a loss." Both Bitcoin and Ether ETFs have been net buyers since June, so "Saylor's just handing his Bitcoin to BlackRock."
- Bitcoin sits a "fingernail" below the 200-week moving average (~$63.3K vs ~$64K MA); Ryan argues buying when crypto is boring is the right time; David leans moderately more bearish (40% odds we've bottomed), keeping an open mind to Ben Cowan's October-bottom call.
- Gold is back: +14% since July 17 to ~$4,500, clearing that level for the first time since June 5; Michael Howell attributes the move to the PBOC resuming liquidity injections (China's store-of-value demand, given crypto bans and burnt property investors); the Bitcoin/gold ratio shows a ~69% drawdown, off its bottom.
- Robinhood Chain: #1 Ethereum L2 by revenue with $3.6M in July (its first full month) — ~38% of all L2 revenue; app-layer protocol TVL ~$1B, over $1B stablecoins, stock-token TVL tripled ($10M to $30M); Base is now #3 behind Polygon PoS. A Cole (Pudgy Penguins creator) NFT mint — Spritehood, 37,000 NFTs at $17 — sold out for ~$1.3M.
- FOMO vs Pump: FOMO is the breakout consumer wallet of the cycle (40K new signups/month, 30K new active traders, 400-500K trades) — essentially a social trading front-end for PumpFun; Pump wants to own the whole vertical, so the two are fighting over users, fees and token rebates.
- Fidelity filed to stake up to 100% of its ETH ETF holdings (~$1B AUM) and pay quarterly cash distributions — setting up a live experiment vs BlackRock's staked ETF (which accrues yield in NAV instead of paying dividends); Ryan argues Wall Street likes ETH precisely for its yield ("internet bond").
- ETH issuance-cap EIP: effectively dead — "you need a lot of consensus to change ETH monetary policy and we don't even have a little bit"; EF-adjacent people won't spend social capital defending it; Jerome remains publicly committed. Ryan: annualized ETH issuance is under 0.8%, same as or less than Bitcoin — a good place to be.
- Clarity Act at ~18% on Polymarket: a September vote is expected but delays benefit Democrats and banks; silver lining is the Paul Atkins SEC potentially doing parts of Clarity by regulation (token safe harbor reports) and stablecoin yield surviving.
- Venice (VVV) on-chain burn hit 7 consecutive days of all-time highs (~$14K/day on Aug 10) despite token price falling from its $20 blow-off top.
